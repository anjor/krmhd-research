\section{Lagrangian linearity}\label{sec:lagrangian}

By theorem~\ref{thm:slice}, each slice is a passive scalar advected by the velocity $\boldsymbol U_u = \uE + su\bhat$ of (\ref{eq:Uu}). The $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ part of this velocity is the same for all slices, and the streaming part is directed along the perturbed field. When the field lines are frozen into the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, the flow moves the field lines and the slices together, so that in the frame of the flow each slice streams along field lines that do not move. In this section we make this statement exact, list the hypotheses it needs, and derive its consequences for the quantities that are used to diagnose transfer in velocity space: the Hermite spectrum and flux, the slice correlations, and the distribution of the compressive fluctuations over wavenumbers along the field.

\subsection{Earlier statements}\label{lag:earlier}

The linearity of the KRMHD kinetic equation in the Lagrangian frame of the Alfv\'enic flow is due to \citet[\S6.3]{Schekochihin2009}. They transformed the kinetic equation to the Lagrangian frame of the flow $\uE$, used the Cauchy solution for the perturbed magnetic field to show that in this frame $\bhat\cdot\nabla$ is the derivative along the field lines taken at $t = 0$ (their equations 214--216), and concluded that the kinetic equation is linear in that frame. In their \S6.2.4 they remarked that the ballistic response $\exp(-\ii k_\parallel v_\parallel t)$ is also the nonlinear solution if $t$ and $k_\parallel$ are read as Lagrangian variables, and in \S6.3 they drew the consequence that the density and field-strength fluctuations do not cascade to small scales along the moving field lines, an apparent cascade in $k_z$ being due to the perpendicular deformation of the field lines. \citet[\S\S2.6 and 4.1]{Kunz2015} restated the result for pressure-anisotropic plasmas: in the Lagrangian frame of the Alfv\'enic fluctuations, the compressive fluctuations obey a one-dimensional linear equation that can be solved independently of the Alfv\'enic turbulence. For a passive scalar in MHD turbulence, \citet[\S2.3.1]{LithwickGoldreich2001} had observed that without dissipation the scalar is frozen to the field lines and cannot cascade along them, and argued that dissipation, which cannot be neglected, restores a parallel cascade. The lemma underneath these statements is the classical one that $\boldsymbol B/\rho$ is Lie-dragged by the flow, which gives Cauchy's solution of the ideal induction equation \citep[example~3]{Webb2014}.

We add three things to these statements. We write out the solution operator (theorem~\ref{thm:frozen}). We state the hypotheses explicitly and identify the terms that violate them (\S\ref{lag:breaking}). And we use the fact that the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ map preserves volume to turn the theorem into exact statements about volume-integrated statistics, which are the subject of corollaries~\ref{cor:singlepoint}--\ref{cor:kpar}. None of the works cited above considers the Hermite spectrum or echoes.

\subsection{The frozen-line theorem}\label{lag:theorem}

We treat one compressive branch at a time and write its state at the point $\boldsymbol r$ as $g(\boldsymbol r,t)$. In the continuum, $g$ is the function $G(\boldsymbol r,\cdot,t)$ of $u$ and $L$ is the operator (\ref{eq:L}); in a truncated hierarchy, $g = (g_0,\dots,g_M)$ is the vector of Hermite coefficients and $L$ stands for the ladder matrix $A$. With a velocity-space operator $\mathcal C$ for collisions or hypercollisions, the kinetic equation (\ref{eq:branch}) reads
\begin{equation}\label{lag:kin}
\partial_t g + \{\Phi,g\} + s\gradpar(Lg) = -\mathcal C g .
\end{equation}
The Laguerre sectors with $l\ge2$ obey the same equation with $\Lambda = \infty$, and all branches and sectors are advected by the same flow (\ref{eq:sameflow}), so everything in this section applies to the whole compressive distribution at once.

We write $D_t = \partial_t + \{\Phi,\cdot\}$ for the convective derivative, $\gradpar(t) = \bhat(t)\cdot\nabla = \partial_z + \{\Psi(\cdot,t),\cdot\}$ for the parallel gradient at time $t$, $\langle\cdot\rangle$ for the volume average over the periodic box, and $\boldsymbol X_t$ for the flow map of $\uE$, defined by $\partial_t\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi) = \uE(\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi),t)$ and $\boldsymbol X_0(\boldsymbol\xi) = \boldsymbol\xi$. The point $\boldsymbol\xi$ labels the fluid element that is at $\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi)$ at time $t$. The relabelling operator
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal X_t h := h\circ\boldsymbol X_t^{-1}
\end{equation*}
takes a function $h$ of the labels to the function of position that has the value $h(\boldsymbol\xi)$ at the point $\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi)$. The residual of the flux equation,
\begin{equation*}
R := \partial_t\Psi + \{\Phi,\Psi\} - \partial_z\Phi ,
\end{equation*}
is proportional to the parallel electric field of the Alfv\'enic fluctuations, and the first of equations (\ref{eq:rmhd}) is the statement $R = 0$. The hypotheses are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyp}
\begin{minipage}{0.86\linewidth}
\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\leftmargin}{2.9em}\setlength{\labelwidth}{2.5em}\setlength{\labelsep}{0.4em}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.6ex}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{0pt}}
\item[(H1)] \emph{Ideal induction.} On $[0,T]$ the residual $R$ depends on $z$ and $t$ only. A function of $z$ and $t$ alone carries no $\boldsymbol b_\perp$; in GANDALF, $R = 0$. Nothing is assumed about the momentum equation: $\Phi$ may be forced, viscous or prescribed.
\item[(H2)] \emph{Kinetic equation.} The branch has $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, and $g$ obeys (\ref{lag:kin}), where $\mathcal C$ is a linear operator on the velocity variable that acts at each point $\boldsymbol r$ separately, with a kernel that depends neither on $\boldsymbol r$ nor on the fields (it may depend on $t$). The hypercollisions of GANDALF, which are diagonal in $m$, are an example. There is no spatial dissipation of $g$ and no source.
\item[(H3)] \emph{Smoothness.} $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are smooth functions of $\boldsymbol r$ on the periodic box and depend continuously on $t\in[0,T]$, where $T$ does not exceed the time up to which the Alfv\'enic fields stay smooth. Then $\boldsymbol X_t$ is a volume-preserving diffeomorphism of the box for every $t\in[0,T]$.
\end{list}
\end{minipage}
\end{equation}
Continuity in $t$ suffices, so white-noise forcing of $\Phi$, which gives fields that are continuous but not differentiable in time, is allowed. We write $\gradpar(0)$ for the parallel gradient of the initial field and define the frozen generator
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal L_0 := s\gradpar(0)L + \mathcal C .
\end{equation*}
Its solution operator $e^{-t\mathcal L_0}$ (the propagator, if $\mathcal C$ depends on $t$) solves the linear problem in which the field lines keep their initial shape and there is no flow.

\begin{theorem}[Frozen-line theorem]\label{thm:frozen}
Under the hypotheses (\ref{eq:hyp}), a solution $g$ of (\ref{lag:kin}) on $[0,T]$ that is smooth in $\boldsymbol r$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen}
g(t) = \mathcal X_t\,e^{-t\mathcal L_0}\,g(0), \qquad \mathcal L_0 = s\gradpar(0)L + \mathcal C, \qquad 0\le t\le T .
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the function of the labels $\hat g(\boldsymbol\xi,t) := g(\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi),t)$ obeys $\partial_t\hat g + s\gradpar(0)(L\hat g) = -\mathcal C\hat g$: in the labels, $g$ evolves as in the static field $\bhat(0)$ with no flow.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $\bhat = \zhat + \zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$ one has $\uE\times\bhat = \nabla_\perp\Phi + \{\Phi,\Psi\}\zhat$, and therefore
\begin{equation*}
\partial_t\bhat - \nabla\times(\uE\times\bhat) = \zhat\times\nabla R .
\end{equation*}
The right-hand side vanishes if and only if $R$ depends on $z$ and $t$ only, so (H1) is equivalent to the ideal induction equation for the full field $\bhat$. Because $\nabla\cdot\uE = 0$, the flow map preserves volume, and the ideal induction equation has Cauchy's solution
\begin{equation*}
\bhat\big(\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi),t\big) = \frac{\partial\boldsymbol X_t}{\partial\boldsymbol\xi}\,\bhat(\boldsymbol\xi,0) .
\end{equation*}
For a smooth function $h$ of the labels, the chain rule then gives $\bhat(t)\cdot\nabla(h\circ\boldsymbol X_t^{-1}) = [\bhat(0)\cdot\nabla h]\circ\boldsymbol X_t^{-1}$, that is,
\begin{equation}\label{lag:intertwine}
\gradpar(t)\,\mathcal X_t = \mathcal X_t\,\gradpar(0) .
\end{equation}
In RMHD variables the same relation follows from the identity
\begin{equation}\label{lag:commutator}
[D_t,\gradpar]\,f = \{R,f\},
\end{equation}
which holds for every $f$ and is the Jacobi identity for the bracket. Under (H1) the right-hand side vanishes. For a time-independent $h$ we have $D_t(\mathcal X_th) = 0$, hence $D_t(\gradpar(t)\mathcal X_th) = 0$, so $\gradpar(t)\mathcal X_th$ is carried by the flow from its initial value $\gradpar(0)h$, which is (\ref{lag:intertwine}).

Now write $g(t) = \mathcal X_t\hat g(t)$. By the chain rule and (\ref{lag:kin}), $\partial_t\hat g = (D_tg)\circ\boldsymbol X_t = -[s\gradpar(t)(Lg) + \mathcal Cg]\circ\boldsymbol X_t$. Because $L$ and $\mathcal C$ act on the velocity variable at each point, with kernels that do not depend on $\boldsymbol r$, they commute with composition by $\boldsymbol X_t$, and by (\ref{lag:intertwine}) $[\gradpar(t)(Lg)]\circ\boldsymbol X_t = \gradpar(0)(L\hat g)$. Hence $\partial_t\hat g = -\mathcal L_0\hat g$, with $\mathcal L_0$ independent of $t$ (apart from any time dependence of $\mathcal C$) and $\hat g(0) = g(0)$, which is (\ref{eq:frozen}).
\end{proof}

The proof uses the induction equation and the fact that $L$ and $\mathcal C$ act at each point, and nothing else. The branch operator acts at each point because the field equations of KRMHD are algebraic (\S\ref{sec:model}). The momentum equation never enters. In the labels the problem is linear, with coefficients that do not depend on time, whatever the Alfv\'enic turbulence does; the flow only relabels the points.

The algebra of the proof does not involve $\Lambda$; the condition $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$ in (H2) makes the frozen problem well posed. For $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, $L$ is self-adjoint in the free-energy product (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}) and $\gradpar(0)$ is antisymmetric on the periodic box, so $s\gradpar(0)L$ is antisymmetric in the product $\int[\cdot,\cdot]\,\dd^3r$, and without collisions $e^{-t\mathcal L_0}$ conserves the free energy. For $0<\Lambda<1$, $L$ has a pair of complex-conjugate eigenvalues $u_*$ and $\bar u_*$ (\S\ref{sec:slices}). In a uniform field, a mode with wavenumber $k$ along the field whose velocity profile is the corresponding eigenfunction then grows at the rate $s|k\,\mathrm{Im}\,u_*|$, which has no upper bound, so the frozen problem is ill-posed and (\ref{eq:frozen}) holds only for solutions that exist. The KRMHD branches have $\Lambda^+>1$ and $\Lambda^-<0$ for all parameters (\S\ref{sec:model}), and the Laguerre sectors have $\Lambda = \infty$, so in KRMHD the condition is always met.

Hypothesis (H3) is a genuine restriction. Whether solutions of the ideal three-dimensional RMHD equations stay smooth for all time is not known, so the theorem is a statement about the interval on which they do. Numerical checks are limited further, to times at which the Alfv\'enic fields themselves are resolved. In RK4 integrations of GANDALF's right-hand side \citep{Kanekar2025} with $\mathrm{rms}\,|\nabla_\perp\Phi| = \mathrm{rms}\,|\nabla_\perp\Psi| = 1.5$, Cauchy's formula for the flow alone is satisfied at $t = 0.5$ only to a relative error of $0.25$, $0.27$ and $0.17$ on $32^3$, $48^3$ and $64^3$ grids.

Without collisions, (\ref{eq:frozen}) decouples into slices. The transform $\Tr$ acts on $u$ alone, with a kernel that does not depend on position, so it commutes with $\mathcal X_t$ and with $\gradpar(0)$, and it takes $L$ to multiplication by $u$ (\S\ref{sec:slices}). The unit-weight amplitudes (\ref{eq:aunit}) therefore obey
\begin{equation}\label{lag:sliceform}
a_u(t) = \mathcal X_t\,T_{sut}\,a_u(0), \qquad T_\ell := e^{-\ell\gradpar(0)} .
\end{equation}
Because $\bhat(0)$ is smooth and divergence-free and has $\bhat(0)\cdot\zhat = 1$, the operator $T_\ell$ moves a function a distance $\Delta z = \ell$ along the field lines of $\bhat(0)$, and the $T_\ell$ form a group of unitary operators on $L^2$ of the box, $T_\ell T_{\ell'} = T_{\ell+\ell'}$. Each slice is carried rigidly along the initial field lines at the speed $su$, and the flow then relabels the points. This is the exact form of the remark of \citet[\S6.2.4]{Schekochihin2009} that the ballistic response is the nonlinear solution in Lagrangian variables. In a truncated hierarchy, (\ref{lag:sliceform}) holds for the $M+1$ slices of theorem~\ref{thm:trunc}, with $u$ replaced by the eigenvalues $u_j$. With collisions, $\mathcal C$ and $L$ do not commute in general, the exponential in (\ref{eq:frozen}) cannot be split, and the slices stay coupled through $\mathcal C$ as in (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}); the theorem still removes the flow.

\subsection{Single-point statistics}\label{lag:singlepointsec}

Since $\boldsymbol X_t$ preserves volume, $\int\mathcal X_th\,\dd^3r = \int h\,\dd^3\xi$ for every integrable $h$. Together with (\ref{lag:intertwine}), this removes the flow from every volume integral of a local function of $g$ and of its derivatives along the field.

\begin{corollary}[Single-point statistics]\label{cor:singlepoint}
Assume (\ref{eq:hyp}), and let $h(t) := e^{-t\mathcal L_0}g(0)$ be the solution of the frozen problem. For every function $O$ of the local state of $g$ and of finitely many of its derivatives along the field,
\begin{equation}\label{lag:singlepoint}
\int O\big(g,\gradpar(t)g,\gradpar(t)^2g,\dots\big)(\boldsymbol r,t)\,\dd^3r = \int O\big(h,\gradpar(0)h,\gradpar(0)^2h,\dots\big)(\boldsymbol\xi,t)\,\dd^3\xi .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By (\ref{eq:frozen}) and (\ref{lag:intertwine}), $\gradpar(t)^kg(t) = \mathcal X_t\gradpar(0)^kh(t)$ for every $k$. The integrand on the left is therefore $\mathcal X_t$ applied to the integrand on the right, and $\mathcal X_t$ preserves volume integrals.
\end{proof}

We call the left-hand side of (\ref{lag:singlepoint}) a single-point statistic and write it $\mathcal O_t[g]$; the subscript records the time at which the field-aligned derivatives are taken. The local state is all the velocity-space information at one point: the function $G(\boldsymbol r,\cdot)$, or all the $g_m(\boldsymbol r)$, of every branch and Laguerre sector. The corollary covers the Hermite spectrum $W(m,t)$ and the free energy; the variances of $\delta n$ and $\delta B_\parallel$; the slice energies and the full slice correlation matrix $C(u,u';t) = \langle a_ua_{u'}\rangle$, because the slice amplitudes are linear functions of the local state; the collisional dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\nu$, a volume average of $[g,\mathcal Cg]$; the Hermite flux $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ written with the full $\gradpar$ (\ref{eq:fluxCD}); and the one-point probability distributions of the $g_m$, with all their moments and quantiles. It does not cover spectra in $k_\perp$ or $k_z$ or fluxes resolved in $k_\perp$ shells, which involve Fourier filters that do not commute with $\mathcal X_t$; the flux computed with $\partial_z$ in place of $\gradpar$, which involves a derivative that is not taken along the field; or two-point statistics at a fixed Eulerian separation.

Without collisions, (\ref{lag:sliceform}) together with the group property and the unitarity of $T_\ell$ gives the slice correlations in closed form:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozencorr}
C(u,u';t) = \big\langle a_u(0)\,T_{s(u'-u)t}\,a_{u'}(0)\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
The correlation of two slices at time $t$ is the correlation of the initial slices at the field-aligned separation $s(u'-u)t$ along the initial field lines. Under (\ref{eq:hyp}), (\ref{eq:frozencorr}) integrates the two-slice law of proposition~\ref{prop:twoslice} in closed form, and phase mixing is the decay of these correlations with separation. Since the Hermite spectrum is a quadratic form in the slice correlations (\S\ref{sec:decorrelation}), it is fixed by (\ref{eq:frozencorr}). In a truncated hierarchy, where $g_m = \sum_j (r_j)_m\,a_j/\sqrt{n_j}$,
\begin{equation*}
\langle g_m^2\rangle(t) = \sum_{j,l}\frac{(r_j)_m\,(r_l)_m}{\sqrt{n_jn_l}}\,\big\langle a_j(0)\,T_{s(u_l-u_j)t}\,a_l(0)\big\rangle .
\end{equation*}

We tested corollary~\ref{cor:singlepoint} with RK4 integrations of GANDALF's right-hand side at $M = 8$, $\Lambda = \sqrt5$ and $s = 1$, starting from random fields with $\mathrm{rms}\,|\nabla_\perp\Phi| = \mathrm{rms}\,|\nabla_\perp\Psi| = 1$, for which the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ term is 2.9 times the streaming term in the equation for $g_0$. The initial fields are the same physical fields at every resolution, so a statistic covered by the corollary must converge to the frozen-line prediction as the grid is refined, and one that is not covered need not. At $t = 0.062$ the relative difference in $W(m)$, $\sum_m|W(m)-W_{\rm fr}(m)|/\sum_mW_{\rm fr}(m)$, is $3.4\times10^{-4}$, $2.0\times10^{-5}$, $8.9\times10^{-6}$ and $2.2\times10^{-7}$ on $16^3$, $24^3$, $32^3$ and $48^3$ grids. The slice correlation matrix, the field-aligned flux, the fourth moments and $\varepsilon_\nu$ converge in the same way, whereas $W(k_\perp)$ differs from its frozen-line counterpart by $0.21$ at every resolution. The linear prediction with $\partial_z$ in place of $\gradpar(0)$, which has neither the flow nor the field-line perturbation, misses $W(m)$ by $6\%$ and the flux by $44\%$. In a pointwise check along $40$ Lagrangian markers, with different random fields of the same amplitude, the largest difference between $g(\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi),t)$ and $h(\boldsymbol\xi,t)$ at $t = 0.1$ is $3.8\times10^{-3}$, $3.4\times10^{-4}$, $5.8\times10^{-5}$ and $1.6\times10^{-6}$ of $\max|h|$ on the same four grids, against $0.18$ for the comparison without relabelling. At $t = 0.25$, about three eddy times, the differences in $W(m)$, $C$, $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ and the quantiles still decrease from $24^3$ to $48^3$, while those in the fourth moments and $\varepsilon_\nu$ are not monotone at these resolutions, because the passive field has reached the grid scale. Through GANDALF's own Lawson-RK4 time step on a $32^3$ grid, the difference in $W(m)$ at $t = 0.064$ is $9.7\times10^{-6}$, $9.3\times10^{-6}$ and $9.9\times10^{-6}$ for $\Delta t = 0.002$, $0.001$ and $0.0005$, the spatial error of that grid; the IMEX scheme is discussed in appendix~\ref{app:timestepping}. Section~\ref{sec:numerics} and figure~\ref{fig:frozen} give the full comparison.

\subsection{Forcing}\label{lag:forcingsec}

With a source $F(\boldsymbol r,t)$ added to the right-hand side of (\ref{lag:kin}), the change of variables gives $\partial_t\hat g = -\mathcal L_0\hat g + \mathcal X_t^{-1}F(t)$, and Duhamel's formula in the labels reads
\begin{equation}\label{lag:duhamel}
g(t) = \mathcal X_t\Big[e^{-t\mathcal L_0}g(0) + \int_0^t e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_0}\,\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}F(t')\,\dd t'\Big] .
\end{equation}
By (\ref{lag:intertwine}), $\mathcal X_{t'}e^{-\tau\mathcal L_0}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1} = e^{-\tau\mathcal L_{t'}}$ with $\mathcal L_{t'} := s\gradpar(t')L + \mathcal C$, so the source injected at $t'$ contributes $\mathcal X_t\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_{t'}}F(t')$ to $g(t)$. Each injection phase-mixes along the field lines frozen at its injection time and is then relabelled by the flow between $t'$ and $t$. (For a time-dependent $\mathcal C$ the exponentials become propagators.)

For a single realisation, a quadratic statistic of $g(t)$ contains cross terms between injections at different times, and these involve the relabelling between the injections. For forcing that is white in time they vanish in the mean. To state this precisely, let $(\mathcal F_t)_{t\ge0}$ be a filtration, the information available at time $t$, to which $g(0)$, the Alfv\'enic fields and the forcing are adapted, and let the source be $F\,\dd t = \dd Y$, where $Y$ is a Wiener process with spatially smooth realisations and covariance operator $\Sigma$ in position and velocity, whose increment $Y(t'')-Y(t')$ is independent of $\mathcal F_{t'}$ for $t''>t'$. We call such forcing non-anticipating. The Alfv\'enic fields may be driven by a noise that is correlated with $Y$ at the same instant, because the flow responds to such a kick only after it, while the integrand of (\ref{lag:duhamel}) at $t'$ depends only on the flow before $t'$. What is excluded is a flow at time $t'$ that depends on the forcing of $g$ after $t'$.

\begin{corollary}[White forcing]\label{cor:forcing}
Let the forcing be non-anticipating in the sense just defined, assume (\ref{eq:hyp}) for every realisation, with the source added to (\ref{lag:kin}), and let $\mathcal O_t$ be a single-point statistic (\ref{lag:singlepoint}) whose integrand is a quadratic form. Then
\begin{equation}\label{lag:meanforced}
\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_t[g(t)] = \mathrm E\,\mathcal O_0\big[e^{-t\mathcal L_0}g(0)\big] + \int_0^t\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_{t'}\big[e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_{t'}}\chi\big]\,\dd t' ,
\end{equation}
where $\chi$ is a zero-mean random field with covariance $\Sigma$, independent of the Alfv\'enic fields. In particular, the mean Hermite spectrum $\mathrm E\,W(m,t)$ is the sum of the linear responses to the individual injections, each phase-mixing along the field lines frozen at its own injection time, with no further effect of the flow.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the labels the forced equation is the linear equation $\dd\hat g = -\mathcal L_0\hat g\,\dd t + \mathcal X_t^{-1}\dd Y$, whose solution is the bracket in (\ref{lag:duhamel}) with the integral read as an It\^o integral. The integral is well defined because its integrand, $e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_0}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}$, is fixed by $\mathcal F_{t'}$. Since the noise is additive and $\boldsymbol X_t$ is differentiable in $t$, the It\^o and Stratonovich readings of the forced equation coincide. By corollary~\ref{cor:singlepoint}, $\mathcal O_t[g(t)] = \mathcal O_0[\hat g(t)]$. The cross term between $e^{-t\mathcal L_0}g(0)$ and the stochastic integral has zero mean, because the first is fixed by $\mathcal F_0$ and the integral has zero mean given $\mathcal F_0$. The It\^o isometry turns the mean of the remaining term into $\int_0^t\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_0[e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_0}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}\chi]\,\dd t'$. Finally, volume preservation and (\ref{lag:intertwine}) at time $t'$ give $\mathcal O_0[\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}y] = \mathcal O_{t'}[y]$ for every $y$, and with $y = \mathcal X_{t'}e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_0}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}\chi = e^{-(t-t')\mathcal L_{t'}}\chi$ this is (\ref{lag:meanforced}).
\end{proof}

The same argument applies, with no limit to be taken, to forcing applied as kicks $g\to g + \Delta Y_n$ at times $t_n$, where each $\Delta Y_n$ has zero mean and is independent of the state just before $t_n$ (an Alfv\'enic kick at the same instant is again allowed): the integral in (\ref{lag:meanforced}) is replaced by the sum of $\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_{t_n}[e^{-(t-t_n)\mathcal L_{t_n}}\Delta Y_n]$ over the kicks with $t_n\le t$. The forcing routines of GANDALF act in this way: once per time step they add an independent increment whose amplitude is proportional to $\Delta t^{-1/2}$.

The corollary has a precise scope. It concerns the mean: for a single realisation, $W(m,t)$ contains cross terms between injections at different times. It needs white forcing: for forcing correlated over a time $\tau_c$, the mean cross terms between injections less than about $\tau_c$ apart survive, and they contain the relabelling $\mathcal X_{t''}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}$ between the two injection times, that is, the flow. It holds for quadratic statistics only: a quartic statistic such as $\langle g_m^4\rangle$ contains products of contributions from two injection times at the same point, which again involve that relabelling. Finally, each injection needs (\ref{eq:hyp}) on $[t',t]$. Because (H1) excludes resistivity and forcing of $\Psi$, the magnetic energy has no sink, and a forced ideal state does not settle into a statistically steady state in the usual way. Equation (\ref{lag:meanforced}) is exact on any interval on which the solution stays smooth, and it says nothing about a resistive steady state.

We checked corollary~\ref{cor:forcing} with RK4 integrations of GANDALF's right-hand side at $M = 8$, $\Lambda = \sqrt5$ and $s = 1$. In $\Phi$-forced ideal turbulence, with $z^+$ and $z^-$ receiving the same white-noise increment at every step, three impulsive injections into $g$ were made at $t = 0$, $0.04$ and $0.08$; the eddy time is about $0.1$. At $t = 0.16$ the response to each injection differs from the frozen-line prediction from its own injection time by $4.2\times10^{-4}$, $7.2\times10^{-4}$ and $2.3\times10^{-4}$ on a $48^3$ grid, down from $2.4\times10^{-3}$, $3.1\times10^{-3}$ and $1.0\times10^{-3}$ on $24^3$. For each of the $2^3$ sign patterns of the injections, the cross terms are 5--11\% of $W$; averaging over the patterns removes them by construction. When the same pattern is injected twice with the same sign, which models forcing correlated over the gap, the cross term is $0.90$, $0.69$ and $0.49$ of the diagonal part for gaps of $0.04$, $0.08$ and $0.12$, and an estimate that omits the relabelling between the injections misses it by 3\%, 12\% and 38\%. A sharper test gives $\Phi$ kicks of order unity at two injection instants, $t = 0$ and $0.06$, carrying the same random signs as the two injections into $g$, so that the flow after each injection depends on its sign. The sign-averaged $W(m)$ at $t = 0.15$ equals the averaged sum of the frozen-line responses to $3.4\times10^{-5}$, $2.3\times10^{-7}$, $1.6\times10^{-8}$ and $9.8\times10^{-11}$ on $16^3$, $24^3$, $32^3$ and $48^3$ grids, although the Eulerian response to the first injection changes by 26\% when the sign of the second kick is reversed. If the kick that carries the sign of the second injection is applied before that injection, so that the forcing anticipates, the difference stays at $2.2\times10^{-4}$ at every resolution.

\subsection{Field-aligned wavenumbers}\label{lag:kparsec}

The theorem also fixes how the compressive fluctuations are distributed over wavenumbers along the field. For each $t$ the field $\bhat(t)$ is smooth and divergence-free, so the translations along its field lines form a strongly continuous group of unitary operators on $L^2$ of the box, and by Stone's theorem their generator, $\mathcal K_t := -\ii\gradpar(t)$, is self-adjoint. We call its spectral variable $\kappa$ the field-aligned wavenumber and write $E_t$ for its spectral measure, so that $E_t(I)f$ is the part of $f$ with field-aligned wavenumbers in the Borel set $I\subset\mathbb R$. In a uniform field, $\mathcal K_t = -\ii\partial_z$, $\kappa = k_z$, and $E_t(I)$ is the Fourier filter onto $k_z\in I$. When the field lines are tangled, $\mathcal K_t$ need not have eigenfunctions, and the spectral measure takes the place of the Fourier transform along the field. With the free-energy norm $\|f\|^2 := \int[\bar f,f]\,\dd^3r$, we define the field-aligned spectrum of the free energy,
\begin{equation*}
\mu_t(I) := \big\|E_t(I)\,g(t)\big\|^2 .
\end{equation*}

\begin{corollary}[Field-aligned wavenumbers]\label{cor:kpar}
Assume (\ref{eq:hyp}). For every Borel set $I\subset\mathbb R$ and $0\le t\le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{lag:kparsub}
E_t(I)\,g(t) = \mathcal X_t\,e^{-t\mathcal L_0}\,E_0(I)\,g(0) .
\end{equation}
The part of $g$ with field-aligned wavenumbers in $I$ therefore evolves, in the labels, from the part of $g(0)$ with field-aligned wavenumbers in $I$ alone, and the spectral subspaces of $\mathcal K_0$ are invariant under the dynamics in the labels. Without collisions, $\mu_t(I) = \mu_0(I)$ for every $I$, and the same holds for the field-aligned spectrum of each slice. With a dissipative $\mathcal C$, that is, one with $\mathrm{Re}\,[\bar f,\mathcal Cf]\ge0$ for all $f$, $\mu_t(I)$ does not increase.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By (\ref{lag:intertwine}) and the unitarity of $\mathcal X_t$, $\mathcal K_t = \mathcal X_t\mathcal K_0\mathcal X_t^{-1}$, so $E_t(I) = \mathcal X_tE_0(I)\mathcal X_t^{-1}$. The projection $E_0(I)$ acts on position only and commutes with $\gradpar(0)$, so it commutes with $\mathcal L_0$ and with $e^{-t\mathcal L_0}$, and (\ref{eq:frozen}) gives (\ref{lag:kparsub}). Since $\mathcal X_t$ preserves the norm, $\mu_t(I) = \|e^{-t\mathcal L_0}E_0(I)g(0)\|^2$. Without collisions, $e^{-t\mathcal L_0}$ preserves the norm, and on each slice it acts as $T_{sut} = e^{-\ii sut\mathcal K_0}$, which multiplies the component at field-aligned wavenumber $\kappa$ by the phase $e^{-\ii su\kappa t}$. With a dissipative $\mathcal C$, the derivative of $\|e^{-t\mathcal L_0}f\|^2$ is $-2\,\mathrm{Re}\int[\bar y,\mathcal Cy]\,\dd^3\xi\le0$, with $y = e^{-t\mathcal L_0}f$.
\end{proof}

For a real $g$ the spectral measure is symmetric, $\mu_t(-I) = \mu_t(I)$, because $\gradpar$ is real and $\mathcal K_t$ therefore anticommutes with complex conjugation; the parts $E_t(0,\infty)g$ and $E_t(-\infty,0)g$ are complex conjugates of each other. The statement that no part of $g$ changes the sign of its field-aligned wavenumber is therefore the statement that these two complex components evolve separately: by (\ref{lag:kparsub}), neither leaks into the other. In a uniform field they are the parts of $g$ with $k_z>0$ and $k_z<0$. Corollary~\ref{cor:kpar} is the precise form of the conclusion of \citet[\S6.3]{Schekochihin2009} that the compressive fluctuations do not cascade along the moving field lines: under (\ref{eq:hyp}) and without collisions, the distribution of the free energy over field-aligned wavenumbers is constant in time.

The frozen-in shear shows the difference between the field-aligned and the Eulerian wavenumber. Take $\Phi = -Szy$, so that $\uE = Sz\,\hat{\boldsymbol x}$, and $\Psi = 0$ at $t = 0$. The flux equation is solved by $\Psi = -Sty$, so that $\boldsymbol b_\perp = St\,\hat{\boldsymbol x}$ and $\gradpar(t) = \partial_z + St\,\partial_x$; both Laplacians vanish, and the pair is an exact, though not periodic, solution of (\ref{eq:rmhd}). The flow map is $\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi) = (\xi_x + S\xi_zt,\,\xi_y,\,\xi_z)$, so a Fourier mode of the labels, $\exp[\ii(k_x\xi_x + k_y\xi_y + k_z\xi_z)]$, becomes at time $t$ a Fourier mode of position with
\begin{equation}\label{lag:shear}
k_z(t) = k_z - Stk_x, \qquad k_\parallel(t) = k_z(t) + Stk_x = k_z ,
\end{equation}
where $k_\parallel(t)$ is its eigenvalue under $\mathcal K_t$. The Eulerian $k_z$ changes sign at $t = k_z/(Sk_x)$, while the field-aligned wavenumber stays fixed, and in the labels the velocity-space evolution is that of a uniform field with wavenumber $k_z$ along it. A diagnostic that sorts the free energy by the sign of $k_z$ counts this mode as having reversed its parallel wavenumber, although nothing has changed along the field; \S\ref{sec:decorrelation} works out the consequences for decompositions of the Hermite flux based on $k_z$.

\subsection{What breaks the theorem}\label{lag:breaking}

The proof uses (H1) and (H2) and nothing else about the dynamics, and the theorem fails as soon as either fails. Any non-ideal term in the induction equation in general makes $R$ depend on $x$ and $y$, so that the commutator (\ref{lag:commutator}) does not vanish and the field lines are not carried by the flow: resistivity or hyper-resistivity acting on $\Psi$, parallel dissipation applied to $z^\pm$, and any forcing of $\Psi$, for example independent forcing of $z^+$ and $z^-$. Any operator on $g$ that does not commute with the relabelling breaks the theorem as well; perpendicular or parallel spatial dissipation of $g$ is such an operator. Viscosity or forcing acting on $\Phi$ alone does not break it, because the momentum equation never enters the proof. The Fourier truncation of a spectral code is itself an operator that does not commute with the relabelling. The error it causes vanishes as the resolution increases at fixed $t$, but not uniformly in $t$, and it limits the time over which a given resolution follows (\ref{eq:frozen}).

Table~\ref{lag:breakers} tests these statements. Each run starts from the same physical fields as the checks of corollary~\ref{cor:singlepoint} and adds one term to GANDALF's right-hand side, integrated with RK4 at $\Delta t = 0.00125$; the frozen-line prediction carries the same damping of $g$ where the run has one. The three cases that satisfy (H1) and (H2) approach the prediction as the grid is refined, and the four that violate them leave a difference that does not depend on resolution. Only this independence of resolution is a test of the theorem, since the sizes of the non-convergent differences depend on the coefficients chosen. For viscosity and forcing of $\Phi$ the decrease from $24^3$ to $32^3$ is small, so these runs alone are weak evidence. The point is settled by the proof, and by the test of \S\ref{lag:forcingsec} in which kicks of $\Phi$ of order unity leave the mean forced spectrum on the frozen-line prediction to $9.8\times10^{-11}$ on a $48^3$ grid.

\subsection{Echoes}\label{lag:echoes}

Under (\ref{eq:hyp}), $W(m,t)$ and the field-aligned Hermite flux $\Pi_{m+1/2}(t)$ evolve exactly as in the autonomous linear problem $\partial_th = -\mathcal L_0h$ (corollary~\ref{cor:singlepoint}). Any return of free energy to low $m$ that appears in them must therefore already be present in that linear problem, for the given initial data: an initial condition that carries unmixing correlations, which produces a linear echo; the recurrence of a truncated hierarchy; or the slow phase mixing of components with small field-aligned wavenumber. The random advection by the turbulence does not convert phase mixing into unmixing in $W(m)$. With white forcing the same holds for the mean $\mathrm E\,W(m,t)$, injection by injection (corollary~\ref{cor:forcing}). This is the sense in which ideal, smooth dynamics cannot produce stochastic echoes in $W(m)$. The statement concerns single-point statistics. It does not say that the flux computed with $\partial_z$, or a split into phase-mixing and unmixing parts by the sign of $k_z$, shows no backward transfer: \S\ref{sec:decorrelation} shows that such diagnostics report backward transfer for pure phase mixing along tilted field lines. Nor does it constrain fluxes resolved in $k_\perp$, or say that the dynamics is linear in the Eulerian frame, where $W(k_\perp)$ evolves nonlinearly.

The settings in which stochastic echoes have been studied, drift kinetics in a straight guide field \citep{Schekochihin2016,Parker2016} and a solvable Vlasov--Poisson model \citep{AdkinsSchekochihin2018}, stream the particles along a fixed direction, so that the nonlinearity can change the wavenumber along it. In drift kinetics the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow depends on $z$ while the streaming is along $\zhat$, so that
\begin{equation*}
[D_t,\partial_z]\,f = -\{\partial_z\Phi,f\}\neq0 ,
\end{equation*}
and the flow changes the parallel wavenumber of $g$, coupling positive to negative $k_\parallel$. This coupling is the mechanism of the echo; when the advecting flow does not depend on $z$ there is no such coupling and no echo, as noted by \citet[appendix~B]{Schekochihin2016} and \citet[\S5.2]{KanekarThesis2015}. In KRMHD the shear $\partial_z\uE$ that would change $k_\parallel$ also enters the flux equation through $\partial_z\Phi$, and it bends the field lines together with $g$: under (H1) the commutator (\ref{lag:commutator}) vanishes, and by corollary~\ref{cor:kpar} no part of $g$ moves between positive and negative field-aligned wavenumbers. The frozen-in shear (\ref{lag:shear}) is the simplest example: the Eulerian $k_z$ of a mode changes sign while its field-aligned wavenumber stays fixed.

\citet{Meyrand2019} found Hermite spectra a little steeper than $m^{-1}$ in simulations of KRMHD turbulence, against $m^{-1/2}$ for linear phase mixing, and attributed them to stochastic echoes. They described the echo as the scattering of a compressive wave by an Alfv\'en wave propagating in the opposite direction along the field line, which changes the sign of the compressive wave's $k_\parallel$, after which its phase unmixes. \citet{Kawazura2020} likewise read the difference between $m^{-1}$ and $m^{-1/2}$ spectra in their gyrokinetic simulations through stochastic echoes. The theorem does not contradict these measurements, because the simulations were not ideal. \citet{Meyrand2019} dissipated the Alfv\'enic fields with eighth-order hyperviscosity and hyper-resistivity and the kinetic field with a hyperviscous term $-\mu k_\perp^8g_m$, which violate (H1) and (H2), and they noted that with any resistivity the field lines lose their identities, so that the Lagrangian frame is lost after about one eddy-turnover time. The simulations of \citet{Kawazura2020} solve a gyrokinetic system, to which the theorem, derived for KRMHD, does not apply directly. What the theorem shows is that within ideal, smooth KRMHD dynamics the sign of the field-aligned wavenumber cannot change, so an echo of the kind described by \citet{Meyrand2019} requires the non-ideal terms, or a failure of deterministic flux freezing for rough fields in the limit $\eta\to0$. \citet{Eyink2013} found such a breakdown of flux freezing in high-conductivity MHD turbulence, \citet{Eyink2018} argued that in a turbulent inertial range the ideal Ohm's law does not imply flux freezing, and \citet{Jafari2026} argued that magnetic flux is then conserved only statistically, along stochastic path lines. These are statements about rough solutions, which (H3) excludes. Two further measurements of \citet{Meyrand2019} lie outside the theorem for the same reasons. Their Hermite flux was resolved in $k_\perp$ shells; it is not a single-point statistic, and the theorem constrains only its sum over shells. Their measurement of a parallel cascade of the compressive fluctuations along traced field lines, which they attributed to resistive relabelling of the field lines, is excluded under (\ref{eq:hyp}) by corollary~\ref{cor:kpar}, so it too requires a departure from the hypotheses.

\citet{Adkins2025} found, in agreement with \citet{Zhou2023}, that echoes play no significant role in the kinetic reduced electron heating model (KREHM) of \citet{ZoccoSchekochihin2011}, and suggested that the passivity of the compressive sector of KRMHD, which does not act back on the Alfv\'enic fields, may be why echoes appear there. The theorem shows that, for smooth solutions, passivity together with ideal induction excludes stochastic echoes in $W(m)$. Which non-ideal mechanism produces the echoes reported in KRMHD simulations, and whether its effect survives the limit $\eta\to0$, we leave for future work.
