\section{The truncated hierarchy}\label{app:truncated}

\subsection{Weights, closures and eigenvectors}

Let $J$ be the ladder matrix of the zero closure at $\Lambda=\infty$, the symmetric Jacobi matrix of the $h_m$, and $J_c$ that of the copy closure, which for $M\ge2$ differs from $J$ only in $(J_c)_{M,M-1}=\sqrt{M/2}+\sqrt{(M+1)/2}$. A finite $\Lambda$ changes only $A_{10}=(1-1/\Lambda)\,J_{10}$, and column $0$ of $J$ and of $J_c$ has the single non-zero entry $J_{10}=1/\sqrt2$. Hence, with $P_\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(1-1/\Lambda,1,\dots,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{appC:factor}
  A=J\,P_\Lambda\quad\text{(zero closure)},\qquad A=J_c\,P_\Lambda\quad\text{(copy closure, $M\ge2$)},
\end{equation}
the Hermite form of $L=UP$ (\ref{eq:LUP}). For the zero closure $\Pw=P_\Lambda$, and $\Pw A=P_\Lambda JP_\Lambda$ is symmetric. For the copy closure $D_c=\mathrm{diag}(1,\dots,1,p_M)$ satisfies $D_cJ_c=J$, because $p_M(J_c)_{M,M-1}=\sqrt{M/2}$, so that with $\Pw=D_cP_\Lambda$ again $\Pw A=P_\Lambda JP_\Lambda$. We checked that GANDALF's streaming matrix equals $A$ exactly for $M=2$ to $128$, six values of $\Lambda$ and both closures.

Since $LF_u=uF_u$ and the untruncated ladder matrix is the matrix of $L$ in the Hermite basis, the Hermite coefficients $r_m(u)$ of $F_u$ satisfy $\sum_nA_{mn}r_n=u\,r_m$ row by row, with $r_0=M_0[F_u]=1$:
\begin{equation}\label{appC:rec}
  r_1=\sqrt2\,u,\qquad r_2=u\,r_1-\frac{1-1/\Lambda}{\sqrt2}\,r_0,\qquad
  \sqrt{\frac{m+1}{2}}\;r_{m+1}=u\,r_m-\sqrt{\frac m2}\;r_{m-1}\quad(m\ge2).
\end{equation}
Thus $r_m$ is a polynomial of degree $m$ with $r_m(-u)=(-1)^mr_m(u)$, and $r_m=h_m$ at $\Lambda=\infty$. Rows $0$ to $M-1$ of the truncated matrix coincide with those of the infinite matrix for both closures, and row $M$ is the condition on the eigenvalues,
\begin{equation}\label{appC:eigcond}
  r_{M+1}(u_j)=0\quad\text{(zero closure)},\qquad r_{M+1}(u_j)=r_{M-1}(u_j)\quad\text{(copy closure)},
\end{equation}
the second from row $M$ of the copy closure, $u\,r_M=[\sqrt{M/2}+\sqrt{(M+1)/2}\,]\,r_{M-1}$, and the recurrence at $m=M$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of theorem~\ref{thm:trunc}]
(i) $A$ is similar to the symmetric tridiagonal matrix $\Pw^{1/2}A\,\Pw^{-1/2}$, whose off-diagonal entries are non-zero because $A_{01}A_{10}=(1-1/\Lambda)/2>0$, so its eigenvalues are real and simple and the vectors $\Pw^{1/2}\boldsymbol r_j$ are orthogonal. By (\ref{appC:rec}) the vector $(r_0(u),\dots,r_M(u))^{\mathsf T}$ satisfies rows $0$ to $M-1$ of $A\boldsymbol r=u\boldsymbol r$ for every $u$, (\ref{appC:eigcond}) fixes the $u_j$, and $\Pw A=A^{\mathsf T}\Pw$ gives $\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}A=u_j\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}$. (ii) The combination $\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}g$ commutes with the brackets, with $\partial_z$ and with the linear operations, and $\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}A=u_j\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}$ turns $s\gradpar(Ag)$ into $su_j\gradpar(\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}g)$; the converse follows from (iii). (iii) By (i), $R^{\mathsf T}\Pw R=I$, so $g=Ra$ and $\sum_mp_m|g_m|^2=a^\dagger R^{\mathsf T}\Pw R\,a=\sum_j|a_j|^2$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Gauss quadrature}

We prove proposition~\ref{tr:gaussprop}. For the basis functions $e_m$, $\Tr[e_m](u)=\fe{F_u}{e_m}=p_m\,r_m(u)$. (i) By theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} the slice transform is unitary, and the slice amplitude of $e_m$ is $\sqrt\varrho\,p_mr_m$, so $p_m\delta_{mn}=\fe{e_m}{e_n}=\int\varrho\,p_mr_m\,p_nr_n\,\dd u$. (ii) The polynomials $\sqrt{p_m}\,r_m$ are therefore orthonormal for $\varrho\,\dd u$, with the Jacobi matrix $\Pw^{1/2}A\,\Pw^{-1/2}$ by (\ref{appC:rec}), and the zero closure keeps its first $M+1$ rows and columns. By the theorem of \citet{GolubWelsch1969}, the eigenvalues of this truncated matrix are the zeros of $r_{M+1}$ and the nodes of the Gauss rule, and the weights are $\mu_0O_{0j}^2$, with $O$ its orthonormal eigenvectors and $\mu_0=\int\varrho\,\dd u=1/p_0$. Since $O_{mj}=\sqrt{p_m}\,R_{mj}$, the weights are $R_{0j}^2=1/n_j$, and exactness to degree $2M+1$ is Gauss's theorem. (iii) By definition $\boldsymbol r_j$ holds the Hermite coefficients of $F_{u_j}$, and $\Tr[G](u_j)=\sum_mg_mp_mr_m(u_j)=\boldsymbol w_j^{\mathsf T}g=\sqrt{n_j}\,a_j$. We checked (\ref{eq:gauss}) for all degrees up to $2M+1$ in 50-digit arithmetic at $M=6$ and $10$, to $6\times10^{-44}$.

\subsection{The largest phase velocity}

For the zero closure $A=J+\delta A$ exactly, with $\delta A=-(\Lambda\sqrt2)^{-1}\boldsymbol e_1\boldsymbol e_0^{\mathsf T}$ and $\boldsymbol e_m$ the $m$th unit vector. The normalised eigenvectors of $J$ have components $(\lambda^\infty_j)^{1/2}h_m(x_j)$, and $h_0=1$, $h_1(x)=\sqrt2\,x$, so to first order $u_j=x_j-\lambda^\infty_jx_j/\Lambda+O(\Lambda^{-2})$. The weight $\lambda^\infty_M$ is of order $e^{-x_M^2}$ up to an algebraic factor, with $x_M^2=2M+3-2^{2/3}|\alpha_1|(2M+3)^{1/3}+\dots$, so the shift of $u_{\max}$ is about $10^{-49}$ at $M=64$; in double precision $u_{\max}$ is the same for $\Lambda=\sqrt5$, $-\sqrt5$ and $\infty$ to $3\times10^{-13}$ for $M\ge32$. Up to such terms, $u_{\max}$ is the largest zero of $\psi_{M+1}$ for the zero closure and the largest root of $\psi_{M+1}=\psi_{M-1}$ for the copy closure.

The function $\psi_{M+1}$ solves $\psi''+(\kappa^2-x^2)\psi=0$ with $\kappa=\kappa_{M+1}$. With $x=\kappa+(2\kappa)^{-1/3}\xi$ and $\sigma=(2\kappa)^{-4/3}$ this becomes, exactly, $\psi_{\xi\xi}=(\xi+\sigma\xi^2)\psi$. The uniform approximation $\psi=(\chi')^{-1/2}\mathrm{Ai}(\chi)$, with $\chi\,(\chi')^2=\xi+\sigma\xi^2$, gives $\chi=\xi+\sigma\xi^2/5+O(\sigma^2)$, and the term it neglects is $O(\sigma^2)$. The largest zero is at $\chi=\alpha_1$, so $\xi=\alpha_1-\sigma\alpha_1^2/5+O(\sigma^2)$, which is the first line of (\ref{eq:umax}).

For the copy closure at $\Lambda=\infty$, row $M$ requires $\psi_{M+1}=\psi_{M-1}$. Since $x\psi_M=\sqrt{M/2}\,\psi_{M-1}+\sqrt{(M+1)/2}\,\psi_{M+1}$ and $\psi_M'=\sqrt{M/2}\,\psi_{M-1}-\sqrt{(M+1)/2}\,\psi_{M+1}$, this is the Robin condition $\psi_M'+x(\sqrt M+\sqrt{M+1})^{-2}\psi_M=0$ for $\psi_M$, which solves the same equation with $\kappa=\kappa_M$. The coefficient of $\psi_M$ is $1/(2\kappa)$ to leading order, so the condition reads $\psi_\xi+\sigma\psi=0$. With $\psi_\xi\propto\mathrm{Ai}'(\chi)-[\chi''/(2\chi'^2)]\,\mathrm{Ai}(\chi)$ and $\chi''=2\sigma/5$, it becomes $\mathrm{Ai}'(\chi)+(4\sigma/5)\,\mathrm{Ai}(\chi)=0$. Near $\alpha_1'$, where $\mathrm{Ai}''(\alpha_1')=\alpha_1'\mathrm{Ai}(\alpha_1')$, this gives $\chi=\alpha_1'-4\sigma/(5\alpha_1')$, and $\xi=\chi-\sigma\chi^2/5$ gives the second line of (\ref{eq:umax}). Values of $u_{\max}$ up to $M=16384$ confirm both lines.

\subsection{The phase-space helicity at odd and even \texorpdfstring{$M$}{M}}

Since $\PVint F_M\,u^{-1}\,\dd u=0$,
\begin{equation*}
  (B_M)_{mn}=\int\frac{h_m(u)h_n(u)-h_m(0)h_n(0)}{u}\,F_M(u)\,\dd u .
\end{equation*}
The integrand $f_{mn}$ is a polynomial of degree $m+n-1\le2M-1$, so the Gauss--Hermite rule gives $(B_M)_{mn}=\sum_j\lambda^\infty_jf_{mn}(x_j)$ exactly. The nodes come in pairs $\pm x_j$ with equal weights, so $\sum_{x_j\neq0}\lambda^\infty_j/x_j=0$, and $J=O_\infty\,\mathrm{diag}(x_j)\,O_\infty^{\mathsf T}$ with $(O_\infty)_{mj}=(\lambda^\infty_j)^{1/2}h_m(x_j)$. For odd $M$ no node lies at $u=0$, so $(B_M)_{mn}=\sum_j\lambda^\infty_jh_m(x_j)h_n(x_j)/x_j=(J^{-1})_{mn}$, and by (\ref{appC:factor}) $\Pw A^{-1}=J^{-1}$ for every $\Lambda\neq1$; $A$ is invertible because $\det A=(-1)^{(M+1)/2}\prod_{i=0}^{(M-1)/2}A_{2i,2i+1}A_{2i+1,2i}$ vanishes only at $\Lambda=1$. For even $M$ the node $x_{M/2}=0$ contributes $\lambda^\infty_{M/2}f_{mn}(0)=\lambda^\infty_{M/2}[h_m'(0)h_n(0)+h_m(0)h_n'(0)]$ and the other nodes give $(J^+)_{mn}$, which is the second relation of (\ref{eq:Hoddeven}). The conserved form $\Pw A^D$, with the Drazin inverse $A^D$ (eigenvalues $1/u_j$ for $u_j\neq0$ and $0$ at $u=0$), reduces to $J^+$ at $\Lambda=\infty$, so $g^{\mathsf T}(\Pw A^D-B_M)g=-2\lambda^\infty_{M/2}[\boldsymbol h(0)\cdot g][\boldsymbol h'(0)\cdot g]$, with $\lambda^\infty_{M/2}\simeq[\pi/(2M+3)]^{1/2}$.

For the zero closure and even $M$, $\boldsymbol h(0)$ is a left null vector of $A$ for every $\Lambda$: column $0$ of $\boldsymbol h(0)^{\mathsf T}A$ is $h_1(0)A_{10}=0$, the columns $0<n<M$ vanish by the recurrence $\sqrt{(n+1)/2}\,h_{n+1}(0)+\sqrt{n/2}\,h_{n-1}(0)=0$, and column $M$ is $h_{M-1}(0)\sqrt{M/2}=0$ because $h_{M-1}$ is odd. The $r_m$ and $h_m$ obey the same recurrence for $m\ge2$, and $r_2(0)=p_0h_2(0)$, so $r_m(0)=p_0h_m(0)$ for $m\ge1$ and $\boldsymbol w_{M/2}=\Pw\boldsymbol r_{M/2}=p_0\boldsymbol h(0)$. Hence $a_{M/2}^2=\lambda_{M/2}\,p_0^2\,G_M(0)^2/F_M(0)^2$, whose volume average is proportional to the imaginary part of (\ref{eq:Hcomplex}) for the truncated state.

Under the copy closure, $J_c^{-1}=J^{-1}D_c$, so at odd $M$ the conserved form is $g^{\mathsf T}D_cJ^{-1}D_cg$, whose matrix differs from $B_M$ by 33\% at $M=9$ and 23\% at $M=33$, relative to the largest element; at even $M$ the last component of the null vector is multiplied by $p_M$. On GANDALF's right-hand side, at $M=10$, 16 and 64, for both signs of $\Lambda$ and both closures, the slice at $u=0$ obeys $\partial_ta_{M/2}+\{\Phi,a_{M/2}\}=0$ to $2\times10^{-15}$, and its energy is conserved to relative rates of $2\times10^{-16}$.
