\section{Phase mixing as decorrelation of slices}\label{sec:decorrelation}

By theorem~\ref{thm:slice}, one compressive branch is a continuum of passive scalars $a_u$, each carried by its own divergence-free velocity $\boldsymbol U_u=\uE+su\,\bhat$, equation~(\ref{eq:Uu}). Without collisions, sources or dissipation of $G$, the energy $\langle a_u^2\rangle$ of every slice is conserved. A Hermite moment $g_m$ is a fixed linear combination of slices, so free energy can move between Hermite moments only through changes in the correlations between different slices. This section makes the statement precise. We derive the evolution law of the two-slice correlation (section~\ref{dec:sec-twoslice}), write the Hermite flux and the Hermite spectrum in terms of it (sections~\ref{dec:sec-flux} and~\ref{dec:sec-dictionary}), examine the decomposition of the flux into phase-mixing and phase-unmixing parts that is based on the Eulerian wavenumber $k_z$ (section~\ref{dec:sec-tilt}), and show that the invariants of section~\ref{sec:invariants} do not constrain the transfer (section~\ref{dec:sec-fjortoft}).

Throughout this section $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is the average over the periodic box,
\begin{equation*}
  C(u,u')=\langle a_u\,a_{u'}\rangle
\end{equation*}
is the two-slice correlation, and $W(m)=p_m\langle g_m^2\rangle$ is the Hermite spectrum of the free energy. The sum $\sum_mW(m)=\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle$ equals $\int C(u,u)\,\dd u$ by (\ref{eq:aunit}), and it is $2W^\pm/n_iT_i$ in the normalisation of (\ref{eq:WGH}). In the truncated hierarchy of section~\ref{sec:truncated} the slices are the unit-weight amplitudes $a_j$ at the eigenvalues $u_j$ of $A$, $j=0,\dots,M$, and the two-slice correlation is the matrix $C_{jl}=\langle a_ja_l\rangle$.

\subsection{The two-slice law}\label{dec:sec-twoslice}

\begin{proposition}[Two-slice law]\label{prop:twoslice}
Let $\Lam\notin[0,1]$. Let $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ be smooth fields on the periodic box, otherwise arbitrary, and let $G$ obey the branch equation (\ref{eq:branch}) with no collision operator, source or dissipation acting on $G$. Assume that, on the time interval considered, the slice amplitudes $a_u(\boldsymbol r,t)$ are continuously differentiable in $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$ and continuous in $u$. Then, for all $u$ and $u'$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoslice}
  \frac{\dd C(u,u')}{\dd t}=-s\,(u-u')\,\big\langle a_{u'}\,\gradpar a_u\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
In particular, $C(u,u)$ is constant for every $u$, and the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow does not enter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\partial_z+\{\Psi,\cdot\}=\gradpar$, the slice equation (\ref{eq:slice}) reads $\partial_ta_u=-\{\Phi,a_u\}-su\,\gradpar a_u$. The Leibniz rule for the bracket and for $\gradpar$ then gives
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_t(a_ua_{u'})=-\{\Phi,a_ua_{u'}\}-su'\,\gradpar(a_ua_{u'})-s\,(u-u')\,a_{u'}\gradpar a_u .
\end{equation*}
For any smooth $f$, $\{\Phi,f\}=\nabla\cdot(f\,\uE)$ and $\gradpar f=\nabla\cdot(f\,\bhat)$, because $\nabla\cdot\uE=\nabla\cdot\bhat=0$. The first two terms are therefore divergences, and their averages over the periodic box vanish.
\end{proof}

The $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow carries the two slices of a pair together and cannot change their overlap; only the difference of their velocities, $\boldsymbol U_u-\boldsymbol U_{u'}=s(u-u')\,\bhat$, enters (\ref{eq:twoslice}). The diagonal $C(u,u)$ is the energy of slice $u$, whose conservation underlies the invariants of section~\ref{sec:invariants}. The correlation between two different slices changes only through their relative streaming along the perturbed field, at a rate set by the difference of their speeds. This gives phase mixing and phase unmixing a definition that does not refer to any wavenumber: they are the decorrelation and the recorrelation of slices along $\gradpar$. Under ideal induction this can be made explicit. Section~\ref{sec:lagrangian} shows that $C(u,u';t)$ is then the correlation of the initial slices at the field-aligned separation $s(u'-u)t$ along the field lines frozen at $t=0$, equation~(\ref{eq:frozencorr}), so that phase mixing is the decay of the initial correlations with separation along those lines.

The proposition needs no assumption on the Alfv\'enic fields beyond smoothness; they may be forced, dissipated or prescribed. Operators that act on $G$ itself add terms to (\ref{eq:twoslice}). Collisions and sources enter through their slice form (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}), and a velocity-independent dissipation, such as perpendicular hyperdiffusion applied to every $g_m$, acts on each slice separately and changes $C(u,u)$ as well. The same algebra holds for the truncated hierarchy (theorem~\ref{thm:trunc}), with $C_{jl}$ and the nodes $u_j$, $u_l$ in place of $C(u,u')$ and $u$, $u'$. In a pseudo-spectral computation the step from the pointwise identity to (\ref{eq:twoslice}) uses the exact antisymmetry of the discrete bracket under the volume average, so it requires $\Phi$, $\Psi$ and $g$ to lie inside the dealiasing band; the slice equation itself does not. We checked the truncated form of (\ref{eq:twoslice}) to round-off against GANDALF's right-hand side \citep{Kanekar2025}, for $M$ between 8 and 65, $\Lam=\pm\sqrt5$ and $3$, and both closures, in states in which the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ term of a single Fourier mode is as large as its total rate of change. The two sides agree to a relative accuracy of $5\times10^{-15}$, and the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ contribution to the averaged law vanishes to $2\times10^{-16}$.

The average over the box is essential. With $f(\boldsymbol r)=\sum_{\mathbf k}f(\mathbf k)\,e^{\ii\mathbf k\cdot\boldsymbol r}$ and $\{f,h\}_{\mathbf k}$ the Fourier coefficient of the bracket, the correlation of a single Fourier mode, $C_{\mathbf k}(u,u')=a_u(\mathbf k)\,a_{u'}(\mathbf k)^*$, obeys
\begin{equation}\label{dec:pairk}
  \frac{\dd C_{\mathbf k}(u,u')}{\dd t}=-\ii sk_z\,(u-u')\,C_{\mathbf k}(u,u')+N^\Phi_{\mathbf k}(u,u')+N^\Psi_{\mathbf k}(u,u'),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
  N^\Phi_{\mathbf k}=-\{\Phi,a_u\}_{\mathbf k}\,a_{u'}(\mathbf k)^*-a_u(\mathbf k)\,\{\Phi,a_{u'}\}_{\mathbf k}^*,\qquad
  N^\Psi_{\mathbf k}=-su\,\{\Psi,a_u\}_{\mathbf k}\,a_{u'}(\mathbf k)^*-su'\,a_u(\mathbf k)\,\{\Psi,a_{u'}\}_{\mathbf k}^* .
\end{equation*}
The first term rotates the phase of $C_{\mathbf k}$ at the Eulerian rate $sk_z(u-u')$. The $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ term $N^\Phi_{\mathbf k}$ moves correlation between wavenumbers and sums to zero over $\mathbf k$. The term $N^\Psi_{\mathbf k}$ sums to $-s(u-u')\langle a_{u'}\{\Psi,a_u\}\rangle$ and completes $\partial_z$ to $\gradpar$ in (\ref{eq:twoslice}). Mode by mode, therefore, the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ advection does not drop out, and the linear dephasing is measured with $k_z$ rather than along the field. In the states of our checks the largest $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ term of a single mode was 1.0--1.2 times the largest total rate of change of a mode, while the sum of these terms over modes vanished to round-off.

For electrostatic drift kinetics in a straight guide field, and in the one-dimensional linear model of \citet{Kanekar2015}, phase mixing and phase unmixing are separated mode by mode with the sign of the parallel wavenumber. The Hermite moments of each Fourier mode are split into a phase-mixing part, which propagates to larger $m$, and a phase-unmixing part, which propagates to smaller $m$, and the Hermite flux of the mode is proportional to the difference of their energies \citep{Kanekar2015,Schekochihin2016,Parker2016}. In KRMHD the field lines move. \citet{Meyrand2019} noted that the sign would then have to be taken with respect to the perturbed field, that they did not know how to make such a decomposition rigorous, and accordingly they did not use one. The two-slice law describes phase mixing along the perturbed field without reference to a wavenumber. Section~\ref{dec:sec-tilt} examines what is reported when the sign of $k_z$ is used instead.

\subsection{The Hermite flux as a Christoffel--Darboux transform}\label{dec:sec-flux}

Let $E_{\le m}=\sum_{n\le m}W(n)$ be the free energy in the Hermite moments $n\le m$, and let $b_m=p_mA_{m,m+1}$, so that $b_0=(1-1/\Lam)/\sqrt2$ and $b_m=\sqrt{(m+1)/2}$ for $m\ge1$. The symmetry of $\Pw A$ reads $b_m=p_{m+1}A_{m+1,m}$. Under the hypotheses of proposition~\ref{prop:twoslice} the Hermite coefficients obey $\partial_tg+\{\Phi,g\}+s\gradpar(Ag)=0$, the Hermite form of (\ref{eq:branch}). The bracket and $\gradpar$ are antisymmetric under the volume average, so the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ term does not change $E_{\le m}$, and the streaming terms of the moments $n\le m$ cancel in pairs except for the coupling between $m$ and $m+1$. This gives the budget
\begin{equation}\label{dec:budget}
  \frac{\dd E_{\le m}}{\dd t}=-\Pi_{m+1/2},\qquad
  \Pi_{m+1/2}=s\,b_m\big\langle g_m\gradpar g_{m+1}-g_{m+1}\gradpar g_m\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
The flux $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ is the free energy per unit time and per unit volume that passes from moment $m$ to moment $m+1$. It contains the full field-aligned gradient $\gradpar=\partial_z+\{\Psi,\cdot\}$.

To write $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ in slice variables, let $r_n(u)$ be the $n$th Hermite coefficient of the Case--Van Kampen mode $F_u$, so that $\Tr[e_n]=p_nr_n$ for the Hermite basis functions $e_n=H_nF_M/\sqrt{2^nn!}$. Because $LF_u=uF_u$ and $A$ is the matrix of $L$ in the Hermite basis, $r_0=1$, $r_1=\sqrt2\,u$ and
\begin{equation*}
  u\,r_n(u)=A_{n,n+1}\,r_{n+1}(u)+A_{n,n-1}\,r_{n-1}(u),
\end{equation*}
so $r_n$ is a polynomial of degree $n$ (section~\ref{sec:truncated}). The slice amplitude of $e_n$ is $\sqrt{\varrho}\,p_nr_n$, and the transform is unitary (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}), so $p_ng_n=\fe{e_n}{G}=\int\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\,p_nr_n(u)\,a_u\,\dd u$. That is, $g_n=\int\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\,r_n(u)\,a_u\,\dd u$, and the same identity with $G=e_n$ gives $\int\varrho\,r_n^2\,\dd u=1/p_n$. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{dec:kernel}
  E_{\le m}=\iint K_m(u,u')\,C(u,u')\,\dd u\,\dd u',\qquad
  K_m(u,u')=\sqrt{\varrho(u)\varrho(u')}\,\sum_{n\le m}p_n\,r_n(u)\,r_n(u') .
\end{equation}
The kernel obeys a Christoffel--Darboux identity. Multiply the recurrence at $u$ by $p_nr_n(u')$, subtract the same expression with $u$ and $u'$ exchanged, and sum over $n\le m$. Because $p_nA_{n,n+1}=b_n$ and $p_nA_{n,n-1}=b_{n-1}$, the sum telescopes, and
\begin{equation}\label{dec:CD}
  (u'-u)\,K_m(u,u')=b_m\sqrt{\varrho(u)\varrho(u')}\,\big[r_m(u)\,r_{m+1}(u')-r_{m+1}(u)\,r_m(u')\big] .
\end{equation}
Since $K_m$ does not depend on time, (\ref{dec:kernel}) and the two-slice law (\ref{eq:twoslice}) give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fluxCD}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}=-\iint K_m(u,u')\,\frac{\partial C(u,u')}{\partial t}\,\dd u\,\dd u'
  =s\iint(u-u')\,K_m(u,u')\,\big\langle a_{u'}\gradpar a_u\big\rangle\,\dd u\,\dd u' ,
\end{equation}
and by (\ref{dec:CD}) the right-hand side equals the moment form (\ref{dec:budget}). The diagonal pairs $u=u'$ carry no flux, because $\partial_tC(u,u)=0$. The free energy that passes through every $m$ is carried entirely by the correlations between different slices.

The truncated hierarchy has the same structure, with sums over the nodes. With the unit-normalised right eigenvectors $R_{mj}=r_m(u_j)/\sqrt{n_j}$ of the zero closure, for which $R^{\mathsf T}\Pw R=I$, $g_m=\sum_jR_{mj}a_j$ and $a_j=\sum_mp_mR_{mj}g_m$, the flux is
\begin{equation}\label{dec:fluxtrunc}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}=-\sum_{j,l}K_m(j,l)\,\dot C_{jl},\qquad
  K_m(j,l)=\sum_{n\le m}p_n\,R_{nj}R_{nl},\qquad
  b_m\big(R_{mj}R_{m+1,l}-R_{m+1,j}R_{ml}\big)=(u_l-u_j)\,K_m(j,l),
\end{equation}
with $\dot C_{jl}=-s(u_j-u_l)\langle a_l\gradpar a_j\rangle$. The last relation is the Christoffel--Darboux identity of the polynomials $r_n$ at the nodes, and it holds for every $m<M$. The copy closure changes only the last row of $A$ and the weight of $g_M$ (appendix~\ref{app:truncated}); with the eigenvectors normalised by its weight, (\ref{dec:fluxtrunc}) holds unchanged. We checked the budget (\ref{dec:budget}), the identity and the slice form of the flux against GANDALF's right-hand side for $M$ between 8 and 65, $\Lam=\pm\sqrt5$ and 3, and both closures, in states with strong brackets; all hold to $4\times10^{-14}$ or better.

At $\Lam=\infty$, $\varrho=F_M$ and $\sqrt{F_M}\,r_n=\psi_n$, the orthonormal Hermite functions, so $K_m(u,u')=\sum_{n\le m}\psi_n(u)\psi_n(u')$ is the classical Christoffel--Darboux kernel of the Hermite functions. For $|u|,|u'|\ll\sqrt{2m}$ it approaches the sine kernel,
\begin{equation*}
  K_m(u,u')\simeq\frac{\sin[\kappa_m(u-u')]}{\pi\,(u-u')},\qquad \kappa_m=\sqrt{2m+1};
\end{equation*}
on $|u|,|u'|<1.5$ the largest deviation is 0.8\%, 0.4\% and 0.2\% of the maximum of the kernel at $m=64$, 128 and 256. The factor $u-u'$ in (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) cancels the denominator, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fluxsine}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}\simeq\frac{s}{\pi}\iint\sin[\kappa_m(u-u')]\,\big\langle a_{u'}\gradpar a_u\big\rangle\,\dd u\,\dd u'
  =\frac{s}{\pi}\int\sin(\kappa_m\delta)\,\Theta(\delta)\,\dd\delta ,
\end{equation}
where $\Theta(\delta)=\int\langle a_{\bar u-\delta/2}\,\gradpar a_{\bar u+\delta/2}\rangle\,\dd\bar u$ is the pair correlation at the velocity separation $\delta$. It is odd in $\delta$, because $\gradpar$ is antisymmetric under the volume average. In the truncated system a pair of slices $(j,l)$ enters with the weight $s\sqrt{\Delta_j\Delta_l}\,\sin[\kappa_m(u_j-u_l)]/\pi$ multiplying $\langle a_l\gradpar a_j\rangle$, where $\Delta_j$ is the local spacing of the nodes.

Up to the factor $s/\pi$, the flux through $m$ is therefore the sine transform of the antisymmetric pair correlation $\Theta$ at the velocity-space wavenumber $\kappa_m$. The Hermite index selects a Fourier component of the dephasing pattern, not a band of slice separations. The weight $\sin(\kappa_m\delta)/\pi$ does not decay with $|\delta|$: at $m=128$ its envelope stays close to $1/\pi$ out to $|\delta|=6$, and in the random states of our checks the pairs closer than $2\pi/\kappa_m$ carried only 6--22\% of $\sum_{j,l}|K_m(j,l)\,\dot C_{jl}|$. At finite $\Lam$ the kernel approaches the sine form more slowly: on the same range of $u$ the largest deviation is 12.6\%, 8.7\% and 6.1\% of its maximum at $m=64$, 128 and 256 for $\Lam=\sqrt5$, and 4.6\%, 3.4\% and 2.3\% for $\Lam=-\sqrt5$.

The field-line part of the flux, which comes from $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ in $\gradpar$, is not small. Keeping only $\partial_z$ in (\ref{dec:budget}) gives the $k_z$-based flux, a sum over Fourier modes of
\begin{equation}\label{dec:fluxz}
  \Pi^z_{m+1/2}(\mathbf k)=-2s\,b_m\,k_z\,\mathrm{Im}\big[g_m(\mathbf k)^*g_{m+1}(\mathbf k)\big].
\end{equation}
GANDALF's diagnostic \texttt{hermite\_flux} returns $\Pi^z_{m+1/2}(\mathbf k)/(s\,p_m)$: it keeps only $\partial_z$, and it omits the factor $s$ and, at $m=0$, the factor $p_0$. In the strongly nonlinear random states of our checks the field-line part, $\Pi_{m+1/2}-\sum_{\mathbf k}\Pi^z_{m+1/2}(\mathbf k)$, was between 29\% and 253\% of $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ in magnitude, depending on $m$. In slice variables the $k_z$-based flux of a mode is the Christoffel--Darboux filter of (\ref{dec:fluxtrunc}) applied to the Eulerian linear term of (\ref{dec:pairk}) alone,
\begin{equation*}
  \Pi^z_{m+1/2}(\mathbf k)=-\sum_{j,l}K_m(j,l)\,\mathrm{Re}\big[-\ii sk_z\,(u_j-u_l)\,C_{\mathbf k}(j,l)\big],
\end{equation*}
with $C_{\mathbf k}(j,l)=a_j(\mathbf k)\,a_l(\mathbf k)^*$.

\subsection{The Hermite spectrum and the two-slice correlation}\label{dec:sec-dictionary}

At $\Lam=\infty$ the slices are the values of the distribution function, $a_u=G(u)/\sqrt{F_M(u)}$, and $g_m=\int\psi_m(u)\,a_u\,\dd u$. The Hermite spectrum is then a quadratic form in the two-slice correlation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dictionary}
  W(m)=\iint C(u,u')\,\psi_m(u)\,\psi_m(u')\,\dd u\,\dd u' .
\end{equation}
For finite $\Lam$ the same holds with $\psi_m$ replaced by $(p_m\varrho)^{1/2}r_m$, the Hermite coefficients of the unit-normalised Case--Van Kampen modes. If the slices are $\delta$-correlated, $C(u,u')=\mathcal E(u)\,\delta(u-u')$ with a slice-energy density $\mathcal E$, then $W(m)=\int\mathcal E\,\psi_m^2\,\dd u$, and for $\mathcal E=F_M$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefactor}
  \int F_M\,\psi_m^2\,\dd u=\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac12)}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,m!}=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2m}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{8m}+O(m^{-2})\Big).
\end{equation}

\citet{Kanekar2015} computed the exact Hermite spectrum of a single Fourier mode whose density moment is forced by white noise $\chi(t)$, with $\langle\chi(t)\chi^*(t')\rangle=\epsilon_k\,\delta(t-t')$, and damped only by the Landau coupling, as an integral over the real phase velocity $\zeta$ (their eq.~4.10, stated for $m\ge1$). At $\Lam=\infty$ the integrand reduces to $|Z^{(m)}(\zeta)|^2/2^mm!$, where $Z^{(m)}$ is the $m$th derivative of the plasma dispersion function. On the real axis $\mathrm{Im}\,Z^{(m)}(\zeta)=\pi(-1)^mH_m(\zeta)F_M(\zeta)$, and the real part is its Hilbert transform. Parseval's identity for the Hilbert transform then gives
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{1}{2^mm!}\int|Z^{(m)}(\zeta)|^2\,\dd\zeta=\frac{2\pi^2}{2^mm!}\int H_m^2F_M^2\,\dd\zeta=\frac{\sqrt2\,\pi\,\Gamma(m+\frac12)}{m!},
\end{equation*}
and their spectrum becomes, in our units (their $|k|$ is our $s|k_z|$),
\begin{equation}\label{dec:kanekar}
  W(m)=\frac{\pi\epsilon_k}{s|k_z|}\,\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac12)}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,m!} .
\end{equation}
This is (\ref{eq:prefactor}) with the density $\mathcal E=(\pi\epsilon_k/s|k_z|)F_M$: the forced linear state is a state of $\delta$-correlated slices. The closed form is not written in their paper, whose eq.~(4.14) is its large-$m$ limit, and the slice argument covers $m=0$ as well. Density forcing loads every slice with the same pattern (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}); each slice then streams at its own speed $su$, and two slices injected together dephase at the rate $s|k_z(u-u')|$, so that in the statistically steady state the correlation $C$ between different slices vanishes. At finite $\Lam$ the slices are no longer the values of $G$, and the closed form holds only asymptotically. At $\Lam=\sqrt5$ the exact spectrum is 4.7\% below $(1-1/\Lam)$ times (\ref{dec:kanekar}) at $m=1$. At large $m$ it tends to $W(m)\to\epsilon_W/(s|k_z|\sqrt{2m})$, where $\epsilon_W=(1-1/\Lam)\,\epsilon_k$ is the injection rate of $\sum_mW(m)$. This level does not depend on $\Lam$, because of their eq.~(4.15), which in our variables reads $\int F_M\,|\eps(\zeta+\ii0)|^{-2}\,\dd\zeta=1/(1-1/\Lam)$, that is, $\int\varrho\,\dd u=\Lam/(\Lam-1)$.

Correlated slices give other spectra. Let $C(u,u')=[\mathcal E(u)\mathcal E(u')]^{1/2}\,c(u-u')$, with $\mathcal E$ varying slowly on the correlation length of $c$, and let $\hat c(\kappa)=\int c(\delta)\cos(\kappa\delta)\,\dd\delta$ be the spectrum of the slice correlation in the velocity-space wavenumber $\kappa$. For $|u|,|u'|\ll\sqrt{2m}$, $\psi_m(u)\psi_m(u')\approx\{\cos[\sqrt{2m}(u-u')]+\cos[\sqrt{2m}(u+u')-m\pi]\}/(\pi\sqrt{2m})$, and the second term averages out against the slowly varying $\mathcal E$. Then (\ref{eq:dictionary}) gives
\begin{equation}\label{dec:powerlaw}
  W(m)\simeq\Big[\int\mathcal E\,\dd u\Big]\,\frac{\hat c(\sqrt{2m})}{\pi\sqrt{2m}} ,
\end{equation}
so the Hermite index $m$ probes the slice correlation at the velocity-space wavenumber $\sqrt{2m}$. A power-law spectrum $\hat c\propto\kappa^{-p}$ gives $W(m)\propto m^{-(p+1)/2}$, and the $\delta$-correlated case is $p=0$. For $1<p<3$ the same correlation has the second-order structure function $\langle(a_{u+\Delta}-a_u)^2\rangle\propto\Delta^{p-1}$ at small $\Delta$. Exponentially correlated slices, $c=e^{-|\delta|/\ell}$, have $p=2$ for $m\gg1/(2\ell^2)$; for $\ell=0.3$, direct quadrature of (\ref{eq:dictionary}) agrees with (\ref{dec:powerlaw}) to 0.2\% at $m=200$, where the local slope of $W(m)$ is $-1.463$ against $-1.462$ predicted. The correspondence is restricted to power-law spectra. If $\hat c$ decays faster than any power, the terms neglected in (\ref{dec:powerlaw}) dominate at large $m$: for a Gaussian correlation $c=e^{-\delta^2/2\ell^2}$ with $\ell=0.3$, the exact $W(m)$ exceeds (\ref{dec:powerlaw}) by factors of 1.16, 1.40 and 2.06 at $m=50$, 100 and 200. Figure~\ref{fig:dictionary} compares (\ref{eq:dictionary}) with the spectra computed by GANDALF's operator.

That the Hermite index $m$ corresponds to the velocity-space wavenumber $\sqrt{2m}$, so that moments of order $m$ resolve velocity scales of order $\pi/\sqrt{2m}$, is the duality of \citet{Schekochihin2016} and \citet{AdkinsSchekochihin2018}; given it, the relation between the exponents is elementary. \citet[note~81]{Eyink2018} related the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum to a velocity-space regularity exponent of $-1/2$, and \citet{Nastac2024} showed that linear phase mixing produces a spectrum that is flat in the velocity-space wavenumber up to a collisional cutoff, which is the case $p=0$. What the slice picture adds is the representation (\ref{eq:dictionary}) of the Hermite spectrum through the two-slice correlation, the closed form (\ref{dec:kanekar}) of the forced linear spectrum, and the reading of the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum as complete velocity-space decoherence of the slices.

\subsection{The \texorpdfstring{$k_z$}{kz}-based decomposition under field-line tilt}\label{dec:sec-tilt}

Diagnostics of phase mixing in simulations often use the Eulerian wavenumber $k_z$ in place of the field-aligned one. The $k_z$-based flux (\ref{dec:fluxz}) keeps only $\partial_z$ in (\ref{dec:budget}), and the phase-mixing/phase-unmixing split of each Fourier mode is made with $\mathrm{sgn}\,k_z$. The KRMHD diagnostics of \citet[chapter~6]{KanekarThesis2015} and GANDALF's diagnostics \texttt{phase\_mixing\_energy} and \texttt{phase\_unmixing\_energy} work in this way. \citet{Meyrand2019} did not use such a split, for the reason given in section~\ref{dec:sec-twoslice}. To see what the $k_z$-based quantities report when the field lines are tilted, we use a linear model: streaming along a uniformly tilted static field, $\gradpar=\partial_z+b_0\partial_x$, with no flow, so that each Fourier mode evolves with the field-aligned wavenumber $k_\parallel=k_z+b_0k_x$. Appendix~\ref{app:tilt} gives the derivations.

On each mode $\Pi^z_{m+1/2}(\mathbf k)=(k_z/k_\parallel)\,\Pi_{m+1/2}(\mathbf k)$. On the modes with $k_zk_\parallel<0$ the $k_z$-based flux therefore has the wrong sign, and the $\mathrm{sgn}\,k_z$ split exchanges the phase-mixing and phase-unmixing parts. Free streaming from a density perturbation is pure phase mixing, with a field-aligned flux that is non-negative on every mode and at every $m$ and $t$, yet a mode with $k_z<0<k_\parallel$ is reported as pure unmixing. Consider an ensemble of modes with a Gaussian spectrum $S(\mathbf k)$ in $k_z$ and $k_x$, with initial conditions that share one velocity profile, and define the effective tilt angle $\alpha$ by letting $\tan\alpha$ be the ratio of the r.m.s.\ value of $b_0k_x$ to that of $k_z$. The reported net flux and the spectral weight of the misclassified modes are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tiltcos}
  \Pi^z_{m+1/2}(t)=\cos^2\alpha\;\Pi_{m+1/2}(t),\qquad
  \frac{\int_{k_zk_\parallel<0}S\,\dd^3k}{\int S\,\dd^3k}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi},
\end{equation}
for every $m$, $t$ and $\Lam$, and with $m$-diagonal collisions. For pure phase mixing the sign split reports a backward flux although the true backward flux is zero. At fixed $m$ the reported backward share tends to $1/2$ at late times (table~\ref{appE:tab} and appendix~\ref{app:tilt}). Evaluated on an exact pure-phase-mixing solution with $\alpha=45^\circ$, GANDALF's \texttt{phase\_unmixing\_energy} reports an unmixing share of up to 33\% (figure~\ref{fig:tilt}). In critically balanced turbulence $b_\perp\sim k_\parallel/k_\perp$, so $b_0k_x$ is of the same order as $k_z$ and $\tan\alpha$ is of order unity. The $k_z$-based decomposition then assigns an order-unity fraction of phase mixing to unmixing.

The statement that the true backward flux vanishes holds for free streaming and, as we checked numerically for $\Lam=\sqrt5$, for the branch $\Lam^+$ with density initial data. It is not general: for $\Lam=-\sqrt5$ with density data, and for data in $g_1$, the field-aligned flux itself changes sign at low $m$ (appendix~\ref{app:tilt}). The relation (\ref{eq:tiltcos}) for the net flux does not depend on this premise. The difficulty lies with the diagnostics and not with the dynamics. The field-aligned flux (\ref{dec:budget}) and the two-slice law involve no wavenumber, and under ideal induction no weight moves between field-aligned wavenumbers, although the Eulerian $k_z$ of a Fourier mode can change sign (corollary~\ref{cor:kpar}).

\subsection{No Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint}\label{dec:sec-fjortoft}

\citet[section~4.1]{Chandran2026} discuss cascade directions with a generalisation of the argument of \citet{Fjortoft1953}: if two invariants $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B$ have spectra with $E_{\mathcal B}\ge c\,k^n|E_{\mathcal A}|$ for positive constants $c$ and $n$ (their condition 4.1), then $\mathcal A$ cannot cascade to large $k$ at a constant rate. They apply the same argument with $k$ replaced by the Hermite index $m$. In their appendix~B they note that $\Gamma$, whose Hermite form carries the factor $\sqrt{m+1}$, is not sign-definite, so that $W$ and $\Gamma$ do not satisfy the condition, and they expect both to cascade to large $m$. In their conclusion they ask more generally whether the conservation of helicity leads to a bottleneck in velocity space. The slice structure completes their argument for $\Gamma$ and extends it to every invariant of collisionless KRMHD, including the family $\Qphi$ and the Casimirs of section~\ref{sec:invariants}.

We write $e_m=H_mF_M/\sqrt{2^mm!}$ for the Hermite basis function with $g_n(e_m)=\delta_{nm}$, and $\Gamma_m=2b_m\langle g_mg_{m+1}\rangle$, so that $\sum_m\Gamma_m=\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle$; $\Gamma_m$ is the $m$-density of $\Gamma$ in the Hermite form (B32) of CMM, up to normalisation.

\begin{proposition}[No Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint]\label{prop:nofjortoft}
Let $\Lam\notin[0,1]$ and consider one compressive branch of collisionless KRMHD in a periodic box.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $\fe{e_m}{\varphi(L)e_n}=0$ for all $m\ne n$, then $\varphi$ is constant, almost everywhere on $\mathbb R$ (at the nodes $u_j$ in the truncated system). The only members of the family $\Qphi$ with a density diagonal in $m$ are the multiples of $Q_1$.
\item[(b)] A positive invariant can have a Hermite weight that grows with $m$: $Q_{u^2}=\int\fe{LG}{LG}\,\dd^3r\ge0$, and $Q_{u^2}(e_m)/Q_1(e_m)=m+\frac12$ for every $m\ge2$.
\item[(c)] Let $\mathcal B$ be any functional of $G$ that is conserved by the branch equation with $\Phi=\Psi=0$ (in particular, any invariant of collisionless KRMHD), written as a sum $\mathcal B=\sum_mE_{\mathcal B}(m)$. There are no constants $c>0$ and $n>0$ such that $E_{\mathcal B}(m)\ge c\,m^nW(m)$ for every $m$ and every state, and none such that $E_{\mathcal B}(m)\ge c\,m^n|\Gamma_m|$ for every $m$ and every state.
\item[(d)] $\Gamma$ and $H$ are odd under the mirror map (\ref{eq:mirror}). Every mirror-symmetric state has $\Gamma_m=0$ for all $m$ and $H=0$, whatever its $W(m)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) Let $d_{mn}=\fe{e_m}{\varphi(L)e_n}$ and suppose that $d_{mn}=0$ for $m\ne n$. The operator $L$ is symmetric for $\fe{\cdot}{\cdot}$, commutes with $\varphi(L)$, and acts on the Hermite basis as $Le_n=A_{n-1,n}e_{n-1}+A_{n+1,n}e_{n+1}$. The identity $\fe{e_m}{\varphi(L)Le_{m+1}}=\fe{Le_m}{\varphi(L)e_{m+1}}$ therefore reduces to $A_{m,m+1}d_{mm}=A_{m+1,m}d_{m+1,m+1}$. Writing $d_{mm}=p_m\theta_m$ and using $p_mA_{m,m+1}=p_{m+1}A_{m+1,m}\ne0$, we obtain $\theta_{m+1}=\theta_m$ for every $m$. Every matrix element of $\varphi(L)-\theta_0$ in the complete Hermite basis then vanishes, so $\varphi(L)=\theta_0$, and $\varphi=\theta_0$ almost everywhere with respect to the spectral measure of $L$, which is equivalent to Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R$ (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}). (For polynomial $\varphi$ the matrix of $\varphi(L)$ is the banded matrix $\varphi(A)$, and the same argument applies.) In the truncated system the argument applied to the matrix $\Pw\varphi(A)$ gives $\varphi(u_j)=\theta_0$ at every node.

(b) By the symmetry of $L$, $\fe{G}{L^2G}=\fe{LG}{LG}$, which is non-negative because $\fe{\cdot}{\cdot}$ is positive for $\Lam\notin[0,1]$. For $m\ge2$, $Le_m=\sqrt{m/2}\,e_{m-1}+\sqrt{(m+1)/2}\,e_{m+1}$ and $p_{m\pm1}=1$, so $\fe{Le_m}{Le_m}=m/2+(m+1)/2$, while $\fe{e_m}{e_m}=1$.

(c) The fields $\Phi=\Psi=0$ solve the RMHD equations (\ref{eq:rmhd}), and the slice equation (\ref{eq:slice}) reduces to $\partial_ta_u+su\,\partial_za_u=0$. Each slice translates along $z$, so every $\Qphi$ and every Casimir $\int\mathcal F(a_u)\,\dd^3r$ is conserved. Take initial data with $\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle>0$, with finite $\mathcal B$, and with no Fourier component at $k_z=0$. For each mode $g_m(\mathbf k,t)=\int\sqrt{\varrho}\,r_m\,a_u(\mathbf k,0)\,e^{-\ii sk_zut}\,\dd u$. The function $\sqrt\varrho\,r_m\,a_u(\mathbf k,0)$ is integrable in $u$, by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, so $g_m(\mathbf k,t)\to0$ as $t\to\infty$ by the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma. The bound $|g_m(\mathbf k,t)|^2\le p_m^{-1}\int|a_u(\mathbf k,0)|^2\,\dd u$ is summable over $\mathbf k$, so by dominated convergence $W(m,t)\to0$ for every $m$. Since $\sum_mW(m,t)=\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle$ is constant and positive, $\sum_mm^nW(m,t)\ge m_*^n\sum_{m>m_*}W(m,t)$, whose limit is $m_*^n\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle$, for every $m_*$, so $\sum_mm^nW(m,t)\to\infty$. A conserved $\mathcal B$ with $E_{\mathcal B}(m)\ge c\,m^nW(m)$ would satisfy $\mathcal B\ge c\sum_mm^nW(m,t)$ at all times, which is impossible. If in addition $\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle\ne0$, the same argument gives $\Gamma_m(t)\to0$ for every $m$ while $\sum_m\Gamma_m$ stays constant, so $\sum_{m>m_*}|\Gamma_m|$ tends to a limit not less than $|\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle|$, $\sum_mm^n|\Gamma_m|\to\infty$, and the second statement follows in the same way.

(d) By the parity rule of section~\ref{sec:symmetry}, $\langle g_mg_{m+1}\rangle$ is mirror-odd for every $m$, and so is $H$. A mirror-symmetric state therefore has $\Gamma_m=-\Gamma_m=0$ and $H=0$, while $W(m)$, which is mirror-even, can take any value.
\end{proof}

Part (c) is the central statement. Linear phase mixing in a uniform field conserves every member of the family and every Casimir exactly, while it moves all of the free energy, and all of $\Gamma$, to arbitrarily large $m$. No invariant, whatever $m$-density is assigned to it, can therefore bound the Hermite transfer. Free streaming from density data at a single wavenumber shows how a positive invariant with a weight that grows with $m$ accommodates this. $W(m\le m_*,t)$ is the probability that a Poisson variable of mean $\lambda=(sk_zt)^2/2$ does not exceed $m_*$, by (\ref{appE:poisson}); the part of $Q_{u^2}$ diagonal in $m$ grows as $\lambda+\frac12$, and the off-diagonal part, $-\lambda$, keeps $Q_{u^2}$ constant. In a truncated hierarchy the same holds until the recurrence time. At $M=512$ the fraction of the free energy in $m\le10$ falls from 1 to $3\times10^{-29}$ by $sk_zt=14$ for free streaming, and to $3\times10^{-4}$ for $\Lam=\sqrt5$. For data with $\Gamma\ne0$, $g(0)=e_0+0.8\,e_1$ at $M=3000$, the fraction $\sum_{m\le20}|\Gamma_m|/|\sum_m\Gamma_m|$ falls to $4\times10^{-28}$ for free streaming and to $4\times10^{-18}$ for $\Lam=\sqrt5$ by $sk_zt=30$, while $\sum_mm\,W(m)$ grows from 0.6 to 739 and to 530. In two-dimensional hydrodynamics a solution of this kind is impossible, because the energy above a wavenumber $k_*$ is bounded by the enstrophy divided by $k_*^2$.

The reason lies in the slice structure. Every invariant of section~\ref{sec:invariants} is a functional of single-slice statistics: $\Qphi$ depends only on the diagonal $C(u,u)$, a Casimir $\int\mathcal F(a_u)\,\dd^3r$ only on the one-point distribution of a single slice, and a cross-sector invariant (\ref{eq:crosssector}) only on slices of different sectors at the same $u$. The Hermite transfer (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) is $-\iint K_m\,\partial_tC$, a change in the correlation between different slices of one sector, with $\partial_tC(u,u)=0$. The invariants do not see it, and they can neither drive nor block it. The expectation of \citet{Chandran2026} that $\Gamma$, like $W$, is transferred to large $m$ is therefore borne out by the exact solutions considered here, for a reason that does not rely on the sign-indefiniteness of $\Gamma$. The argument concerns exact transient solutions of collisionless KRMHD, in the limit $k_\perp\rho_i\to0$. The criterion of \citet{Chandran2026} refers to inertial-range fluxes in a forced and dissipative steady state, and a steady-state version of the argument is not attempted here. At finite $k_\perp\rho_i$ the velocity-space operator depends on the wavenumber (section~\ref{sec:invariants}), and the argument would have to be redone.
