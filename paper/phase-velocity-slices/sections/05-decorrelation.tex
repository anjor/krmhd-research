\section{Phase mixing as decorrelation of slices}\label{sec:decorrelation}

Without collisions, sources or dissipation of $G$, the energy of every slice is conserved (section~\ref{sec:invariants}). A Hermite moment $g_m$ is a fixed linear combination of slices, so free energy can move between Hermite moments only through changes in the correlations between different slices. Throughout, $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is the average over the periodic box, $C(u,u')=\langle a_u\,a_{u'}\rangle$ is the two-slice correlation, whose diagonal is the slice energy $\mathcal E(u)$ of \eqref{eq:Qphi}, and $W(m)=p_m\langle g_m^2\rangle$ is the Hermite spectrum of the free energy, with $\sum_mW(m)=\int C(u,u)\,\dd u=2W^\pm/n_iT_i$.

\subsection{The two-slice law}\label{dec:sec-twoslice}

\begin{proposition}[Two-slice law]\label{prop:twoslice}
Assume the hypotheses of theorem~\ref{thm:slice}, with no collision operator, source or dissipation acting on $G$, and assume that the slice amplitudes $a_u(\boldsymbol r,t)$ are continuously differentiable in $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$, with derivatives that are continuous in $u$. Then, for all $u$ and $u'$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:twoslice}
  \frac{\dd C(u,u')}{\dd t}=-s\,(u-u')\,\big\langle a_{u'}\,\gradpar a_u\big\rangle .
\end{equation}
In particular, $C(u,u)$ is constant for every $u$, and the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow does not enter.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since $\partial_z+\{\Psi,\cdot\}=\gradpar$, the slice equation \eqref{eq:slice} reads $\partial_ta_u=-\{\Phi,a_u\}-su\,\gradpar a_u$. The bracket and $\gradpar$ obey the Leibniz rule, so
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_t(a_ua_{u'})=-\{\Phi,a_ua_{u'}\}-su'\,\gradpar(a_ua_{u'})-s\,(u-u')\,a_{u'}\gradpar a_u .
\end{equation*}
Since $\nabla\cdot\uE=\nabla\cdot\bhat=0$, $\{\Phi,f\}=\nabla\cdot(f\,\uE)$ and $\gradpar f=\nabla\cdot(f\,\bhat)$, so the first two terms are divergences, and their averages over the periodic box vanish.
\end{proof}

The $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow carries the two slices of a pair together and cannot change their correlation, which changes only through their relative velocity $s(u-u')\,\bhat$, that is, through relative streaming along the perturbed field. Phase mixing and phase unmixing are therefore the decorrelation and the recorrelation of slices along $\gradpar$, a definition that refers to no wavenumber. Under the hypotheses of theorem~\ref{thm:frozen}, \eqref{eq:twoslice} integrates in closed form \eqref{eq:frozencorr}.

The law needs no assumption on the Alfv\'enic fields beyond smoothness; terms that act on $G$ add those of \eqref{eq:sliceforcing}. It holds for the truncated hierarchy, with $C_{jl}=\langle a_ja_l\rangle$ at the nodes $u_j$, under the conditions of section~\ref{sec:truncated}, and we checked it to round-off against GANDALF's right-hand side \citep{Kanekar2025}.

With a straight guide field, in electrostatic drift kinetics \citep{Schekochihin2016,Parker2016} and in the linear model of \citet{Kanekar2015}, the Hermite moments of each Fourier mode are split by the sign of the parallel wavenumber into parts that propagate to larger and to smaller $m$. In KRMHD the field lines move; \citet{Meyrand2019} noted that the sign would then have to be taken with respect to the perturbed field and, not knowing how to make this rigorous, did not use such a split. The two-slice law describes phase mixing along the perturbed field without reference to a wavenumber. Where the field lines are tilted, a split by the sign of $k_z$ differs from a field-aligned one, because it exchanges phase mixing and unmixing on modes whose $k_z$ and field-aligned wavenumber have opposite signs (section~\ref{sec:discussion}).

\subsection{The Hermite flux as a Christoffel--Darboux transform}\label{dec:sec-flux}

With $r_n(u)$ the $n$th Hermite coefficient of the Case--Van Kampen mode $F_u$, a polynomial of degree $n$, the functions $\phi_n=\sqrt{p_n\varrho}\;r_n$ form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$ (section~\ref{sec:slices}), and unitarity gives
\begin{equation}\label{dec:gn}
\begin{gathered}
  g_n=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p_n}}\int\phi_n(u)\,a_u\,\dd u,\\
  E_{\le m}\equiv\sum_{n\le m}W(n)=\iint K_m(u,u')\,C(u,u')\,\dd u\,\dd u',\qquad
  K_m(u,u')=\sum_{n\le m}\phi_n(u)\,\phi_n(u') .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation}
Let $b_m=p_mA_{m,m+1}=p_{m+1}A_{m+1,m}$. In the Hermite form $\partial_tg+\{\Phi,g\}+s\gradpar(Ag)=0$ of \eqref{eq:branch}, the bracket does not change $E_{\le m}$, and in the streaming term only the coupling between $m$ and $m+1$ survives, because $\Pw A$ is symmetric and $\langle g_n\gradpar g_{n'}\rangle$ antisymmetric. With the two-slice law, the slice form of $E_{\le m}$ gives the same rate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fluxCD}
\begin{aligned}
  \frac{\dd E_{\le m}}{\dd t}=-\Pi_{m+1/2},\qquad
  \Pi_{m+1/2}&=s\,b_m\big\langle g_m\gradpar g_{m+1}-g_{m+1}\gradpar g_m\big\rangle\\
  &=-\iint K_m(u,u')\,\frac{\partial C(u,u')}{\partial t}\,\dd u\,\dd u' ,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
the free energy per unit time and volume that passes from moment $m$ to $m+1$. The first form is the Hermite flux of \citet{Schekochihin2016} and \citet{AdkinsSchekochihin2018} with the full field-aligned gradient, as used by \citet[eq.~9]{Meyrand2019}.

The two forms are related by a Christoffel--Darboux identity. Since $\Tr[LG]=u\,\Tr[G]$ and $Le_n=A_{n-1,n}e_{n-1}+A_{n+1,n}e_{n+1}$, the symmetry of $\Pw A$ gives the recurrence $u\,r_n=A_{n,n+1}\,r_{n+1}+A_{n,n-1}\,r_{n-1}$, $r_{-1}=0$. Multiply it at $u$ by $p_nr_n(u')$, subtract the same with $u$ and $u'$ exchanged, and sum over $n\le m$; since $p_nA_{n,n+1}=b_n$ and $p_nA_{n,n-1}=b_{n-1}$, the sum telescopes to
\begin{equation}\label{dec:CD}
  (u-u')\,K_m(u,u')=b_m\sqrt{\varrho(u)\varrho(u')}\,\big[r_{m+1}(u)\,r_m(u')-r_m(u)\,r_{m+1}(u')\big] .
\end{equation}
With \eqref{eq:twoslice}, the second form of $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ is $s\iint(u-u')K_m\langle a_{u'}\gradpar a_u\rangle\,\dd u\,\dd u'$, which \eqref{dec:CD} and \eqref{dec:gn} turn into the first. The pairs $u=u'$ carry no flux: the free energy that passes through every $m$ is carried by the correlations between different slices. The truncated hierarchy has the same structure, with $R_{mj}$ of theorem~\ref{thm:trunc} and $K_m(j,l)=\sum_{n\le m}p_nR_{nj}R_{nl}$, for $m<M$ and both closures (appendix~\ref{app:truncated}); we checked this to round-off with GANDALF's ladder matrix and right-hand side.

At $\Lam=\infty$, $\phi_n=\psi_n$, the orthonormal Hermite functions, and for $|u|,|u'|\ll\sqrt{2m}$ the kernel approaches the sine kernel $\sin[\kappa_m(u-u')]/[\pi(u-u')]$, with $\kappa_m=\sqrt{2m+1}$. The factor $u-u'$ in the pair form cancels the denominator, and for slices concentrated at $|u|\ll\sqrt{2m}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fluxsine}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}\simeq\frac{s}{\pi}\iint\sin[\kappa_m(u-u')]\,\big\langle a_{u'}\gradpar a_u\big\rangle\,\dd u\,\dd u'
  =\frac{s}{\pi}\int\sin(\kappa_m\delta)\,\Theta(\delta)\,\dd\delta ,
\end{equation}
where $\Theta(\delta)=\int\langle a_{\bar u-\delta/2}\,\gradpar a_{\bar u+\delta/2}\rangle\,\dd\bar u$, the pair correlation at velocity separation $\delta$, is odd in $\delta$. Up to $s/\pi$, the flux through $m$ is the sine transform of $\Theta$ at the velocity-space wavenumber $\kappa_m$: the Hermite index selects a Fourier component of the dephasing pattern, not a band of slice separations. At finite $\Lam$ the pair weight $(u-u')K_m$ does not approach $\sin[\kappa_m(u-u')]/\pi$, and its deviation remains of order $1/\pi$ as $m$ grows.

\subsection{The Hermite spectrum and the two-slice correlation}\label{dec:sec-dictionary}

By \eqref{dec:gn}, the Hermite spectrum is a quadratic form in the two-slice correlation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dictionary}
  W(m)=\iint C(u,u')\,\phi_m(u)\,\phi_m(u')\,\dd u\,\dd u' .
\end{equation}
We call the slices $\delta$-correlated if $C(u,u')=e(u)\,\delta(u-u')$ with a density $e\ge0$. They are then completely decorrelated in velocity, and $W(m)=\int e\,\phi_m^2\,\dd u$. For $e=F_M$ at $\Lam=\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:prefactor}
  \int F_M\,\psi_m^2\,\dd u=\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac12)}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,m!}=\frac{1}{\pi\sqrt{2m}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{8m}+O(m^{-2})\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\Gamma$ Euler's gamma function. This spectrum decays only as $m^{-1/2}$, so $\sum_mW(m)$ diverges: $\delta$-correlated slices idealise states with velocity structure on arbitrarily fine scales, such as the collisionless forced state below.

\citet{Kanekar2015} studied a one-dimensional linear model of the form of one branch, with $\Lam=-1/\alpha$ for their parameter $\alpha$, whose density moment is driven by white noise of strength $\epsilon_k$. Their eq.~(4.10), derived without collisions, gives the exact steady-state Hermite spectrum of a Fourier mode for $m\ge1$, which in our variables (their $|k|$ is our $s|k_z|$) reads
\begin{equation*}
  W(m)=\frac{(1-1/\Lam)^2\,\epsilon_k}{2\pi\,s|k_z|}\,\frac{1}{2^mm!}\int\frac{|Z^{(m)}(\zeta)|^2}{|\eps(\zeta+\ii0)|^2}\,\dd\zeta ,
\end{equation*}
where $Z^{(m)}$ is the $m$th derivative of the plasma dispersion function. At $\Lam=\infty$, $\eps=1$, and on the real axis $\mathrm{Im}\,Z^{(m)}=(-1)^m\pi H_mF_M$ and $\mathrm{Re}\,Z^{(m)}=(-1)^m\pi\,\mathsf H[H_mF_M]$, with $\mathsf H$ the Hilbert transform, which preserves the $L^2$ norm. Hence $(2^mm!)^{-1}\int|Z^{(m)}|^2\,\dd\zeta=\sqrt2\,\pi\,\Gamma(m+\frac12)/m!$, and
\begin{equation}\label{dec:kanekar}
  W(m)=\frac{\pi\epsilon_k}{s|k_z|}\,\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac12)}{\sqrt2\,\pi\,m!} ,
\end{equation}
which is \eqref{eq:prefactor} with $e=(\pi\epsilon_k/s|k_z|)F_M$: the forced linear spectrum is the spectrum of $\delta$-correlated slices. The closed form is not written in their paper, whose eq.~(4.14) is its large-$m$ limit, and the slice argument covers $m=0$ as well.

The reason is dephasing. Density forcing loads every slice with the same spatial pattern \eqref{eq:sliceforcing}, two slices loaded together dephase at the rate $s|k_z(u-u')|$, and the steady state superposes all past injections, so the real part of the mean of $a_u(\boldsymbol k)\,a_{u'}(\boldsymbol k)^*$ vanishes for $u\ne u'$. The antisymmetric imaginary part, which does not enter \eqref{eq:dictionary}, carries the injection rate $\epsilon_W=(1-1/\Lam)\,\epsilon_k$ through every $m$. At finite $\Lam$ the slices are again $\delta$-correlated, with $e=\pi(1-1/\Lam)^2\epsilon_k\,\varrho/(s|k_z|)$, which reproduces their eq.~(4.10) exactly, and $(1-1/\Lam)$ times \eqref{dec:kanekar} holds only asymptotically, with the large-$m$ level $\epsilon_W/(s|k_z|\sqrt{2m})$, independent of $\Lam$ by their eq.~(4.15), which in our variables is the normalisation $\int\varrho\,\dd u=1/p_0$.

Correlated slices give steeper spectra. If $C(u,u')=[e(u)e(u')]^{1/2}\,c(u-u')$ at $\Lam=\infty$, with $e$ slowly varying, the large-$m$ form of $\psi_m$ gives $W(m)\simeq[\int e\,\dd u]\,\hat c(\sqrt{2m})/(\pi\sqrt{2m})$, with $\hat c$ the cosine transform of $c$, so a power law $\hat c\propto\kappa^{-p}$ gives $W(m)\propto m^{-(p+1)/2}$ (figure~\ref{fig:dictionary}); the correspondence fails at large $m$ when $\hat c$ decays faster than any power. That $\sqrt{2m}$ acts as a velocity-space wavenumber is the duality noted by \citet{Schekochihin2016} and \citet{AdkinsSchekochihin2018}; \citet[note~81]{Eyink2018} related the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum to a velocity-space regularity exponent of $-1/2$, and \citet{Nastac2024} describe linear phase mixing as producing a spectrum flat in the velocity-space wavenumber, the case $p=0$. The slice picture adds the representation \eqref{eq:dictionary}, the closed form \eqref{dec:kanekar}, and the reading of the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum as complete velocity-space decoherence of the slices.

\subsection{No Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint}\label{dec:sec-fjortoft}

\citet[section~4.1]{Chandran2026} discuss cascade directions in $m$ with the generalisation by \citet{AlexakisBiferale2018} of the argument of \citet{Fjortoft1953}: if $E_{\mathcal I_2}(m)\ge c\,m^n|E_{\mathcal I_1}(m)|$ for two invariants and positive $c$ and $n$, then $\mathcal I_1$ cannot cascade to large $m$ at a constant rate. In their appendix~B they note that this condition fails for $W$ and $\Gamma$, which is not sign-definite, and expect both to cascade to large $m$. We complete their argument for $\Gamma$ and extend it to every invariant that depends on $G$ alone. Let $\Gamma_m=2b_m\langle g_mg_{m+1}\rangle$, so that $\sum_m\Gamma_m=\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle$; up to normalisation, $\Gamma_m$ is the contribution of the pair $(m,m+1)$ to CMM's (B32).

\begin{proposition}[No Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint]\label{prop:nofjortoft}
Let $\Lam\notin[0,1]$ and consider one compressive branch of collisionless KRMHD in a periodic box. Let $\mathcal I$ be a functional of $G$ that is defined and constant along every solution of the branch equation with $\Phi=\Psi=0$ that starts from a state with a finite Hermite expansion in $u$ (in particular, any invariant of collisionless KRMHD that depends on $G$ alone and is defined along these solutions), and let $\mathcal I=\sum_mE_{\mathcal I}(m)$ be any decomposition of it over Hermite indices. No constants $c>0$ and $n>0$ make $E_{\mathcal I}(m)\ge c\,m^nW(m)$, or $E_{\mathcal I}(m)\ge c\,m^n|\Gamma_m|$, hold for every $m$ and every state. Moreover:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] if $\fe{e_m}{\varphi(L)e_n}=0$ for all $m\ne n$, with $\varphi$ real and the $e_n$ in the domain of $\varphi(L)$, then $\varphi$ is constant almost everywhere, so the only members of the family $\Qphi$ that are diagonal in $m$ are the multiples of $Q_1$;
\item[(b)] a positive invariant can have a Hermite weight that grows with $m$: $Q_{u^2}=\int\fe{LG}{LG}\,\dd^3r\ge0$, and $Q_{u^2}(e_m)/Q_1(e_m)=m+\frac12$ for every $m\ge2$;
\item[(c)] every $\Gamma_m$, like $\Gamma$ and $\Hph$ (section~\ref{sec:slices}), is odd under the mirror map \eqref{eq:mirror}, so every mirror-symmetric state has $\Gamma_m=0$ for all $m$ and $\Hph=0$, whatever its $W(m)$.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
With $\Phi=\Psi=0$, which solve \eqref{eq:rmhd}, \eqref{eq:slice} reduces to $\partial_ta_u+su\,\partial_za_u=0$, so every $\Qphi$ and every Casimir \eqref{eq:casimir} is conserved. Take initial data with a finite Hermite expansion in $u$, smooth in space, with $\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle>0$ and no Fourier component at $k_z=0$; $\mathcal I$ is constant along this solution, and $G$ stays in the domain of $L$. By \eqref{dec:gn}, $g_m(\boldsymbol k,t)=\int\sqrt\varrho\,r_m\,a_u(\boldsymbol k,0)\,e^{-\ii sk_zut}\,\dd u$, with an integrand that is integrable in $u$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality, so $g_m(\boldsymbol k,t)\to0$ by the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma. The bound $|g_m(\boldsymbol k,t)|^2\le p_m^{-1}\int|a_u(\boldsymbol k,0)|^2\,\dd u$ is summable over $\boldsymbol k$, so by dominated convergence $W(m,t)\to0$ and $\Gamma_m(t)\to0$ for every $m$. If $E_{\mathcal I}(m)\ge c\,m^nW(m)$ for every $m$ and state, then $E_{\mathcal I}(m)\ge0$ and $\mathcal I\ge c\,m_*^n\sum_{m>m_*}W(m,t)$ for every $m_*$; since $\sum_mW(m,t)=\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle$ is constant, the right-hand side tends to $c\,m_*^n\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle$, which exceeds the constant $\mathcal I$ for $m_*$ large enough. If instead $E_{\mathcal I}(m)\ge c\,m^n|\Gamma_m|$, take data with $\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle\ne0$. Since $G(t)$ stays in the domain of $L$, $\sum_m\Gamma_m(t)$ converges absolutely to the conserved $\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle$, so $\sum_{m>m_*}|\Gamma_m(t)|\ge|\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle-\sum_{m\le m_*}\Gamma_m(t)|\to|\langle\fe{G}{LG}\rangle|$, and the same argument applies.

(a) It suffices to take $n=0$. Since the $e_m$ are complete and $\fe{e_m}{e_0}=p_0\delta_{m0}$, the hypothesis gives $\varphi(L)e_0=\theta\,e_0$ for a constant $\theta$. The slice amplitude of $e_0$, $p_0\sqrt{\varrho(u)}$, is positive for every $u$, and that of $\varphi(L)e_0$ is $\varphi(u)\,p_0\sqrt{\varrho(u)}$, so $\varphi=\theta$ almost everywhere.

(b) By the symmetry of $L$, $\fe{G}{L^2G}=\fe{LG}{LG}\ge0$ for $\Lam\notin[0,1]$. For $m\ge2$, $Le_m=\sqrt{m/2}\,e_{m-1}+\sqrt{(m+1)/2}\,e_{m+1}$ and $p_{m\pm1}=1$, so $\fe{Le_m}{Le_m}=m+\frac12$, while $\fe{e_m}{e_m}=1$.

(c) Since $H_m(-u)=(-1)^mH_m(u)$, the map \eqref{eq:mirror} takes $g_m$ to $(-1)^mg_m(\tilde{\boldsymbol r})$, so it reverses the sign of every $\langle g_mg_{m+1}\rangle$ and leaves $W(m)$ unchanged; a mirror-symmetric state therefore has $\Gamma_m=-\Gamma_m=0$.
\end{proof}

Linear phase mixing in a uniform field thus conserves every invariant of section~\ref{sec:invariants} while it moves all of the free energy, and all of $\Gamma$, to arbitrarily large $m$ (in a truncated hierarchy, until the recurrence time); in two-dimensional hydrodynamics this is impossible, because the energy above a wavenumber $k_*$ is bounded by the enstrophy divided by $k_*^2$. The reason is that every invariant of section~\ref{sec:invariants} depends only on single-slice statistics ($\Qphi$ on the diagonal $C(u,u)$, a Casimir on the one-point distribution of one slice, a cross-sector invariant on slices of different sectors at the same $u$), whereas the Hermite transfer \eqref{eq:fluxCD} is $-\iint K_m\,\partial_tC$, a change in the correlations between different slices of one sector. The expectation of \citet{Chandran2026} that $\Gamma$, like $W$, is transferred to large $m$ is therefore borne out by these exact solutions, for a reason that does not rely on the sign-indefiniteness of $\Gamma$. The argument concerns transient solutions of collisionless KRMHD; CMM's criterion refers to inertial-range fluxes in a forced and dissipative steady state, which this argument does not address.
