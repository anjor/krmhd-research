\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The slice decomposition (theorems~\ref{thm:slice} and~\ref{thm:trunc}) holds for $k_\perp\rho_i\to0$, for each branch and for the Laguerre sectors with $l\ge2$, for any smooth Alfv\'enic fields, which may be forced, resistive or prescribed, and without collisions, forcing or spatial dissipation of the compressive distribution except where stated. The frozen-line theorem needs in addition the hypotheses (\ref{eq:hyp}): an ideal induction equation, no spatial dissipation of the compressive distribution, and Alfv\'enic fields that stay smooth. It allows collisions that act at each point separately, and forcing that is white in time, for which it concerns the means of quadratic statistics.

Without collisions the energy of every slice is conserved, so free energy does not move between phase velocities, and velocity-space transfer is the decorrelation and recorrelation of independent passive scalars by their relative streaming along the perturbed field (proposition~\ref{prop:twoslice}). The invariants fix the distribution of free energy over phase velocity but not the correlations between slices, so they impose no Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint on this transfer (proposition~\ref{prop:nofjortoft}); this concerns transient solutions, not the forced steady states to which CMM's cascade criterion refers. Under (\ref{eq:hyp}), $W(m,t)$ and the field-aligned Hermite flux evolve as in linear phase mixing along the initial field lines, and with white forcing their means are sums of such responses (corollaries~\ref{cor:singlepoint} and~\ref{cor:forcing}). Stochastic echoes in $W(m)$ therefore require a non-ideal induction equation, spatial dissipation of the compressive distribution, forcing correlated in time, or, as the resistivity goes to zero, a failure of flux freezing for fields that do not stay smooth \citep{Eyink2013,Eyink2018,Jafari2026}; the simulations in which echoes were reported \citep{Meyrand2019} dissipate both the magnetic field and the compressive distribution in space, so the theorem does not contradict them.

For simulations, fluxes and splits of the free energy should be computed along the perturbed field rather than from $k_z$, because a split by the sign of $k_z$ exchanges phase mixing and unmixing on modes whose $k_z$ and field-aligned wavenumber have opposite signs. Diagnostics of the phase-space helicity should use an odd truncation order $M$, for which the conserved discrete helicity is CMM's $\Hph$ on the retained moments (\ref{eq:Hoddeven}). Each slice could also be advanced as an independent passive scalar, for example by a semi-Lagrangian method along its velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$ (\ref{eq:Uu}); we have not tested such a scheme.

Hypothesis (H2) of (\ref{eq:hyp}) excludes spatial dissipation of the compressive distribution. Each slice is a passive scalar, and in turbulent flows the rate at which the variance of a passive scalar is dissipated remains finite as the diffusivity goes to zero, which is the dissipative anomaly of passive-scalar mixing \citep{ShraimanSiggia2000,FalkovichGawedzkiVergassola2001}. The $k_\perp$ sink of each slice therefore need not disappear as the dissipation coefficients go to zero, and in turbulence its competition with phase mixing, rather than an ideal echo, may set the Hermite spectrum at large $m$; whether it does is an open question. At finite $k_\perp\rho_i$ the Landau coupling depends on the wavenumber (CMM's 3.10 and 3.14), the advection depends on $v_\perp$ (their 3.16) and the compressive fluctuations act back on the Alfv\'enic ones, so the slices no longer decouple; CMM do not list $\Gamma^\pm$ among the invariants of general gyrokinetics, \citet{CostelloPlunk2025} have extended an invariant of the model of \citet{Plunk2015} to finite Larmor radius, and which invariants survive is open. Velocity-dependent collisions are the only terms that exchange free energy between slices, through the kernel in (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}); how this combines with their decorrelation in turbulence is also open.
