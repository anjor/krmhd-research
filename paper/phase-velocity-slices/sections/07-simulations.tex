\subsection{Set-up}\label{sim:setup}

We integrate (\ref{eq:rmhd}) in Elsasser form with one compressive branch (\ref{mod:hierarchy}), the branch $\Lambda^+$ of (\ref{eq:Lambdapm}) with $\tau=Z=1$, truncated at $m=M$, using GANDALF \citep{Kanekar2025} in double precision. The box is periodic with side $L=1$, so $k_1=2\pi$; $v_A=1$ and $\tau_A=L/v_A=1$. Appendix~\ref{app:runs} gives the time stepping, the averaging windows and the checks of every run.

The Alfv\'enic fluctuations are forced through $\Phi$ alone, so that $z^+$ and $z^-$ receive the same increment, which keeps the forcing out of the induction equation (section~\ref{sec:frozen}). The forcing is white in time and acts on the Fourier modes with $1\le k_\perp/k_1\le2$ and $|k_z|=k_1$, with injection rate $\eps_A=0.3$ except in the runs at lower $\chi$ (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). The density moment $g_0$ is forced on the same band by independent Gaussian increments whose variance is proportional to the time step, so that the expected injection rate is $\eps=0.3$ whatever the time step. We call the spatial pattern of this forcing the injected pattern. The band excludes $k_\perp=0$, where injected free energy would phase mix with no perpendicular cascade. Every injection therefore has the single Eulerian parallel wavenumber $k_z=\pm k_1$, and its field-aligned wavenumbers differ from $k_1$ only through the tilt of the field lines.

GANDALF applies one hyperdissipation, at the rate $\eta\,(k_\perp^2/k_{\perp,\max}^2)^2$, alike to $z^\pm$ and to every $g_m$, with $\eta=300$ except at lower $\chi$; there is no parallel dissipation. Hypercollisions damp $g_m$ at the rate $\nu_m=\nu\,(m/M)^6$ for $m\ge2$ ($\nu_m=0$ for $m\le1$) and so remove its free energy at the rate $2\nu_m$. Free energy moving up the ladder at $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$ reaches $m=xM$ with the fraction $\exp[-(2\sqrt2/13)\,(\nu M^{1/2}/s\kappa)\,x^{13/2}]$ left by the hypercollisions, so we set $\nu=30\,\beta_i^{1/2}(32/M)^{1/2}$, which keeps this fraction fixed as $\beta_i$ and $M$ change. The free energy passes the moments $m\le M/2$ quickly enough that the hypercollisions remove only 3\% of the injected free energy there at $\beta_i=1$ and $M=32$; above $m=M/2$ they remove most of what arrives. Measured against the perpendicular sink they act from lower $m$, and at $M=32$, with the $T$ of table~\ref{tab:runs}, $2\nu_m$ reaches $0.13/T$ at $m=10$--13 and $1/T$ at $m=14$--18. We call $2\le m\le m_{\rm c}$ the collision-free range, with $m_{\rm c}$ the largest $m$ at which $2\nu_m\le0.13/T$, the criterion of the $m_*$ scan (section~\ref{sim:mstar}), and $1/T$ here the median of $D_\perp/W$ over the same range (appendix~\ref{app:runs}). It gives $m_{\rm c}=9$--12 at $M=32$, 19--27 at $M=64$ and 57--210 at $M\ge128$. The $T$ of table~\ref{tab:runs} is the median over $2\le m\le16$ in every run, the same moments at every $\beta_i$ and $M$. These moments are collision-free at $M\ge64$; at $M=32$, $T$ over $2\le m\le m_{\rm c}$ is 3--6\% longer.

We characterise the outer scale by the part of $\Phi$ in the shells $k_\perp/k_1\le2.5$. Its energy-weighted mean $k_\perp$ and $|k_z|$, $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}$ and $k_{z,\mathrm{out}}$, and its rms $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity $u_{\mathrm{out}}$ give $\chi=k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}}/(k_{z,\mathrm{out}}v_A)$, $\tau_{\rm nl}=1/(k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}})$ and $c_T=T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ (section~\ref{tc:scales}). In the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$, $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}=1.55\,k_1$, $k_{z,\mathrm{out}}=1.11\,k_1$ and $u_{\mathrm{out}}=0.71$, so $\chi=0.99$ and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.144$. The Alfv\'enic sector does not depend on $\beta_i$, and its outer scale hardly depends on the resolution (section~\ref{sim:resolution}), so every run of table~\ref{tab:runs} has $\chi$ between 0.92 and 0.99. At $64^3$ there is no inertial range. In the run at $\beta_i=1$ the slope of $E_A(k_\perp)$ over $2\le k_\perp/k_1\le8$ is $-2.2$, and the local slope steepens continuously, from $-1.8$ between $k_\perp/k_1=2$ and 3 to $-4.4$ between 9 and 10. Every result we label as a $64^3$ result comes from runs without an inertial range, and section~\ref{sim:resolution} adds one.

The energy change of every step is split into the parts due to the ideal dynamics, the forcing, the hyperdissipation and the hypercollisions. This gives $D_\perp(m)$ and $D_\nu(m)$ in (\ref{eq:budget}) exactly for every moment. Every 20 steps we record $W(m)$, its perpendicular spectrum, the field-aligned flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}), the $k_z$-only flux, and $E_A=\frac12\langle|\nabla_\perp\Phi|^2+|\nabla_\perp\Psi|^2\rangle$ and its spectrum. Following \citet{Schekochihin2016}, we split the $k_z$-only flux by the sign of $k_z$. For each Fourier mode with $k_z\ne0$ we write $\tilde g_m=(\ii\,\mathrm{sgn}\,k_z)^mg_m$ and $g^\pm_m=(\tilde g_m\pm\tilde g_{m+1})/\sqrt2$, and the $k_z$-only flux becomes $s\,b_m\sum_{\boldsymbol k}|k_z|\,(|g^+_m|^2-|g^-_m|^2)$, summed over the modes and normalised so that $\sum_{\boldsymbol k}|g_m|^2=\langle g_m^2\rangle$. Its first part carries free energy to higher $m$ and is called the phase-mixing part; its second carries free energy to lower $m$ and is called the unmixing part.

Field-aligned wavenumbers are measured along traced field lines, as is done for the Alfv\'enic fields \citep{ChoVishniac2000,Meyrand2019}. On snapshots spaced by $5\,\tau_A$ within the averaging window ($10\,\tau_A$ at $96^3$ and above, and 10--$50\,\tau_A$ in the runs of the $m_*$ scan) we trace 4096 lines of $\bhat=\zhat+\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$ over four box lengths in $z$, sample $g_m$, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ along them, and compute their spectra along the lines, from which we take $\kappa_1(m)$. The distribution $P(\kappa)$ of the injected free energy is measured in the same way, on fresh realisations of the density forcing sampled along the same lines (appendix~\ref{app:methods}). These line spectra estimate the decomposition of section~\ref{fr:kpar}. Moving a field $f$ a distance $\ell$ in $z$ along the field lines is the operator $\exp(\ell\gradpar)$, which preserves volume because $\nabla\cdot\bhat=0$. The correlation $\langle f\exp(\ell\gradpar)f\rangle$ along the lines is therefore the Fourier transform in $\ell$ of the distribution of the variance of $f$ over the eigenvalues $\kappa$ of $-\ii\gradpar$, as the correlation in $z$ is that of the spectrum in $k_z$ in a straight field. Lines started at random points of the plane $z=0$ sample the volume uniformly, because the map along the lines from one plane of constant $z$ to another preserves area. The spectrum along segments of length $4L$, averaged over the lines, therefore estimates the field-aligned spectrum smoothed over the resolution $k_1/4$ of a segment, and a Hann window keeps the leakage from the ends of the segments small (appendix~\ref{app:methods}).

Where we compare a measured quantity with the model, we do so over the collision-free range, which leaves out the moments $m\le1$ that hold the freshly injected free energy and the moments above $m_{\rm c}$, where the hypercollisions and not the perpendicular sink shape $W(m)$. We give the value for the whole averaging window and, as the block range, the range of the same comparison over four consecutive blocks of equal length of the window, each compared with the model for the whole window. $T$ and the ratio of the field-aligned flux to the phase-mixing flux, over $8\le m\le M/2$ (section~\ref{sim:flux}), use other ranges, for reasons given where they appear.

\subsection{No return flux}\label{sim:flux}

In every run, at every resolution, the field-aligned flux is the flux that $W(m)$ carries by phase mixing at its measured field-aligned wavenumber, with no deficit that grows with $m$. We show this first for the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$. Its break lies far beyond $m=M$ (section~\ref{sim:break}), so it tests (i) and (ii) but not the break.

The perpendicular dissipation removes free energy from each moment nearly in proportion to its free energy, as (\ref{eq:uniformsink}) assumes. Over the collision-free range $2\le m\le m_{\rm c}=9$, $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ lies between 0.39 and 0.44, with a systematic rise of 13\% (block range 11--16\%) that section~\ref{sim:beta} traces to the age of the free energy (figure~\ref{fig:wm}b). We take $1/T=0.437$, its median over $2\le m\le16$, so $T=2.29=15.9\,\tau_{\rm nl}$. Because $T$ is measured from $D_\perp/W$, this checks the assumption of a uniform sink and does not test the model independently. Above $m=M/2$ the ratio rises, to $D_\perp T/W=3.4$ at $m=32$, because the hypercollisions remove the slowly phase-mixing free energy first, and what survives has larger $\kappa$ and sits at larger $k_\perp$, where the hyperdissipation is stronger.

The independent test is the relation between the flux and the spectrum. Over the collision-free range the moments $g_m$ have $\kappa_1(m)$ between $1.13\,k_1$ and $1.17\,k_1$, against $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$ for the injected pattern. Taken at the snapshot times at which $\kappa_1(m)$ is measured, so that $\Pi$, $W$ and $\kappa_1(m)$ come from the same instants, the measured flux is 0.98--0.99 times $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$, as in the uniform-sink model, which has no return flux and lies a little below 1 because the sink removes free energy while it phase mixes. With $\Pi$ and $W$ averaged over the whole window the ratio is 0.957--0.965 (block range 0.955--0.970). There is no room for a return flux of more than a few per cent.

With this $T$ and the $P(\kappa)$ of the injected pattern, the model reproduces $W(m)$ to within 7\% over the collision-free range (block range 5--10\%; figure~\ref{fig:wm}a), and the field-aligned flux lies 4--6\% above the model (block range $+1$ to $+9\%$; figure~\ref{fig:flux}). Only in the last moments, $m\ge27$, where the hypercollisions dominate, does the flux fall below the model, by up to 13\%, and $W$ by 14\% at $m=32$; a sink that grows with the age of the free energy acts in this direction.

The free energy phase mixes at the wavenumbers at which it was injected, including the spread that the tilt of the field lines gives them. With a single $\kappa=k_1$ the model gives $W$ 15--16\% too high over the collision-free range. With the field-aligned distribution of $g_0$ itself, whose mean is $0.61\,k_1$ because free energy at small $\kappa$ leaves $m=0$ slowly, the model departs from $W$ by up to 11\%, and by up to 25\% in the run at $M=64$, against 6\% there with the injected $P(\kappa)$.

The split of the $k_z$-only flux by the sign of $k_z$ reports a backward flux where the field-aligned flux has none. At $\beta_i=1$ its unmixing part is $-0.11$ to $-0.12$ times its phase-mixing part over the collision-free range (figure~\ref{fig:flux}), and at $\beta_i=0.3$ it grows from $-0.12$ to $-0.13$ times the phase-mixing part at $64^3$ to $-0.27$ to $-0.37$ at $256^3$. The split assigns to the backward direction the forward flux of modes whose $k_z$ and field-aligned wavenumber have opposite signs (\ref{lag:shear}).

The same holds in every other run (figure~\ref{fig:noecho}). We quote the ratio, taken at the snapshot times, for $8\le m\le M/2$. A return flux would lower it most at large $m$, while at small $m$ the sink alone lowers the ratio of the uniform-sink model, by up to 8\% at $m=2$ and $\beta_i=0.05$; the range extends to $M/2$ because the model includes the hypercollisions. In every run, including those at $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$ and those of the $m_*$ scan (section~\ref{sim:mstar}), the ratio is 0.95--1.01, against 0.96--1.00 for the uniform-sink model, and in no run does it fall as $m$ grows. With $\Pi$ and $W$ averaged over the whole window it is 0.93--1.02 in the runs of table~\ref{tab:runs} and 0.85--1.00 in those of the $m_*$ scan. The 0.85, at $m=35$ in the run at $\beta_i=0.05$ and $\chi=0.96$, comes from the estimator. $W(35)$ is intermittent, 0.49 to 1.06 times its window mean at the eight snapshot times, so the window means of $\Pi$ and $W$ do not match the snapshots that give $\kappa_1(m)$; taken at those times the ratio is 0.97. In the runs of the $m_*$ scan, whose collision-free range reaches much older free energy, the flux falls below the model with a uniform sink as $m$ grows, to as much as 37\% below it at $m=256$ at $\chi=0.40$, as a sink that grows with the age of the free energy makes it.

\label{sim:mf1}\citet{Meyrand2019} forced the compressive fluctuations through $g_1$, the parallel flow, on modes with $|k_z|\le2k_1$. To see what this changes, we repeated the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=64$ with $g_1$ forced in place of $g_0$, on the same band of $k_\perp$ but on $|k_z|=k_1$ and $2k_1$, at the same rate. The Alfv\'enic turbulence is the same, with $\chi=0.95$ and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.153$, and so is the perpendicular sink, $1/T=0.427$ against 0.421. The injected pattern has $\kappa_1=1.63\,k_1$ along the field lines, against $1.12\,k_1$. The spectrum is steeper at small $m$, with a local slope of $-0.66$ over $2\le m\le8$ against $-0.54$, and the same at larger $m$, $-0.71$ over $8\le m\le32$ against $-0.72$; it is nowhere close to $m^{-1}$. The model with the traced $P(\kappa)$, injected at $m=1$, reproduces $W(m)$ to within 3\% over the collision-free range $2\le m\le21$ (block range 4--6\%), and the field-aligned flux lies 3--4\% above it (block range $+1$ to $+7\%$), with no deficit at large $m$. Forcing $g_1$ on two parallel wavenumbers therefore adds no return flux. At $m=16$ the field-aligned flux still carries 75\% of the injected free energy.

\subsection{The field-aligned wavenumber}\label{sim:kpar}

The free energy keeps the field-aligned wavenumbers at which it was injected, at every resolution from $64^3$ to $256^3$. The injected pattern has $k_z=k_1$ but $\kappa_1=1.10$--$1.14\,k_1$ along the field lines in every run with $g_0$ forced, because the lines are tilted. In the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$, where $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$, the rms of the tilt $\boldsymbol b_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol k_\perp$ over the pattern is $0.89\,k_1$, and the rms field-aligned wavenumber is $1.34\,k_1$. The Alfv\'enic turbulence, and with it the tilt, is the same in all these runs, so the differences in $\kappa_1$ come from sampling different snapshots.

Figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(a) shows the spectrum of the free energy, summed over $m$, along the field lines and along $z$ in the runs at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $M=64$ on $64^3$ to $256^3$ grid points. We quote every share beyond one threshold, $4\,k_1$, both as a share of the free energy and as a share of $\sum_\kappa\kappa W(\kappa)$. By (\ref{eq:pmflux}), at each $m$ the second is the share of the phase-mixing flux, which free energy at large $\kappa$ carries in larger proportion because it phase mixes faster (figure~\ref{fig:kpar}d). Along the field lines the bulk of the spectrum does not change with the resolution. Its mean field-aligned wavenumber is 1.00--$1.03\,k_1$ at every resolution, and 0.2--0.4\% of the free energy lies beyond $4\,k_1$, against 0.5--0.7\% for the injected pattern. The free energy is weighted towards smaller $\kappa$ than the injection, because free energy at larger $\kappa$ reaches the collisional range sooner. Measured along $z$, the same free energy has a spectrum that extends further with each doubling of the resolution, as a parallel cascade would, and a cascade along the field would extend the field-aligned spectrum in the same way.

Along the field lines a tail beyond $4\,k_1$ grows with the resolution as well, but it stays small. Averaged over the snapshots, it holds 0.23\% of the free energy at $64^3$ and 0.40\% at $256^3$, where one snapshot reaches 0.79\%. Its share of the phase-mixing flux grows from 1.1\% to 2.8\%, so at $256^3$ 97\% of the phase-mixing flux along the lines is at $\kappa\le4\,k_1$. In the frozen-$\kappa$ model, in which each $\kappa$ of the injected pattern phase mixes at its own $\kappa$ against the uniform sink and the hypercollisions of the run, this share is 1.0\% at $64^3$ and 1.2\% at $256^3$. The excess of the measured share over the model thus grows from 0.1 to 1.6 percentage points, of which 1.0 lies beyond $8\,k_1$. Over the same resolutions the share of the phase-mixing flux beyond $4\,k_1$ grows from 11\% to 48\% along $z$, and the $\kappa$-weighted share of the velocity $\boldsymbol u_\perp$ along the lines from 6\% to 24\%. We have not traced the cause of the tail; a slow relabelling of the field lines by the hyperdissipation (section~\ref{tc:limits}) is one candidate.

The Alfv\'enic fields do cascade along the field lines, and $g_0$ does not follow them. Figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(b) compares the spectra of the whole velocity $\boldsymbol u_\perp$ and of $g_0$ along the same lines. Beyond $4\,k_1$ the velocity holds 1.5\%, 2.7\%, 3.2\% and 5.6\% of its energy at $64^3$, $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$, a share that grows as the inertial range lengthens, while $g_0$ holds 0.11\%, 0.14\%, 0.18\% and 0.23\% of its variance there, 14 to 25 times less. Its tail grows with the resolution as well, but from an amplitude more than ten times smaller. \citet{Meyrand2019} found that the spectra of the density and field-strength fluctuations along the perturbed lines track that of the velocity; in our runs the spectrum of $g_0$ does not.

Filtered into bands of $k_\perp$, $g_0$ seems to gain field-aligned structure at small perpendicular scales: its $\kappa_1$ grows from about $1.0\,k_1$ in the band $k_\perp\le2k_1$ to 2.0--$2.1\,k_1$ in the band $8\le k_\perp/k_1\le16$ at $64^3$ and $128^3$ (figure~\ref{fig:kpar}c). Filtering mixes neighbouring field lines, which separate along the field, so a filtered field shows field-aligned structure that the whole field does not have. A copy of $g_0$ that is frozen to the field lines by construction (appendix~\ref{app:frozencopy}) shows the same growth, to within 4\% band by band at both resolutions, whereas $\kappa_1$ of $\Phi$ grows to $3.1\,k_1$. The band-resolved growth therefore comes from the filtering, and the spectra of the whole fields, figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(a,b), are the test of a parallel cascade.

\subsection{The break}\label{sim:break}

To measure the break, $M$ must be large enough that $m_*$ lies below $M/2$. In the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$ it lies far beyond $m=M$, at $(c_T^2/2)\beta_i/\chi^2=127$ with the $T$ of that run. Free energy injected at $m=0$ reaches $m=16$ after about $0.8\,\tau_A$ and $m=32$ after $1.1\,\tau_A$, against $T=2.3\,\tau_A$, and the hypercollisions remove 59\% of the injected free energy (block range 56--61\%). The exponential factor in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) therefore reduces $W$ by only 29\% at $m=16$ and 39\% at $m=32$, and at $\beta_i=1$ the test of the shape shows that there is no deficit, not that the sink produces a break.

Lowering $\beta_i$ at fixed Alfv\'enic forcing brings the break down, as test (iii) expects. We ran $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1, each at $M=32$ and $M=64$, and $\beta_i=1$ also at $M=64$; the branch is $\Lambda^+=6.2206$ and $19.198$. At $\beta_i=0.1$ the break with the measured $T$, $m_*\approx6$--10, lies well below $M/2$, and $W(m)$ falls below $m^{-1/2}$ from small $m$ (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a).

We fit $W(m)\sqrt{2m}\propto\exp(-\sqrt{2m}/\ell_*)$, the form (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) with the decay length $\ell_*$ of section~\ref{tc:scales} left free, over the collision-free range $2\le m\le m_{\rm c}$. The form is the large-$m$ limit of the model, and over a short range from $m=2$ it biases the fitted $\ell_*$ upwards, so we fit the model spectrum in the same way and compare the two decay lengths. The fit range spans 0.7 decay lengths at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=32$, 1.5 at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=64$, and 1.5 to 3.4 in the runs of the $m_*$ scan (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). In these runs the decay length of the data is 0.92--1.10 times that of the model (block range 0.87--1.20). At $\beta_i=0.3$ and 1 and $\chi\approx1$ it spans only 0.3 and 0.08 decay lengths at $M=32$, and at most 0.6 at $M=64$ at any resolution, so $\ell_*$ is not determined there; the fitted value is 1.2 to 1.7 times that of the model.

At $64^3$ and $\chi\approx0.95$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\beta_i=0.05$ to 6.6 and 24.2 at $\beta_i=0.1$ and 0.3 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}a). A weighted power-law fit gives $m_*\propto\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$. The error is only the scatter of the fits to the four blocks; the three runs also differ in $M$ (128, 64 and 192) and in their windows. The model values follow $\beta_i^{1.31\pm0.04}$, and the ratio of the model value to the measured $m_*$ is 0.88, 1.05 and 1.30 at $\beta_i=0.05$, 0.1 and 0.3 (block ranges 0.84--0.94, 0.95--1.14 and 1.17--1.39). The model lies above at $\beta_i=0.3$, where $c_T$ is larger, 13.6 against 10.9 and 11.0, because the moments $2\le m\le16$ that set $T$ hold younger free energy at larger $\beta_i$ (section~\ref{sim:beta}). The fitted breaks and the model values therefore give exponents of 1.09 and 1.31. The resolution changes $m_*$ far more than either. In the runs at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $M=64$, fitted over $2\le m\le32$, it moves from 26 at $64^3$ to 86 at $256^3$, by more than a factor of three, although that range spans less than one decay length at either resolution, so both values extrapolate the fit (section~\ref{sim:resolution}). The value 24.2 at $\beta_i=0.3$ above comes instead from the run at $M=192$ and the fit over its own window. Fitted over $2\le m\le32$, that run gives 30.1, and its $m_*$ falls as the range widens, so the closeness of 24.2 and 26 is a coincidence. We have no runs above $64^3$ at $\beta_i=0.05$ and 0.1, so the exponent is a $64^3$ value.

At $\beta_i=0.05$ and $64^3$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\chi=0.96$ to 7.0, 14.9 and 60.5 at $\chi=0.75$, 0.56 and 0.40 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}b). Computed from the unrounded $\chi$, the local exponent $-\dd\ln m_*/\dd\ln\chi$ between neighbouring runs is $2.50\pm0.29$, $2.69\pm0.16$ and $4.09\pm0.13$, so in these runs the break grows faster than $\chi^{-2}$ as $\chi$ falls. It does so through $T$. In these runs $T$ stays at 0.71--0.74 times $E_A/\eps_A$, while $E_A/\eps_A$ grows from $14.7\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.96$ to $35.4\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.40$, so $c_T$ rises from 10.9 to 25.9. The factor of 2.4 by which $E_A/(\eps_A\tau_{\rm nl})$ grows is the factor by which $\chi$ falls, as in weak turbulence, where the cascade time at the outer scale is $\tau_{\rm nl}/\chi$. The runs differ in $\eta$ as well as in $\chi$, and at fixed $\chi$, $T$ changes by 18--35\% with each doubling of the resolution (section~\ref{sim:resolution}), so these runs do not separate the dependence of $m_*$ on $\chi$ from its dependence on the dissipation. The model value follows the measured $m_*$ to within 12\% for $\chi\ge0.56$, but at $\chi=0.40$ it is 167, 2.8 times the measured value; section~\ref{sim:beta} traces this to the age dependence of the sink.

\subsection{The age of the free energy}\label{sim:beta}

At $64^3$ the perpendicular sink is not uniform in $m$ but depends on the age of the free energy, in the same way at every $\beta_i$. In the runs at $64^3$ and $\chi\approx1$, $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ rises with $m$, from 0.33--0.43 at $m\le1$ to 0.46, 0.53 and 0.62 at $m=16$ for $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1, and the rise is steeper at lower $\beta_i$, where free energy reaches a given $m$ later. Plotted against the time $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ since injection, the sinks of these runs fall on one curve (figure~\ref{fig:beta}b), with an rms scatter of 3.2\% (block range 3.8--6.9\%) about a joint fit $D_\perp/W=g_1-(g_1-g_0)\,e^{-\vartheta/\vartheta_s}$, with $g_1=0.69$, $g_0=0.29$ and $\vartheta_s=1.43\,\tau_A$, against 12.4\% (block range 10--18\%) about the same form in $m$. This saturating form is that of the age-dependent sink below. The measured $D_\perp/W$ is about 0.4 at the smallest ages, and beyond $2.5\,\tau_A$, where almost all the points come from the run at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=64$, it levels off at 0.63, flat to 1\%.
This favours the age over the streaming speed as the cause. The parallel velocities that dominate moment $m$ stream at about $s\sqrt{2m}$, which at a fixed age scales as $s^2$ and differs by a factor of ten between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1, yet the sinks fall on one curve. The attribution to age rests on this collapse alone, since the run with $s=0$ that would measure the passive-scalar sink directly was not made (section~\ref{tc:channels}).

A single rate $1/T$, the median over $2\le m\le16$, therefore removes too much free energy at small $m$. At $\beta_i=1$ the measured $W(m)$ is within 7\% of the model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ over the collision-free range, at both $M$ (block range up to 10\% and 14\%). At lower $\beta_i$ it lies above the model at every $m$ of that range, by up to 20\% and 39\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1 at $M=32$ and by up to 20\% and 42\% at $M=64$ (block range up to 29\% and 52\%), and the field-aligned flux lies above the model as well (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a). The median rate $1/T$ rises from 0.44 to 0.59 between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1 for the same reason: at low $\beta_i$ the moments $2\le m\le16$ hold older free energy.

To test this directly we let each injection evolve by the linear hierarchy at its field-aligned wavenumber, with the run's hypercollisions, and remove it at a rate $\gamma(\vartheta)$ that depends only on its age, the same at every $m$. With $S(\vartheta)=\exp(-\int_0^\vartheta\gamma)$ the steady state is
\begin{equation}
  W(m)=\eps\int P(\kappa)\int_0^\infty E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)\,S(\vartheta)\,\dd\vartheta\,\dd\kappa ,
\end{equation}
with $E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)$ the free energy at moment $m$ of a unit injection at age $\vartheta$, and $D_\perp(m)$ is the same integral with $\gamma S$ in place of $S$; for $\gamma=1/T$ this is the uniform-sink model. Fitting $\gamma(\vartheta)=\gamma_0+(\gamma_1-\gamma_0)(1-e^{-\vartheta/\vartheta_c})$ to $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.1$ ($M=64$) gives $\gamma_0=0.03$, $\gamma_1=0.67$ and $\vartheta_c=0.76\,\tau_A$, about five outer eddy-turnover times. Fresh free energy is hardly removed, and the sink switches on in the time the cascade takes to carry it to the dissipation range of these runs. That time depends on the resolution, so $\gamma(\vartheta)$ is a property of the dissipation range of our $64^3$ runs, not of the plasma. $D_\perp/W$ exceeds $\gamma_0$ even at small $m$ because each moment also holds older free energy at small $\kappa$. With this $\gamma(\vartheta)$ and no parameter fitted to them, the model gives $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 1 to within 5\% for $m\ge2$ and $m\ge4$ (block range up to 7\% and 12\%), across a factor of ten in $\beta_i$. It gives $W(m)$ to within 7\% for $m\ge8$ in all three runs (block range up to 15\%), and lies above the measured $W$ at the smallest $m$, by 7\% at $m=1$ in the run used for the fit and by 8--14\% for $m\le3$ in the other two (figure~\ref{fig:age}).

The age dependence also explains why the model overestimates $m_*$ at $\chi=0.40$ (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). There $D_\perp/W$ varies by a factor of 7.1 over the collision-free range, against 1.2, 1.5 and 2.0 at $\chi=0.96$, 0.75 and 0.56, so the median over $2\le m\le16$ does not represent the sink over the range $m\le171$ on which the break is measured. With the rate averaged over each run's fit range instead, uniformly in $\sqrt{2m}$ as the fit weights it, $s\kappa_1T$ agrees with the fitted $\ell_*$ to within 9\% at $\chi=0.40$ and to within 5\% in every other run of table~\ref{tab:mstar}. The decay beyond the break is therefore still set by phase mixing at $\kappa_1$ against the perpendicular sink, as in (\ref{eq:Wage}).

\subsection{Dependence on resolution}\label{sim:resolution}

The results of sections~\ref{sim:flux} and~\ref{sim:kpar} hold at every resolution, but $T$ does not converge. Doubling $M$ from 32 to 64 leaves $W(m)$ unchanged to within about 5\% for $1\le m\le10$ and changes $1/T$ by at most 6\% at every $\beta_i$.

We repeated the runs at $M=64$ on $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$ grid points at $\beta_i=0.3$ and on $128^3$ at $\beta_i=1$, with the hyperdissipation normalised to the grid, so that the inertial range lengthens with $N$. The compensated spectrum $k_\perp^{5/3}E_A$ stays within 20\% of its value at $k_\perp=4\,k_1$ over $2\le k_\perp/k_1\le5$ at $64^3$, and up to $k_\perp/k_1=8$, 12 and 32 at $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$. The outer scale is unchanged, with $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.150$--0.158 and $\chi=0.92$--0.97 in all six runs. $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ falls at every $m$, by 18--35\% with each doubling of $N$ (figure~\ref{fig:resolution-n}), and $c_T$ rises from 13.2 at $64^3$ to 16.4, 18.4 and 24.6 at $\beta_i=0.3$, and from 15.4 to 22.6 at $\beta_i=1$. At $\beta_i=0.3$, $T$ grows by 5--6 outer eddy-turnover times with each doubling, with no sign of levelling off up to $256^3$. The break moves out with $T$, from $m_*\approx26$ at $64^3$ to 86 at $256^3$, both from the fit over $2\le m\le32$ that extrapolates beyond its range (section~\ref{sim:mstar}), and $W(m)$ at $m\le16$ rises by up to 43\%.

With the $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ measured at each resolution, the model follows these changes. At $128^3$ and $256^3$ it describes $W(m)$ over the collision-free range to within $-9\%$ and $+13\%$ (block range up to 17\%), and the field-aligned flux lies 4--15\% above it (block range $+3$ to $+20\%$), with no deficit that grows with $m$ (section~\ref{sim:flux}). At these $\beta_i$ the collision-free range spans less than one decay length, so the decay rate is not determined at any resolution (section~\ref{sim:break}). The sink varies more with $m$ at higher resolution: at $\beta_i=0.3$ the ratio of its largest to its smallest value over $2\le m\le m_{\rm c}$ grows from 1.44 at $64^3$ to 1.72 at $256^3$. One $T$ therefore describes the sink less well as the resolution increases.
