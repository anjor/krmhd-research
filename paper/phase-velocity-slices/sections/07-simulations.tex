\subsection{Set-up}\label{sim:setup}

We integrate (\ref{eq:rmhd}) in Elsasser form with one compressive branch (\ref{mod:hierarchy}), the branch $\Lambda^+$ of (\ref{eq:Lambdapm}) with $\tau=Z=1$, truncated at $m=M$ with the zero closure, using GANDALF \citep{Kanekar2025} in double precision. The box is periodic with side $L=1$, so $k_1=2\pi$; $v_A=1$ and $\tau_A=L/v_A=1$. Appendix~\ref{app:runs} gives the time stepping, the averaging windows and the checks of every run.

The Alfv\'enic fluctuations are forced through $\Phi$ alone, so that $z^+$ and $z^-$ receive the same increment, which keeps the forcing out of the induction equation (section~\ref{sec:frozen}). The forcing is white in time and acts on the Fourier modes with $1\le k_\perp/k_1\le2$ and $|k_z|=k_1$, with injection rate $\eps_A=0.3$ except in the runs at lower $\chi$ (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). Excluding $k_z=0$ from the band avoids the two-dimensional condensate that a band including it drove at this resolution. The density moment $g_0$ is forced on the same band, white in time, with its amplitude rescaled at every step so that the expected injection rate is $\eps=0.3$; the band excludes $k_\perp=0$, where injected free energy would phase mix with no perpendicular cascade. Every injection therefore has the single Eulerian parallel wavenumber $k_z=\pm k_1$, and its field-aligned wavenumbers differ from $k_1$ only through the tilt of the field lines.

GANDALF applies the hyper-resistivity $\eta\,(k_\perp^2/k_{\perp,\max}^2)^2$ alike to $z^\pm$ and to every $g_m$, with $\eta=300$ except at lower $\chi$; there is no parallel dissipation. Hypercollisions damp $g_m$ at the rate $\nu\,(m/M)^6$ for $m\ge2$ and so remove its free energy at the rate $2\nu\,(m/M)^6$. The attenuation they impose on free energy streaming up the ladder depends on $\nu\sqrt M/s$, so we set $\nu=30\,\beta_i^{1/2}(32/M)^{1/2}$, which keeps it fixed as $\beta_i$ and $M$ change. Free energy moving up the ladder at $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$ passes the moments $m\le M/2$ quickly enough that the hypercollisions remove only 3\% of the injected free energy there at $\beta_i=1$ and $M=32$; above $m=M/2$ they remove most of what arrives.

We characterise the outer scale by the part of $\Phi$ in the shells $k_\perp/k_1\le2.5$: its energy-weighted mean $k_\perp$ and $|k_z|$, $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}$ and $k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}$, and its rms $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity $u_{\mathrm{out}}$ give the nonlinearity parameter $\chi=k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}}/k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}$ and the outer eddy time $\tau_{\rm nl}=1/(k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}})$. In the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$, $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}=1.55\,k_1$, $k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}=1.11\,k_1$ and $u_{\mathrm{out}}=0.71$, so $\chi=0.99$ (0.85 with the rms field-aligned wavenumber of the same field in place of $|k_z|$) and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.144$. The Alfv\'enic sector does not depend on $\beta_i$, and its outer scale hardly depends on the resolution (section~\ref{sim:resolution}), so every run of table~\ref{tab:runs} has $\chi$ between 0.92 and 0.99. At $64^3$ the inertial range is short: in the run at $\beta_i=1$, $E_A(k_\perp)$ follows $k_\perp^{-5/3}$ only over $2\le k_\perp/k_1\le8$ and is steeper than $k_\perp^{-4}$ from $k_\perp/k_1=9$. Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists the runs at $\chi\approx1$.

The energy change of every step is split into the parts due to the ideal dynamics, the forcing, the hyper-resistivity and the hypercollisions. This gives $D_\perp(m)$ and $D_\nu(m)$ in (\ref{eq:budget}) exactly for every moment. Every 20 steps we record $W(m)$, its perpendicular spectrum, the field-aligned flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) and the $k_z$-only flux, the split of the $k_z$-only flux into phase-mixing and unmixing parts by the sign of $k_z$ \citep{Schekochihin2016}, and $E_A=\frac12\langle|\nabla_\perp\Phi|^2+|\nabla_\perp\Psi|^2\rangle$ and its spectrum. Field-aligned wavenumbers are measured along traced field lines, as is done for the Alfv\'enic fields \citep{ChoVishniac2000,Meyrand2019}. On snapshots spaced by $5\,\tau_A$ within the averaging window ($10\,\tau_A$ at $96^3$ and above, and 10--$50\,\tau_A$ in the runs of the $m_*$ scan) we trace 4096 lines of $\bhat=\zhat+\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$ over four box lengths in $z$, sample $g_m$, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ along them, and compute the spectra along the lines; $\kappa_1$ denotes the first moment of $|\kappa|$. The distribution $P(\kappa)$ of the injected free energy is measured in the same way, on fresh realisations of the density forcing sampled along the same lines (appendix~\ref{app:methods}).

\subsection{No return flux}\label{sim:flux}

In every run the free energy phase mixes at its injected field-aligned wavenumber, with no return flux. We show this first for the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$. Its Hermite spectrum is marginally resolved, with 1.1\% of the free energy at $m>3M/4$, and its break lies far beyond $m=M$ (section~\ref{sim:break}), so we treat it as a pilot that tests predictions (i) and (ii) but not the break.

The dotted line is the formula (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) with $\kappa=k_z$ and no collisions. (b) The rates at which the perpendicular dissipation (circles) and the hypercollisions (squares) remove free energy from moment $m$, divided by $W(m)$ and multiplied by $T$.   \label{fig:wm}

The perpendicular dissipation removes free energy from each moment nearly in proportion to its free energy, as (\ref{eq:uniformsink}) assumes. $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ lies between 0.37 and 0.47 for $0\le m\le16$, with a systematic rise of 20\% over $1\le m\le16$ that section~\ref{sim:beta} traces to the age of the free energy (figure~\ref{fig:wm}b). We take $1/T=0.437$, its median over $2\le m\le16$, so $T=2.29=15.9\,\tau_{\rm nl}$. The budget (\ref{eq:budget}) closes to $4\times10^{-4}\,\eps$ in every moment with the field-aligned flux, and only to 8\% with the $k_z$-only flux, as prediction (i) states.

  \label{fig:flux}

With this $T$ and the $P(\kappa)$ of the injected pattern, the model has no free parameter. It reproduces $W(m)$ to within 8\% for $1\le m\le30$ (figure~\ref{fig:wm}a) and the field-aligned flux to within 3--7\% for $m\le24$ (figure~\ref{fig:flux}); for $1\le m\le16$ the measured flux lies 4--7\% above the model. The moments $g_m$ have $\kappa_1$ between $1.07\,k_1$ and $1.19\,k_1$ for $1\le m\le21$, with no trend in $m$, against $1.14\,k_1$ for the injected pattern, and the flux corresponds to phase mixing at $\Pi_{m+1/2}/[s\sqrt{2(m+1)}\,W(m)]=1.02$--$1.10\,k_1$ for $3\le m\le23$, so there is no room for a return flux of more than a few per cent. Only in the last moments, $m\ge27$, where the hypercollisions dominate, does the flux fall below the model, by up to 12\%, and $W$ by 14\% at $m=32$; a sink that grows with the age of the free energy acts in this direction. The tilt of the field lines matters: with a single $\kappa=k_1$ the model gives $W$ 15--16\% too high for $2\le m\le16$, and with a Gaussian distribution of the tilt in place of the traced one the measured $W(m)$ ranges from 0.72 to 1.22 times the model. With the field-aligned distribution of $g_0$ itself, whose mean is $0.61\,k_1$ because free energy at small $\kappa$ leaves $m=0$ slowly, it gives $W$ too low by a factor of up to 1.9 at $m=24$: the free energy phase mixes at the wavenumbers at which it was injected.

A split of the $k_z$-only flux into phase-mixing and unmixing parts by the sign of $k_z$ \citep{Schekochihin2016} reports a backward flux where the field-aligned flux has none: at $\beta_i=1$ its unmixing part is $-0.11$ to $-0.15$ times its phase-mixing part for $1\le m\le16$ (figure~\ref{fig:flux}), and at $\beta_i=0.3$ it grows from about $-0.13$ times the phase-mixing part at $64^3$ to $-0.27$ to $-0.35$ at $256^3$, while the field-aligned flux shows no deficit at any resolution. The split assigns to the backward direction the forward flux of modes whose $k_z$ and field-aligned wavenumber have opposite signs (\ref{lag:shear}), and such modes become more common as the tilt of the field lines grows with the resolution. In every run of table~\ref{tab:runs} the per-$m$ budget closes to better than $5\times10^{-4}\,\eps$ with the field-aligned flux, and only to 2--8\% with the $k_z$-only flux.

In the other runs of table~\ref{tab:runs} neither $W(m)$ nor the field-aligned flux falls increasingly below the model at large $m$ (sections~\ref{sim:beta} and~\ref{sim:resolution}). In the runs of the $m_*$ scan (section~\ref{sim:mstar}), whose collision-free range reaches much older free energy, the flux falls relative to the model with a uniform sink as $m$ grows, to as much as 37\% below it at $m=256$ at $\chi=0.40$, as a sink that grows with the age of the free energy makes it. The flux there is still the one that $W(m)$ carries at its measured field-aligned wavenumber: it lies between 0.85 and 1.00 times $s\sqrt{2(m+1)}\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$ for $8\le m\le M/2$ in every run of the scan, without the deficit growing with $m$ that a return flux would cause. No run shows a return flux.

\label{sim:mf1}\citet{Meyrand2019} forced the compressive fluctuations through $g_1$, the parallel flow, on modes with $|k_z|\le2k_1$. To see what this changes, we repeated the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=64$ with $g_1$ forced in place of $g_0$, on the same band of $k_\perp$ but on $|k_z|=k_1$ and $2k_1$, at the same rate. The Alfv\'enic turbulence is the same, with $\chi=0.95$ and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.153$, and so is the perpendicular sink, $1/T=0.427$ against 0.421. The injected pattern has $\kappa_1=1.63\,k_1$ along the field lines, against $1.12\,k_1$. The spectrum is steeper at small $m$, with a local slope of $-0.66$ over $2\le m\le8$ against $-0.54$, and the same at larger $m$, $-0.71$ over $8\le m\le32$ against $-0.72$; it is nowhere close to $m^{-1}$. The model with the traced $P(\kappa)$, injected at $m=1$, reproduces $W(m)$ to within 3.5\% for $0\le m\le32$, and the field-aligned flux lies 2--4\% above it for $m\ge1$, with no deficit at large $m$, so forcing $g_1$ on two parallel wavenumbers adds no return flux. At $m=16$ the field-aligned flux carries 75\% of the injected free energy, against 66\% with $g_0$ forced, and the hypercollisions remove 61\% of it, against 50\%, because the larger injected $\kappa$ carries the free energy to the collisional range sooner; the model gives 60\% and 48\%.

\subsection{The field-aligned wavenumber}\label{sim:kpar}

The free energy keeps the field-aligned wavenumbers at which it was injected, at every resolution. The injected pattern has $k_z=k_1$ but $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$ along the field lines at $\beta_i=1$ and $M=32$ (1.10--$1.14\,k_1$ in the runs at $\beta_i=0.3$ below), because the lines are tilted: the rms of the tilt $\boldsymbol b_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol k_\perp$ over the pattern is $0.89\,k_1$, and the rms field-aligned wavenumber is $1.34\,k_1$.

Figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(a) shows the spectrum of the free energy, summed over $m$, along the field lines and along $z$ in the runs at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $M=64$ on $64^3$ to $256^3$ grid points. Along the field lines it does not change with the resolution. At every resolution 3.7--4.4\% of the free energy lies beyond $2\,k_1$, against 6.9--8.2\% for the injected pattern, and its mean field-aligned wavenumber is 1.00--$1.03\,k_1$; the free energy is weighted towards smaller $\kappa$ than the injection, because free energy at larger $\kappa$ reaches the collisional range sooner. Measured along $z$, the same free energy shows a spectrum that extends further with each doubling of the resolution, as a parallel cascade would: the share beyond $8\,k_1$ grows from 0.2\% at $64^3$ to 0.9\%, 1.8\% and 4.3\% at $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$. A cascade along the field would extend the field-aligned spectrum in the same way. What grows along the field lines is only a tail beyond about $4\,k_1$, which holds 0.2\% of the free energy at $64^3$ and 0.4\% at $256^3$.

The Alfv\'enic fields do cascade along the field lines, and $g_0$ does not follow them. Figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(b) compares the spectra of the whole velocity $\boldsymbol u_\perp$ and of $g_0$ along the same lines. Beyond $4\,k_1$ the velocity holds 1.5\%, 2.7\% and 3.2\% of its energy at $64^3$, $96^3$ and $128^3$, a share that grows as the inertial range lengthens, while $g_0$ holds 0.11\%, 0.14\% and 0.18\% of its variance there, 14 to 19 times less; its tail grows with the resolution as well, but from an amplitude more than ten times smaller. A passive field that inherited the parallel cascade of the Alfv\'enic fields would have a spectrum along the lines like that of the velocity.

Filtered into bands of $k_\perp$, $g_0$ seems to gain field-aligned structure at small perpendicular scales: its $\kappa_1$ grows from about $1.0\,k_1$ in the band $k_\perp\le2k_1$ to 2.0--$2.1\,k_1$ in the band $8\le k_\perp/k_1\le16$ at $64^3$ and $128^3$ (figure~\ref{fig:kpar}c). This growth does not come from structure of $g_0$ along the field. Filtering mixes neighbouring field lines, which separate along the field, so a filtered field shows field-aligned structure that the whole field does not have. To measure this effect we built from each snapshot a copy of $g_0$ that is frozen to the field lines by construction: it equals $g_0$ on the planes $z=0$, $L/4$, $L/2$ and $3L/4$ and is carried along the field lines between them, so that along every line it varies only on scales longer than $L/4$ (appendix~\ref{app:frozencopy}). Band by band, the field-aligned wavenumber of the frozen copy is that of $g_0$ to within 4\% at both resolutions, whereas that of $\Phi$ grows to $3.1\,k_1$. The growth of the band-resolved wavenumber of $g_0$ therefore comes from the filtering. Over the whole field, $g_0$ has 0.11--0.18\% of its variance beyond $4\,k_1$, against 0.05--0.07\% for its frozen copy. The band-resolved wavenumber of a passive field therefore measures the filtering as much as the field, and the spectra of the whole fields, figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(a,b), are the test of a parallel cascade.

\subsection{The break}\label{sim:break}

At $\beta_i=1$ the break lies far beyond the highest moment we keep, $m=M=32$: $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$ is 103 for $\kappa=k_1$ and 185 for the rms field-aligned wavenumber, and $c_T^2\beta_i/(2\chi^2)=127$ with $c_T=T/\tau_{\rm nl}$. Free energy injected at $m=0$ reaches $m=16$ after about $0.8\,\tau_A$ and $m=32$ after $1.1\,\tau_A$, against $T=2.3\,\tau_A$, and the hypercollisions remove 57--61\% of the injected free energy (the range over the four blocks). The exponential factor in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) therefore reduces $W$ by only 29\% at $m=16$ and 39\% at $m=32$, and at $\beta_i=1$ the test of the shape confirms that there is no deficit, not that the sink produces a break.

(b) The perpendicular sink $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ against the time $\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ since injection of the free energy at $m$, with $\kappa_1$ the mean field-aligned wavenumber of the injected pattern, for $1\le m\le M/2$; the runs at $M=64$ (solid) reach older free energy than those at $M=32$ (dotted); the thin line is the joint fit $0.50+0.12\ln[\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)]$.}

Lowering $\beta_i$ at fixed Alfv\'enic forcing moves the break into the resolved range, as prediction (iii) states. We ran $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1, each at $M=32$ and $M=64$, and $\beta_i=1$ also at $M=64$; the branch is $\Lambda^+=6.2206$ and $19.198$. With $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ from $\beta_i=1$, (\ref{eq:mstar}) gives $m_*\approx31$--56 at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $m_*\approx10$--19 at $\beta_i=0.1$. At $\beta_i=0.1$ the break with the measured $T$, $m_*\approx6$--10, lies well inside the resolved range, and $W(m)$ falls below $m^{-1/2}$ from small $m$ (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a). The continuum formula (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) with $\kappa=k_1$ and the measured $T$ follows the measured spectrum to about 10\% up to $m\approx16$, where the hypercollisions take over at $M=32$; at $M=64$ the spectrum continues to fall steeply between $m\approx15$ and 40. At $\beta_i=0.3$ the spectrum bends at correspondingly larger $m$.

The decay beyond the break has the predicted rate. We fit $W(m)\sqrt{2m}\propto\exp(-\sqrt{2m}/\ell_*)$ over $2\le m\le M/2$, below the collisional range. The fitted $\ell_*$ is 16.8, 8.3 and 3.8 at $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1 ($M=32$), and 15.7, 7.2 and 3.6 at $M=64$, against $s\kappa_1T=16.4$, 8.0, 3.8, 16.8, 7.6 and 3.7 from the measured $T$ and the injected $\kappa_1$: the measured decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is the predicted one to within 7\% in every run. At $\beta_i=1$ the fit range spans less than half a decay length ($\sqrt{2m}\le\sqrt M=5.7$ and 8 against $\ell_*\approx16$), so the break $m_*=\ell_*^2/2\approx140$ there extrapolates the fit well beyond $m=M$; at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=64$ the range spans 1.7 decay lengths and $m_*\approx7$.

To measure $m_*$ against $\beta_i$ and $\chi$ we made the runs of table~\ref{tab:mstar}, with $M$ large enough in each that the break lies below $M/2$. At $\chi\approx0.95$ we ran $\beta_i=0.05$ and 0.3, and we use the run at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=64$ of table~\ref{tab:runs}. At $\beta_i=0.05$ we lowered $\chi$ to 0.75, 0.56 and 0.40 by lowering the Alfv\'enic injection rate $\eps_A$ from 0.3 to 0.1, 0.031 and 0.0035. As $\chi$ decreases the hyper-resistive dissipation of $E_A$ moves to larger scales unless $\eta$ decreases with it, so we lowered $\eta$ in proportion to $\chi$, to 219 and 156, and at $\chi=0.40$ set $\eta=33$ and $\eps_A$ by short runs at $M=2$; the median shell of the hyper-resistive dissipation of $E_A$ is then $k_\perp/k_1=9$--11 in every run. We fit over a range set by each run's own decay, from $m=2$ to $\sqrt{2m}=2+1.5\,\ell_*$; the collision-free range holds 1.7 to 3.5 decay lengths, and at the top of the fit range the hypercollisional rate $2\nu_m$ is at most 13\% of $1/T$. The model value is $(s\kappa_1T)^2/2$, with $1/T$ the median rate over $2\le m\le16$ of each run and $\kappa_1$ from lines traced on its snapshots.

At $\chi\approx0.95$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\beta_i=0.05$ to 6.6 and 24.2 at $\beta_i=0.1$ and 0.3 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}a). A weighted fit gives $m_*\propto\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$, close to the $m_*\propto\beta_i$ of (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}). The model values follow $\beta_i^{1.31\pm0.04}$. They agree with the measured $m_*$ to within 12\% at $\beta_i\le0.1$ and lie 30\% above it at $\beta_i=0.3$, where $T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ is larger, 13.6 against 10.9 and 11.0, because the moments $2\le m\le16$ that set $T$ hold younger free energy at larger $\beta_i$ (section~\ref{sim:beta}).

At $\beta_i=0.05$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\chi=0.96$ to 7.0, 14.9 and 60.5 at $\chi=0.75$, 0.56 and 0.40 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}b). Between neighbouring runs the local exponent $-\dd\ln m_*/\dd\ln\chi$ is 2.5, 2.7 and 4.1. Down to $\chi\approx0.56$ the break therefore grows somewhat faster than the $\chi^{-2}$ of (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}), and below it the growth steepens towards $\chi^{-4}$; no single power law describes the four runs. The model value follows the measured $m_*$ to within 12\% for $\chi\ge0.56$, but at $\chi=0.40$ it is 167, 2.8 times the measured value. Both departures come from the perpendicular sink (section~\ref{sim:beta}).

\subsection{The age of the free energy}\label{sim:beta}

The perpendicular sink is not uniform in $m$ but depends on the age of the free energy, in the same way at every $\beta_i$. In the runs at $64^3$ and $\chi\approx1$, $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ rises with $m$, from 0.33--0.43 at $m\le1$ to 0.46, 0.53 and 0.62 at $m=16$ for $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1, and the rise is steeper at lower $\beta_i$, where free energy reaches a given $m$ later. Plotted against the time $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ since injection, the sinks of these runs fall on one curve (figure~\ref{fig:beta}b), with an rms scatter of 4.5\% about a joint fit $D_\perp/W=0.50+0.12\ln\vartheta$, against 12.6\% when plotted against $m$. $D_\perp/W$ is about 0.4 at the smallest ages and levels off at about 0.63 for ages beyond about $2.5\,\tau_A$. This is what is expected of a passive scalar whose variance reaches the dissipation scale only after the cascade has had time to act (section~\ref{tc:limits}). This favours the age over the streaming of the slices as the cause: at a fixed age the streaming speed of the slices that dominate moment $m$, $s\sqrt{2m}$, scales as $s^2$ and differs by a factor of ten between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1, yet the sinks fall on one curve.

A single rate $1/T$, the median over $2\le m\le16$, therefore removes too much free energy at small $m$. At $\beta_i=1$ the measured $W(m)$ is within 7\% of the model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ for $1\le m\le M/2$, at both $M$. At lower $\beta_i$ it lies above the model at every $m$, by up to 21\% and 39\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1 at $M=32$ and by up to 20\% and 42\% at $M=64$, and the field-aligned flux lies above the model as well (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a). The median rate $1/T$ rises from 0.44 to 0.59 between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1 for the same reason: at low $\beta_i$ the moments $2\le m\le16$ hold older free energy.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{h1_age_model}
  \caption{$W(m)$ divided by the two-channel model with the age-dependent sink $\gamma(\vartheta)$ fitted at $\beta_i=0.1$ (solid) and with the uniform sink $1/T$ of each run (dotted), for the runs at $M=64$.}
  \label{fig:age}
\end{figure}

To test this directly we let each injection evolve by the linear hierarchy at its field-aligned wavenumber, with the run's hypercollisions, and remove it at a rate $\gamma(\vartheta)$ that depends only on its age, the same at every $m$. With $S(\vartheta)=\exp(-\int_0^\vartheta\gamma)$ the steady state is
\begin{equation}
  W(m)=\eps\sum_\kappa P(\kappa)\int_0^\infty E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)\,S(\vartheta)\,\dd\vartheta ,
\end{equation}
with $E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)$ the free energy at moment $m$ of a unit injection at age $\vartheta$, and $D_\perp(m)$ is the same integral with $\gamma S$ in place of $S$; for $\gamma=1/T$ this is the uniform-sink model. Fitting $\gamma(\vartheta)=\gamma_0+(\gamma_1-\gamma_0)(1-e^{-\vartheta/\vartheta_c})$ to $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.1$ ($M=64$) gives $\gamma_0=0.03$, $\gamma_1=0.67$ and $\vartheta_c=0.76\,\tau_A$, about five outer eddy times: fresh free energy is hardly removed, and the sink switches on as the cascade carries it to the dissipation scale; $D_\perp/W$ exceeds $\gamma_0$ even at small $m$ because each moment also holds older free energy at small $\kappa$. With this $\gamma(\vartheta)$ and no parameter fitted to them, the model gives $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 1 to within 5\% for $m\ge2$ and $m\ge4$, across a factor of ten in $\beta_i$. It gives $W(m)$ to within 7\% for $m\ge8$ in all three runs, and lies above the measured $W$ at the smallest $m$, by 7\% at $m=1$ in the run used for the fit and by 8--14\% for $m\le3$ in the other two (figure~\ref{fig:age}).

The same property explains the departures of $m_*$ from $\chi^{-2}$ (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). With the age and the rate both measured in units of $\tau_{\rm nl}$, the sinks of the runs at $\chi\ge0.56$ agree to within about 10\% for $\vartheta\ge12\,\tau_{\rm nl}$: the rate switches on over the first 10--$15\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ and levels off at $0.087$--$0.094/\tau_{\rm nl}$, slightly lower at smaller $\chi$. The range $2\le m\le16$ that defines $T$ covers the same ages in units of $\tau_A$ in every run at $\beta_i=0.05$, but as $\chi$ decreases and $\tau_{\rm nl}$ grows it holds free energy that is younger in units of $\tau_{\rm nl}$, from 8.5--$24\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.96$ to 5--$14\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.56$. The sink there is weaker, so $T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ rises from 10.9 to 13.9 and $m_*$ grows faster than $\chi^{-2}$. At $\chi=0.40$ the moments $2\le m\le16$ hold free energy of age 3--$8\,\tau_{\rm nl}$, and the sink itself changes: it switches on as at larger $\chi$, reaches $0.069/\tau_{\rm nl}$ between $\vartheta=12$ and $16\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ and falls to $0.055/\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $30\,\tau_{\rm nl}$, where the runs at larger $\chi$ are at 0.087--$0.094/\tau_{\rm nl}$. The cascade then removes the free energy more slowly per outer eddy time, as expected once the outer-scale turbulence is weak and its cascade time exceeds $\tau_{\rm nl}$. Over $1\le m\le M/2$ the rate $D_\perp/W$ varies by a factor of 10 at $\chi=0.40$, against 1.4, 1.7 and 2.5 at $\chi=0.96$, 0.75 and 0.56, so the median over $2\le m\le16$ no longer represents the rate over the range $m\le171$ on which the break is measured, and $(s\kappa_1T)^2/2$ overestimates $m_*$. With the rate averaged over each run's fit range instead, uniformly in $\sqrt{2m}$ as the fit weights it, $s\kappa_1T$ agrees with the fitted $\ell_*$ to within 9\% at $\chi=0.40$ and to within 5\% in every other run of table~\ref{tab:mstar}. The decay beyond the break is therefore still set by phase mixing at $\kappa_1$ against the perpendicular sink, as in (\ref{eq:Wage}). What departs from (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}) at small $\chi$ is the assumption that the sink rate is a fixed multiple of $1/\tau_{\rm nl}$.

\subsection{Dependence on resolution}\label{sim:resolution}

Doubling $M$ from 32 to 64 leaves $W(m)$ unchanged to within 5\% for $1\le m\le10$ and changes $1/T$ by at most 6\% at every $\beta_i$. What changes is the share of the free energy that reaches the hypercollisions. When the collisional range moves from $m>16$ to $m>32$ it falls from 59\% to 50\% at $\beta_i=1$, from 43\% to 27\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and from 20\% to 10\% at $\beta_i=0.1$, and the model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ gives the shares to within 0.06 at $M=32$ and 0.02 at $M=64$. The collisional share of the dissipation is therefore not a converged quantity at $M=32$: free energy that the hypercollisions remove at a low cutoff is removed at small perpendicular scales when the cutoff is raised.

The perpendicular resolution changes the sink. We repeated the runs at $M=64$ on $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$ grid points at $\beta_i=0.3$ and on $128^3$ at $\beta_i=1$, with the hyper-resistivity normalised to the grid, so that the inertial range lengthens with $N$: the slope of $E_A$ fitted over the middle 60\% of the resolved shells goes from $-4.1$ at $64^3$ to $-2.9$ at $256^3$. The outer scale is unchanged, with $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.150$--0.158 and $\chi=0.92$--0.97 in all six runs. $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ falls at every $m$, by 18--35\% with each doubling of $N$ (figure~\ref{fig:resolution-n}), and $T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ rises from 13.2 at $64^3$ to 16.4, 18.4 and 24.6 at $\beta_i=0.3$, and from 15.4 to 22.6 at $\beta_i=1$. At $\beta_i=0.3$, $T$ grows by 5--6 outer eddy times with each doubling, with no sign of levelling off up to $256^3$. The break moves out with it, from $m_*\approx26$ at $64^3$ to 86 at $256^3$ (from the fit over $2\le m\le M/2$, which at $256^3$ extrapolates beyond $m=M$), the share of the free energy removed by hypercollisions rises from 27\% to 46\%, and $W(m)$ at $m\le16$ rises by up to 43\%.

The model follows these changes. At $128^3$ and $256^3$, with the $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ measured there, it describes $W(m)$ to within $-9\%$ and $+13\%$ and the collisional shares to within 0.02, and the field-aligned flux lies 3--15\% above it, with no deficit at large $m$. The measured decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is within 5\% of $1/(s\kappa_1T)$, except at $\beta_i=0.3$ at $128^3$ and $256^3$, where it is 14\% and 17\% faster.

At the resolutions we reach, then, the free energy keeps its injected field-aligned wavenumber (section~\ref{sim:kpar}), but the rate of the perpendicular sink is not independent of the dissipation scale, and prediction (iv) is not confirmed. Either the dissipation range of our runs reaches almost to the forcing scales and makes the sink artificially fast, in which case $T$ levels off once the inertial range is long enough, or the perpendicular cascade of the slices has no dissipative anomaly, in which case $T$, and with it $m_*$, keeps growing as the dissipation weakens. Up to $256^3$ we see no sign of levelling off, but our runs cannot exclude it at higher resolution.
