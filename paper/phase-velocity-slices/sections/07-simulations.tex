\subsection{Set-up}\label{sim:setup}

We integrate (\ref{eq:rmhd}) in Elsasser form with one compressive branch (\ref{mod:hierarchy}), the branch $\Lambda^+$ of (\ref{eq:Lambdapm}) with $\tau=Z=1$, truncated at $m=M$ with the zero closure, using GANDALF \citep{Kanekar2025} in double precision. The box is periodic with side $L=1$, so $k_1=2\pi$; $v_A=1$ and $\tau_A=L/v_A=1$. Appendix~\ref{app:runs} gives the time stepping, the averaging windows and the checks of every run.

The Alfv\'enic fluctuations are forced through $\Phi$ alone, so that $z^+$ and $z^-$ receive the same increment, which keeps the forcing out of the induction equation (section~\ref{sec:frozen}). The forcing is white in time and acts on the Fourier modes with $1\le k_\perp/k_1\le2$ and $|k_z|=k_1$, with injection rate $\eps_A=0.3$ except in the runs at lower $\chi$ (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). The density moment $g_0$ is forced on the same band, white in time, with an amplitude set at every step from the time step, so that the expected injection rate is $\eps=0.3$. The band excludes $k_\perp=0$, where injected free energy would phase mix with no perpendicular cascade. Every injection therefore has the single Eulerian parallel wavenumber $k_z=\pm k_1$, and its field-aligned wavenumbers differ from $k_1$ only through the tilt of the field lines.

GANDALF applies one hyperdissipation, at the rate $\eta\,(k_\perp^2/k_{\perp,\max}^2)^2$, alike to $z^\pm$ and to every $g_m$, with $\eta=300$ except at lower $\chi$; there is no parallel dissipation. Hypercollisions damp $g_m$ at the rate $\nu_m=\nu\,(m/M)^6$ for $m\ge2$ ($\nu_m=0$ for $m\le1$) and so remove its free energy at the rate $2\nu_m$. Free energy moving up the ladder at $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$ reaches $m=xM$ after the hypercollisions have removed all but the fraction $\exp(-\int2\nu_m\,\dd t)$ of it, and with $\dd t=\dd m/(s\kappa\sqrt{2m})$ the exponent is $(2\sqrt2/13)\,(\nu M^{1/2}/s\kappa)\,x^{13/2}$. It depends on $\nu$, $M$ and $s$ only through $\nu\sqrt M/s$, so we set $\nu=30\,\beta_i^{1/2}(32/M)^{1/2}$, which keeps it fixed as $\beta_i$ and $M$ change. The free energy passes the moments $m\le M/2$ quickly enough that the hypercollisions remove only 3\% of the injected free energy there at $\beta_i=1$ and $M=32$; above $m=M/2$ they remove most of what arrives.

We characterise the outer scale by the part of $\Phi$ in the shells $k_\perp/k_1\le2.5$. Its energy-weighted mean $k_\perp$ and $|k_z|$, $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}$ and $k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}$, and its rms $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity $u_{\mathrm{out}}$ give the nonlinearity parameter $\chi=k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}}/k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}$ and the outer eddy-turnover time $\tau_{\rm nl}=1/(k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}})$, and we write $c_T=T/\tau_{\rm nl}$. In the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$, $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}=1.55\,k_1$, $k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}=1.11\,k_1$ and $u_{\mathrm{out}}=0.71$, so $\chi=0.99$ and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.144$. The Alfv\'enic sector does not depend on $\beta_i$, and its outer scale hardly depends on the resolution (section~\ref{sim:resolution}), so every run of table~\ref{tab:runs} has $\chi$ between 0.92 and 0.99. At $64^3$ the inertial range is short: in the run at $\beta_i=1$, $E_A(k_\perp)$ follows $k_\perp^{-5/3}$ only over $2\le k_\perp/k_1\le8$ and is steeper than $k_\perp^{-4}$ from $k_\perp/k_1=9$. Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists the runs at $\chi\approx1$.

The energy change of every step is split into the parts due to the ideal dynamics, the forcing, the hyperdissipation and the hypercollisions. This gives $D_\perp(m)$ and $D_\nu(m)$ in (\ref{eq:budget}) exactly for every moment. Every 20 steps we record $W(m)$, its perpendicular spectrum, the field-aligned flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) and the $k_z$-only flux, the split of the $k_z$-only flux into phase-mixing and unmixing parts by the sign of $k_z$ \citep{Schekochihin2016}, and $E_A=\frac12\langle|\nabla_\perp\Phi|^2+|\nabla_\perp\Psi|^2\rangle$ and its spectrum. Field-aligned wavenumbers are measured along traced field lines, as is done for the Alfv\'enic fields \citep{ChoVishniac2000,Meyrand2019}. On snapshots spaced by $5\,\tau_A$ within the averaging window ($10\,\tau_A$ at $96^3$ and above, and 10--$50\,\tau_A$ in the runs of the $m_*$ scan) we trace 4096 lines of $\bhat=\zhat+\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$ over four box lengths in $z$, sample $g_m$, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ along them, and compute their spectra along the lines, from which we take $\kappa_1(m)$. The distribution $P(\kappa)$ of the injected free energy is measured in the same way, on fresh realisations of the density forcing sampled along the same lines (appendix~\ref{app:methods}). Where we compare the model with the data, the range in parentheses is the range over four consecutive blocks of equal length of the averaging window, each compared with the model for the whole window (appendix~\ref{app:runs}).

\subsection{No return flux}\label{sim:flux}

In every run, at every resolution, the field-aligned flux is the flux that $W(m)$ carries by phase mixing at its measured field-aligned wavenumber, with no deficit that grows with $m$. We show this first for the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$. Its break lies far beyond $m=M$ (section~\ref{sim:break}), so it tests (i) and (ii) but not the break.

The dotted line is the formula (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) with $\kappa=k_z$ and no collisions. (b) The rates at which the perpendicular dissipation (circles) and the hypercollisions (squares) remove free energy from moment $m$, divided by $W(m)$ and multiplied by $T$. The shading marks $m>M/2$, where the hypercollisions act.}
  \label{fig:wm}

The perpendicular dissipation removes free energy from each moment nearly in proportion to its free energy, as (\ref{eq:uniformsink}) assumes. $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ lies between 0.37 and 0.47 for $0\le m\le16$, with a systematic rise of 20\% over $1\le m\le16$ (18--23\%) that section~\ref{sim:beta} traces to the age of the free energy (figure~\ref{fig:wm}b). We take $1/T=0.437$, its median over $2\le m\le16$, so $T=2.29=15.9\,\tau_{\rm nl}$. Because $T$ is measured from $D_\perp/W$, this checks the assumption of a uniform sink and does not test the model independently. The budget (\ref{eq:budget}) closes to within $4\times10^{-4}\,\eps$ in every moment with the field-aligned flux, and only to within 8\% of $\eps$ with the $k_z$-only flux.

  \label{fig:flux}

The independent test is the relation between the flux and the spectrum. The moments $g_m$ have $\kappa_1(m)$ between $1.07\,k_1$ and $1.19\,k_1$ for $1\le m\le21$, with no trend in $m$, against $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$ for the injected pattern. For $1\le m\le16$ the measured flux is 0.96--0.97 times $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$ (0.95--0.98). The uniform-sink model, which has no return flux, gives 0.97--0.99, a little below 1 because the sink removes free energy while it phase mixes. Read the other way, the flux corresponds to phase mixing at $\Pi_{m+1/2}/(s\,c_mW(m))=1.09$--$1.12\,k_1$ for $3\le m\le23$ (1.09--1.13), inside the range of $\kappa_1(m)$. There is no room for a return flux of more than a few per cent.

With this $T$ and the $P(\kappa)$ of the injected pattern, the model reproduces $W(m)$ to within 8\% for $1\le m\le30$ (9--12\%; figure~\ref{fig:wm}a) and the field-aligned flux to within 3--7\% for $m\le24$ ($-1$ to $+9\%$; figure~\ref{fig:flux}). Only in the last moments, $m\ge27$, where the hypercollisions dominate, does the flux fall below the model, by up to 13\%, and $W$ by 14\% at $m=32$; a sink that grows with the age of the free energy acts in this direction. The tilt of the field lines matters. With a single $\kappa=k_1$ the model gives $W$ 15--16\% too high for $2\le m\le16$, and with a Gaussian distribution of the tilt in place of the traced one the measured $W(m)$ ranges from 0.72 to 1.22 times the model. With the field-aligned distribution of $g_0$ itself, whose mean is $0.61\,k_1$ because free energy at small $\kappa$ leaves $m=0$ slowly, the model gives $W$ too low by a factor of up to 1.9 at $m=24$. The free energy phase mixes at the wavenumbers at which it was injected.

A split of the $k_z$-only flux into phase-mixing and unmixing parts by the sign of $k_z$ \citep{Schekochihin2016} reports a backward flux where the field-aligned flux has none. At $\beta_i=1$ its unmixing part is $-0.11$ to $-0.15$ times its phase-mixing part for $1\le m\le16$ (figure~\ref{fig:flux}), and at $\beta_i=0.3$ it grows from about $-0.13$ times the phase-mixing part at $64^3$ to $-0.27$ to $-0.35$ at $256^3$. The split assigns to the backward direction the forward flux of modes whose $k_z$ and field-aligned wavenumber have opposite signs (\ref{lag:shear}). In every run of table~\ref{tab:runs} the per-$m$ budget closes to better than $5\times10^{-4}\,\eps$ with the field-aligned flux, and only to 2--8\% of $\eps$ with the $k_z$-only flux.

The same holds in every other run. For $8\le m\le M/2$ the field-aligned flux is 0.93--1.02 times $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$ in the runs of table~\ref{tab:runs}, which include $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$, and 0.85--1.00 in the runs of the $m_*$ scan (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). The uniform-sink model gives 0.96--0.99 in every run, and in no run does the measured ratio fall as $m$ grows, as a return flux would make it. In the runs of the $m_*$ scan, whose collision-free range reaches much older free energy, the flux falls below the model with a uniform sink as $m$ grows, to as much as 37\% below it at $m=256$ at $\chi=0.40$, as a sink that grows with the age of the free energy makes it.

\label{sim:mf1}\citet{Meyrand2019} forced the compressive fluctuations through $g_1$, the parallel flow, on modes with $|k_z|\le2k_1$. To see what this changes, we repeated the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=64$ with $g_1$ forced in place of $g_0$, on the same band of $k_\perp$ but on $|k_z|=k_1$ and $2k_1$, at the same rate. The Alfv\'enic turbulence is the same, with $\chi=0.95$ and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.153$, and so is the perpendicular sink, $1/T=0.427$ against 0.421. The injected pattern has $\kappa_1=1.63\,k_1$ along the field lines, against $1.12\,k_1$. The spectrum is steeper at small $m$, with a local slope of $-0.66$ over $2\le m\le8$ against $-0.54$, and the same at larger $m$, $-0.71$ over $8\le m\le32$ against $-0.72$; it is nowhere close to $m^{-1}$. The model with the traced $P(\kappa)$, injected at $m=1$, reproduces $W(m)$ to within 3.5\% for $0\le m\le32$ (4--8\% for $m\ge2$), and the field-aligned flux lies 2--4\% above it for $m\ge1$ ($-0.4$ to $+7\%$), with no deficit at large $m$. Forcing $g_1$ on two parallel wavenumbers therefore adds no return flux. At $m=16$ the field-aligned flux still carries 75\% of the injected free energy.

\subsection{The field-aligned wavenumber}\label{sim:kpar}

The free energy keeps the field-aligned wavenumbers at which it was injected, at every resolution from $64^3$ to $256^3$. The injected pattern has $k_z=k_1$ but $\kappa_1=1.10$--$1.14\,k_1$ along the field lines in every run with $g_0$ forced, because the lines are tilted. In the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$, where $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$, the rms of the tilt $\boldsymbol b_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol k_\perp$ over the pattern is $0.89\,k_1$, and the rms field-aligned wavenumber is $1.34\,k_1$. The Alfv\'enic turbulence, and with it the tilt, is the same in all these runs, so the differences in $\kappa_1$ come from sampling different snapshots.

Figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(a) shows the spectrum of the free energy, summed over $m$, along the field lines and along $z$ in the runs at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $M=64$ on $64^3$ to $256^3$ grid points. Along the field lines it does not change with the resolution. At every resolution 3.7--4.4\% of the free energy lies beyond $2\,k_1$, against 6.9--8.2\% for the injected pattern, and its mean field-aligned wavenumber is 1.00--$1.03\,k_1$. The free energy is weighted towards smaller $\kappa$ than the injection, because free energy at larger $\kappa$ reaches the collisional range sooner. Measured along $z$, the same free energy shows a spectrum that extends further with each doubling of the resolution, as a parallel cascade would: the share beyond $8\,k_1$ grows from 0.2\% at $64^3$ to 0.9\%, 1.8\% and 4.3\% at $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$. A cascade along the field would extend the field-aligned spectrum in the same way. What grows along the field lines is only a tail beyond about $4\,k_1$, which holds 0.2\% of the free energy at $64^3$ and 0.4\% at $256^3$. The tail is small, but it does grow with the resolution, as a slow relabelling of the field lines by the hyperdissipation would make it (section~\ref{tc:limits}).

The Alfv\'enic fields do cascade along the field lines, and $g_0$ does not follow them. Figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(b) compares the spectra of the whole velocity $\boldsymbol u_\perp$ and of $g_0$ along the same lines. Beyond $4\,k_1$ the velocity holds 1.5\%, 2.7\% and 3.2\% of its energy at $64^3$, $96^3$ and $128^3$, a share that grows as the inertial range lengthens, while $g_0$ holds 0.11\%, 0.14\% and 0.18\% of its variance there, 14 to 19 times less. Its tail grows with the resolution as well, but from an amplitude more than ten times smaller. \citet{Meyrand2019} found that the spectra of the density and field-strength fluctuations along the perturbed lines track that of the velocity; in our runs the spectrum of $g_0$ does not.

Filtered into bands of $k_\perp$, $g_0$ seems to gain field-aligned structure at small perpendicular scales: its $\kappa_1$ grows from about $1.0\,k_1$ in the band $k_\perp\le2k_1$ to 2.0--$2.1\,k_1$ in the band $8\le k_\perp/k_1\le16$ at $64^3$ and $128^3$ (figure~\ref{fig:kpar}c). This growth does not come from structure of $g_0$ along the field. Filtering mixes neighbouring field lines, which separate along the field, so a filtered field shows field-aligned structure that the whole field does not have. To measure this effect we built from each snapshot a copy of $g_0$ that is frozen to the field lines by construction and varies along them only on scales longer than $L/4$ (appendix~\ref{app:frozencopy}). Band by band, the field-aligned wavenumber of the frozen copy is that of $g_0$ to within 4\% at both resolutions, whereas that of $\Phi$ grows to $3.1\,k_1$. The growth of the band-resolved wavenumber of $g_0$ therefore comes from the filtering, and the spectra of the whole fields, figure~\ref{fig:kpar}(a,b), are the test of a parallel cascade. Over the whole field, $g_0$ has 0.11--0.18\% of its variance beyond $4\,k_1$, against 0.05--0.07\% for its frozen copy.

\subsection{The break}\label{sim:break}

The break is resolved only at low $\beta_i$, and we measure it at $64^3$ only. In the run at $\beta_i=1$ it lies far beyond the highest moment we keep, $m=M=32$. With the $T$ of that run, $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$ is 103 for $\kappa=k_1$ and 185 for the rms field-aligned wavenumber, and $(c_T^2/2)\beta_i/\chi^2=127$. Free energy injected at $m=0$ reaches $m=16$ after about $0.8\,\tau_A$ and $m=32$ after $1.1\,\tau_A$, against $T=2.3\,\tau_A$, and the hypercollisions remove 59\% of the injected free energy (56--61\%). The exponential factor in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) therefore reduces $W$ by only 29\% at $m=16$ and 39\% at $m=32$, and at $\beta_i=1$ the test of the shape shows that there is no deficit, not that the sink produces a break.

(b) The perpendicular sink $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ against the time $\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ since injection of the free energy at $m$, with $\kappa_1$ the mean field-aligned wavenumber of the injected pattern, for $1\le m\le M/2$; the runs at $M=64$ (solid) reach older free energy than those at $M=32$ (dotted); the thin line is the joint fit $0.50+0.12\ln[\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)]$.}

Lowering $\beta_i$ at fixed Alfv\'enic forcing moves the break into the resolved range, as test (iii) expects. We ran $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1, each at $M=32$ and $M=64$, and $\beta_i=1$ also at $M=64$; the branch is $\Lambda^+=6.2206$ and $19.198$. With $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ from $\beta_i=1$, (\ref{eq:mstar}) gives $m_*\approx31$--56 at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $m_*\approx10$--19 at $\beta_i=0.1$. At $\beta_i=0.1$ the break with the measured $T$, $m_*\approx6$--10, lies well inside the resolved range, and $W(m)$ falls below $m^{-1/2}$ from small $m$ (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a).

We fit $W(m)\sqrt{2m}\propto\exp(-\sqrt{2m}/\ell_*)$, the form (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) with the decay length $\ell_*$ of section~\ref{tc:scales} left free, over $2\le m\le M/2$, below the collisional range. The fitted $\ell_*$ is 16.8, 8.3 and 3.8 at $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1 ($M=32$), and 15.7, 7.2 and 3.6 at $M=64$, against $s\kappa_1T=16.4$, 8.0, 3.8, 16.8, 7.6 and 3.7 from the measured $T$ and the injected $\kappa_1$. The measured decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is the predicted one to within 7\% in every run (0.89--1.24 times it). Only at $\beta_i=0.1$ does the fit range reach well beyond the break, to 1.7 decay lengths at $M=64$, where $m_*\approx7$. At $\beta_i=0.3$ it ends near the break, and at $\beta_i=1$ it spans less than half a decay length ($\sqrt{2m}\le\sqrt M=5.7$ and 8 against $\ell_*\approx16$), so the break $m_*=\ell_*^2/2\approx140$ there extrapolates the fit well beyond $m=M$.

To measure $m_*$ against $\beta_i$ and $\chi$ we made the runs of table~\ref{tab:mstar}, with $M$ large enough in each that the break lies below $M/2$. At $\chi\approx0.95$ we ran $\beta_i=0.05$ and 0.3, and we use the run at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=64$ of table~\ref{tab:runs}. At $\beta_i=0.05$ we lowered $\chi$ to 0.75, 0.56 and 0.40 by lowering the Alfv\'enic injection rate $\eps_A$ from 0.3 to 0.1, 0.031 and 0.0035. As $\chi$ decreases the hyperdissipation of $E_A$ moves to larger scales unless $\eta$ decreases with it. For the target values $\chi=0.7$ and 0.5 we set $\eta=300\,\chi/0.96$, which gives 219 and 156. At the target $\chi=0.35$ this rule gave a spectrum of $E_A$ that failed our check on its slope, and we used instead $\eta\propto\chi^2$, calibrated with short runs at $M=2$ and $\eta=28$, 40 and 56, which gives $\eta=33$. The collision-free range holds 1.7 to 3.5 decay lengths, and at the top of the fit range the hypercollisional rate $2\nu_m$ is at most 13\% of $1/T$. The model value is $(s\kappa_1T)^2/2$, with $1/T$ the median rate over $2\le m\le16$ of each run and $\kappa_1$ from lines traced on its snapshots.

At $64^3$ and $\chi\approx0.95$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\beta_i=0.05$ to 6.6 and 24.2 at $\beta_i=0.1$ and 0.3 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}a). A weighted fit gives $m_*\propto\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$, close to the $m_*\propto\beta_i$ of (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}) at fixed $c_T$, where the error is the scatter of the fits to the four blocks. The model values follow $\beta_i^{1.31\pm0.04}$, and the ratio of the model value to the measured $m_*$ is 0.88, 1.05 and 1.30 at $\beta_i=0.05$, 0.1 and 0.3. The model lies above at $\beta_i=0.3$, where $c_T$ is larger, 13.6 against 10.9 and 11.0, because the moments $2\le m\le16$ that set $T$ hold younger free energy at larger $\beta_i$ (section~\ref{sim:beta}). The resolution changes $m_*$ far more than the scatter of the blocks: at $\beta_i=0.3$ it moves from 26 at $64^3$ to 86 at $256^3$ (section~\ref{sim:resolution}). The exponent may change less, since between $64^3$ and $128^3$, $c_T$ rises by about the same factor at $\beta_i=1$ and 0.3, $1.47\pm0.04$ and $1.40\pm0.07$.

At $\beta_i=0.05$ and $64^3$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\chi=0.96$ to 7.0, 14.9 and 60.5 at $\chi=0.75$, 0.56 and 0.40 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}b). Computed from the unrounded $\chi$, the local exponent $-\dd\ln m_*/\dd\ln\chi$ between neighbouring runs is $2.50\pm0.29$, $2.69\pm0.16$ and $4.09\pm0.13$, so the break grows faster than $\chi^{-2}$ as $\chi$ falls. It does so through $T$. In these runs $T$ stays at 0.71--0.74 times $E_A/\eps_A$, while $E_A/\eps_A$ grows from $14.7\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.96$ to $35.4\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.40$, so $c_T$ rises from 10.9 to 25.9. The runs differ in $\eta$ as well as in $\chi$, and at fixed $\chi$, $T$ changes by 18--35\% with each doubling of the resolution (section~\ref{sim:resolution}), so these runs do not separate the dependence of $m_*$ on $\chi$ from its dependence on the dissipation. The model value follows the measured $m_*$ to within 12\% for $\chi\ge0.56$, but at $\chi=0.40$ it is 167, 2.8 times the measured value; section~\ref{sim:beta} traces this to the age dependence of the sink.

\subsection{The age of the free energy}\label{sim:beta}

At $64^3$ the perpendicular sink is not uniform in $m$ but depends on the age of the free energy, in the same way at every $\beta_i$. In the runs at $64^3$ and $\chi\approx1$, $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ rises with $m$, from 0.33--0.43 at $m\le1$ to 0.46, 0.53 and 0.62 at $m=16$ for $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1, and the rise is steeper at lower $\beta_i$, where free energy reaches a given $m$ later. Plotted against the time $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ since injection, the sinks of these runs fall on one curve (figure~\ref{fig:beta}b), with an rms scatter of 4.5\% (4.7--7.4\%) about a joint fit $D_\perp/W=0.50+0.12\ln\vartheta$, against 12.6\% (10--18\%) when plotted against $m$. $D_\perp/W$ is about 0.4 at the smallest ages and levels off at about 0.63 for ages beyond about $2.5\,\tau_A$. This favours the age over the streaming speed as the cause. The parallel velocities that dominate moment $m$ stream at about $s\sqrt{2m}$, which at a fixed age scales as $s^2$ and differs by a factor of ten between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1, yet the sinks fall on one curve. A run with $s=0$ in the same flow, which we have not made, would measure the sink of a passive scalar directly.

A single rate $1/T$, the median over $2\le m\le16$, therefore removes too much free energy at small $m$. At $\beta_i=1$ the measured $W(m)$ is within 7\% of the model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ for $1\le m\le M/2$, at both $M$. At lower $\beta_i$ it lies above the model at every $m$, by up to 21\% and 39\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1 at $M=32$ and by up to 20\% and 42\% at $M=64$ (up to 29\% and 52\% in single blocks), and the field-aligned flux lies above the model as well (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a). The median rate $1/T$ rises from 0.44 to 0.59 between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1 for the same reason: at low $\beta_i$ the moments $2\le m\le16$ hold older free energy.

  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{h1_age_model}
  \caption{$W(m)$ divided by the two-channel model with the age-dependent sink $\gamma(\vartheta)$ fitted at $\beta_i=0.1$ (solid) and with the uniform sink $1/T$ of each run (dotted), for the runs at $64^3$ and $M=64$.}
  \label{fig:age}
\end{figure}

To test this directly we let each injection evolve by the linear hierarchy at its field-aligned wavenumber, with the run's hypercollisions, and remove it at a rate $\gamma(\vartheta)$ that depends only on its age, the same at every $m$. With $S(\vartheta)=\exp(-\int_0^\vartheta\gamma)$ the steady state is
\begin{equation}
  W(m)=\eps\sum_\kappa P(\kappa)\int_0^\infty E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)\,S(\vartheta)\,\dd\vartheta ,
\end{equation}
with $E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)$ the free energy at moment $m$ of a unit injection at age $\vartheta$, and $D_\perp(m)$ is the same integral with $\gamma S$ in place of $S$; for $\gamma=1/T$ this is the uniform-sink model. Fitting $\gamma(\vartheta)=\gamma_0+(\gamma_1-\gamma_0)(1-e^{-\vartheta/\vartheta_c})$ to $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.1$ ($M=64$) gives $\gamma_0=0.03$, $\gamma_1=0.67$ and $\vartheta_c=0.76\,\tau_A$, about five outer eddy-turnover times. Fresh free energy is hardly removed, and the sink switches on in the time the cascade takes to carry it to the dissipation range of these runs, which depends on the resolution. $D_\perp/W$ exceeds $\gamma_0$ even at small $m$ because each moment also holds older free energy at small $\kappa$. With this $\gamma(\vartheta)$ and no parameter fitted to them, the model gives $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 1 to within 5\% for $m\ge2$ and $m\ge4$ (4--12\%), across a factor of ten in $\beta_i$. It gives $W(m)$ to within 7\% for $m\ge8$ in all three runs (up to 15\%), and lies above the measured $W$ at the smallest $m$, by 7\% at $m=1$ in the run used for the fit and by 8--14\% for $m\le3$ in the other two (figure~\ref{fig:age}).

The age dependence also explains why the model overestimates $m_*$ at $\chi=0.40$ (section~\ref{sim:mstar}). There $D_\perp/W$ varies by a factor of 10 over $1\le m\le M/2$, against 1.4, 1.7 and 2.5 at $\chi=0.96$, 0.75 and 0.56, so the median over $2\le m\le16$ does not represent the sink over the range $m\le171$ on which the break is measured. With the rate averaged over each run's fit range instead, uniformly in $\sqrt{2m}$ as the fit weights it, $s\kappa_1T$ agrees with the fitted $\ell_*$ to within 9\% at $\chi=0.40$ and to within 5\% in every other run of table~\ref{tab:mstar}. The decay beyond the break is therefore still set by phase mixing at $\kappa_1$ against the perpendicular sink, as in (\ref{eq:Wage}).

\subsection{Dependence on resolution}\label{sim:resolution}

The results of sections~\ref{sim:flux} and~\ref{sim:kpar} hold at every resolution, but $T$ does not converge. Doubling $M$ from 32 to 64 leaves $W(m)$ unchanged to within about 5\% for $1\le m\le10$ and changes $1/T$ by at most 6\% at every $\beta_i$. What changes is the share of the free energy that reaches the hypercollisions. When the collisional range moves from $m>16$ to $m>32$ it falls from 59\% to 50\% at $\beta_i=1$, from 43\% to 27\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and from 20\% to 10\% at $\beta_i=0.1$, and the model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ gives the shares to within 0.06 at $M=32$ and 0.02 at $M=64$. In these runs, free energy that the hypercollisions remove at the lower cutoff is removed at small perpendicular scales when the cutoff is raised, so the collisional share is set by the truncation and has not converged at $M=32$.

We repeated the runs at $M=64$ on $96^3$, $128^3$ and $256^3$ grid points at $\beta_i=0.3$ and on $128^3$ at $\beta_i=1$, with the hyperdissipation normalised to the grid, so that the inertial range lengthens with $N$: the slope of $E_A$ fitted over the middle 60\% of the resolved shells goes from $-4.1$ at $64^3$ to $-2.9$ at $256^3$. The outer scale is unchanged, with $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.150$--0.158 and $\chi=0.92$--0.97 in all six runs. $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ falls at every $m$, by 18--35\% with each doubling of $N$ (figure~\ref{fig:resolution-n}), and $c_T$ rises from 13.2 at $64^3$ to 16.4, 18.4 and 24.6 at $\beta_i=0.3$, and from 15.4 to 22.6 at $\beta_i=1$. At $\beta_i=0.3$, $T$ grows by 5--6 outer eddy-turnover times with each doubling, with no sign of levelling off up to $256^3$. It also grows relative to $E_A/\eps_A$, from 0.88 times it at $64^3$ to 1.59 times at $256^3$, so the closeness of $T$ to $E_A/\eps_A$ (section~\ref{tc:scales}) holds at $64^3$ only. The break moves out with $T$, from $m_*\approx26$ at $64^3$ to 86 at $256^3$ (from the fit over $2\le m\le M/2$, which at $256^3$ extrapolates beyond $m=M$), the share of the free energy removed by hypercollisions rises from 27\% to 46\%, and $W(m)$ at $m\le16$ rises by up to 43\%.

With the $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ measured at each resolution, the model follows these changes. At $128^3$ and $256^3$ it describes $W(m)$ to within $-9\%$ and $+13\%$ ($-10\%$ to $+17\%$) and the collisional shares to within 0.02, and the field-aligned flux lies 3--15\% above it. The flux is 0.93--1.02 times $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$ for $8\le m\le M/2$ in these runs, as at $64^3$, with no deficit that grows with $m$. The fitted decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is within 5\% of $1/(s\kappa_1T)$, except at $\beta_i=0.3$ at $128^3$ and $256^3$, where it is 14\% and 17\% faster. That is not a return flux, which would lower the flux relative to $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$. It is consistent with the sink, which rises more steeply with $m$ at higher resolution: at $\beta_i=0.3$ the ratio of its largest to its smallest value over $1\le m\le M/2$ grows from 1.55 at $64^3$ to 1.91 at $256^3$, so the median over $2\le m\le16$ underestimates it where the decay is fitted.

At the resolutions we reach, then, the free energy keeps its injected field-aligned wavenumber and phase mixes with no return flux, but $T$ has not converged. Either the dissipation range of our runs reaches almost to the forcing scales and makes the sink artificially fast, in which case $T$ levels off once the inertial range is long enough, or the perpendicular cascade of the free energy has no dissipative anomaly, in which case $T$, and with it $m_*$, keeps growing as the dissipation weakens. Up to $256^3$ we see no sign of levelling off, but our runs cannot exclude it at higher resolution.
