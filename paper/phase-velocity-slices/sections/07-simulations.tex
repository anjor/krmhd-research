\section{Simulations}\label{sec:simulations}

\subsection{Set-up}\label{sim:setup}

We integrate (\ref{eq:rmhd}) in Elsasser form with one compressive branch (\ref{mod:hierarchy}), the branch $\Lambda^+$ of (\ref{eq:Lambdapm}) with $\tau=Z=1$, truncated at $M=32$ with the zero closure, using GANDALF \citep{Kanekar2025} in double precision: version 0.7.0 on a CPU for the runs at $M=32$, and version 0.8.1, whose solver is the same, on a GPU for the runs at $M=64$ (section~\ref{sim:beta}). The box is periodic with side $L=1$ and $64^3$ grid points, so $k_1=2\pi$; $v_A=1$ and $\tau_A=L/v_A=1$. This section reports the run at $\beta_i=1$, where $\Lambda^+=\sqrt5$; section~\ref{sim:beta} describes the runs at lower $\beta_i$.

The Alfv\'enic fluctuations are forced through $\Phi$ alone, so that $z^+$ and $z^-$ receive the same increment, which keeps the forcing out of the induction equation (section~\ref{sec:frozen}). The forcing is white in time and acts on the Fourier modes with $1\le k_\perp/k_1\le2$ and $|k_z|=k_1$, with injection rate $\eps_A=0.3$. Excluding $k_z=0$ from the band avoids the two-dimensional condensate that a band including it drove at this resolution. The density moment $g_0$ is forced on the same band, white in time, with its amplitude rescaled at every step so that the expected injection rate is $\eps=0.3$; the band excludes $k_\perp=0$, where injected free energy would phase mix with no perpendicular cascade. Every injection therefore has the single Eulerian parallel wavenumber $k_z=\pm k_1$, and its field-aligned wavenumbers differ from $k_1$ only through the tilt of the field lines.

GANDALF applies the hyper-resistivity $\eta\,(k_\perp^2/k_{\perp,\max}^2)^2$, with $\eta=300$, alike to $z^\pm$ and to every $g_m$; there is no parallel dissipation. Hypercollisions damp $g_m$ at the rate $\nu\,(m/M)^6$ with $\nu=30$ for $m\ge2$, and so remove its free energy at the rate $2\nu\,(m/M)^6$. Free energy moving up the ladder at $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$ passes the moments $m\le M/2$ quickly enough that they remove only 3\% of the injected free energy there; above $m=M/2$ they remove most of what arrives. The time step is GANDALF's Lawson--Runge--Kutta scheme \citep[Lawson-RK4;][]{Lawson1967}, which integrates the streaming exactly through an integrating factor and treats the nonlinear terms with explicit RK4. Following section~\ref{hm:trunc}, $\Delta t=0.8\times2\sqrt2/\rho$, where $\rho$ is the spectral radius of the explicit slice-advection operator at the extreme nodes $u=\pm u_{\max}$, computed by a Lanczos iteration every 25 steps, or the Alfv\'enic limit $0.8\,\Delta x/\max|\nabla_\perp z^\pm|$ if that is smaller. The Alfv\'enic turbulence is first spun up with the same forcing and dissipation. The run then starts from $g=0$ and lasts $53\,\tau_A$, and all averages are over the final $50\,\tau_A$, $3\le t\le53$; $W$ reaches its mean level within about $3\,\tau_A$.

We characterise the outer scale by the part of $\Phi$ in the shells $k_\perp/k_1\le2.5$. Its energy-weighted mean $k_\perp$ and $|k_z|$ are $k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}=9.7=1.55\,k_1$ and $k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}=6.97=1.11\,k_1$, and its rms $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity is $u_{\mathrm{out}}=0.71$. The nonlinearity parameter is $\chi=k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}}/k_{\parallel,\mathrm{out}}=0.99$ (0.85 with the rms field-aligned wavenumber of the same field in place of $|k_z|$), and the outer eddy time is $\tau_{\rm nl}=1/(k_{\perp,\mathrm{out}}u_{\mathrm{out}})=0.144$. The measured free-energy injection rate over the window is $\eps=0.307$.

\subsection{Diagnostics}\label{sim:diag}

The energy change of every step is split into the parts due to the ideal dynamics, the forcing, the hyper-resistivity and the hypercollisions. This gives $D_\perp(m)$ and $D_\nu(m)$ in (\ref{eq:budget}) exactly for every moment, and the remainder is the time-stepping error of the ideal part. Every 20 steps we record $W(m)$, its perpendicular spectrum $W(m,k_\perp)$, the field-aligned flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) and the $k_z$-only flux, the split of the $k_z$-only flux into phase-mixing and unmixing parts by the sign of $k_z$ \citep{Schekochihin2016}, the slice amplitudes $a_j$ at the $M+1$ nodes of theorem~\ref{thm:trunc} with their correlation matrix $C_{jl}=\langle a_ja_l\rangle$, and the Alfv\'enic energy $E_A=\frac12\langle|\nabla_\perp\Phi|^2+|\nabla_\perp\Psi|^2\rangle$ and its spectrum.

Field-aligned wavenumbers are measured along traced field lines, as is done for the Alfv\'enic fields \citep{ChoVishniac2000,Meyrand2019}. On snapshots spaced by $5\,\tau_A$ within the averaging window ($t=10$, 15, \dots, 50 in this run; every $10\,\tau_A$ at $96^3$ and above) we trace 4096 lines of $\bhat=\zhat+\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$ over four box lengths in $z$, sample $g_m$, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ along them, the last three also filtered into the bands $k_\perp/k_1\in[0,2]$, $[2,4]$, $[4,8]$ and $[8,16]$, and compute the spectra along the lines. We report $\kappa_1$, the first moment of $|\kappa|$. The distribution $P(\kappa)$ of the injected free energy is measured in the same way, on fresh realisations of the density forcing sampled along the same lines. Appendix~\ref{app:methods} gives the details and the computation of the model.

Table~\ref{tab:gates} lists the checks, and figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the run. Energy is conserved by the time step and balanced between injection and dissipation. The Alfv\'enic energy fluctuates by 12\% about its mean, which at this box size is intrinsic to the forced turbulence. The block means of the averages used below, each divided by the injection rate of its block, agree to within 9\% except $D_\perp$ at $m=0$ (15.5\%), so the averages are converged to about that level. The perpendicular spectrum $E_A(k_\perp)$ follows $k_\perp^{-5/3}$ only over $2\le k_\perp/k_1\le8$ and is steeper than $k_\perp^{-4}$ from $k_\perp/k_1=9$, so the inertial range is short. The hierarchy is marginally resolved: 1.1\% of the free energy lies at $m>3M/4$. We therefore treat this run as a pilot, which tests predictions (i), (ii), (v) and (vi) but cannot place the break $m_*$ inside the hierarchy (section~\ref{sim:beta1}).

\emph{(i) A uniform sink.} Figure~\ref{fig:wm}(b) shows $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$. It lies between 0.37 and 0.47 for $0\le m\le16$; over $1\le m\le16$ it varies by a factor of 1.20 between its extremes and by 6\% in rms, and rises to 0.54 at $m=24$, where the hypercollisions dominate. We take $1/T=0.437$, its median over $2\le m\le16$, so $T=2.29=15.9\,\tau_{\rm nl}$. The budget (\ref{eq:budget}) closes to $4\times10^{-4}\,\eps$ in every moment with the field-aligned flux, and to 8\% with the $k_z$-only flux. The perpendicular dissipation is therefore close to an $m$-independent sink, as (\ref{eq:uniformsink}) assumes, with a systematic rise of 20\% over $1\le m\le16$. Two causes are possible: slices with large $|u|$, which dominate the high moments, may be cascaded faster (section~\ref{tc:limits}), or the free energy at higher $m$, which was injected longer ago, has had more time to reach small perpendicular scales. The runs at lower $\beta_i$ (section~\ref{sim:beta}) decide between them: at a fixed time since injection the streaming speed of the slices that dominate moment $m$, $s\sqrt{2m}$, scales as $s^2$ and differs by a factor of ten between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1, yet the sinks of all runs fall on one curve against that time (figure~\ref{fig:beta}b), which favours the second.

\emph{Field-aligned wavenumbers.} The injected pattern, which has $k_z=k_1$, has $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$ along the field lines; the rms of the tilt $\boldsymbol b_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol k_\perp$ over the pattern is $0.89\,k_1$, and the rms field-aligned wavenumber is $1.34\,k_1$. The moments $g_m$ have $\kappa_1$ between $1.07\,k_1$ and $1.19\,k_1$ for $1\le m\le21$, with no trend in $m$, and the wavenumber implied by the flux, $\Pi_{m+1/2}/[s\sqrt{2(m+1)}\,W(m)]$, lies between $1.02\,k_1$ and $1.10\,k_1$ for $3\le m\le23$. Figure~\ref{fig:kpar} resolves $\kappa_1$ in $k_\perp$. In the bands centred on $k_\perp/k_1=1.5$, 3, 6 and 12, $g_0$ has $\kappa_1/k_1=0.91$, 0.97, 1.36 and 2.04, whereas $\Phi$ has 1.11, 1.55, 2.12 and 3.18 and $\Psi$ has 1.13, 1.47, 1.94 and 2.60. The Alfv\'enic fields gain parallel structure from the outer scale on, though more slowly than $k_\perp^{2/3}$ at this resolution. The density moment keeps the injected field-aligned wavenumber up to $k_\perp=3k_1$, exceeds it by 20\% in the band $4\le k_\perp/k_1\le8$ at the end of the inertial range, and reaches $2.04\,k_1$ only in the band $8\le k_\perp/k_1\le16$, where $E_A$ is steeper than $k_\perp^{-4}$. Measured with $k_z$, $g_0$ would appear to have $\kappa_1/k_1=0.78$, 0.93, 1.83 and 3.12, close to the Alfv\'enic values at small scales. In this run, then, $g$ acquires substantial structure along the field only at the end of the inertial range and in the dissipation range, where the hyper-resistivity reconnects the field lines and the perpendicular sink is already removing it. At $128^3$, with a longer inertial range, $g_0$ gains field-aligned structure more gradually and throughout it (section~\ref{sim:resolution}).

\emph{(ii) The shape of $W(m)$ and the flux.} With $T$ from (i) and $P(\kappa)$ of the injected pattern, the model reproduces $W(m)$ to within 8\% for $1\le m\le30$, with an rms deviation of 3.3\% in $\ln W$ over $2\le m\le16$ (figure~\ref{fig:wm}a, green), and the field-aligned flux to within 3--7\% for $m\le24$ (figure~\ref{fig:flux}a). The measured flux lies 4--7\% above the model for $1\le m\le16$. Only in the last moments, $m\ge27$, where the hypercollisions dominate, does it fall below the model, by up to 12\%, and $W$ by 14\% at $m=32$; a sink that grows with the age of the free energy (section~\ref{sim:beta}) acts in this direction. The flux that $W(m)$ would carry if all of it phase mixed at its measured $\kappa_1(m)$ is close to the measured flux, so there is no room for a return flux of more than a few per cent. With a single $\kappa=k_1$ the model gives $W$ 14--16\% too high, because it omits the tilt. With the field-aligned distribution of $g_0$ itself in place of that of the injected pattern, it overdamps, giving $W$ too low by a factor of up to 1.9 at $m=24$: the free energy phase mixes at the wavenumber at which it was injected, not at the larger one that $g_0$ shows at small perpendicular scales.

\emph{The break.} With these values the break lies far beyond the hierarchy: $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$ is 103 for $\kappa=k_1$ and 185 for the rms field-aligned wavenumber, and $c_T^2\beta_i/(2\chi^2)=127$ with $c_T=T/\tau_{\rm nl}$. Free energy injected at $m=0$ reaches $m=16$ after about $0.8\,\tau_A$ and $m=32$ after $1.1\,\tau_A$, against $T=2.3\,\tau_A$, and the hypercollisions remove 57--61\% of the injected free energy (the range over the four blocks). At $\beta_i=1$ the exponential factor in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) therefore reduces $W$ by only 29\% at $m=16$ and 39\% at $m=32$, and the test of the shape is correspondingly weak: it confirms that there is no deficit, not that the sink produces a break.

\emph{(v) Eulerian diagnostics.} The $k_z$-only flux split by the sign of $k_z$ has an unmixing part of $-0.11$ to $-0.15$ times its phase-mixing part for $1\le m\le16$, and $-0.52$ at $m=0$ (figure~\ref{fig:flux}a), whereas the field-aligned flux is reproduced by a model that has no return flux. A diagnostic of this kind would report a backward flux of about 12\% of the forward one where, by the field-aligned measures, there is none. With the rms field-aligned and Eulerian wavenumbers of $g_m$ measured in the run, about $1.2\,k_1$ and $1.8$--$1.9\,k_1$, the estimate of prediction (v) gives a ratio of 0.09--0.11 for $m\ge1$, against the measured 0.11--0.15: the backward flux of the split is accounted for by the tilt of the field lines, with no field-aligned return flux (figure~\ref{fig:tilt}).

\emph{(vi) Slice correlations.} The width (\ref{eq:deltauc}) is $\delta u_c=0.05$--0.07, below the spacing of the nodes, about 0.4 near $u=0$ at $M=32$, so it cannot be resolved here. The model, evaluated at the nodes, reproduces the measured normalised correlations $C_{jl}/(C_{jj}C_{ll})^{1/2}$ to an rms difference of 0.027 (figure~\ref{fig:flux}b).

\subsection{Dependence on \texorpdfstring{$\beta_i$}{beta} at fixed \texorpdfstring{$\chi$}{chi}}\label{sim:beta}

To bring the break into the hierarchy, we ran the same set-up at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $0.1$, each at $M=32$ and $M=64$, and at $\beta_i=1$ also at $M=64$. The Alfv\'enic sector does not depend on $\beta_i$, so the same Alfv\'enic forcing gives the same $\chi$ (the outer-scale value is 0.93--0.99 in every run); the branch is $\Lambda^+=6.2206$ and $19.198$. The attenuation of free energy that streams up the ladder by hypercollisions $\nu(m/M)^6$ depends on $\nu\sqrt{M}/s$, so we set $\nu=30\,\beta_i^{1/2}$ at $M=32$ and $\nu=30\,\beta_i^{1/2}(32/M)^{1/2}$ at $M=64$, which keeps the total attenuation fixed while the collisional range moves from $m>16$ to $m>32$. With $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ from $\beta_i=1$, (\ref{eq:mstar}) gives $m_*\approx31$--56 at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $m_*\approx10$--19 at $\beta_i=0.1$. The runs at $\beta_i=0.1$ and at $M=64$ use a safety factor of 0.4 on the Alfv\'enic limit of the time step, with both limits updated every step, and last $66\,\tau_A$, with averages over $16\le t\le66$.

Table~\ref{tab:runs} lists the runs. All conserve energy to within $2.1\times10^{-3}$ of the injection and balance injection and dissipation to within 2\%. The free energy fluctuates more slowly as $\beta_i$ decreases: its integrated correlation time is about 0.8, 1.4 and $3.6\,\tau_A$ at $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1 (at $M=32$), so a window of $50\,\tau_A$ holds fewer independent samples, and at $\beta_i=0.1$ the four block means differ by up to 23\%, about the scatter expected from that correlation time. We quote the block spread as the uncertainty of the averages, about $\pm12\%$ on $W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.1$. In the run at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $M=32$ the free energy was still settling during the first $12\,\tau_A$ of the nominal window, which we therefore start at $t=15.5$. The spectra show no sign of numerical instability at small scales in any run.

\emph{The break enters the hierarchy.} At $\beta_i=0.1$ the predicted break, $m_*\approx6$--10 with the measured $T$, lies well inside the resolved range, and $W(m)$ falls below $m^{-1/2}$ from small $m$ (figure~\ref{fig:beta}a). The continuum formula (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) with $\kappa=k_1$ and the measured $T$ follows the measured spectrum to about 10\% up to $m\approx16$, where the hypercollisions take over at $M=32$; at $M=64$ the spectrum continues to fall steeply between $m\approx15$ and 40. At $\beta_i=0.3$ the spectrum bends at correspondingly larger $m$. To measure the break we fit $W(m)\sqrt{2m}\propto\exp(-\sqrt{2m}/\ell_*)$ over $2\le m\le M/2$, below the collisional range. The fitted $\ell_*$ is 16.8, 8.3 and 3.8 at $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1 ($M=32$), and 15.7, 7.2 and 3.6 at $M=64$, against $s\kappa_1T=16.4$, 8.0, 3.8, 16.8, 7.6 and 3.7 from the measured $T$ and the injected $\kappa_1$: the measured decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is the predicted one to within 7\% in every run. The break $m_*=\ell_*^2/2$ falls from about 140 at $\beta_i=1$ to about 7 at $\beta_i=0.1$, by a factor of about 20 for a factor of 10 in $\beta_i$, because $m_*\propto\beta_iT^2$ and $T$ itself decreases at low $\beta_i$ (below).

\emph{Comparison with the model at lower $\beta_i$.} At $\beta_i=1$ the measured $W(m)$ is within 7\% of the model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ for $1\le m\le M/2$, at both $M$. At lower $\beta_i$ it lies above the model at every $m$, by up to 21\% and 39\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1 at $M=32$ and by up to 20\% and 42\% at $M=64$, and the field-aligned flux lies above the model as well. Neither falls increasingly below the model at large $m$ in any run, so there is no sign of a return flux. The excess comes from the assumption of a uniform sink: $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ rises with $m$, from 0.33--0.43 at $m\le1$ in every run to 0.46, 0.53 and 0.62 at $m=16$ for $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1, so a single rate $1/T$, the median over $2\le m\le16$, removes too much free energy at small $m$. The rise is steeper at lower $\beta_i$, where free energy reaches a given $m$ later. Plotted against the time $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ since injection, the sinks of all runs fall on one curve (figure~\ref{fig:beta}b), with an rms scatter of 4.5\% about a joint fit $D_\perp/W=0.50+0.12\ln\vartheta$, against 12.6\% when plotted against $m$. The sink is about 0.4 for the youngest free energy and levels off at about 0.63 for ages beyond about $2.5\,\tau_A$. The perpendicular sink is therefore a property of the age of the free energy, the same at every $\beta_i$ at fixed $\chi$, as expected for a passive scalar whose variance reaches the dissipation scale only after the cascade has had time to act (section~\ref{tc:limits}). The median rate $1/T$ rises from 0.44 to 0.59 between $\beta_i=1$ and 0.1 for the same reason: at low $\beta_i$ the moments $2\le m\le16$ hold older free energy. To test this directly we let each injection evolve by the linear hierarchy at its field-aligned wavenumber, with the run's hypercollisions, and remove it at a rate $\gamma(\vartheta)$ that depends only on its age $\vartheta$, the time since injection, the same at every $m$; with $S(\vartheta)=\exp(-\int_0^\vartheta\gamma)$, the steady state is $W(m)=\eps\sum_\kappa P(\kappa)\int_0^\infty E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)\,S(\vartheta)\,\dd\vartheta$, with $E_m(\vartheta;\kappa)$ the free energy at moment $m$ of a unit injection at age $\vartheta$, and $D_\perp(m)$ is the same integral with $\gamma S$ in place of $S$ (for $\gamma=1/T$ this is the uniform-sink model). Fitting $\gamma(\vartheta)=\gamma_0+(\gamma_1-\gamma_0)(1-e^{-\vartheta/\vartheta_c})$ to $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.1$ ($M=64$) gives $\gamma_0=0.03$, $\gamma_1=0.67$ and $\vartheta_c=0.76\,\tau_A$, about five outer eddy times: fresh free energy is hardly removed, and the sink switches on as the cascade carries it to the dissipation scale. With this $\gamma(\vartheta)$ and no parameter fitted to them, the model gives $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 1 to within 5\% for $m\ge2$ and $m\ge4$, across a factor of ten in $\beta_i$ (figure~\ref{fig:age}). It places $W(m)$ 5--18\% above the measured values at small $m$, by about the same amount in all three runs, the fitted one included, so this offset does not come from the dependence on $\beta_i$ but from the injected $P(\kappa)$ or the treatment of the lowest moments.

\subsection{The break against \texorpdfstring{$\beta_i$}{beta} and \texorpdfstring{$\chi$}{chi}}\label{sim:mstar}

Of the runs in table~\ref{tab:runs}, only those at $\beta_i=0.1$ place the break well inside the collision-free range $m\le M/2$. To measure $m_*$ against $\beta_i$ and $\chi$ we made the runs of table~\ref{tab:mstar}, all on $64^3$ points with the forcing of section~\ref{sim:setup}, with $M$ large enough in each that the break lies below $M/2$, and with $\nu=30\,\beta_i^{1/2}(32/M)^{1/2}$ as in section~\ref{sim:beta}. At $\chi\approx0.95$ we ran $\beta_i=0.05$ and 0.3, and we use the run at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $M=64$ of table~\ref{tab:runs}. At $\beta_i=0.05$ we lowered $\chi$ to 0.75, 0.56 and 0.40 by lowering the Alfv\'enic injection rate $\eps_A$ from 0.3 to 0.1, 0.031 and 0.0035. As $\chi$ decreases the hyper-resistive dissipation of $E_A$ moves to larger scales unless $\eta$ decreases with it, so we lowered $\eta$ from 300 to 219 and 156, in proportion to $\chi$, and to 33 at $\chi=0.40$, where $\eta$ and $\eps_A$ were set by short runs at $M=2$. The median shell of the hyper-resistive dissipation of $E_A$ is then $k_\perp/k_1=9$--11 in every run. At $\beta_i=0.05$ the free energy fluctuates slowly, so these runs are averaged over 160--$300\,\tau_A$.

We measure the break from a fit of $W(m)\sqrt{2m}\propto\exp(-\sqrt{2m}/\ell_*)$, as in section~\ref{sim:beta}, but over a range set by each run's own decay: from $m=2$ to $\sqrt{2m}=2+1.5\,\ell_*$, found by iterating from the whole range $2\le m\le M/2$. Then $m_*=\ell_*^2/2$. The collision-free range holds 1.7 to 3.5 decay lengths in these runs (table~\ref{tab:mstar}), and at the top of the fit range the hypercollisional rate $2\nu_m$ is at most 13\% of the perpendicular rate $1/T$. The model value is $(s\kappa_1T)^2/2$, with $T$ the median rate over $2\le m\le16$ of each run and $\kappa_1$ from lines traced on its snapshots.

\emph{Dependence on $\beta_i$.} At $\chi\approx0.95$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\beta_i=0.05$ to 6.6 and 24.2 at $\beta_i=0.1$ and 0.3 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}a). A weighted fit gives $m_*\propto\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$, close to the $m_*\propto\beta_i$ of (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}). The model values follow $\beta_i^{1.31\pm0.04}$. They agree with the measured $m_*$ to within 12\% at $\beta_i\le0.1$ and lie 30\% above it at $\beta_i=0.3$, where $T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ is larger, 13.6 against 10.9 and 11.0, because the moments $2\le m\le16$ that set $T$ hold younger free energy at larger $\beta_i$ (section~\ref{sim:beta}).

\emph{Dependence on $\chi$.} At $\beta_i=0.05$ the break grows from $m_*=3.7$ at $\chi=0.96$ to 7.0, 14.9 and 60.5 at $\chi=0.75$, 0.56 and 0.40 (figure~\ref{fig:mstar}b). Between neighbouring runs the local exponent $-\dd\ln m_*/\dd\ln\chi$ is 2.5, 2.7 and 4.1. Down to $\chi\approx0.56$ the break therefore grows somewhat faster than the $\chi^{-2}$ of (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}), and below it the growth steepens towards $\chi^{-4}$; no single power law describes the four runs. The model value follows the measured $m_*$ to within 12\% for $\chi\ge0.56$, but at $\chi=0.40$ it is 167, 2.8 times the measured value.

Both departures come from the perpendicular sink. With the age $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa_1)$ and the rate $D_\perp/W$ both measured in units of the outer eddy time $\tau_{\rm nl}$, the sinks of the runs at $\chi\ge0.56$ agree to within about 10\% for $\vartheta\ge12\,\tau_{\rm nl}$: the rate switches on over the first 10--$15\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ and levels off at $0.087$--$0.094/\tau_{\rm nl}$, slightly lower at smaller $\chi$. The range $2\le m\le16$ that defines $T$ covers the same ages in units of $\tau_A$ in every run at $\beta_i=0.05$, but as $\chi$ decreases and $\tau_{\rm nl}$ grows it holds free energy that is younger in units of $\tau_{\rm nl}$, from 8.5--$24\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.96$ to 5--$14\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $\chi=0.56$. The sink there is weaker, so $T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ rises from 10.9 to 13.9 and $m_*$ grows faster than $\chi^{-2}$. At $\chi=0.40$ the moments $2\le m\le16$ hold free energy of age 3--$8\,\tau_{\rm nl}$, and the sink itself changes: it switches on as at larger $\chi$, reaches $0.069/\tau_{\rm nl}$ between $\vartheta=12$ and $16\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ and falls to $0.055/\tau_{\rm nl}$ at $30\,\tau_{\rm nl}$, where the runs at larger $\chi$ are at 0.087--$0.094/\tau_{\rm nl}$. The cascade then removes the free energy more slowly per outer eddy time, as expected once the outer-scale turbulence is weak and its cascade time exceeds $\tau_{\rm nl}$. Over $1\le m\le M/2$ the rate $D_\perp/W$ varies by a factor of 10 at $\chi=0.40$, against 1.4, 1.7 and 2.5 at $\chi=0.96$, 0.75 and 0.56, so the median over $2\le m\le16$ no longer represents the rate over the range $m\le171$ on which the break is measured, and $(s\kappa_1T)^2/2$ overestimates $m_*$. With the rate averaged over each run's fit range instead, uniformly in $\sqrt{2m}$ as the fit weights it, $s\kappa_1T$ agrees with the fitted $\ell_*$ to within 9\% at $\chi=0.40$ and to within 5\% in every other run of table~\ref{tab:mstar}. The decay beyond the break is therefore still set by phase mixing at $\kappa_1$ against the perpendicular sink, as in (\ref{eq:Wage}). What departs from (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}) at small $\chi$ is the assumption that the sink rate is a fixed multiple of $1/\tau_{\rm nl}$.

\subsection{Dependence on resolution}\label{sim:resolution}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{h1_resolution_m}

(a) $W(m)/\eps$. (b) $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$.}

Doubling $M$ leaves the spectra in the range both runs resolve nearly unchanged (figure~\ref{fig:resolution}). For $1\le m\le10$, $W(m)$ at $M=64$ is within 1.01--1.05 of its value at $M=32$ at $\beta_i=1$, 0.96--0.99 at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 1.00--1.01 at $\beta_i=0.1$, inside the block uncertainty. Between $m=12$ and 16 the runs at $M=32$ fall below those at $M=64$, by up to 7\% beyond that offset at $\beta_i=1$ and 5\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1, because there the hypercollisions of the $M=32$ runs begin to compete with the perpendicular sink: at $\beta_i=1$ the rate $2\nu(m/M)^6$ at which they remove free energy equals $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ near $m=14$ and is twice it at $m=16$. The rate $1/T$ changes by $-4$\%, 6\% and 2\% at $\beta_i=1$, 0.3 and 0.1. What changes is the share of the free energy that reaches the hypercollisions: it falls from 59\% to 50\% at $\beta_i=1$, from 43\% to 27\% at $\beta_i=0.3$ and from 20\% to 10\% at $\beta_i=0.1$ when the collisional range moves from $m>16$ to $m>32$. The model predicts this. Free energy injected at $\kappa$ reaches the collisional cutoff $m_c$ with probability $\exp(-\sqrt{2m_c}/(s\kappa T))$, which with $m_c=M/2$, the measured $T$ and $\kappa=\kappa_1$ gives 71\% and 63\% at $\beta_i=1$, 50\% and 36\% at $\beta_i=0.3$, and 23\% and 12\% at $\beta_i=0.1$. This single-$\kappa$ estimate reproduces the factor by which the share falls but overestimates its level by 15--35\%, because free energy injected at small $\kappa$ is removed before it reaches $m_c$. The model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ gives the shares themselves to within 0.06 at $M=32$ and 0.02 at $M=64$: 56\%, 38\% and 17\% at $M=32$, and 48\%, 26\% and 9\% at $M=64$. The collisional share of the dissipation is therefore not a converged quantity at $M=32$: at fixed $\beta_i$ and $\chi$, free energy that would be dissipated by collisions at a low cutoff is removed at small perpendicular scales when the cutoff is raised.

We repeated the runs at $M=64$ on $128^3$ grid points at $\beta_i=1$ and 0.3, and on $96^3$ and $256^3$ at $\beta_i=0.3$, with the hyper-resistivity normalised to the grid. The runs at $96^3$ and $128^3$ start from the $64^3$ Alfv\'enic spin-up, zero-padded in Fourier space to the finer grid, with $g=0$, last $66\,\tau_A$ and are averaged over $16\le t\le66$. The run at $256^3$ starts from the final state of the $128^3$ run at $\beta_i=0.3$, zero-padded with $g$ kept, lasts $60\,\tau_A$ and is averaged over $10\le t\le60$; the values of $1/T$ in its four blocks, 0.245, 0.271, 0.248 and 0.265, show no trend. Field lines are traced every $10\,\tau_A$, at $256^3$ with $\Delta z=L/256$ and a fifth band, $16\le k_\perp/k_1\le32$. The higher resolution lengthens the inertial range: the spectral slope of $E_A$ over the fitted shells is $-4.1$ at $64^3$, $-3.8$ at $96^3$, $-3.5$ at $128^3$ and $-2.9$ at $256^3$. The outer scale is unchanged, with $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.150$--0.158 and $\chi=0.92$--0.97 in all six runs. The perpendicular sink is not (figure~\ref{fig:resolution-n}). Between $64^3$ and $128^3$, $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ falls by 22--35\% at every $m\le32$ at $\beta_i=0.3$, most at $2\le m\le6$, and by 18--33\% at $\beta_i=1$, where at $128^3$ it is nearly independent of $m$, 0.26--0.31 for $m\le16$. Between $128^3$ and $256^3$ at $\beta_i=0.3$ it falls by a further 19--29\%, to 0.20--0.31 for $m\le16$. $T/\tau_{\rm nl}$ rises from 13.2 to 16.4, 18.4 and 24.6 at $\beta_i=0.3$ and from 15.4 to 22.6 at $\beta_i=1$, and the share of the free energy dissipated by hypercollisions rises from 27\% to 34\%, 38\% and 46\%, and from 50\% to 60\%. $W(m)$ rises with $T$: for $m\le16$ it is up to 23\% higher at $128^3$ and 43\% higher at $256^3$ than at $64^3$ at $\beta_i=0.3$, and up to 10\% higher at $128^3$ at $\beta_i=1$.

The model follows these changes. The fitted decay length is $\ell_*=9.0$ at $96^3$, 9.7 at $128^3$ and 13.1 at $256^3$ against $s\kappa_1T=9.4$, 11.0 and 15.3 at $\beta_i=0.3$, and $\ell_*=24.0$ against $s\kappa_1T=24.8$ at $\beta_i=1$ and $128^3$. The measured decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is therefore within 5\% of the model's, except at $\beta_i=0.3$ and $128^3$ and $256^3$, where it is 14\% and 17\% faster. The break moves out with $T$: at $\beta_i=0.3$ from $m_*\approx26$ at $64^3$ to 41, 47 and 86, and at $\beta_i=1$ from 124 to about 290. At $128^3$, $W(m)$ lies within $-4\%$ and $+13\%$ of the model at $\beta_i=0.3$ and within $-9\%$ and 0 at $\beta_i=1$, and the field-aligned flux 8--15\% and 3--6\% above it; at $256^3$, $W(m)$ lies within $-5\%$ and $+11\%$ of the model and the field-aligned flux 7--13\% above it. The model with the traced $P(\kappa)$ gives the collisional shares to within 0.02, and the per-$m$ budget closes to $5\times10^{-4}\,\eps$ or better in every run.

The field-aligned wavenumber of $g_0$ also changes. In the bands centred on $k_\perp/k_1=1.5$, 3, 6 and 12 it is 1.02, 1.27, 1.52 and $2.07\,k_1$ at $128^3$, against 0.98, 1.08, 1.37 and $2.04\,k_1$ at $64^3$, while $\Phi$ has 1.08, 1.66, 2.18 and $3.12\,k_1$. At $\beta_i=1$ the values for $g_0$ are 0.94, 1.10, 1.46 and $1.97\,k_1$ at $128^3$, against 0.88, 1.06, 1.35 and $2.00\,k_1$ at $64^3$, and $\Phi$ has 1.14, 1.60, 2.21 and $3.07\,k_1$. At $256^3$ and $\beta_i=0.3$, $g_0$ has 1.02, 1.19, 1.59, 2.26 and $3.21\,k_1$ in the bands centred on 1.5, 3, 6, 12 and $24\,k_1$, and $\Phi$ has 1.11, 1.64, 2.24, 3.26 and $4.72\,k_1$. With the longer inertial range, $g_0$ gains structure along the field gradually across it, roughly as $k_\perp^{1/3}$ at $128^3$ and $k_\perp^{0.4}$ at $256^3$ against about $k_\perp^{1/2}$ for $\Phi$, rather than only at the dissipation scale. Measured with $k_z$, its wavenumber in the band at $12\,k_1$ would be $3.68\,k_1$ at $128^3$ and $4.38\,k_1$ at $256^3$, so Eulerian measures still overstate the parallel structure of $g$. The unmixing part of the $k_z$-split flux grows with the resolution. At $\beta_i=0.3$ it is about $-0.13$ times the phase-mixing part at $64^3$, $-0.16$ to $-0.19$ at $96^3$, $-0.20$ to $-0.25$ at $128^3$ and $-0.27$ to $-0.35$ at $256^3$; at $\beta_i=1$ it is $-0.10$ to $-0.13$ at $64^3$ and $-0.17$ to $-0.22$ at $128^3$. The rms Eulerian wavenumber of $g_m$ grows from about $1.8\,k_1$ at $64^3$ to $2.6\,k_1$ at $128^3$ and 3.6--$4.8\,k_1$ at $256^3$, while its field-aligned wavenumber rises only from about $1.25\,k_1$ to $1.35\,k_1$. The tilt estimate of prediction (v) gives 0.16--0.21 at $96^3$, 0.22--0.29 at $128^3$ and 0.38--0.45 at $256^3$ (figure~\ref{fig:tilt}). It follows the growth of the measured ratio but exceeds it by up to 0.05 at $128^3$ and by 0.10--0.11 at $256^3$; the estimate assumes a single field-aligned wavenumber and a Gaussian tilt, and loses accuracy as the tilt grows. The backward flux of the split therefore grows with the tilt at the resolved scales and stays below the estimate for no return flux, while the field-aligned flux shows no deficit.

At the resolutions we reach, then, neither the rate of the perpendicular sink nor the field-aligned wavenumber of $g$ is independent of the dissipation scale, and prediction (iv) is not confirmed. Either the dissipation range at $64^3$ reached almost to the forcing scales and made the sink artificially fast, in which case $T$ should level off at higher resolution, or the perpendicular cascade of the slices has no dissipative anomaly, in which case $T$, and with it $m_*$, keep growing as the dissipation weakens. The values at $64^3$, $96^3$ and $128^3$, each with a standard error of 2--4\% from the spread of $1/T$ over four blocks, are fitted equally well by a $T$ that levels off and by one that does not. The first form, $T/\tau_{\rm nl}=27.3-14.2\,(N/64)^{-2/3}$, follows if each increase in resolution adds to $T$ a time proportional to the eddy time at the new dissipation scale, as in a cascade with a dissipative anomaly. The second, $T/\tau_{\rm nl}=13.2+7.6\ln(N/64)$, grows without bound. The two differ by 1\% at $96^3$ and 10\% at $256^3$, so we ran $256^3$ to separate them. With the uncertainties of the three values propagated through the fits, they predict $T/\tau_{\rm nl}=21.7\pm0.9$ and $23.8\pm1.2$ there. The run gives $24.6\pm0.6$, which is 2.7 combined standard deviations above the first form and 0.6 from the second. Fitted to all four values with their uncertainties, the form that levels off has $\chi^2=7.3$ for two degrees of freedom, and the logarithm $\chi^2=0.4$, with $T/\tau_{\rm nl}=13.0+8.3\ln(N/64)$ in an unweighted fit. $T$ grows by $5.3\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ from $64^3$ to $128^3$ and by $6.2\,\tau_{\rm nl}$ from $128^3$ to $256^3$, so up to $256^3$, $T$ grows by about the same amount with each doubling of the resolution, with no sign that it converges. This disfavours the levelling-off form fitted to the three lower resolutions, though it cannot exclude a $T$ that levels off at resolutions beyond those reached here. At $\beta_i=1$, $T$ grows by about the same factor between $64^3$ and $128^3$, 1.47 against 1.40 at $\beta_i=0.3$, so the trend is much the same at both $\beta_i$.

\subsection{Forcing the parallel flow}\label{sim:mf1}

\citet{Meyrand2019} forced the compressive fluctuations through $g_1$, the parallel flow, on modes with $|k_z|\le2k_1$. To see what this changes, we repeated the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=64$ with $g_1$ forced in place of $g_0$, on the same band of $k_\perp$ but on $|k_z|=k_1$ and $2k_1$, at the same rate. The Alfv\'enic turbulence is the same, with $\chi=0.95$ and $\tau_{\rm nl}=0.153$, and so is the perpendicular sink: $1/T=0.427$ against 0.421, with $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ rising from 0.34 at $m\le1$ to 0.45 at $m=16$, as with $g_0$ forced. The injected pattern has $\kappa_1=1.63\,k_1$ along the field lines, against $1.12\,k_1$.

The spectrum is steeper at small $m$, with a local slope of $-0.66$ over $2\le m\le8$ against $-0.54$, and the same at larger $m$, $-0.71$ over $8\le m\le32$ against $-0.72$; it is nowhere close to $m^{-1}$. The model with the traced $P(\kappa)$, injected at $m=1$, reproduces $W(m)$ to within 3.5\% for $0\le m\le32$, and the field-aligned flux lies 2--4\% above it for $m\ge1$, with no deficit at large $m$, so forcing $g_1$ on two parallel wavenumbers adds no return flux. At $m=16$ the field-aligned flux carries 75\% of the injected free energy, against 66\% with $g_0$ forced, and the hypercollisions remove 61\% of it, against 50\%, because the larger injected $\kappa$ carries the free energy to the collisional range sooner. The model gives 60\% and 48\%.

The $k_z$ split behaves differently. Its unmixing part is $-0.39$ times the phase-mixing part at the forced moment, $-0.17$ at $m=2$ and $-0.08$ to $-0.11$ for $4\le m\le16$, while the tilt estimate of prediction (v) gives 0.03--0.04 for $m\ge2$. The estimate assumes a single field-aligned wavenumber and a Gaussian tilt independent of it, and with this forcing it accounts for less than half of the measured ratio for $2\le m\le16$. Since the field-aligned flux follows the model, the excess of the split is not a return flux along the field.
