\section{Checks with GANDALF}\label{sec:numerics}

We checked the results of sections~\ref{sec:slices} to~\ref{sec:truncated} with the GANDALF code \citep{Kanekar2025}, % TODO(author): cite the GANDALF release used for the final runs.
which integrates (\ref{eq:rmhd}) with the truncated Hermite hierarchy of one branch (section~\ref{sec:model}). All computations are in double precision on a CPU, and the GANDALF runs use its fourth-order Lawson--Runge--Kutta time step \citep[Lawson-RK4;][]{Lawson1967}. The box is periodic, with side length 1 and $k_1=2\pi$. As section~\ref{sec:truncated} requires, the fastest slice sets the time step: $\Delta t$ is chosen with a safety factor of one half on the smaller of the Alfv\'enic limit and $2\sqrt2/\rho$, the stability limit of RK4 on the imaginary axis, with $\rho=k_{\perp,\max}\max_j|\nabla_\perp(\Phi+su_j\Psi)|$. The configuration files and scripts accompany the paper.

\subsection{Spectral density and phase velocities}\label{num:spectrum}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{spectrum}
  \caption{(a) The spectral density $\varrho(u)$ of (\ref{eq:varrho}) for $\Lambda=\sqrt5$, $-\sqrt5$ and $\infty$ (lines), and the Gauss weights $\lambda_j$ of GANDALF's ladder matrix at $M=16$ with the zero closure, divided by the node spacing $\Delta u_j=(u_{j+1}-u_{j-1})/2$, at the nodes $u_j$ (markers in the colour of each line). (b) The largest phase velocity $u_{\max}$ of GANDALF's ladder matrix at $\Lambda=\sqrt5$, minus $(2M+3)^{1/2}$, for $M=4$ to $1024$ with the zero closure (circles) and the copy closure (squares), and the three-term (solid) and two-term (dashed) asymptotic forms (\ref{eq:umax}).}
  \label{fig:spectrum}
\end{figure}

In figure~\ref{fig:spectrum}(a), $\int\varrho\,\dd u=1/p_0$ to round-off, and by (\ref{eq:gauss}) $\lambda_j/\Delta u_j$ approximates $\varrho(u_j)$; for GANDALF's ladder matrix at $M=16$ the two differ on $|u_j|<3$ by at most 8.2\%, 1.8\% and 0.47\% for $\Lambda=\sqrt5$, $-\sqrt5$ and $\infty$. In figure~\ref{fig:spectrum}(b) the three-term forms (\ref{eq:umax}) follow the largest eigenvalue of GANDALF's ladder matrix down to $M=4$ for both closures, and it does not depend on $\Lambda$ to round-off.

\subsection{Slices in a turbulent run}\label{num:snapshots}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{snapshots}
  \caption{A density blob in decaying Alfv\'enic turbulence, on the plane $z=0$ (coordinates in units of the box side $L=1$). GANDALF run on a $64^3$ grid with $M=16$ (zero closure), $\Lambda=\sqrt5$ and $\beta_i=1$; balanced Gaussian random Elsasser fields with $1\le k_\perp/k_1\le3$, $|k_z|/k_1\le2$, $\chi=\bar k_\perp z_{\rm rms}/\bar k_\parallel=1.00$, with energy-weighted mean wavenumbers $\bar k_\perp=2.45\,k_1$ and $\bar k_\parallel=1.17\,k_1$ and $z_{\rm rms}=0.478$ the rms of $|\nabla_\perp z^\pm|$; hyper-resistivity damps $z^\pm$ and every $g_m$ at the rate $2(k_\perp/k_{\perp,\max})^4$; no forcing or hypercollisions; $g(0)=f\,e_0$, with $f$ a Gaussian of standard deviation $1/8$ in $x$ and $y$ and $1/4$ in $z$; 148 steps to $t=1.5\,t_{\rm nl}$, where $t_{\rm nl}=1/(\bar k_\perp z_{\rm rms})=0.1357$ is the outer eddy time. (a, b) The density moment $g_0$ at $t=0$ and $1.5\,t_{\rm nl}$. (c--e) The slices $a_j/(p_0R_{0j})$ at $t=1.5\,t_{\rm nl}$ at the nodes $su_j=-1.0218$, $u_j=0$ and $su_j=+1.0218$; all three equal $f$ at $t=0$. Each group of panels has one symmetric colour scale.}
  \label{fig:snapshots}
\end{figure}

The run of figure~\ref{fig:snapshots} releases a density blob, $g(0)=f\,e_0$, into decaying Alfv\'enic turbulence with $\chi=1.00$. Its hyper-resistivity acts alike on $z^\pm$ and every $g_m$, so by (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}) it damps each slice separately. Since $a_j=\sum_mp_mR_{mj}g_m$, every slice starts as $p_0R_{0j}f$, and by (\ref{eq:truncslice}) each is then carried by its own stream function $\Phi+su_j\Psi$, which at $su_j=-1.0218$, $0$ and $+1.0218$ is mainly $z^-$, $\Phi$ alone and mainly $z^+$ (proposition~\ref{prop:elsasser}). These three slices start from the same pattern and end visibly different, and $g_0=\sum_jR_{0j}a_j$ mixes them.

At $t=0$, $0.5\,t_{\rm nl}$ and $1.5\,t_{\rm nl}$, GANDALF's $\partial_tg$ projected on the slices agrees with $-\{\Phi+su_j\Psi,a_j\}-su_j\partial_za_j$, computed with brackets that do not use GANDALF, to $5.2\times10^{-16}$ of the largest slice rate, while the bracket term is 0.56 to 2.2 times the streaming term; advecting every slice by $\Phi$ alone gives relative errors of 0.30 to 1.46, except at $u_j=0$. Over $1.5\,t_{\rm nl}$ each slice energy $\int a_j^2\,\dd^3r$ changes only by the hyper-resistive loss of that slice, to $1.2\times10^{-6}$ of its initial energy, so no free energy passes between slices.

\begin{figure}[t]

  \caption{(a) The Hermite spectrum $W(m)$ in units of its large-$m$ form $\int e\,\dd u/(\pi\sqrt{2m})$. Lines: $\delta$-correlated slices, with the closed form (\ref{eq:prefactor}) for $e\propto F_M$ and with (\ref{eq:dictionary}), evaluated by Gauss quadrature, for $e\propto\varrho$ at $\Lambda=\sqrt5$ and $-\sqrt5$. Markers: the steady state of one Fourier mode whose $g_0$ is driven by white noise, for $\Lambda=\infty$, $\sqrt5$ and $-\sqrt5$, from GANDALF's ladder matrix at $M=600$ with the outgoing closure $g_{M+1}=-\ii\,\mathrm{sgn}(k_z)\,g_M$ (the closure of \citealt[eq.~4.36]{Kanekar2015} with their $R_{M+1}=1$) and the Lyapunov equation for the steady covariance. (b) The local slope $\dd\ln W/\dd\ln m$ from (\ref{eq:dictionary}) for correlated slices with $e=F_M$ and $\hat c(\kappa)=(1+\kappa^2\ell^2)^{-p/2}$, where $\kappa$ is the velocity-space wavenumber, for $p=0.5$ to $2.5$ with $\ell=1$ (solid) and $\ell=0.15$ (dashed); the dotted lines are $-(p+1)/2$.}
  \label{fig:dictionary}
\end{figure}

By section~\ref{sec:decorrelation}, the forced linear steady state (markers in figure~\ref{fig:dictionary}a) is the spectrum of $\delta$-correlated slices with $e\propto\varrho$ (lines). At $\Lambda=\infty$ it differs from the closed form (\ref{eq:prefactor}) by at most $3.1\times10^{-4}$, and at $\Lambda=\sqrt5$ and $-\sqrt5$ from the spectrum with $e\propto\varrho$ by at most $1.5\times10^{-4}$ and $4.2\times10^{-4}$, the size of the closure error. At $m=1$ it is 4.7\% below $(1-1/\Lambda)$ times its value at $\Lambda=\infty$ for $\Lambda=\sqrt5$ and 5.2\% above it for $\Lambda=-\sqrt5$, so this proportionality holds only at large $m$. In figure~\ref{fig:dictionary}(b) the local slope is within $3\times10^{-5}$ of $-(p+1)/2$ at $m=10^4$ for $\ell=1$; for $\ell=0.15$ it reaches these values at larger $m$, where $\sqrt{2m}\,\ell\gg1$.

\subsection{Frozen field lines}\label{num:frozen}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{frozen}
  \caption{Test of the frozen-line theorem. Ideal GANDALF runs (no dissipation, forcing or collisions) from one initial state at $N^3$ grid points, with $M=8$ (zero closure), $\Lambda=\sqrt5$, $\beta_i=1$, $\mathrm{rms}\,|\nabla_\perp\Phi|=\mathrm{rms}\,|\nabla_\perp\Psi|=1$, random $g_0$ and $g_1$, $\chi=\bar k_\perp z_{\rm rms}/\bar k_\parallel=2.21$ as defined in figure~\ref{fig:snapshots} (1.56 with the rms of $|\uE|$), the term $\{\Phi,g_0\}$ 2.91 times the streaming term $s\partial_z(Ag)_0$ in rms, and $\Delta t=0.0005$. (a) The Hermite spectrum $W(m)$ at $t=0.125$ for $N=64$: initial state (crosses), GANDALF (circles), frozen-line prediction (solid line) and $k_z$-only prediction (dashed line). (b) The relative difference between GANDALF and the frozen-line prediction at $t=0.125$ against $N$, for the single-point statistics $W(m)$, $C_{jl}$, $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ with $\gradpar$ and $\langle g_m^4\rangle$ (filled markers), and for $W(k_\perp)$, $W(|k_z|)$ and $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ with $\partial_z$ (open markers).}
  \label{fig:frozen}
\end{figure}

The runs of figure~\ref{fig:frozen} integrate one initial state, drawn on an $8^3$ grid and embedded, at $N=16$, 20, 32, 40 and 64, to $t=0.25$, about three eddy times. At $t=0.25$ the free energy and the Alfv\'enic energy drift by at most $2.6\times10^{-8}$ and $4.3\times10^{-5}$. We compare each run with the frozen-line prediction $h(t)=e^{-tL_0}g(0)$, computed with GANDALF's right-hand side for $g$ with $\Phi=0$ and $\Psi=\Psi(0)$ held fixed, and with the $k_z$-only prediction ($\Phi=\Psi=0$). By corollary~\ref{cor:singlepoint}, $W(m)$, $C_{jl}=\langle a_ja_l\rangle$, the flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) with $\gradpar(t)$ in the run and $\gradpar(0)$ in the prediction, and $\langle g_m^4\rangle$ match the frozen-line prediction up to the Fourier truncation error, which vanishes as $N$ grows at fixed $t$; $W(k_\perp)$, $W(|k_z|)$ and the flux with $\partial_z$ are not covered.

At $t=0.125$ the relative difference for the covered statistics falls by two to three orders of magnitude from $N=16$ to 64, to between $1.5\times10^{-5}$ for $W(m)$ and $1.4\times10^{-4}$ for the flux, while for $W(k_\perp)$, $W(|k_z|)$ and the flux with $\partial_z$ it stays between 0.13 and 0.38 at every $N$ (figure~\ref{fig:frozen}b). The $k_z$-only prediction misses $W(m)$ by 15\% and the field-aligned flux by 37\%. At $N=64$ halving $\Delta t$ changes the relative differences by at most 2.1\%. At every $N$ the difference grows from $t=0.062$ to $0.125$, as the Fourier truncation error is not uniform in $t$, which restricts such tests to times at which the Alfv\'enic fields stay resolved.

Two further statements of section~\ref{sec:lagrangian} were tested in the same way. In ideal turbulence forced through $\Phi$, the response of $W(m)$ to each white injection into $g$ converges with $N$ to phase mixing along the field lines frozen at its injection time, and the average over the signs of the injections is the sum of these responses, as corollary~\ref{cor:forcing} states, although single realisations contain cross terms. With one term added, the departure from the frozen-line prediction still falls with $N$ for viscosity or forcing of $\Phi$, but not for resistivity or forcing of $\Psi$ or for damping of $g$ in $k_\perp$ or $k_z$.

\subsection{Further checks and the scripts}\label{num:rhs}

The other checks cited in sections~\ref{sec:model} to~\ref{sec:truncated} use GANDALF's right-hand side. For random states with $M=2$ to 65, $\Lambda=\pm\sqrt5$ and 3, $\beta_i$ from 0.37 to 1.7 and both closures, its $\partial_tg$ satisfies (\ref{eq:truncslice}) to $2\times10^{-13}$, with brackets several times the streaming term, and fails at order one when the sign of $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ is reversed. Slices at $su_j=-1$ obey proposition~\ref{prop:elsasser} to $5.5\times10^{-16}$ and $1.6\times10^{-15}$, and translate rigidly when $z^-=0$, to $1.4\times10^{-14}$. The two-slice law (\ref{eq:twoslice}) holds to $5\times10^{-15}$ and the two forms of (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) agree to $5\times10^{-14}$. The forms $Q^{(M)}_\varphi$ are conserved to relative rates of $3.2\times10^{-17}$, whereas $\int a_j^4\,\dd^3r$ changes at relative rates of $4.9\times10^{-4}$ and more, so only the quadratic invariants of section~\ref{sec:invariants} survive Fourier truncation.

The self-checking scripts accompanying this paper are in the folders \texttt{continuum/}, \texttt{truncated/} and \texttt{dynamics/} of \texttt{derivations/}, and \texttt{derivations/review/} holds independent re-derivations that share no code with them. Each script prints PASS or FAIL for every check, and the figures are produced by the scripts in \texttt{scripts/} and \texttt{analysis/} from the configuration files in \texttt{configs/}.
