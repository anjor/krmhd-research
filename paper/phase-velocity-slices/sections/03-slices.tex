\section{Phase-velocity slices}\label{sec:slices}

This section diagonalises the operator $L$ of one branch and shows that the diagonalising transform commutes with the nonlinear terms of the kinetic equation. We consider the branch equation (\ref{eq:branch}),
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+s\,\gradpar(LG)=0,\qquad
  LG=uG-\frac{uF_M}{\Lambda}\,M_0[G],
\end{equation*}
in which $G(\boldsymbol r,u,t)$ is $G^+$ or $G^-$, with $\Lambda=\Lambda^+$ or $\Lambda^-$ from (\ref{eq:Lambdapm}), or a Laguerre coefficient $G_l$ with $l\ge2$, for which $\Lambda=\infty$. The Alfv\'enic fields $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are treated as given functions of $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$, and nothing in this section uses the RMHD equations (\ref{eq:rmhd}). Three properties of (\ref{eq:branch}) are used: $L$ acts on $u$ alone, its coefficient $1/\Lambda$ is the same at every point and on every scale, and the brackets $\{\Phi,\cdot\}$ and $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ act in the same way at every $u$.

\subsection{The free-energy product and the factorisation \texorpdfstring{$L=UP$}{L = UP}}\label{sl:product}

Let $\mathcal W$ be the space of real functions $G(u)$ with $\ip{G}{G}<\infty$, where
\begin{equation*}
  \ip{f}{g}=\int\frac{f\,g}{F_M}\,\dd u .
\end{equation*}
Since $M_0[g]=\ip{F_M}{g}$ and $\ip{F_M}{F_M}=1$, the operator $\mathsf Q=F_M\,M_0$ is the orthogonal projector onto $F_M$ in this inner product. The free-energy product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fe}
  \fe{f}{g}=\ip{f}{g}-\frac{M_0[f]\,M_0[g]}{\Lambda}=\ip{f}{Pg},\qquad
  P=I-\frac{\mathsf Q}{\Lambda}=I-\frac{F_M\,M_0}{\Lambda} .
\end{equation}
For a branch, $\tfrac12 n_iT_i\fe{G^\pm}{G^\pm}$ averaged over the volume is the free energy $W^\pm$ whose conservation S09 established, written in the form of CMM's eq.~(B13); section~\ref{sec:invariants} returns to this identification. Writing $G=M_0[G]\,F_M+G_\perp$ with $M_0[G_\perp]=0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{sl:split}
  \fe{G}{G}=\ip{G_\perp}{G_\perp}+p_0\,M_0[G]^2,\qquad p_0=1-\frac{1}{\Lambda} .
\end{equation}
By (\ref{sl:split}) the product is positive definite if and only if $p_0>0$, that is, for $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, and then
\begin{equation*}
  \min(1,p_0)\,\ip{G}{G}\le\fe{G}{G}\le\max(1,p_0)\,\ip{G}{G},
\end{equation*}
so that $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$ is a Hilbert space whose norm is equivalent to that of $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$. Since $\Lambda^+>1$ and $\Lambda^-<0$ for all $\beta_i$ and $\tau/Z$, and $p_0=1$ for $\Lambda=\infty$, this covers every compressive sector of KRMHD; S09 (\S6.2.3) noted that $W^\pm$ is positive definite for this reason. At $\Lambda=1$ the product is degenerate, with null vector $F_M$. For $0<\Lambda<1$ it has exactly one negative direction, since $\fe{F_M}{F_M}=p_0<0$ while $\fe{G}{G}>0$ for every non-zero $G$ with $M_0[G]=0$.

Let $U$ be multiplication by $u$, $(UG)(u)=uG(u)$, with domain $D(U)=\{G\in\mathcal W:\ uG\in\mathcal W\}$; it is self-adjoint in $(\mathcal W,\ip{\cdot}{\cdot})$. Since $UPG=uG-uF_M\,M_0[G]/\Lambda$, the branch operator factorises as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LUP}
  L=UP .
\end{equation}
Because $P$ and $U$ are symmetric in $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$, (\ref{eq:fe}) and (\ref{eq:LUP}) give
\begin{equation*}
  \fe{f}{Lg}=\ip{Pf}{UPg}
  =\int\frac{u\,f\,g}{F_M}\,\dd u-\frac{M_0[f]\,M_1[g]+M_1[f]\,M_0[g]}{\Lambda},
\end{equation*}
where the last step uses $M_1[F_M]=0$. The right-hand side is symmetric in $f$ and $g$, so $L$ is symmetric in the free-energy product. It is also self-adjoint on $D(U)$. The map $G\mapsto P^{1/2}G$ is unitary from $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$ onto $(\mathcal W,\ip{\cdot}{\cdot})$ and carries $L$ into $P^{1/2}UP^{1/2}$, which differs from $U$ by a bounded symmetric operator of rank two (appendix~\ref{app:spectral}). Since $P$ is invertible for $\Lambda\neq1$, (\ref{eq:LUP}) also gives
\begin{equation}\label{sl:PLinv}
  PL^{-1}=U^{-1},\qquad\text{that is,}\qquad
  \fe{f}{L^{-1}g}=\int\frac{f\,g}{u\,F_M}\,\dd u\quad\text{for } g/u\in\mathcal W .
\end{equation}
The form (\ref{sl:PLinv}) contains no $\Lambda$; section~\ref{sec:invariants} uses it for CMM's phase-space helicity.

In the Hermite representation of section~\ref{sec:model}, let $e_m=H_m(u)F_M(u)/\sqrt{2^mm!}$, so that $G=\sum_mg_me_m$. The $e_m$ are orthonormal in $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$, the matrix of $P$ is the weight matrix $\Pw=\mathrm{diag}(p_m)$, and the matrix of $U$ is the Hermite Jacobi matrix $J$, with zero diagonal and $J_{m,m+1}=J_{m+1,m}=\sqrt{(m+1)/2}$. The product (\ref{eq:fe}) becomes $\fe{F}{G}=\sum_mp_mf_mg_m$, and the factorisation (\ref{eq:LUP}) becomes $A=J\Pw$ for the ladder matrix of section~\ref{sec:model}, which is why $\Pw A=\Pw J\Pw$ is symmetric.

\subsection{Resolvent and dielectric function}\label{sl:resolventsec}

For $\mathrm{Im}\,z\neq0$, the equation $(z-L)X=G$ gives $X=[G-uF_M\,M_0[X]/\Lambda]/(z-u)$, and taking $M_0$ of both sides determines $M_0[X]$:
\begin{equation}\label{sl:resolvent}
  (z-L)^{-1}G=\frac{1}{z-u}\left[G-\frac{uF_M}{\Lambda\,\eps(z)}\int\frac{G(v)}{z-v}\,\dd v\right],
\end{equation}
with the dielectric function
\begin{equation}\label{eq:eps}
  \eps(\zeta)=1-\frac{1}{\Lambda}\int\frac{vF_M(v)}{v-\zeta}\,\dd v=1-\frac{1+\zeta Z(\zeta)}{\Lambda},
  \qquad Z(\zeta)=\int\frac{F_M(x)}{x-\zeta}\,\dd x\quad(\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta>0).
\end{equation}
Here $Z$ is the plasma dispersion function of \citet{FriedConte1961}, and $\eps=0$ is S09's dispersion relation (185), $\zeta Z(\zeta)=\Lambda-1$. For $\mathrm{Im}\,\zeta<0$ the integral in (\ref{eq:eps}) equals $\overline{\eps(\bar\zeta)}$, and this is the function that enters (\ref{sl:resolvent}) in the lower half plane. The analytic continuation of (\ref{eq:eps}) across the real axis is a different function, whose zeros are the Landau roots. On the real axis the Plemelj formula gives
\begin{equation}\label{sl:epsRI}
  \eps(u+\ii0)=\eps_R(u)+\ii\,\eps_I(u),\qquad
  \eps_R=1-\frac{1+u\,\mathrm{Re}\,Z(u)}{\Lambda},\qquad
  \eps_I=-\frac{\pi\,u\,F_M(u)}{\Lambda},
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{Re}\,Z(u)=\PVint F_M(x)\,(x-u)^{-1}\,\dd x$ is minus twice the Dawson function. With the Hilbert transform $\mathsf H[f](u)=\pi^{-1}\PVint f(v)\,(v-u)^{-1}\,\dd v$, the real and imaginary parts are related by $\eps_R-1=\mathsf H[\eps_I]$.

By (\ref{sl:resolvent}), the resolvent exists wherever $\eps\neq0$. Appendix~\ref{app:spectral} shows that the non-real eigenvalues of $L$ are the zeros of $\eps$, and that a real eigenvalue can occur only at $u=0$ and only for $\Lambda=1$. The zeros of $\eps$ in the upper half plane are counted by the argument of \citet{Penrose1960}. As $u$ runs over the real line, the point $1+uZ(u+\ii0)$ starts and ends at the origin, its imaginary part $\pi uF_M(u)$ has the sign of $u$, and it crosses the real axis only at the point $1$, which it reaches at $u=0$. The curve therefore winds once around every point of $(0,1)$ and around no real point outside $[0,1]$. Hence $\eps$ has no zeros in the closed upper half plane for $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, and exactly one, on the imaginary axis, for $0<\Lambda<1$. In the latter case $L$ has the eigenvalues $\pm\ii\gamma$, where $\gamma>0$ solves $1-\sqrt\pi\,\gamma\,e^{\gamma^2}\mathrm{erfc}(\gamma)=\Lambda$, and the slice decomposition below is incomplete (appendix~\ref{app:spectral}). At $\Lambda=1$, $\eps(0+\ii0)=0$ and $F_M$ is an eigenfunction with eigenvalue zero embedded in the continuous spectrum. The branch $\Lambda^+$ approaches this case at high beta. For $\Lambda=1+\delta$ the least-damped zero of the analytic continuation of $\eps$ is $\zeta=-\ii\delta/\sqrt\pi\,[1+O(\delta)]$, and with $\Lambda^+-1\simeq\beta_i^{-1}$ for $\beta_i\gg1$ the corresponding damping rate, $|k_\parallel|v_A/\sqrt{\pi\beta_i}$, is the Barnes damping rate \citep{Barnes1966} in the form of S09's eq.~(190).

The spectral density of the branch is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:varrho}
  \varrho(u)=\frac{F_M(u)}{|\eps(u+\ii0)|^2} .
\end{equation}
For $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$ it is positive, smooth and even, with
\begin{equation*}
  \int\varrho\,\dd u=\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda-1}=\frac{1}{p_0},\qquad
  \varrho(0)=\frac{F_M(0)}{p_0^2},\qquad
  \frac{\varrho(u)}{F_M(u)}\to1\quad(|u|\to\infty),
\end{equation*}
and $\varrho=F_M$ for $\Lambda=\infty$. At $\Lambda=1$ it behaves as $\pi^{-3/2}u^{-2}$ near $u=0$ and is not integrable; this is the trace of the embedded eigenvalue. Figure~\ref{fig:spectrum} shows $\varrho$ for $\Lambda=\pm\sqrt5$.

\subsection{Case--Van Kampen modes and the slice transform}\label{sl:transformsec}

For real $u$, the equation $(L-u)F=0$ has the distributional solutions
\begin{equation}\label{sl:Fu}
  F_u(v)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\mathrm P\frac{vF_M(v)}{v-u}+\eps_R(u)\,\delta(v-u),\qquad
  M_0[F_u]=1,\qquad M_1[F_u]=u,
\end{equation}
which are the Case--Van Kampen modes of the branch \citep{vanKampen1955,Case1959}. The normalisation $M_0[F_u]=1$ fixes the weight of the $\delta$-function. Then $(v-u)F_u=vF_M/\Lambda$, and $(L-u)F_u=(v-u)F_u-vF_M\,M_0[F_u]/\Lambda=0$. Each mode consists of particles at $v=u$ together with a principal-value part, which is the response of the Maxwellian to the moment $M_0$ that those particles carry. For $\Lambda=\infty$ the principal-value part vanishes and $F_u=\delta(v-u)$.

The slice transform is the projection on these modes in the free-energy product,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transform}
  \Tr[G](u)=\fe{F_u}{G}
  =\frac{\eps_R(u)\,G(u)}{F_M(u)}+\frac{u}{\Lambda}\,\PVint\frac{G(v)}{v-u}\,\dd v
  =\frac{\eps_R\,G-\eps_I\,\mathsf H[G]}{F_M}(u).
\end{equation}
Since $LF_u=uF_u$ and $L$ is symmetric,
\begin{equation}\label{sl:TL}
  \Tr[LG](u)=u\,\Tr[G](u),\qquad \Tr[F_M](u)=1-\frac{1}{\Lambda}\quad\text{for every }u ;
\end{equation}
appendix~\ref{app:spectral} verifies the first identity directly from (\ref{eq:transform}). The unit-weight slice amplitude is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aunit}
  a_u=\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;\Tr[G](u)=\frac{\eps_R\,G-\eps_I\,\mathsf H[G]}{\sqrt{F_M}\;|\eps|}(u),
\end{equation}
with $\varrho$ from (\ref{eq:varrho}), and we write $\mathcal A$ for the linear map $G\mapsto a$. For $\Lambda=\infty$, $a_u=G(u)/\sqrt{F_M(u)}$: the Laguerre coefficients $G_l$ with $l\ge2$ are already slices.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:spectrum}
Let $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, the value $\Lambda=\infty$ included. Then:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $L$, with domain $D(U)$, is self-adjoint in $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$;
\item[(ii)] $L$ has no eigenvalues, and its spectrum is the real line, purely absolutely continuous and of multiplicity one;
\item[(iii)] $\mathcal A$ is a unitary map from $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$ onto $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$, and $\mathcal ALG=u\,\mathcal AG$ for $G\in D(U)$.
\end{itemize}
In particular, for every bounded Borel function $\varphi$,
\begin{equation}\label{sl:parseval}
  \fe{G}{G}=\int a_u^2\,\dd u,\qquad \fe{G}{\varphi(L)G}=\int\varphi(u)\,a_u^2\,\dd u,
\end{equation}
and $G$ is recovered from its slices by
\begin{equation}\label{sl:inverse}
  G=\int\varrho(u)\,\Tr[G](u)\,F_u\,\dd u=\eps_R\,c+\eps_I\,\mathsf H[c],\qquad c=\varrho\,\Tr[G]=\sqrt\varrho\;a .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:spectral} proves theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}. Part~(i) follows from the rank-two form of $P^{1/2}LP^{-1/2}$. A perturbation of rank two is of trace class, so by the Kato--Rosenblum theorem \citep[][chapter~X]{Kato1966} the absolutely continuous part of $L$ is unitarily equivalent to $U$, whose spectrum is the real line with multiplicity one. Because $\eps$ has no zeros in the closed upper half plane, $1/\eps$ is continuous up to the real axis, and the resolvent form $\fe{G}{(z-L)^{-1}G}$ has continuous boundary values on the real axis for a dense set of $G$. This excludes eigenvalues and singular continuous spectrum, which gives part~(ii). Stone's formula then gives the spectral density of $L$ in the state $G$ as $\varrho\,\Tr[G]^2=a_u^2$, which is (\ref{sl:parseval}), and the inversion (\ref{sl:inverse}), which requires only that $\eps$ have no zeros in the upper half plane, shows that $\mathcal A$ is onto. For complex $G$, such as a Fourier coefficient, $a_u^2$ in (\ref{sl:parseval}) is replaced by $|a_u|^2$.

The modes (\ref{sl:Fu}) are those of \citet{vanKampen1955} and \citet{Case1959}. The inverse (\ref{sl:inverse}) is the G-transform $\mathcal G[c]=\eps_Rc+\eps_I\mathsf H[c]$ of \citet{MorrisonPfirsch1992}, \citet{Morrison1994} and \citet{Morrison2000}, and \citet{Morrison2000} proved for the linearised Vlasov--Poisson system at fixed wavenumber that it is invertible when the Penrose condition holds. \citet{Heninger2018} applied it to electrostatic drift kinetics (remark~\ref{sl:remlit}). Theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} fixes the weights: $\mathcal G$ is unitary from $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u/\varrho)$ onto $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$, with inverse $G\mapsto\varrho\,\Tr[G]$, and the corresponding map onto $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$ is $\mathcal A=\varrho^{-1/2}\mathcal G^{-1}$. In terms of the modes, part~(iii) contains the completeness relation $G=\int\varrho\,\Tr[G]\,F_u\,\dd u$ and the orthogonality relation $\fe{F_u}{F_{u'}}=\delta(u-u')/\varrho(u)$. By (\ref{sl:parseval}), the slices divide the free energy of a branch among phase velocities, with no cross terms between different $u$.

\subsection{The slice theorem}\label{sl:slicesec}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:slice}
Assume that
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, the value $\Lambda=\infty$ included;
\item[(ii)] $\Phi(\boldsymbol r,t)$ and $\Psi(\boldsymbol r,t)$ are real functions on $\mathbb T^3\times[0,T]$, where $\mathbb T^3$ is a periodic box, and their gradients are continuous and Lipschitz in $\boldsymbol r$, uniformly in $t$; otherwise $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are arbitrary, and in particular they need not satisfy (\ref{eq:rmhd}): they may obey resistive or forced RMHD, or be prescribed;
\item[(iii)] $G$ and $UG$ are continuously differentiable functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ with values in $\mathcal W$.
\end{itemize}
Then $G$ solves (\ref{eq:branch}) if and only if, for almost every $u\in\mathbb R$, the slice amplitude $a_u(\boldsymbol r,t)$ of (\ref{eq:aunit}) solves
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice}
  \partial_ta_u+\{\Phi+su\Psi,\,a_u\}+su\,\partial_za_u=0,
\end{equation}
that is, the advection equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Uu}
  \partial_ta_u+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u=0,\qquad
  \boldsymbol U_u=\zhat\times\nabla_\perp(\Phi+su\Psi)+su\,\zhat=\uE+su\,\bhat,\qquad
  \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol U_u=0 .
\end{equation}
The same holds with $a_u$ replaced by $\Tr[G](u)$. By (ii) the velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$ is Lipschitz in $\boldsymbol r$, so that each slice is transported along the characteristics of $\boldsymbol U_u$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}(iii), $\mathcal A$ is a bounded, injective linear map that acts on $u$ only, through a kernel that does not depend on $\boldsymbol r$ or $t$. It therefore commutes with $\partial_t$, $\partial_x$, $\partial_y$ and $\partial_z$ applied to continuously differentiable $\mathcal W$-valued functions, and with multiplication by functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$; hence it commutes with $\{\Phi,\cdot\}$, with $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ and with $\gradpar=\partial_z+\{\Psi,\cdot\}$. By (iii), $LG=UG-uF_M\,M_0[G]/\Lambda$ is continuously differentiable, and $\mathcal ALG=u\,\mathcal AG$ then gives
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal A\big[\partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+s\,\gradpar(LG)\big]
  =\partial_ta+\{\Phi,a\}+s\,u\,\gradpar a,
\end{equation*}
with $a=\mathcal AG$ and $u$ acting by multiplication. Since $u\,\gradpar a_u=u\,\partial_za_u+\{u\Psi,a_u\}$, the right-hand side is the left-hand side of (\ref{eq:slice}). Because $\mathcal A$ is injective, the expression in square brackets vanishes if and only if the right-hand side vanishes in $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$, that is, for almost every $u$. The identity $\{\chi,a\}=(\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\chi)\cdot\nabla a$ gives (\ref{eq:Uu}), and $\nabla\cdot(\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\chi)=0$ gives $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol U_u=0$. Since $\varrho(u)$ is positive and finite, dividing (\ref{eq:slice}) by $\sqrt{\varrho(u)}$ gives the statement for $\Tr[G](u)$.
\end{proof}

Each slice is a passive scalar carried by the divergence-free velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$, and slices with different $u$ do not interact. The statement is three-dimensional and includes the part $s\{\Psi,LG\}$ of the streaming that follows the perturbed field lines. The velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$ is that of the guiding centre of an ion that drifts with $\uE$ and streams along the perturbed field line $\bhat=\zhat+\boldsymbol b_\perp$ at the constant parallel velocity $v_\parallel=u\vth$, which is $su$ in units of $v_A$. The parallel electric field and the mirror force, which couple the ions to $\delta n$ and $\delta B$ and enter (\ref{eq:branch}) through the Landau coupling $uF_M\,M_0[G]/\Lambda$, do not appear in (\ref{eq:slice}). Their effect is contained in the relation (\ref{eq:aunit}) between $a_u$ and $G$: the slice at $u$ is the projection of $G$ on the mode $F_u$, which consists of the particles at $v=u$ together with the response (\ref{sl:Fu}) of the Maxwellian to them. The distribution $G(\boldsymbol r,u,t)$ at fixed $u$ is not a passive scalar, since the Landau coupling links it to all other velocities; the dressed combination $a_u$ is. In a straight field the slice streams at $su$, the parallel phase velocity of the mode $F_u$, and we call $u$ the phase velocity of the slice.

The proof uses two properties of the branch equation. The Landau coupling is linear in $G$, local in $\boldsymbol r$, and has the coefficient $1/\Lambda$, which does not depend on the wavenumber, so a single transform diagonalises $L$ at every wavenumber; and the brackets act in the same way at every $u$. Neither property holds for the Vlasov--Poisson system, in which the analogue of $\Lambda$ is $-(k\lambda_D)^2$ (section~\ref{sec:model}) and the nonlinear term acts on velocity. That the slices are also passive is a further property of KRMHD: $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ obey (\ref{eq:rmhd}), which does not involve $G$. When the advecting fields depend on the distribution, as the electrostatic potential does in the drift-kinetic system of \citet{Heninger2018}, the transformed components obey equations of the form (\ref{eq:slice}) but remain coupled through those fields.

The slice at $u=0$ is advected by $\uE$ alone. By (\ref{eq:transform}), $\Tr[G](0)=p_0\,G(0)/F_M(0)$, so this slice is the distribution at $v_\parallel=0$ itself, which obeys $\partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}=0$ there because $(LG)(0)=0$. Section~\ref{sec:invariants} relates it to CMM's equation for the particles with $v_\parallel=0$ in gyrokinetics.

In terms of the Elsasser potentials $z^\pm=\Phi\pm\Psi$, the stream function of the slice at $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elsasserpot}
  \Phi+su\Psi=\tfrac12\big[(1+su)\,z^++(1-su)\,z^-\big] .
\end{equation}
By (\ref{eq:elsasserpot}), the slices with $su=\pm1$, that is, with $v_\parallel=\pm v_A$, are advected by a single Elsasser field (proposition~\ref{prop:elsasser}).

The two branches and the sector $\Lambda=\infty$ have different transforms, because their values of $\Lambda$ differ, but the velocity (\ref{eq:Uu}) depends only on $u$. Applying theorem~\ref{thm:slice} to each of them, with $a^\pm_u$ the slices of $G^\pm$ and $a^{(l)}_u=G_l(u)/\sqrt{F_M(u)}$ for $l\ge2$, gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sameflow}
  \big(\partial_t+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla\big)\big(a^+_u,\,a^-_u,\,a^{(2)}_u,\,a^{(3)}_u,\dots\big)=0 .
\end{equation}
By (\ref{eq:sameflow}), the compressive sector of KRMHD is a continuum of passive scalars labelled by phase velocity, and at each $u$ the slices of all sectors are carried by the same flow. Section~\ref{sec:truncated} shows that the truncated Hermite hierarchy integrated by GANDALF decomposes in the same way, with the slices at the eigenvalues of the truncated ladder matrix. We checked that decomposition to round-off against GANDALF's right-hand side for $\Lambda=\pm\sqrt5$ and $\beta_i=0.3$, $0.5$ and $1$, with brackets evaluated independently of GANDALF and up to several times larger than the streaming term; reversing the sign of $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ in the test gives errors of order one (section~\ref{sec:numerics}).

\subsection{Dissipation, collisions and forcing}\label{sl:forcingsec}

Theorem~\ref{thm:slice} concerns the collisionless and unforced branch equation. Consider instead
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+s\,\gradpar(LG)=\mathcal BG+\mathcal CG+S,
\end{equation*}
where $\mathcal B$ is a linear operator that acts on $\boldsymbol r$ alone and in the same way at every $u$, $\mathcal C$ is a linear operator that acts on $u$ alone and in the same way at every $\boldsymbol r$, and $S(\boldsymbol r,u,t)$ is a source. Under the hypotheses of theorem~\ref{thm:slice}, and with $\mathcal BG$, $\mathcal CG$ and $S$ continuous functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ with values in $\mathcal W$, the argument of its proof gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sliceforcing}
  \partial_ta_u+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u=\mathcal B\,a_u+\int\hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')\,a_{u'}\,\dd u'+\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;\Tr[S](u),
  \qquad
  \hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')=\sqrt{\varrho(u)\,\varrho(u')}\;\fe{F_u}{\mathcal CF_{u'}} .
\end{equation}

An operator $\mathcal B$ commutes with $\mathcal A$ and appears unchanged in every slice equation. Damping at a rate that depends only on $k_\perp$ or $k_z$, applied with the same coefficient to every Hermite moment, is of this kind; GANDALF applies its hyper-resistivity and its parallel damping in this way \citep{Kanekar2025}. Such terms damp each slice and do not couple slices. We checked (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}) to round-off with a damping term proportional to $k_\perp^4$.

An operator $\mathcal C$ that depends on velocity couples the slices through the kernel $\hat{\mathcal C}$. The kernel is explicit for an operator that is diagonal in the Hermite basis, $\mathcal Ce_m=-\nu_me_m$. Examples are the Lenard--Bernstein operator in $v_\parallel$, $\mathcal CG=\nu\,\partial_u(uG+\tfrac12\partial_uG)$, for which $\nu_m=\nu m$, and GANDALF's hypercollisions, for which $\nu_m=\nu(m/M)^n$ for $m\ge2$ and $\nu_0=\nu_1=0$. Let $r_m(u)$ be the $m$th Hermite coefficient of the mode $F_u$. It is a polynomial of degree $m$ (section~\ref{sec:truncated}), and by (\ref{sl:Fu})
\begin{equation*}
  r_0(u)=M_0[F_u]=1,\qquad r_1(u)=\sqrt2\,M_1[F_u]=\sqrt2\,u .
\end{equation*}
Since $\fe{F}{G}=\sum_mp_mf_mg_m$,
\begin{equation}\label{sl:Ten}
  \Tr[e_m](u)=p_m\,r_m(u).
\end{equation}
The functions $e_m/\sqrt{p_m}$ form an orthonormal basis of $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$, so by theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} their images $\phi_m=\mathcal A[e_m/\sqrt{p_m}]=\sqrt{p_m\varrho}\;r_m$ form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$, and
\begin{equation*}
  \hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')=-\sum_m\nu_m\,\phi_m(u)\,\phi_m(u') .
\end{equation*}
This kernel is $-\nu\,\delta(u-u')$ when $\nu_m=\nu$ for every $m$, and it couples different slices otherwise. The coupling by GANDALF's hypercollisions is not weak: for $n=6$ and $M=24$, the largest off-diagonal element of their matrix in the unit-weight slice variables of the truncated hierarchy (section~\ref{sec:truncated}) is $0.40$ times the largest diagonal element. Collisions are therefore the only term in (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}) that exchanges free energy between slices.

Forcing of the Hermite moment $g_m$ by a field $f(\boldsymbol r,t)$ is the source $S=f\,e_m$, and by (\ref{sl:Ten}) it gives the slice at $u$ the source $\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\,p_m\,r_m(u)\,f$. For the two lowest moments,
\begin{equation}\label{sl:sources}
  S=f\,e_0:\quad \Big(1-\frac{1}{\Lambda}\Big)\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;f,\qquad\qquad
  S=f\,e_1:\quad \sqrt2\,u\,\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;f .
\end{equation}
Forcing of $g_0=M_0[G]$ therefore puts the same spatial pattern $f$ into every slice, with an amplitude proportional to $\sqrt\varrho$. For forcing that is white in time, the free energy injected per unit $u$ is proportional to $\varrho(u)$, with the normalised density $p_0\varrho(u)$, which integrates to one: injection at $m=0$ is uniform with respect to the spectral measure $\varrho\,\dd u$. Forcing of $g_1$ gives the slice at $u$ the amplitude $\sqrt2\,u\sqrt{\varrho(u)}$, which is odd in $u$. The slices at $u$ and $-u$ receive the same pattern with opposite signs, and the injected free energy has the density $2u^2\varrho(u)$, which integrates to $\fe{e_1}{e_1}=1$. In general, white forcing of $g_m$ injects free energy with the normalised density $\phi_m^2=p_m\varrho\,r_m^2$. If $g_0$ and $g_1$ are forced by fields $f_0$ and $f_1$ that are white in time, with $\langle f_i(\boldsymbol r,t)\,f_j(\boldsymbol r,t')\rangle=c_{ij}\,\delta(t-t')$, the mean rate at which $a_u^2$ is injected is
\begin{equation*}
  \varrho(u)\,\big[p_0^2\,c_{00}+2\sqrt2\,p_0\,c_{01}\,u+2u^2\,c_{11}\big].
\end{equation*}
The cross term is odd in $u$. It injects no free energy, but it injects $\int u\,a_u^2\,\dd u$, the quantity that section~\ref{sec:invariants} identifies with CMM's invariant $\Gamma$, at the rate $\sqrt2\,p_0\,c_{01}$ per unit volume (see also section~\ref{sec:symmetry}). We checked (\ref{sl:Ten}) and (\ref{sl:sources}) to round-off by quadrature of (\ref{eq:transform}), and on the increments produced by GANDALF's routine for forcing Hermite moments, projected on the slices of the truncated hierarchy.

\begin{remark}\label{sl:remlit}
Parts of this structure are known in other settings. For the sector $\Lambda=\infty$, theorem~\ref{thm:slice} restates S09's eqs~(210) and~(212): the Laguerre coefficients with $l\ge2$ obey $\dd G_l/\dd t+v_\parallel\gradpar G_l=0$, which S09 describe as a passively mixed, undamped ballistic mode, and at each $v_\parallel$ this is (\ref{eq:slice}) for $a_u=G_l(u)/\sqrt{F_M(u)}$. What the transform adds is the treatment of the two branches, whose Landau coupling it absorbs into the slices. \citet{Heninger2018} applied the G-transform to the electrostatic drift-kinetic ion equation with Boltzmann electrons in a slab. There quasineutrality also makes the kernel independent of the wavenumber, and their transform is the one used here with $\Lambda=-T_i/(ZT_e)$, which is the limit of $\Lambda^-$ as $\beta_i\to0$. In their system the transformed components stream along the uniform guide field and are carried by one $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, but the potential is proportional to $\int g\,\dd v_\parallel$, so the components remain coupled through it, and there is no streaming along perturbed field lines. \citet{Plunk2015} used a transform that commutes with $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ advection to prove a nonlinear invariant at each velocity for a quasi-two-dimensional drift-kinetic model without parallel streaming. \citet{Tassi2015,Tassi2022} constructed normal fields of truncated Hermite hierarchies of electromagnetic fluid models, associated with the zeros of Hermite polynomials and advected in two dimensions by stream functions of the form $\phi-\lambda_iv_{\mathrm{the}}A_\parallel/c$; in three dimensions they remain coupled through the self-consistent fields, except the field with $\lambda_i=0$. Other two-dimensional precedents are listed in section~\ref{sec:intro}. In KRMHD the Landau coupling is linear, local and independent of the wavenumber, so it is absorbed into $L$, and the Alfv\'enic fields do not depend on $G$; this is why the decomposition is exact and passive in three dimensions.
\end{remark}
