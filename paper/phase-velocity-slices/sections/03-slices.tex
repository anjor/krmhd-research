\section{Phase-velocity slices}\label{sec:slices}

This section shows that each branch (\ref{eq:branch}) is a continuum of passive scalars, or slices, labelled by the parallel phase velocity. The Alfv\'enic fields $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are given functions of $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$; (\ref{eq:rmhd}) is not used.

\subsection{Without the Landau coupling}\label{sl:nocoupling}

For $\Lambda=\infty$ the operator $L$ is multiplication by $u$, and (\ref{eq:branch}) holds separately at every $u$:
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+su\,\gradpar G=0 .
\end{equation*}
Since $\gradpar=\partial_z+\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ and $\{f,G\}=(\zhat\times\nabla_\perp f)\cdot\nabla G$, this is the advection equation $\partial_tG+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla G=0$ with the velocity
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Uu}
  \boldsymbol U_u=\zhat\times\nabla_\perp(\Phi+su\Psi)+su\,\zhat=\uE+su\,\bhat,\qquad\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol U_u=0 .
\end{equation}
At each $u$, $G$ is a passive scalar, carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed field at the speed $su$, which is the motion of the guiding centre of a free ion with $v_\parallel=u\vth$. Values of $G$ at different $u$ do not interact. This is the exact equation for the Laguerre coefficients $G_l$ with $l\ge2$ (S09, eqs~210 and~212).

\subsection{With the Landau coupling}\label{sl:transformsec}

For finite $\Lambda$, $LG=uG-(uF_M/\Lambda)\,M_0[G]$ ties every velocity to the density $M_0[G]$, through the parallel electric field and the mirror force, so $G$ at fixed $u$ is no longer a passive scalar. Two properties of $L$ restore the picture. First, $L$ acts on velocity only and is the same at every point and at every wavenumber, because the field relations (\ref{mod:fields}) are algebraic. Second, $L$ is self-adjoint in the free-energy product
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fe}
  \fe{f}{g}=\int\frac{fg}{F_M}\,\dd u-\frac{M_0[f]\,M_0[g]}{\Lambda},
\end{equation}
which is positive definite for $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, as for every compressive branch of KRMHD ($\Lambda^+>1$, $\Lambda^-<0$; section~\ref{sec:model}). It therefore has a complete set of generalised eigenfunctions, the Case--Van Kampen modes $F_u$ of the branch \citep{vanKampen1955,Case1959}, with $LF_u=uF_u$ for every real $u$. Each is a beam of particles at $v_\parallel=u\vth$ dressed by the response of the Maxwellian, through the Landau coupling, to the density of the beam. Expanding $G$ in these modes is a fixed linear change of variables in velocity, $G\mapsto a_u$, with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aunit}
  a_u=\frac{\diel_R\,G-\diel_I\,\mathsf H[G]}{\sqrt{F_M}\;|\diel|}(u),
\end{equation}
where $\diel(u+\ii0)=\diel_R+\ii\diel_I$ is the dielectric function of the branch on the real axis, which contains the plasma dispersion function, and $\mathsf H$ is the Hilbert transform in $u$ (appendix~\ref{app:spectral}, (\ref{sl:epsRI})). For $\Lambda=\infty$, $\diel=1$ and $a_u=G/\sqrt{F_M}$. The change of variables is invertible, divides the free energy among phase velocities without cross terms, and turns $L$ into multiplication by $u$:
\begin{equation}\label{sl:parseval}
  \fe{G}{G}=\int a_u^2\,\dd u,\qquad G\mapsto a_u\ \text{ takes }\ LG\mapsto u\,a_u
\end{equation}
(theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}). Its inverse is, up to normalisation, the G-transform that \citet{MorrisonPfirsch1992} and \citet{Morrison2000} introduced for linearised Vlasov--Poisson; \citet{Heninger2018} applied it to electrostatic drift kinetics, where the transformed components remain coupled through the potential. The function $\varrho=F_M/|\diel|^2$ that appears below is the spectral density of the branch, with $\int\varrho\,\dd u=1/p_0$ and $\varrho=F_M$ for $\Lambda=\infty$.

\subsection{The slice theorem}\label{sl:slicesec}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:slice}
Assume that (i) $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, the value $\Lambda=\infty$ included; (ii) $\Phi(\boldsymbol r,t)$ and $\Psi(\boldsymbol r,t)$ are real functions on $\mathbb T^3\times[0,t_1]$, with $\mathbb T^3$ a periodic box, whose gradients are continuous and Lipschitz in $\boldsymbol r$, uniformly in $t$; they need not satisfy (\ref{eq:rmhd}) and may obey resistive or forced RMHD, or be prescribed; (iii) $G$ and $uG$ are continuously differentiable functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ with values in the space $\mathcal W$ of functions of $u$ with finite $\int G^2F_M^{-1}\,\dd u$ (appendix~\ref{app:spectral}). Then $G$ solves (\ref{eq:branch}) if and only if, at every $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ and for almost every $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice}
  \partial_ta_u+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u=\partial_ta_u+\{\Phi+su\Psi,\,a_u\}+su\,\partial_za_u=0,
\end{equation}
with $\boldsymbol U_u$ of (\ref{eq:Uu}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The map $\mathcal A:G\mapsto a$ of (\ref{eq:aunit}) is bounded and injective (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}) and acts on $u$ only, through a kernel independent of $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$, so it commutes with $\partial_t$, with spatial derivatives and with multiplication by functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$, hence with $\{\Phi,\cdot\}$, $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ and $\gradpar$. With $\mathcal ALG=u\,\mathcal AG$ this gives $\mathcal A[\partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+s\,\gradpar(LG)]=\partial_ta+\{\Phi,a\}+su\,\gradpar a$, and $u\,\gradpar a_u=u\,\partial_za_u+\{u\Psi,a_u\}$ gives (\ref{eq:slice}).
\end{proof}

With the Landau coupling, therefore, each slice is again a passive scalar carried by $\boldsymbol U_u$, in three dimensions, and slices with different $u$ do not interact; the Landau coupling has been absorbed into the definition of the slices. The parallel electric field and the mirror force, which enter through the Landau coupling, only dress the slices: the distribution at fixed $u$ is not a passive scalar, but its projection $a_u$ on $F_u$ is. In terms of the Elsasser potentials, the stream function of the slice at $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elsasserpot}
  \Phi+su\Psi=\tfrac12\big[(1+su)\,z^++(1-su)\,z^-\big],
\end{equation}
so the slices at $su=-1$ and $+1$ are advected by $z^-$ alone and by $z^+$ alone, and the slice at $u=0$, the distribution at $v_\parallel=0$, by $\Phi$ alone. The sectors have different transforms, but $\boldsymbol U_u$ depends only on $u$, so at each $u$ the slices of $G^+$, $G^-$ and every $G_l$ with $l\ge2$ are carried by the same flow:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sameflow}
  \big(\partial_t+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla\big)\big(a^+_u,\,a^-_u,\,a^{(2)}_u,\,a^{(3)}_u,\dots\big)=0 .
\end{equation}
The compressive sector of KRMHD is therefore a continuum of passive scalars labelled by phase velocity.

\subsection{Dissipation, collisions and forcing}\label{sl:forcingsec}

Let the right-hand side of (\ref{eq:branch}) be $-\mathcal BG-\mathcal CG+F$, where the linear operators $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal C$ act on $\boldsymbol r$ alone and on $u$ alone, respectively, and $F$ is a source. The proof of theorem~\ref{thm:slice} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sliceforcing}
  \partial_ta_u+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u=-\mathcal B\,a_u-\int\hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')\,a_{u'}\,\dd u'+\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;\Tr[F](u),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathcal C}$ is the kernel of $\mathcal A\,\mathcal C\,\mathcal A^{-1}$. Damping at a rate that depends only on $k_\perp$ or $k_z$, applied with the same coefficient to every Hermite moment, is of the form $\mathcal B$ and damps each slice separately. With $e_m=H_m(u)F_M(u)/\sqrt{2^mm!}$ and $r_m(u)$ the $m$th Hermite coefficient of $F_u$, a polynomial of degree $m$ with $r_0=1$ and $r_1=\sqrt2\,u$, one has $\Tr[e_m](u)=p_m\,r_m(u)$, and the functions $\phi_m=\sqrt{p_m\varrho}\;r_m$ form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$. For $\mathcal Ce_m=\nu_me_m$, as for hypercollisions $\nu_m=\nu(m/M)^n$, the kernel is $\hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')=\sum_m\nu_m\,\phi_m(u)\,\phi_m(u')$, which couples different slices unless all $\nu_m$ are equal; collisions are the only term in (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}) that exchanges free energy between slices. Forcing of $g_0$ by a field $f(\boldsymbol r,t)$, $F=f\,e_0$, gives every slice the same spatial pattern, with amplitude $(1-1/\Lambda)\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\,f$.

Figure~\ref{fig:snapshots} releases a density blob, $g(0)=f\,e_0$, into decaying Alfv\'enic turbulence with $\chi=1.00$. Every slice starts with the pattern $f$ and is then carried by its own stream function $\Phi+su_j\Psi$, which at $su_j=-1.0218$, $0$ and $+1.0218$ is mainly $z^-$, $\Phi$ alone and mainly $z^+$; the three slices end visibly different, and $g_0$ mixes them. GANDALF's $\partial_tg$ projected on the slices agrees with (\ref{eq:truncslice}), computed with brackets that do not use GANDALF, to $5.2\times10^{-16}$ of the largest slice rate, while advecting every slice by $\Phi$ alone gives relative errors of 0.30 to 1.46. Over the run each slice energy changes only by the hyper-resistive loss of that slice, to $1.2\times10^{-6}$ of its initial energy, so no free energy passes between slices.
