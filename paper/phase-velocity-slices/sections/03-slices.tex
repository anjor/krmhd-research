\section{Phase-velocity slices}\label{sec:slices}

This section diagonalises the operator $L$ of (\ref{eq:branch}) and shows that the diagonalising transform commutes with the nonlinear terms. The Alfv\'enic fields $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ are given functions of $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$; (\ref{eq:rmhd}) is not used.

\subsection{The slice transform}\label{sl:transformsec}

Let $\mathcal W$ be the space of real functions $G(u)$ with $\ip{G}{G}<\infty$, where $\ip{f}{g}=\int fg\,F_M^{-1}\,\dd u$, so that $\mathsf Q=F_M\,M_0$ is the orthogonal projector onto $F_M$. The free-energy product is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fe}
  \fe{f}{g}=\ip{f}{g}-\frac{M_0[f]\,M_0[g]}{\Lambda}=\ip{f}{Pg},\qquad P=I-\frac{\mathsf Q}{\Lambda},
\end{equation}
which is positive definite if and only if $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, as for every compressive sector of KRMHD. With $U$ the multiplication by $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LUP}
  L=UP ,
\end{equation}
so $\fe{f}{Lg}=\ip{Pf}{UPg}$ is symmetric in $f$ and $g$, and $L$ is self-adjoint in the free-energy product (appendix~\ref{app:spectral}). For $\mathrm{Im}\,z>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{sl:resolvent}
  (z-L)^{-1}G=\frac{1}{z-u}\left[G-\frac{uF_M}{\Lambda\,\eps(z)}\int\frac{G(v)}{z-v}\,\dd v\right],\qquad
  \eps(\zeta)=1-\frac{1+\zeta Z(\zeta)}{\Lambda},
\end{equation}
where $Z$ is the plasma dispersion function of \citet{FriedConte1961}, not the charge number, and we write $\eps$ also for its continuation into the lower half plane, whose zeros are the Landau roots. On the real axis
\begin{equation}\label{sl:epsRI}
  \eps(u+\ii0)=\eps_R(u)+\ii\,\eps_I(u),\qquad
  \eps_R=1-\frac{1+u\,\mathrm{Re}\,Z(u)}{\Lambda},\qquad
  \eps_I=-\frac{\pi\,u\,F_M(u)}{\Lambda},
\end{equation}
and $\mathsf H[f](u)=\pi^{-1}\PVint f(v)\,(v-u)^{-1}\,\dd v$ is the Hilbert transform. By the argument of \citet{Penrose1960}, $\eps$ has no zeros in the closed upper half plane for $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$. The spectral density of the branch,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:varrho}
  \varrho(u)=\frac{F_M(u)}{|\eps(u+\ii0)|^2},
\end{equation}
is then positive, smooth and even, with $\int\varrho\,\dd u=1/p_0$, and $\varrho=F_M$ for $\Lambda=\infty$.

For real $u$ the equation $(L-u)F=0$ has the Case--Van Kampen solutions \citep{vanKampen1955,Case1959}
\begin{equation}\label{sl:Fu}
  F_u(v)=\frac{1}{\Lambda}\,\mathrm P\frac{vF_M(v)}{v-u}+\eps_R(u)\,\delta(v-u),\qquad M_0[F_u]=1,
\end{equation}
a beam of particles at $v=u$ dressed by the response of the Maxwellian, through the Landau coupling, to the density of the beam; for $\Lambda=\infty$, $F_u=\delta(v-u)$. The slice transform is the projection on these modes in the free-energy product, and the slice amplitude is its unit-weight form,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transform}
  \Tr[G](u)=\fe{F_u}{G}=\frac{\eps_R\,G-\eps_I\,\mathsf H[G]}{F_M}(u),\qquad
  \Tr[LG](u)=u\,\Tr[G](u),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:aunit}
  a_u=\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;\Tr[G](u)=\frac{\eps_R\,G-\eps_I\,\mathsf H[G]}{\sqrt{F_M}\;|\eps|}(u),
\end{equation}
and $\mathcal A$ denotes the map $G\mapsto a$. For $\Lambda=\infty$, $a_u=G(u)/\sqrt{F_M(u)}$, so the Laguerre coefficients with $l\ge2$ are already slices. The inverse of the transform is the G-transform of \citet{MorrisonPfirsch1992}, \citet{Morrison1994} and \citet{Morrison2000}.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:spectrum}
Let $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, the value $\Lambda=\infty$ included. Then $L$, with the domain of $U$, is self-adjoint in $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$, its spectrum is the real line, purely absolutely continuous and simple, and $\mathcal A$ is a unitary map from $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$ onto $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$ with $\mathcal ALG=u\,\mathcal AG$. In particular, for every bounded Borel function $\varphi$,
\begin{equation}\label{sl:parseval}
  \fe{G}{G}=\int a_u^2\,\dd u,\qquad \fe{G}{\varphi(L)G}=\int\varphi(u)\,a_u^2\,\dd u,
\end{equation}
and $G$ is recovered from its slices by
\begin{equation}\label{sl:inverse}
  G=\int\varrho(u)\,\Tr[G](u)\,F_u\,\dd u=\eps_R\,c+\eps_I\,\mathsf H[c],\qquad c=\sqrt\varrho\;a .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

Appendix~\ref{app:spectral} gives the proof. The slices divide the free energy among phase velocities, without cross terms.

\subsection{The slice theorem}\label{sl:slicesec}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:slice}
Assume that (i) $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, the value $\Lambda=\infty$ included; (ii) $\Phi(\boldsymbol r,t)$ and $\Psi(\boldsymbol r,t)$ are real functions on $\mathbb T^3\times[0,t_1]$, with $\mathbb T^3$ a periodic box, whose gradients are continuous and Lipschitz in $\boldsymbol r$, uniformly in $t$; they need not satisfy (\ref{eq:rmhd}) and may obey resistive or forced RMHD, or be prescribed; (iii) $G$ and $UG$ are continuously differentiable functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ with values in $\mathcal W$. Then $G$ solves (\ref{eq:branch}) if and only if, at every $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ and for almost every $u$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slice}
  \partial_ta_u+\{\Phi+su\Psi,\,a_u\}+su\,\partial_za_u=0,
\end{equation}
which is the advection equation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Uu}
  \partial_ta_u+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u=0,\qquad
  \boldsymbol U_u=\zhat\times\nabla_\perp(\Phi+su\Psi)+su\,\zhat=\uE+su\,\bhat,\qquad
  \nabla\cdot\boldsymbol U_u=0 .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}, $\mathcal A$ is bounded and injective and acts on $u$ only, through a kernel independent of $\boldsymbol r$ and $t$, so it commutes with $\partial_t$, with spatial derivatives and with multiplication by functions of $(\boldsymbol r,t)$, hence with $\{\Phi,\cdot\}$, $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$ and $\gradpar$. With $\mathcal ALG=u\,\mathcal AG$ this gives $\mathcal A[\partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+s\,\gradpar(LG)]=\partial_ta+\{\Phi,a\}+su\,\gradpar a$, and $u\,\gradpar a_u=u\,\partial_za_u+\{u\Psi,a_u\}$ gives (\ref{eq:slice}). The identity $\{\chi,a\}=(\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\chi)\cdot\nabla a$ gives (\ref{eq:Uu}).
\end{proof}

Each slice is a passive scalar carried by the divergence-free velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$, in three dimensions, and slices with different $u$ do not interact. The velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$ is that of the guiding centre of a free ion that drifts with $\uE$ and streams along $\bhat$ at $v_\parallel=u\vth$. The parallel electric field and the mirror force, which enter through the Landau coupling, only dress the slices: the distribution at fixed $u$ is not a passive scalar, but its projection $a_u$ on $F_u$ is. In terms of the Elsasser potentials, the stream function of the slice at $u$ is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:elsasserpot}
  \Phi+su\Psi=\tfrac12\big[(1+su)\,z^++(1-su)\,z^-\big],
\end{equation}
so the slices at $su=-1$ and $+1$ are advected by $z^-$ alone and by $z^+$ alone, and the slice at $u=0$, the distribution at $v_\parallel=0$, by $\Phi$ alone. The sectors have different transforms, but $\boldsymbol U_u$ depends only on $u$, so at each $u$ the slices of $G^+$, $G^-$ and every $G_l$ with $l\ge2$ are carried by the same flow:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sameflow}
  \big(\partial_t+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla\big)\big(a^+_u,\,a^-_u,\,a^{(2)}_u,\,a^{(3)}_u,\dots\big)=0 .
\end{equation}
The compressive sector of KRMHD is therefore a continuum of passive scalars labelled by phase velocity.

\subsection{Dissipation, collisions and forcing}\label{sl:forcingsec}

Let the right-hand side of (\ref{eq:branch}) be $-\mathcal BG-\mathcal CG+F$, where the linear operators $\mathcal B$ and $\mathcal C$ act on $\boldsymbol r$ alone and on $u$ alone, respectively, and $F$ is a source. The proof of theorem~\ref{thm:slice} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sliceforcing}
  \partial_ta_u+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u=-\mathcal B\,a_u-\int\hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')\,a_{u'}\,\dd u'+\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\;\Tr[F](u),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathcal C}$ is the kernel of $\mathcal A\,\mathcal C\,\mathcal A^{-1}$. Damping at a rate that depends only on $k_\perp$ or $k_z$, applied with the same coefficient to every Hermite moment, is of the form $\mathcal B$ and damps each slice separately. With $e_m=H_m(u)F_M(u)/\sqrt{2^mm!}$ and $r_m(u)$ the $m$th Hermite coefficient of $F_u$, a polynomial of degree $m$ with $r_0=1$ and $r_1=\sqrt2\,u$, one has $\Tr[e_m](u)=p_m\,r_m(u)$, and the functions $\phi_m=\sqrt{p_m\varrho}\;r_m$ form an orthonormal basis of $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$. For $\mathcal Ce_m=\nu_me_m$, as for hypercollisions $\nu_m=\nu(m/M)^n$, the kernel is $\hat{\mathcal C}(u,u')=\sum_m\nu_m\,\phi_m(u)\,\phi_m(u')$, which couples different slices unless all $\nu_m$ are equal; collisions are the only term in (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}) that exchanges free energy between slices. Forcing of $g_0$ by a field $f(\boldsymbol r,t)$, $F=f\,e_0$, gives every slice the same spatial pattern, with amplitude $(1-1/\Lambda)\sqrt{\varrho(u)}\,f$.

Figure~\ref{fig:snapshots} releases a density blob, $g(0)=f\,e_0$, into decaying Alfv\'enic turbulence with $\chi=1.00$. Every slice starts with the pattern $f$ and is then carried by its own stream function $\Phi+su_j\Psi$, which at $su_j=-1.0218$, $0$ and $+1.0218$ is mainly $z^-$, $\Phi$ alone and mainly $z^+$; the three slices end visibly different, and $g_0$ mixes them. GANDALF's $\partial_tg$ projected on the slices agrees with (\ref{eq:truncslice}), computed with brackets that do not use GANDALF, to $5.2\times10^{-16}$ of the largest slice rate, while advecting every slice by $\Phi$ alone gives relative errors of 0.30 to 1.46. Over the run each slice energy changes only by the hyper-resistive loss of that slice, to $1.2\times10^{-6}$ of its initial energy, so no free energy passes between slices.
