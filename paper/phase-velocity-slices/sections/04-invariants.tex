\section{Invariants}\label{sec:invariants}

Theorem~\ref{thm:slice} states that each slice amplitude $a_u$ is a passive scalar carried by the divergence-free velocity $\boldsymbol U_u=\uE+su\,\bhat$ of (\ref{eq:Uu}). This section derives the invariants of one compressive branch from that statement. Throughout, the hypotheses of theorem~\ref{thm:slice} hold: $\Lam\notin[0,1]$, the value $\Lam=\infty$ included; $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ have Lipschitz gradients in a periodic box of volume $V$ and are otherwise arbitrary, so that they may be forced, resistive or prescribed; and $G$, with the regularity assumed there, obeys the branch equation (\ref{eq:branch}) with no collisions, dissipation or sources acting on it. We write $\langle\cdot\rangle=V^{-1}\int\dd^3r\,(\cdot)$ for the volume average. Theorem~\ref{thm:slice} gives the slice equation for almost every $u$, so statements about a single slice hold for almost every $u$, and for every $u$ when $a_u$ depends continuously on $u$; we do not repeat this qualification. The last subsection leaves KRMHD and states what can be proved at finite $\kperp\rho_i$.

\subsection{The quadratic family}\label{inv:family}

For a function $\varphi$ on the real line, the operator $\varphi(L)$ is defined by the functional calculus of the self-adjoint operator $L$ (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}), so that the slice amplitude of $\varphi(L)G$ is $\varphi(u)\,a_u$. Because $G\mapsto a$ is unitary from $(\mathcal W,\fe{\cdot}{\cdot})$ onto $L^2(\mathbb R,\dd u)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Qphi}
  \Qphi\equiv\int\dd^3r\,\fe{G}{\varphi(L)G}=\int\dd u\;\varphi(u)\int\dd^3r\,a_u^2=V\!\int\varphi(u)\,\mathcal E(u)\,\dd u,
  \qquad \mathcal E(u)\equiv\langle a_u^2\rangle .
\end{equation}
For unbounded $\varphi$ we take the last two expressions as the definition of $\Qphi$ whenever the integral converges. The slice energy $\mathcal E(u)$ is the distribution of free energy over phase velocity: its integral is $\langle\fe{G}{G}\rangle$, and $\tfrac12\mathcal E(u)\,\dd u$ is the free energy per unit volume, in units of $n_iT_i$, carried by the slices with phase velocities in $[u,u+\dd u]$. In the notation of section~\ref{sec:decorrelation}, $\mathcal E(u)$ is the diagonal $C(u,u)$ of the two-slice correlation.

\begin{proposition}\label{inv:energy}
Under the hypotheses stated at the beginning of this section, in particular with no collisions, dissipation or sources acting on $G$, the slice energy $\mathcal E(u)$ is independent of time. Hence $\Qphi$ is constant in time for every $\varphi$ for which the integral in (\ref{eq:Qphi}) exists at $t=0$, either absolutely or, for $\varphi=1/u$, as a principal value.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By (\ref{eq:Uu}), $\partial_ta_u^2+\boldsymbol U_u\cdot\nabla a_u^2=0$. Since $\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol U_u=0$, this is $\partial_ta_u^2+\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol U_u\,a_u^2)=0$, and integration over the periodic box gives $\dd\mathcal E(u)/\dd t=0$. The function $\mathcal E$ is therefore the same at all times, and so is every integral of it.
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{inv:energy} states that, without collisions, free energy does not move between phase velocities. The family $\{\Qphi\}$ carries exactly the information contained in $\mathcal E(u)$. What changes in time is the correlation between different slices, which is the subject of section~\ref{sec:decorrelation}. A dissipation that acts in the same way at every velocity, such as $\kperp$ hyperdiffusion applied with one coefficient to every $g_m$, enters each slice equation unchanged (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}) and reduces $\mathcal E(u)$ at every $u$ without exchange between slices. Collisions that depend on velocity couple the slices.

Whether $\Qphi$ is finite depends on $\varphi$ and on the state, and by proposition~\ref{inv:energy} it suffices to check this at $t=0$. For $\varphi(u)=u^n$ the integral in (\ref{eq:Qphi}) converges absolutely if
\begin{equation*}
  \int\dd^3r\int\big(1+u^{2k}\big)\,\frac{G^2}{F_M}\,\dd u<\infty\qquad\text{for an integer }k\ge n/2 .
\end{equation*}
Indeed $|u|^n\le1+u^{2k}$, and $\int u^{2k}a_u^2\,\dd u=\fe{L^kG}{L^kG}$ is finite when $G$ lies in the domain of $L^k$. By (\ref{eq:LUP}) this is the domain of $U^k$, because $P$ differs from the identity by an operator of rank one whose range, spanned by $F_M$, lies in the domain of every power of $U$. For $\varphi(u)=1/u$ we define
\begin{equation*}
  Q_{1/u}=\lim_{\delta\to0^+}V\!\int_{|u|>\delta}\frac{\mathcal E(u)}{u}\,\dd u .
\end{equation*}
The limit exists when $\mathcal E$ is H\"older continuous near $u=0$. This holds when $G(\boldsymbol r,\cdot)$ is H\"older continuous near $u=0$, uniformly in $\boldsymbol r$: by (\ref{eq:aunit}), $a_u$ is a combination of $G$ and its Hilbert transform with coefficients that are smooth in $u$, and the Hilbert transform of a square-integrable function that is H\"older continuous near a point is H\"older continuous near that point.

The polynomial members can also be obtained without the transform. Since $L$ is symmetric, $\fe{G}{L^2G}=\fe{LG}{LG}$, and with $L=UP$ a short calculation gives
\begin{equation*}
  Q_{u^2}=\int\dd^3r\left[\int\frac{u^2G^2}{F_M}\,\dd u-\frac{2M_0M_2+M_1^2}{\Lam}+\frac{M_0^2}{2\Lam^2}\right],\qquad M_k=M_k[G].
\end{equation*}
In general $\fe{G}{L^nG}$ is $\int u^nG^2F_M^{-1}\,\dd u$ minus a quadratic form in $M_0,\dots,M_n$ with coefficients that depend on $\Lam$. Taking moments of (\ref{eq:branch}) gives the hierarchy $(\partial_t+\{\Phi,\cdot\})M_k=-s\gradpar M_{k+1}+(s\,c_{k+1}/\Lam)\gradpar M_0$, with $c_k=M_k[F_M]$, whose members $k=0$ and $k=1$ are equations (B23) and (B24) of CMM \citep{Chandran2026}. With it, $\dd Q_{u^n}/\dd t$ reduces to a combination of the antisymmetric integrals $\int\dd^3r\,M_a\gradpar M_b$ that cancels; we checked this symbolically for $n\le8$. We also checked against GANDALF's right-hand side that the members of the truncated family of section~\ref{sec:truncated}, which include $\Gamma$ and, for the zero closure, $W$, are conserved to round-off.

Per-velocity quadratic invariants are known in simpler settings. For the linear problem at a single wavenumber and without forcing, \citet{PlunkParker2014} noted that any $u$-weighted integral of the squared transformed distribution is conserved, which is the linear, single-wavenumber form of the conservation of every $\Qphi$. \citet{Plunk2015} proved a nonlinear per-velocity quadratic invariant for a quasi-two-dimensional drift-kinetic model without parallel streaming; there the linear operator depends on the wavenumber only through an overall factor, so the transform that diagonalises it commutes with $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ advection. In the transformed electrostatic drift-kinetic equation of \citet[eq.~85]{Heninger2018}, for a three-dimensional slab with Boltzmann electrons, every transformed component streams along the uniform guide field and all components are carried by one $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow; without collisions and sources this equation implies per-slice invariants as well, although none is stated there. What the two nonlinear systems share with KRMHD is a linear operator in velocity that couples the distribution to one of its velocity moments, as the Landau coupling does in KRMHD, and whose eigenmodes do not depend on the wavenumber, so that the transform commutes with the advection; in all three the advecting flow is divergence free. Passivity is not needed: in the two drift-kinetic systems the slices remain coupled through the potential, which is a velocity moment of the distribution, and each slice still conserves its energy. \citet{Kunz2015} remarked that, with collisions ordered out of their pressure-anisotropic generalisation of KRMHD, the free-energy invariant they consider is one of an infinite number of invariants of the system; they did not specify the others.

\subsection{The invariants \texorpdfstring{$W$, $\Gamma$ and $\Hph$}{W, Gamma and H}}\label{inv:cmm}

The free energy $W^\pm$ of each branch, which CMM write as $W^\pm_{\mathrm{compr}}$ (their B13), is conserved (S09). CMM found two further quadratic invariants for each branch, the phase-space helicity $\Hph^\pm$, which they write as $H^\pm_{\textrm{ph-sp}}$ (their B18), and the invariant $\Gamma^\pm$ (their B25). Written in the variable $u=v_\parallel/\vth$, with $G^\pm(v_\parallel)\,\dd v_\parallel=G^\pm(u)\,\dd u$, these are the members $\varphi=1$, $u$ and $1/u$ of (\ref{eq:Qphi}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:WGH}
\begin{aligned}
  W^\pm&=\frac{n_iT_i}{2}\,\big\langle\fe{G^\pm}{G^\pm}\big\rangle=\frac{n_iT_i}{2}\int\mathcal E^\pm(u)\,\dd u,\\
  \Gamma^\pm&=\frac{\vth}{2}\,\big\langle\fe{G^\pm}{LG^\pm}\big\rangle=\frac{\vth}{2}\int u\,\mathcal E^\pm(u)\,\dd u,\\
  \Hph^\pm&=\frac{n_iT_i}{2\vth}\,\Big\langle\PVint\frac{(G^\pm)^2}{uF_M}\,\dd u\Big\rangle=\frac{n_iT_i}{2\vth}\,\PVint\frac{\mathcal E^\pm(u)}{u}\,\dd u .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Here $L$, the product $\fe{\cdot}{\cdot}$ and $\mathcal E^\pm$ are those of branch $\pm$, with $\Lam=\Lam^\pm$, and $\vth=s$ in units with $v_A=1$. The first expression in each line is CMM's definition rewritten in $u$. The second follows from (\ref{eq:Qphi}), and for $\Hph^\pm$ from (\ref{inv:pv}) below. The normalisations are CMM's: $W^\pm$ carries the factor $n_iT_i$ and $\Gamma^\pm$ does not, so that $n_iT_i\Gamma^\pm$ has the dimensions of $\vth W^\pm$. The free energy $\tilde W$ and the helicity $\tilde\Hph$ of the Laguerre sectors with $l\ge2$ (CMM's $\tilde W_{\mathrm{compr}}$ and $\tilde H_{\textrm{ph-sp}}$, their B14 and B19) are the members $\varphi=1$ and $\varphi=1/u$ for $\Lam=\infty$, with the prefactors of (\ref{eq:WGH}), summed over $l$. In the Hermite representation of section~\ref{sec:model},
\begin{equation*}
  \fe{G}{G}=\sum_mp_m\,g_m^2=\sum_mg_m^2-\frac{g_0^2}{\Lam},\qquad
  \tfrac12\fe{G}{LG}=\tfrac12\,g^{\mathsf T}\Pw A\,g=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\Big(\sum_m\sqrt{m+1}\,g_mg_{m+1}-\frac{g_0g_1}{\Lam}\Big),
\end{equation*}
which are the integrands of CMM's (B31) and (B32) divided by $n_iT_i/2$ and by $\vth$, respectively. The helicity has no banded form in the Hermite coefficients: for the basis functions $e_m$ of section~\ref{sec:slices}, $\PVint e_me_n\,(uF_M)^{-1}\,\dd u$ is non-zero whenever $m+n$ is odd and the odd index exceeds the even one. Its truncation at odd and even $M$ is treated in section~\ref{sec:truncated}, equation~(\ref{eq:Hoddeven}).

The kernel of $\Hph^\pm$ contains no $\Lam$. This follows from $L=UP$. The operator $P$ is invertible for $\Lam\neq1$, with $P^{-1}=I+F_MM_0/(\Lam-1)$, so that $PL^{-1}=PP^{-1}U^{-1}=U^{-1}$, as noted in section~\ref{sec:slices}, and
\begin{equation*}
  \fe{G}{L^{-1}G}=\ip{G}{PL^{-1}G}=\ip{G}{U^{-1}G}=\int\frac{G^2}{uF_M}\,\dd u .
\end{equation*}
This holds literally when $G/u$ lies in $\mathcal W$. For a general $G$, which need not vanish at $u=0$, the principal value follows from the resolvent form $R_G(z)=\fe{G}{(z-L)^{-1}G}$ with $\mathrm{Im}\,z>0$, which by theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum} is the Stieltjes transform of $a_u^2$. Writing $(z-L)X=G$ as $(z-u)X=G-uF_M\,M_0[X]/\Lam$ and taking $M_0$ gives $M_0[X]=C_G(z)/\eps(z)$, with $C_G(z)=\int G(v)\,(z-v)^{-1}\,\dd v$ and $\eps$ the dielectric function (\ref{eq:eps}). Substituting $X$ into $\fe{G}{X}$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{inv:resolvent}
  R_G(z)=\int\frac{a_u^2}{z-u}\,\dd u=\int\frac{G^2}{(z-u)F_M}\,\dd u-\frac{z}{\Lam}\,\frac{C_G(z)^2}{\eps(z)} .
\end{equation}
Let $G$ be H\"older continuous near $u=0$, and let $z=\ii\eta$ with $\eta\to0^+$. By the Sokhotski--Plemelj formula the two integrals in (\ref{inv:resolvent}) tend to $-\PVint a_u^2u^{-1}\,\dd u-\ii\pi a_0^2$ and to $-\PVint G^2(uF_M)^{-1}\,\dd u-\ii\pi G(0)^2/F_M(0)$. The last term tends to zero, because $C_G(\ii\eta)$ stays bounded and $\eps(\ii\eta)\to\eps(0+\ii0)=1-1/\Lam\neq0$. Hence, at every point $\boldsymbol r$,
\begin{equation}\label{inv:pv}
  \PVint\frac{a_u^2}{u}\,\dd u=\PVint\frac{G^2}{uF_M}\,\dd u,\qquad a_0^2=\frac{G(0)^2}{F_M(0)} .
\end{equation}
The helicity kernel, multiplication by $1/(uF_M)$, is therefore the same for both branches and for the Laguerre sectors, and $\Hph^\pm$ depends on $\Lam^\pm$ only through $G^\pm$. The kernels of $W^\pm$ and $\Gamma^\pm$ in the product $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$ are $P$ and $PUP$. They contain $\Lam$ through the rank-one part of $P$, and neither is a multiplication operator in $u$.

\subsection{The complex helicity and the slice at zero velocity}\label{inv:complex}

CMM's gyrokinetic helicity (their 2.15) uses the weight $1/(v_\parallel-\ii0)$ rather than a principal value, and the imaginary part in their Plemelj split (2.17) is the contribution of $v_\parallel=0$. The analogue for one branch, defined like (B18) but with this weight, is the member $\varphi(u)=1/(u-\ii0)=\mathrm P(1/u)+\ii\pi\delta(u)$ of the family. By (\ref{eq:aunit}) and (\ref{inv:pv}),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Hcomplex}
  \Hph^\pm_{\mathrm c}\equiv\frac{n_iT_i}{2\vth}\Big\langle\int\frac{(G^\pm)^2}{(u-\ii0)F_M}\,\dd u\Big\rangle
  =\Hph^\pm+\ii\,\frac{\pi n_iT_i}{2\vth}\,\big\langle\varrho(0)\,\Tr[G^\pm](0)^2\big\rangle
  =\Hph^\pm+\ii\,\frac{\pi n_iT_i}{2\vth}\,\Big\langle\frac{G^\pm(\boldsymbol r,0,t)^2}{F_M(0)}\Big\rangle .
\end{equation}
The imaginary part is $\pi n_iT_i\,\mathcal E^\pm(0)/(2\vth)$, proportional to the slice energy at $u=0$, which proposition~\ref{inv:energy} conserves. As section~\ref{sec:slices} showed, the slice at $u=0$ is the distribution at $v_\parallel=0$ itself, $a_0=G(\boldsymbol r,0,t)/F_M(0)^{1/2}$, and because $(LG)(0)=0$ it obeys
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_tG(\boldsymbol r,0,t)+\{\Phi,G(\boldsymbol r,0,t)\}=0 .
\end{equation*}
It is advected by $\uE$ alone, with neither streaming nor the field-line term $\{\Psi,\cdot\}$. CMM showed that in full gyrokinetics the function $\psi_s$ of their (2.18), evaluated at $v_\parallel=0$, is advected by the gyroaveraged potential $\langle\chi\rangle_{\boldsymbol R_s}$ alone (their 2.22), and that this gives the conservation of $\mathrm{Im}\,\Hph$ at each $v_\perp$ (their 2.23). In KRMHD the streaming term of the kinetic equation (CMM's 3.25) vanishes at $v_\parallel=0$, and their statement becomes the $u=0$ member of the slice family; (\ref{eq:Hcomplex}) is its form for one branch.

\subsection{Non-quadratic and cross-sector invariants}\label{inv:casimirs}

Each slice is a Lagrangian invariant of the volume-preserving flow $\boldsymbol U_u$: if $\boldsymbol X_{u,t}$ is the flow map of $\boldsymbol U_u$, then $a_u(\boldsymbol X_{u,t}(\boldsymbol r_0),t)=a_u(\boldsymbol r_0,0)$. Since $\boldsymbol X_{u,t}$ preserves volume, for every $u$ and every function $\mathcal F$ for which the integral exists,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:casimir}
  \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int\dd^3r\,\mathcal F(a_u)=0 .
\end{equation}
For smooth solutions this also follows from $\mathcal F'(a)\{\Phi+su\Psi,a\}=\{\Phi+su\Psi,\mathcal F(a)\}=\nabla\cdot[\mathcal F(a)\,\zhat\times\nabla(\Phi+su\Psi)]$ and $\mathcal F'(a)\,\partial_za=\partial_z\mathcal F(a)$. The quadratic member $\mathcal F(a)=a^2$ gives $V\mathcal E(u)$. By (\ref{eq:sameflow}), the slice amplitudes $a^\pm_u$ of the two branches and $a^{(l)}_u=G_l(u)/F_M(u)^{1/2}$ of the Laguerre sectors with $l\ge2$ are carried by the same velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$ and share its flow map. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:crosssector}
  \frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int\dd^3r\,\mathcal F\big(a^+_u,a^-_u,a^{(2)}_u,a^{(3)}_u,\dots\big)=0,\qquad\text{for example}\qquad\frac{\dd}{\dd t}\int\dd^3r\,a^+_u\,a^-_u=0 .
\end{equation}
Integrating the last relation over $u$ with any weight gives quadratic invariants that couple the two branches.

These statements concern fields that are continuous in space. Under spatial Fourier--Galerkin truncation, as in pseudo-spectral codes, only the quadratic members survive, because the projected bracket conserves $\int a^2\,\dd^3r$ when $\Phi$, $\Psi$ and $a$ lie inside the dealiasing band (section~\ref{sec:truncated}), but in general not $\int\mathcal F(a)\,\dd^3r$; we confirmed this for $\mathcal F(a)=a^4$ on GANDALF's right-hand side. A Hermite code that evolved both branches would not conserve the cross-branch forms exactly either, because the discrete phase velocities of the two branches, the eigenvalues of the ladder matrices at $\Lam^+$ and at $\Lam^-$, differ. The exception is $u=0$, which is an eigenvalue for both branches when $M$ is even (section~\ref{sec:truncated}).

Invariants labelled by a velocity variable are known in two dimensions ($\partial_z=0$) in several reduced models. In two-field models of collisionless reconnection, two Lagrangian invariants are advected by the generalised stream functions $\phi\pm(\rho_s/d_e)\psi$ of those models \citep{Schep1994,Cafaro1998,Grasso2001}, and in their drift-kinetic version the distribution at each value of the parallel canonical momentum is a Lagrangian invariant with its own stream function \citep{Liseikina2004,Pegoraro2004}. Two-dimensional gyrokinetics has an invariant for every pair $(v_\perp,v_\parallel)$ (S09, appendix~F), the low-beta equations of \citet{ZoccoSchekochihin2011} have a family of Lagrangian invariants parametrised by $v_\parallel$, and \citet{ZhuHammett2010} used per-velocity quadratic invariants that survive Galerkin truncation to construct absolute equilibria of two-dimensional gyrokinetics. In each of these models the terms with parallel gradients break the invariants in three dimensions. In three dimensions, \citet{Tassi2022} showed that Hermite truncations of electromagnetic drift-kinetic models with an odd number of moments contain one field that is advected by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow alone and has an infinite family of Casimirs \citep[see also][]{Tassi2023}; it is the truncated counterpart of the slice at $u=0$ (section~\ref{sec:truncated}). Invariants of the form (\ref{eq:casimir}) also follow, without being stated, from the transformed drift-kinetic equation of \citet{Heninger2018}, whose components stream along the uniform guide field. For $u\neq0$, where the slices stream along the perturbed field lines, the non-quadratic members of (\ref{eq:casimir}) and the cross-sector invariants (\ref{eq:crosssector}) are new; the quadratic member of (\ref{eq:casimir}) is the slice energy of proposition~\ref{inv:energy}, whose precedents are discussed in section~\ref{inv:family}.

\subsection{The local conservation law and \texorpdfstring{$\Gamma$}{Gamma} as a flux}\label{inv:flux}

The free energy of each branch is also conserved locally.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:localflux}
Under the same hypotheses, the free-energy density per unit $n_iT_i$, $w=\tfrac12\fe{G}{G}$, and the density $\gamma=\tfrac{s}{2}\fe{G}{LG}$ satisfy, at every point,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:localflux}
  \partial_tw+\nabla\cdot\big(w\,\uE+\gamma\,\bhat\big)=0 .
\end{equation}
In slice variables $w=\tfrac12\int a_u^2\,\dd u$ and $\gamma=\tfrac{s}{2}\int u\,a_u^2\,\dd u$, so that $w\,\uE+\gamma\,\bhat=\tfrac12\int a_u^2\,\boldsymbol U_u\,\dd u$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The product $\fe{\cdot}{\cdot}$ acts on $u$ at fixed $\boldsymbol r$ with a kernel that does not depend on $\boldsymbol r$, so every derivation $D$ in $\boldsymbol r$ satisfies $D\fe{f}{h}=\fe{Df}{h}+\fe{f}{Dh}$. Both $\{\Phi,\cdot\}$ and $\gradpar$ are such derivations, and both commute with $L$, which acts on $u$ only. Since $L$ is symmetric in $\fe{\cdot}{\cdot}$ at each point,
\begin{equation*}
  \gradpar\fe{G}{LG}=\fe{\gradpar G}{LG}+\fe{G}{\gradpar LG}=\fe{\gradpar LG}{G}+\fe{G}{\gradpar LG}=2\,\fe{G}{\gradpar LG}.
\end{equation*}
Taking $\fe{G}{\cdot}$ of (\ref{eq:branch}) therefore gives $\partial_tw+\{\Phi,w\}+\gradpar\gamma=0$. Finally, $\{\Phi,w\}=\uE\cdot\nabla w=\nabla\cdot(w\,\uE)$ and $\gradpar\gamma=\bhat\cdot\nabla\gamma=\nabla\cdot(\gamma\,\bhat)$, because $\nabla\cdot\uE=\nabla\cdot\bhat=0$. The slice forms follow from the unitarity of $G\mapsto a$ and from the fact that the slice amplitude of $LG$ is $u\,a_u$.
\end{proof}

By the last statement of proposition~\ref{prop:localflux}, each slice carries its local free-energy density $\tfrac12a_u^2$ at its own velocity $\boldsymbol U_u$. The proof uses the symmetry of $L$ but not the transform. The same law therefore holds for the Hermite hierarchy truncated with the zero closure, regarded as partial differential equations in $\boldsymbol r$, with $w=\tfrac12g^{\mathsf T}\Pw g$ and $\gamma=\tfrac s2g^{\mathsf T}\Pw Ag$, since $\Pw A$ is symmetric; for the copy closure it holds with the weight $p_M$ of section~\ref{sec:truncated} in the last entry of $\Pw$. A pseudo-spectral discretisation in $\boldsymbol r$ keeps only the volume average of the law, and only when $\Phi$, $\Psi$ and $g$ lie inside the dealiasing band. Averaged over the box, (\ref{eq:localflux}) gives $\dd W^\pm/\dd t=0$. Since $\uE$ has no $z$ component and $\hat b_z=1$, the $z$ component of the volume-averaged flux is $\langle\gamma\rangle$, which by (\ref{eq:WGH}) is $\Gamma^\pm$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gammaflux}
  \Gamma^\pm=\zhat\cdot\big\langle w\,\uE+\gamma\,\bhat\big\rangle,\qquad
  \frac{n_iT_i\,\Gamma^\pm}{W^\pm}=\vth\,\frac{\int u\,\mathcal E^\pm(u)\,\dd u}{\int\mathcal E^\pm(u)\,\dd u}.
\end{equation}
Thus $n_iT_i\Gamma^\pm$ is the net flux of the free energy $W^\pm$ along $\boldsymbol B_0$: the $z$ component of the volume-averaged free-energy flux, or equivalently the free-energy flux per unit area through the planes $z=\mathrm{const}$, averaged over $z$. The perpendicular components of the volume-averaged flux, $\langle w\,\uE+\gamma\,\boldsymbol b_\perp\rangle$, need not vanish, and $\Gamma^\pm$ does not determine them. The ratio $n_iT_i\Gamma^\pm/W^\pm$ is the mean of the parallel velocities $u\vth$ of the slices, weighted by their energies, and $\Gamma^\pm=0$ when $\mathcal E^\pm(u)$ is even in $u$. CMM derived the conservation of $\Gamma^\pm$ from the moment equations (their B20--B26); its reading as a field-aligned flux of free energy is new.

\subsection{Finite \texorpdfstring{$\kperp\rho_i$}{k-perp rho-i}}\label{inv:finite}

The results above use two properties of KRMHD: the kernel $P\varphi(L)$ of every $\Qphi$ in the product $\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$ is the same at every wavenumber, and the velocity that advects $G$ does not depend on $v_\perp$. Both fail at finite $\kperp\rho_i$, where in addition the compressive fluctuations act back on the Alfv\'enic ones. In the kinetic equation of CMM's isothermal-electron-fluid (ITEF) system (their 3.16), the ion distribution is advected by the gyroaveraged potential, which depends on $v_\perp$, and by the potential $(\rho_i\vth/2)\hat v_\perp^2\hat J_1\delta B/B$, where $\hat v_\perp=v_\perp/\vth$ and $\hat J_0$, $\hat J_1$, $\hat\Gamma_0$ and $\hat\Gamma_1$ are CMM's gyroaveraging operators (their 3.11--3.13). The field relations contain $\hat\Gamma_0$ and $\hat\Gamma_1$ (CMM's 3.10 and 3.14), whose symbols depend on $\kperp\rho_i$, so that a Landau coupling, where one can be defined, depends on the wavenumber, as in the gyrokinetic form of the transform of \citet[eq.~79]{Heninger2018}. The invariant of \citet{Plunk2015} likewise rests on a transform that does not depend on the wavenumber, which requires $k^2\rho^2\ll1$. \citet{CostelloPlunk2025} have extended an invariant of that quasi-two-dimensional model to finite Larmor radius. What can be proved concerns the advection terms alone.

Let $\theta(\boldsymbol r,\boldsymbol v,t)=\sum_{\boldsymbol k}\theta_{\boldsymbol k}(\boldsymbol v)\,e^{\ii\boldsymbol k\cdot\boldsymbol r}$ be real and advected by a prescribed real potential $\chi(\boldsymbol r,t)$, so that
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_t\theta_{\boldsymbol k}=\sum_{\boldsymbol p+\boldsymbol q=\boldsymbol k}(\boldsymbol p\times\boldsymbol q)_z\,\chi_{\boldsymbol p}\,\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}\theta_{\boldsymbol q}.
\end{equation*}
Here $\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}=I$ for advection that does not depend on velocity, which is the equation $\partial_t\theta+\{\chi,\theta\}=0$, and $\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}$ is multiplication by $J_0(p_\perp v_\perp/\Omega_i)$ when each velocity is advected by the gyroaveraged potential. Consider the quadratic forms $\mathcal Q_K=\sum_{\boldsymbol k}\theta_{\boldsymbol k}^\dagger K_{\boldsymbol k}\theta_{\boldsymbol k}$, with $K_{\boldsymbol k}$ Hermitian operators on functions of velocity and $K_{-\boldsymbol k}=\bar K_{\boldsymbol k}$. Writing $\dd\mathcal Q_K/\dd t=2\,\mathrm{Re}\sum_{\boldsymbol k}\theta_{\boldsymbol k}^\dagger K_{\boldsymbol k}\partial_t\theta_{\boldsymbol k}$ and relabelling the terms of the complex conjugate by $(\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q)\to(-\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol p)$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{inv:triad}
  \frac{\dd\mathcal Q_K}{\dd t}=\sum_{\boldsymbol p,\boldsymbol q}(\boldsymbol p\times\boldsymbol q)_z\,\mathrm{Re}\Big[\chi_{\boldsymbol p}\,\theta_{\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol p}^\dagger\big(K_{\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol p}\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}-\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}K_{\boldsymbol q}\big)\theta_{\boldsymbol q}\Big].
\end{equation}
For $\theta$ supported on $\pm\boldsymbol q$ and $\pm(\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol p)$ and $\chi$ on $\pm\boldsymbol p$, with $\boldsymbol p$ and $\boldsymbol q$ in general position, the sum (\ref{inv:triad}) has four equal terms.

\begin{lemma}[Advection independent of velocity]\label{inv:lemA}
Let $\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}=I$. Then $\mathcal Q_K$ is conserved for every $\chi$ and every $\theta$ if and only if $K_{\boldsymbol k}$ is the same for all $\boldsymbol k$ with $\boldsymbol k_\perp\neq0$. The operators $K_{\boldsymbol k}$ with $\boldsymbol k_\perp=0$ are unconstrained, and no condition is placed on how $K_{\boldsymbol k}$ acts on velocity.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The coefficient $(\boldsymbol p\times\boldsymbol q)_z$ vanishes when $\boldsymbol q_\perp=0$ and when $(\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol p)_\perp=0$, so every term of (\ref{inv:triad}) that survives involves two wavevectors with non-zero perpendicular parts. If $K_{\boldsymbol k}$ is the same for all of these, every term vanishes. Conversely, let $K_{\boldsymbol k_1}\neq K_{\boldsymbol k_2}$, with $\boldsymbol k_{1\perp}$ and $\boldsymbol k_{2\perp}$ non-zero and not parallel. The triad with $\boldsymbol q=\boldsymbol k_1$ and $\boldsymbol p=\boldsymbol k_2-\boldsymbol k_1$ has $(\boldsymbol p\times\boldsymbol q)_z\neq0$, and $\theta_{\boldsymbol q}$, $\theta_{\boldsymbol q+\boldsymbol p}$ and $\chi_{\boldsymbol p}$ can be chosen to make $\dd\mathcal Q_K/\dd t$ non-zero. Wavevectors with parallel perpendicular parts are compared through a third wavevector.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Gyroaveraged advection]\label{inv:lemAp}
Let $\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}$ be multiplication by $J_0(p_\perp v_\perp/\Omega_i)$, and let $K_{\boldsymbol k}=K$ for all $\boldsymbol k$. Then $\mathcal Q_K$ is conserved for every $\chi$ and every $\theta$ if and only if $K$ commutes with every $\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}$, that is, if and only if $K$ is decomposable over $v_\perp$: it may act in any way on $v_\parallel$, but separately at each value of $v_\perp$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $K_{\boldsymbol k}=K$ the operator in (\ref{inv:triad}) is the commutator $[K,\mathcal J_{\boldsymbol p}]$. If it vanishes for every $\boldsymbol p$, so does $\dd\mathcal Q_K/\dd t$. If it is non-zero for some $\boldsymbol p$, which then has $\boldsymbol p_\perp\neq0$, a triad with $(\boldsymbol p\times\boldsymbol q)_z\neq0$ and suitable $\theta$ makes $\dd\mathcal Q_K/\dd t$ non-zero. The functions $J_0(np_0v_\perp/\Omega_i)$, $n=1,2,\dots$, where $p_0$ is the smallest non-zero perpendicular wavenumber of the box, take different values at any two values of $v_\perp$ and do not all vanish at any one value, as their large-$n$ asymptotic form shows. By the Stone--Weierstrass theorem, an operator that commutes with all of them therefore commutes with multiplication by every bounded function of $v_\perp$, that is, it is decomposable over $v_\perp$; the converse is immediate.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Helicity kernel]\label{inv:lemB}
Let $L_{\boldsymbol k}=UP_{\boldsymbol k}$ with $P_{\boldsymbol k}=I-F_MM_0/\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}$, where $\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}\notin[0,1]$ may depend on $\boldsymbol k$, and let $\fe{\cdot}{\cdot}_{\boldsymbol k}$ be the free-energy product with $\Lam=\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}$. Then $P_{\boldsymbol k}L_{\boldsymbol k}^{-1}=U^{-1}$ on the range of $U$ for every $\boldsymbol k$. The helicity form $\fe{G}{L_{\boldsymbol k}^{-1}G}_{\boldsymbol k}=\PVint G^2(uF_M)^{-1}\,\dd u$, in the principal-value sense of (\ref{inv:pv}), is therefore the same at every wavenumber, and its kernel is a multiplication operator. The kernels $P_{\boldsymbol k}$ and $P_{\boldsymbol k}UP_{\boldsymbol k}$ of the forms with $\varphi=1$ and $\varphi=u$ depend on $\boldsymbol k$ through $\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The argument of section~\ref{inv:cmm} applies at each $\boldsymbol k$. The operator $P_{\boldsymbol k}^{-1}=I+F_MM_0/(\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}-1)$ maps the domain of $U$ onto itself, so $L_{\boldsymbol k}^{-1}=P_{\boldsymbol k}^{-1}U^{-1}$ on the range of $U$ and $P_{\boldsymbol k}L_{\boldsymbol k}^{-1}=U^{-1}$. The principal-value form follows from (\ref{inv:resolvent}) and (\ref{inv:pv}) with $\Lam=\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}$.
\end{proof}

Lemmas~\ref{inv:lemA} and~\ref{inv:lemAp} concern advection by a prescribed potential, and lemma~\ref{inv:lemB} is an identity for the branch operator with a coupling that depends on the wavenumber. For velocity-independent advection a conserved form must have the same kernel at all wavenumbers with $\kperp\neq0$, and for gyroaveraged advection a kernel that is the same at all wavenumbers is conserved only if it is decomposable over $v_\perp$. A coupling $\Lam_{\boldsymbol k}$ that depends on the wavenumber violates the first condition for the forms with $\varphi=1$ and $\varphi=u$, but not for the helicity. Regarded as forms in $g(v_\perp,v_\parallel)$, the branch forms $W^\pm$, $\Gamma^\pm$ and $\Hph^\pm$ violate the second, because $G^\pm$ are weighted integrals of $g$ over $v_\perp$ (CMM's B2, B3 and B5). CMM's gyrokinetic helicity, whose kernel $1/(v_\parallel F_{0s})$ acts on $\psi_s$ and is a multiplication operator in velocity, satisfies both conditions, and CMM's proof shows that the remaining terms cancel.

The lemmas do not give a necessary condition for invariance under the full equations. Terms that come from the field equations can make a form invariant even when its kernel depends on $\boldsymbol k$ and couples different velocities. The gyrokinetic free energy (CMM's 2.13; S09) is such a form when it is written in terms of the gyrocentre distribution. We checked this mechanism in a two-dimensional electrostatic gyrokinetic model with Boltzmann electrons and gyroaveraged advection: the field energy, whose kernel depends on $\boldsymbol k$ and couples velocities, is conserved to round-off when the potential is computed from quasineutrality, and it is not conserved when the same advection acts with a prescribed potential.

What happens to $\Gamma^\pm$ at finite $\kperp\rho_i$ is open. CMM report $W$ and $\Hph$ as the quadratic invariants of general gyrokinetics; $\Gamma^\pm$ appears in their work only for KRMHD, and they do not state that no further invariants exist. Under the KRMHD ordering, in which CMM's normalised potentials exceed $\delta n/n$ and $\delta B/B$ by a factor $(\kperp\rho_i)^{-1}$ (S09; CMM, \S3.2), five terms of the ITEF equations that are absent from KRMHD are of relative order $\kperp\rho_i$: the advection of the ion distribution by $(\rho_i\vth/2)\hat v_\perp^2\hat J_1\delta B/B$ in the kinetic equation (CMM's 3.16); the term $(Z\vth/2\tau)\gradpar(\delta n/n)$ in CMM's equation (3.6) for the parallel vector potential; the term $-(\rho_i\vth/2)\{(Z/\tau)\delta n/n,\delta B/B\}$ in the electron continuity equation (3.7); and the terms in which $1-\hat\Gamma_0$ and $1-\hat\Gamma_1$ act on the potential, in quasineutrality (3.10) and in perpendicular pressure balance (3.14). The corrections that $\hat J_0$ and $\hat J_1$ make to the gyroaverages are of relative order $\kperp^2\rho_i^2$ (CMM's 3.11). Whether some correction of $\Gamma^\pm$ at order $\kperp\rho_i$ is conserved in the presence of these terms has not been computed. In the two-dimensional electrostatic model described above, quasineutrality gives an operator of the form $L_{\boldsymbol k}=UP_{\boldsymbol k}$, with a rank-one part of $P_{\boldsymbol k}$ that depends on $\kperp$. There the analogue of $\Gamma$, $\sum_{\boldsymbol k}\fe{g}{L_{\boldsymbol k}g}_{\boldsymbol k}$, is conserved at $\kperp\rho_i=0$ and is broken by the nonlinearity at a rate proportional to $(\kperp\rho_i)^2$ (log--log slope $1.99$ over a factor of eight in $\rho_i$). That model lacks the electromagnetic terms of order $\kperp\rho_i$ listed above and does not settle the question for gyrokinetics.
