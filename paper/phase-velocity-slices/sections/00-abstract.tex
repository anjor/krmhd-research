We show that the compressive fluctuations of kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics are exactly a continuum of passive scalars, or slices, labelled by the parallel phase velocity $u$. The slices are the projections of the compressive distribution on the Case--Van Kampen modes of a velocity-space operator that is self-adjoint and independent of wavenumber. Without collisions they evolve independently in any smooth three-dimensional Alfv\'enic fields, each carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed magnetic field at its own speed. The free energy, the phase-space helicity and the invariant $\Gamma$ of Chandran et al.\ (2026) weight the slice energies by $1$, $1/u$ and $u$, and $\Gamma$ is the net free-energy flux along the guide field. Phase mixing is the decorrelation of slices; fully decorrelated slices give the $m^{-1/2}$ Hermite spectrum, and the invariants place no Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint on velocity-space transfer. With ideal induction, smooth fields and no forcing or spatial dissipation of the distribution, volume-integrated single-point statistics evolve as linear phase mixing along the initial field lines, so there are no stochastic echoes in the Hermite spectrum. A Hermite hierarchy truncated at order $M$ is $M+1$ slices for either closure, $g_{M+1}=0$ or $g_{M+1}=g_{M-1}$, and for $g_{M+1}=0$ they sit at Gauss quadrature nodes. We check these results with the GANDALF code.
