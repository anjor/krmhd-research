In kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics (KRMHD) the compressive fluctuations are a kinetic field carried by the Alfv\'enic turbulence. Phase mixing moves their free energy to fine structure in parallel velocity and the turbulence moves it to small perpendicular scales, and the Hermite spectrum $W(m)$ records the competition between the two. We show that the compressive sector is exactly a continuum of passive scalars, one for each parallel phase velocity, each carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed magnetic field at its own speed. The nonlinearity therefore moves no free energy between phase velocities and, while the field lines stay frozen into the flow, none between field-aligned wavenumbers. We propose that its only effect on $W(m)$ is to remove free energy from every Hermite moment at the same rate, the rate at which the turbulence carries a passive scalar to the dissipation scale, while phase mixing proceeds at the field-aligned wavenumber at which the free energy was injected. This gives $W(m)\propto m^{-1/2}$ at small $m$ and a decay exponential in $m^{1/2}$ beyond a break at $m_*\sim\beta_i/\chi^2$, where $\chi$ is the nonlinearity parameter. In simulations of forced KRMHD turbulence at $\beta_i=1$, the model with independently measured inputs reproduces $W(m)$ and the Hermite flux along the field, and there is no return flux from high to low $m$; a backward flux appears only in diagnostics based on the Eulerian parallel wavenumber. At $\beta_i=0.3$ and $0.1$ the break moves into the resolved range, as predicted. There the spectrum lies above the model, because the perpendicular sink grows with the age of the free energy in the same way at every $\beta_i$. How the sink and the field-aligned wavenumber depend on the perpendicular resolution is not yet settled.
