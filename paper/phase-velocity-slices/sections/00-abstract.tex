In kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics the compressive fluctuations are a kinetic field advected passively by Alfv\'enic turbulence. We show that this field is exactly a continuum of passive scalars labelled by parallel phase velocity. The operator of each kinetic branch is self-adjoint in the free-energy inner product and independent of wavenumber, so its Case--Van Kampen transform commutes with the nonlinearity. Without collisions, the transformed components, or slices, evolve independently for any smooth three-dimensional Alfv\'enic fields: each moves with the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streams along the perturbed magnetic field at its own velocity. The slice energies give a family of invariants including the free energy and Chandran, Mallet and Meyrand's $\Gamma$ and $\Hph$; $\Gamma$ measures the net free-energy flux along the guide field. A Hermite hierarchy truncated at order $M$ consists of $M+1$ slices, at the Gauss quadrature nodes of the Case--Van Kampen spectral measure when the next moment is set to zero. Phase mixing is the decorrelation of slices along the field, the Hermite flux is a Christoffel--Darboux transform of its rate, and the invariants do not prevent transfer to arbitrarily high order. With ideal induction, smooth Alfv\'enic fields and no forcing or spatial dissipation of the distribution, every volume-integrated single-point statistic evolves as linear phase mixing along the initial field lines, so the Hermite spectrum shows no stochastic echoes. A mirror symmetry relates mirror-image forced ensembles, and the slices at $v_\parallel=\pm v_A$ are each advected by a single Elsasser field. The results are checked with the GANDALF code.
