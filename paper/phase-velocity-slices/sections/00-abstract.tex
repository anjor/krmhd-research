In kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics (KRMHD) the compressive fluctuations are a kinetic field carried by the Alfv\'enic turbulence. Phase mixing moves their free energy to fine structure in parallel velocity and the turbulence moves it to small perpendicular scales, and the Hermite spectrum $W(m)$ records the competition between the two. We show that the compressive sector is exactly a continuum of passive scalars, one for each parallel phase velocity, each carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed magnetic field at its own speed. The nonlinearity therefore moves no free energy between phase velocities and, while the field lines stay frozen into the flow, none between field-aligned wavenumbers. We propose that its only effect on $W(m)$ is to remove free energy from every Hermite moment at the same rate, the rate at which the turbulence carries a passive scalar to the dissipation scale, while phase mixing proceeds at the field-aligned wavenumber at which the free energy was injected. This gives $W(m)\propto m^{-1/2}$ at small $m$ and a decay exponential in $m^{1/2}$ beyond a break at $m_*\sim\beta_i/\chi^2$, where $\chi$ is the nonlinearity parameter. In simulations of forced KRMHD turbulence at $\beta_i=1$, the model, with its two inputs measured independently, reproduces $W(m)$ and the Hermite flux along the field lines, and there is no return flux from high to low $m$. Lowering $\beta_i$ moves the break into the resolved range, as predicted. With the Hermite resolution raised until the break is resolved, $m_*$ grows about in proportion to $\beta_i$ for $0.05\le\beta_i\le0.3$. Against $\chi$, at $\beta_i=0.05$, it grows faster than $\chi^{-2}$, and about as $\chi^{-4}$ between $\chi\approx0.56$ and 0.40, where the perpendicular sink per outer eddy time weakens as the turbulence becomes weak. The model is too simple in one respect: the perpendicular sink is not the same at every $m$ but grows with the time since the free energy was injected, in the same way at every $\beta_i$, so at $\beta_i=0.3$ and 0.1 the spectrum lies above the model. At $\beta_i=0.3$ the field-aligned spectrum of the free energy does not change with the resolution, apart from a tail holding less than 0.5\% of it, but the time in which the sink removes the free energy has not converged: it grows by about the same amount with each doubling up to $256^3$ grid points, so whether the break becomes independent of the dissipation scale, as the model assumes, remains open.
