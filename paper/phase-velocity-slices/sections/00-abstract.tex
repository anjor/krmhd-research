In kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics, phase mixing moves the free energy of compressive fluctuations to fine structure in parallel velocity, Alfv\'enic turbulence moves it to small perpendicular scales, and the Hermite spectrum $W(m)$ records the competition. We show that while the field lines stay frozen into the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, the nonlinearity moves no free energy between Hermite moments or between field-aligned wavenumbers. We propose that in turbulence the nonlinearity acts on $W(m)$ only through the perpendicular cascade, which removes free energy from every moment in the same time $T$, as from a passive scalar, while phase mixing proceeds at the field-aligned wavenumber $\kappa$ of the injected free energy. This gives $W(m)\propto m^{-1/2}$ up to a break at $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$, with $s=\beta_i^{1/2}$, and a decay exponential in $m^{1/2}$ beyond it. In forced simulations on $64^3$ to $256^3$ grid points, the field-aligned Hermite flux is the phase-mixing flux of $W(m)$ at the measured $\kappa$, with no return flux, and the field-aligned spectrum of the free energy stays at the injected wavenumbers. The other results hold at $64^3$, where $T$ is 0.7 to 1.1 times the energy-containing time of the Alfv\'enic turbulence, or 11 to 16 outer eddy-turnover times, so the break lies at 60 to 130 times $\beta_i$. The break grows as $\beta_i^{1.09}$ for $0.05\le\beta_i\le0.3$, and the removal rate grows with the age of the free energy, alike at every $\beta_i$, through the dissipation range of these runs. $T$ grows with the resolution, so whether the break becomes independent of the dissipation remains open.
