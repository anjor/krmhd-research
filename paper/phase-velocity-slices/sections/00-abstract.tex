In kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics (KRMHD), phase mixing moves the free energy of the compressive fluctuations to fine structure in parallel velocity, the Alfv\'enic turbulence moves it to small perpendicular scales, and the Hermite spectrum $W(m)$ records the competition. We show that while the field lines stay frozen into the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, the nonlinearity moves no free energy between Hermite moments or between wavenumbers along the field. We propose that in turbulence the nonlinearity acts on $W(m)$ only through the perpendicular cascade, which removes free energy from every Hermite moment in the same time $T$, while phase mixing proceeds at the field-aligned wavenumber $\kappa$ of the injected free energy. This gives $W(m)\propto m^{-1/2}$ up to a break at $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$, with $s=\beta_i^{1/2}$, and a decay exponential in $m^{1/2}$ beyond it. In forced simulations on $64^3$ to $256^3$ grid points, the Hermite flux along the field is the phase-mixing flux of $W(m)$ at the measured field-aligned wavenumber, with no return flux, and the spectrum of the free energy along the field stays at the injected wavenumbers. The other results are measured at $64^3$. $T$ is 11 to 16 outer eddy-turnover times, so at nonlinearity parameter $\chi\approx1$ the break lies at 60 to 130 times $\beta_i$, not near $\beta_i$. The break grows as $\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$ for $0.05\le\beta_i\le0.3$ and faster than $\chi^{-2}$ as $\chi$ falls, and the removal rate grows with the age of the free energy, alike at every $\beta_i$. $T$ grows with the resolution, so whether the break becomes independent of the dissipation remains open.
