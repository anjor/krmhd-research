Using the Case--Van Kampen transform of the branch operator, which is self-adjoint and independent of wavenumber, we show that the compressive fluctuations of kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics are exactly a continuum of passive scalars, or slices, labelled by parallel phase velocity. Without collisions the slices evolve independently in any smooth three-dimensional Alfv\'enic fields, each carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed field at its own speed. The free energy and Chandran et al.'s invariants $\Gamma$ and $\Hph$ weight the slice energies by $1$, $u$ and $1/u$, and $\Gamma$ is the field-aligned free-energy flux. Phase mixing is the decorrelation of slices, which sets the Hermite spectrum, and the invariants place no Fj{\o}rtoft-type constraint on velocity-space transfer. With ideal induction, smooth fields and no forcing or spatial dissipation of the distribution, volume-integrated single-point statistics evolve as linear phase mixing along the initial field lines, without stochastic echoes in the Hermite spectrum. A Hermite hierarchy closed by $g_{M+1}=0$ is $M+1$ slices at Gauss quadrature nodes. We check these results with the GANDALF code.
