\section{A two-channel model of the Hermite spectrum}\label{sec:twochannel}

In a turbulent steady state the compressive distribution is forced and dissipated, and the hypotheses (\ref{eq:hyp}) fail in two ways: the induction equation is not ideal, and $g$ is dissipated in space. The exact results still constrain what the nonlinearity can do. It moves no free energy between slices (section~\ref{hm:energy}), and as long as the field lines stay frozen into the flow it moves none between field-aligned wavenumbers (corollary~\ref{cor:kpar}). This section builds a model on the assumption that the two failures matter only through the removal of free energy at small perpendicular scales, and lists the predictions by which it can be tested.

\subsection{The two channels}\label{tc:channels}

\emph{Phase mixing at a frozen field-aligned wavenumber.} With white forcing, the mean of $W(m)$ is a sum of linear responses to the individual injections, each phase mixing along the field lines frozen at its injection time (corollary~\ref{cor:forcing}). Each injection has, along those lines, a distribution of field-aligned wavenumbers $\kappa$, which it keeps (corollary~\ref{cor:kpar}). By (\ref{eq:linforced}), free energy injected at $\kappa$ moves up the Hermite ladder at the speed $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$ and carries the flux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pmflux}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}\simeq s\kappa\sqrt{2m}\,W(m)\qquad(m\gg1),
\end{equation}
with no part returning to lower $m$.

\emph{A uniform sink.} Each slice is a passive scalar, which the turbulence carries to the dissipation scale, where the spatial dissipation removes it, slice by slice (section~\ref{hm:energy}). For passive scalars in turbulence the rate at which variance is dissipated stays finite as the diffusivity goes to zero \citep{ShraimanSiggia2000,FalkovichGawedzkiVergassola2001}, and the time a scalar takes to reach the dissipation scale is set by the flow, not by the diffusivity. All slices share the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, and in its frame they differ only by their streaming along the field lines (theorem~\ref{thm:frozen}), which moves them along the lines but does not by itself refine them across the lines. We therefore assume that the turbulence cascades every slice in the same way. If the perpendicular spectrum of $g_m$ then has the same shape for every $m$, the dissipation, which acts alike on every moment, removes free energy from each moment in proportion to its free energy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformsink}
  D_\perp(m)=\frac{W(m)}{T},
\end{equation}
with one time $T$ for all $m$. Equivalently, for single-point statistics such as $W(m)$, the nonlinearity of KRMHD acts as a damping at a rate $1/T$ that does not depend on $m$. Section~\ref{tc:limits} replaces it by a rate that depends on the age of the free energy, which the simulations require (section~\ref{sim:beta}).

In the steady state of (\ref{eq:budget}), treated as continuous in $m$ and without collisions, the flux (\ref{eq:pmflux}) changes with $m$ only through the sink (\ref{eq:uniformsink}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wbalance}
  \frac{\dd\Pi}{\dd m}=-\frac{W(m)}{T}=-\frac{\Pi(m)}{s\kappa T\sqrt{2m}} .
\end{equation}
Since $\dd\sqrt{2m}/\dd m=1/\sqrt{2m}$, this integrates to $\ln[\Pi(m)/\Pi(0)]=-\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa T)$. All the free energy enters the ladder through $g_0$, so $\Pi(0)=\eps$, the rate of injection into $g_0$, and $W=\Pi/(s\kappa\sqrt{2m})$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wmodel}
  W(m)=\frac{\eps}{s\kappa\sqrt{2m}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{s\kappa T}\Big).
\end{equation}
The prefactor is the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum of linear phase mixing, whose flux $\eps$ is the same at every $m$. The exponential can be read off by following the free energy up the ladder. By (\ref{eq:pmflux}) it moves at $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$, so $\dd\sqrt{2m}/\dd t=s\kappa$, and it reaches $m$ at the age
\begin{equation}\label{eq:age}
  \vartheta_m=\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{s\kappa} .
\end{equation}
The sink removes it at the rate $1/T$ on the way, so the fraction that reaches $m$ is $\exp(-\vartheta_m/T)$, which is the exponential in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}). Because the injected pattern is fixed in the Eulerian frame and the field lines are tilted, the injected free energy has a distribution $P(\kappa)$ of field-aligned wavenumbers, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wspread}
  W(m)=\eps\int P(\kappa)\,\frac{1}{s\kappa\sqrt{2m}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{s\kappa T}\Big)\,\dd\kappa ,
\end{equation}
with each $\kappa$ breaking at its own $m$, so that a broad $P(\kappa)$ turns the stretched exponential into a more gradual decline. For a single $\kappa$ the local slope is $\dd\ln W/\dd\ln m=-\tfrac12\big(1+\sqrt{m/m_*}\big)$, which passes through $-1$ at $m=m_*$ and $-3/2$ at $m=4m_*$, so the spectrum is close to $m^{-1}$ around the break without being a power law. A spread of $\kappa$ makes the decline more gradual, because the free energy at large $\kappa$ phase mixes fastest and survives to the largest $m$; for a distribution with a Gaussian tail of width $\sigma$, (\ref{eq:Wspread}) falls at very large $m$ as $\exp[-c\,(m/s^2T^2\sigma^2)^{1/3}]$ with $c$ of order one, more slowly than the single-$\kappa$ exponential. With the distribution of the injected pattern measured at $\beta_i=1$ and $M=64$ (section~\ref{sec:simulations}; mean $1.12\,k_1$ and rms $1.34\,k_1$, with $k_1$ the wavenumber of the box) and $m_*$ evaluated at the mean, the local slope of (\ref{eq:Wspread}) stays within $0.15$ of $-1$ over a factor of five in $m$ around $m_*$ and reaches only $-2$ at $16\,m_*$ and $-2.4$ at $64\,m_*$, against $-2.5$ and $-4.5$ for a single $\kappa$. A spectrum measured over a limited range near $m_*$ can therefore look like a power law somewhat steeper than $m^{-1}$. Hypercollisions add the sink $D_\nu(m)$ to (\ref{eq:Wbalance}). For the comparison with simulations we solve, for each $\kappa$, the forced linear hierarchy (\ref{mod:hierarchy}) with $\gradpar$ replaced by $\ii\kappa$, with the damping rate $1/(2T)$ added to every amplitude and with the hypercollisions of the run, and sum over the measured $P(\kappa)$ (appendix~\ref{app:methods}). This reproduces (\ref{eq:Wspread}) at large $m$ and is exact for the linear problem at small $m$. Once $T$ and $P(\kappa)$ are measured, the model has no free parameter.

The form (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) is that of \citet[eq.~112]{ZoccoSchekochihin2011} for the Hermite spectrum of growing modes, with their $2\gamma$ replaced by $1/T$, and \citet[eqs~2.58--2.59]{Schekochihin2016} solved the same continuity equation in $m$ with the collisional sink $2\nu m$ in place of $1/T$. What the model adds is the identification of the sink with the passive-scalar cascade, which the exact results of sections~\ref{sec:hermite} and~\ref{sec:frozen} make the only effect of the nonlinearity on $W(m)$ once the field lines are frozen.

\subsection{Scales}\label{tc:scales}

The exponential in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) matters once the age (\ref{eq:age}) of the free energy reaches the cascade time $T$. The spectrum therefore follows $m^{-1/2}$ up to the break
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mstar}
  m_*=\frac{(s\kappa T)^2}{2},
\end{equation}
and beyond it decays exponentially in $\sqrt m$, not as a power law.

The dependence of $m_*$ on the plasma parameters follows from estimating the two times at the outer scale. The field-aligned wavenumber $\kappa$ is S09's $k_{\parallel0}$ (\S6.3), the wavenumber along the perturbed field, which without a parallel cascade stays that of the large-scale stirring. It is therefore of the order of the parallel wavenumber $k_\parallel$ of the outer-scale turbulence. Phase mixing then carries free energy to moment $m$ in the time
\begin{equation}
  \vartheta_m\sim\frac{\sqrt m}{k_\parallel\vth},
\end{equation}
and the perpendicular cascade removes it in a time of the order of the outer eddy-turnover time,
\begin{equation}
  T\sim\frac{1}{k_\perp u_\perp},
\end{equation}
where $u_\perp$ is the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity at the outer scale. The free energy reaches $m$ before the cascade removes it, $\vartheta_m\lesssim T$, as long as
\begin{equation}
  \sqrt m\lesssim\frac{k_\parallel\vth}{k_\perp u_\perp}=\frac{\vth}{v_A}\,\frac{k_\parallel v_A}{k_\perp u_\perp}=\frac{\beta_i^{1/2}}{\chi},
\end{equation}
where $\vth/v_A=\beta_i^{1/2}$ and $\chi=k_\perp u_\perp/(k_\parallel v_A)$ is the nonlinearity parameter. Hence
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mstar-scaling}
  m_*\sim\frac{\beta_i}{\chi^2} .
\end{equation}
This is the phase-mixing threshold $k_\parallel\vth\sim\sqrt m\,k_\perp u_\perp$ of \citet[eq.~4.5]{Schekochihin2016}, written in KRMHD variables, with its coefficient fixed by (\ref{eq:mstar}) and the cascade time; for $\chi\sim1$ it is the crossover $m\sim\beta_i$ stated by \citet{Kawazura2019}. The estimate takes $T$ to be a fixed multiple of the outer eddy time $1/(k_\perp u_\perp)$; section~\ref{sim:mstar} finds that $m_*$ grows somewhat faster than $\chi^{-2}$ down to $\chi\approx0.56$ and about as $\chi^{-4}$ below it, because the sink per eddy time is not fixed. If the collisional cutoff $m_c$ lies below $m_*$, most of the injected free energy reaches it and is dissipated by collisions; if it lies above, most is dissipated at small perpendicular scales, and the collisional share is about $\exp(-\sqrt{2m_c}/(s\kappa T))$.

In slice variables the break is a correlation width. The forcing loads every slice with the same pattern, and two slices with phase velocities $u$ and $u'$ then dephase at the rate $s\kappa|u-u'|$ while the sink removes them at the rate $1/T$. Averaged over the age of the free energy, their correlation is
\begin{equation}
  c(u-u')\propto\int_0^\infty e^{-\vartheta/T}\cos[s\kappa(u-u')\vartheta]\,\dd\vartheta=\frac{T}{1+s^2\kappa^2T^2(u-u')^2},
\end{equation}
a Lorentzian of width
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deltauc}
  \delta u_c=\frac{1}{s\kappa T}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m_*}} .
\end{equation}
By (\ref{eq:corrspec}) its cosine transform at $q=\sqrt{2m}$ is the exponential factor in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}). Slices closer than $\delta u_c$ in phase velocity have not dephased by the time the sink removes them, and the spectrum breaks at the moment $m_*$ whose resolution in phase velocity, $1/\sqrt{2m}$, equals this width.

\subsection{Predictions}\label{tc:predictions}

The model predicts the following, each measurable independently of the others.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \emph{Uniform sink.} $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ is independent of $m$, and the budget (\ref{eq:budget}) closes with the flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) computed with the full $\gradpar$, but not with the $k_z$-only flux.
\item[(ii)] \emph{Shape.} With $T$ from (i) and $P(\kappa)$ measured along field lines for the injected pattern, $W(m)$ and the field-aligned flux follow the model. A return flux would make both fall below it at large $m$.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Dependence on $\beta_i$.} At fixed $\chi$ the Alfv\'enic turbulence does not depend on $\beta_i$, so $P(\kappa)$ is unchanged and, if $T$ is too, $m_*\propto\beta_i$: the break moves into a fixed range of $m$ as $\beta_i$ decreases. With a sink that depends on the age of the free energy (section~\ref{tc:limits}), the effective $T$ falls somewhat as $\beta_i$ decreases, because the same moments then hold older free energy.
\item[(iv)] \emph{Anomaly.} $T$ stays finite and does not depend on the dissipation coefficients as the resolution increases. Section~\ref{sim:resolution} tests this at four resolutions.
\item[(v)] \emph{Eulerian diagnostics.} A split of the flux by the sign of $k_z$ assigns to the backward direction the forward flux of modes whose $k_z$ and field-aligned wavenumber have opposite signs (\ref{lag:shear}), and so reports a backward flux where the field-aligned flux has none. If the free energy at moment $m$ phase mixes at field-aligned wavenumber $\kappa$ while the split sorts it by $k_z=\kappa-\delta$, with $\delta=\boldsymbol b_\perp\cdot\boldsymbol k_\perp$ Gaussian with variance $\sigma^2=\langle k_z^2\rangle-\kappa^2$, the expected ratio of the unmixing to the phase-mixing part is $[\sigma\mathcal N(\zeta)-\kappa(1-\mathcal C(\zeta))]/[\kappa\,\mathcal C(\zeta)+\sigma\mathcal N(\zeta)]$ with $\zeta=\kappa/\sigma$ and $\mathcal N$, $\mathcal C$ the standard normal density and distribution function; it grows with the tilt and so with the range of resolved $k_\perp$.
\item[(vi)] \emph{Slice correlations.} The correlation between slices falls off over $\delta u_c$ (\ref{eq:deltauc}).
\end{itemize}
The echo picture \citep{Meyrand2019} predicts instead a return flux along the field, which reduces the net field-aligned flux and $W(m)$ below the model at large $m$, and a field-aligned wavenumber of $g$ that grows with $k_\perp$ through the inertial range as $g$ inherits the parallel cascade of the Alfv\'enic fields.

\subsection{Limits of the model}\label{tc:limits}

The model rests on two assumptions that the exact results do not guarantee. First, it assumes that the field lines stay frozen long enough for the injected $\kappa$ to be kept, although the hyper-resistivity of any simulation, and resistivity in a plasma, reconnect them. \citet{Meyrand2019} argued that this relabelling gives $g$ the parallel structure of the Alfv\'enic fields within about one eddy-turnover time. Second, it assumes that the perpendicular cascade has a dissipative anomaly. \citet{DrivasEyink2017} showed that anomalous dissipation of a passive scalar requires Lagrangian spontaneous stochasticity, the non-uniqueness of trajectories as the diffusivity goes to zero, and \citet[\S VIII.C]{Eyink2018} argued that the same stochasticity breaks flux freezing in a turbulent inertial range. If the two go together, the frozen $\kappa$ of the first assumption must fail at the scales where the anomaly of the second operates. The model can survive this only if the free energy that phase mixes sits at scales where the field lines are still effectively frozen and the relabelling acts on free energy that the perpendicular sink is already removing. Section~\ref{sec:simulations} measures the field-aligned wavenumber of $g$ as a function of $k_\perp$ to see where this holds. The model also takes the cascade rate to be the same for every slice; slices with large $|u|$ stream fast along wandering field lines, which may refine them across the lines faster than slow slices, and prediction~(i) measures how large this effect is. At $128^3$ and $256^3$ the field-aligned wavenumber of $g_0$ grows through the inertial range, roughly as $k_\perp^{1/3}$ to $k_\perp^{0.4}$, more slowly than that of the Alfv\'enic fields, and the sink rate falls by about 30\% with each doubling of the resolution up to $256^3$ (section~\ref{sim:resolution}). At the resolutions reached here the first assumption therefore holds only approximately and the second does not hold.

If the sink depends on the time $\vartheta$ since injection rather than being uniform, as section~\ref{sim:beta} finds, free energy injected at $\kappa$ reaches $m$ at the age $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa)$, and (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wage}
  W(m)=\frac{\eps}{s\kappa\sqrt{2m}}\exp\!\Big(-\int_0^{\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa)}\gamma(\vartheta)\,\dd\vartheta\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\gamma(\vartheta)$ the rate at which the perpendicular cascade removes free energy of age $\vartheta$. For a passive scalar injected at the outer scale, $\gamma$ is small at first, while the cascade carries the fresh pattern towards the dissipation scale, and approaches a constant once the scalar's spectrum has filled in. A uniform $1/T$ then removes too much free energy at small $m$ and too little at large $m$. The function $\gamma(\vartheta)$ belongs to the Alfv\'enic turbulence, not to $\beta_i$, so at fixed $\chi$ one $\gamma(\vartheta)$ should describe every $\beta_i$.
