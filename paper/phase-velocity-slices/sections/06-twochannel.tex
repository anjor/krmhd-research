\section{A two-channel model of the Hermite spectrum}\label{sec:twochannel}

In a turbulent steady state the compressive distribution is forced and dissipated, and two of the conditions of section~\ref{fr:solution} fail: the induction equation is not ideal, and $G$ is dissipated in space. The exact result still constrains what the nonlinearity can do. As long as the field lines stay frozen into the flow, it moves no free energy between Hermite moments (section~\ref{fr:single}) or between field-aligned wavenumbers (section~\ref{fr:kpar}). This section builds a model on the assumption that the two failures matter only through the removal of free energy at small perpendicular scales, and says which parts of the model the simulations can test.

\subsection{The two channels}\label{tc:channels}

\emph{Phase mixing at a frozen field-aligned wavenumber.} With white forcing, the mean of $W(m)$ is a sum of linear responses to the individual injections, each phase mixing along the field lines frozen at its injection time (section~\ref{fr:forcing}). Each injection has, along those lines, a distribution $P(\kappa)$ of field-aligned wavenumbers $\kappa$, which it keeps (section~\ref{fr:kpar}). We write $\kappa_1$ for the mean of $|\kappa|$ over $P(\kappa)$, and $\kappa_1(m)$ for the mean of $|\kappa|$ over the spectrum of $g_m$ along the field lines. By (\ref{eq:cm}) with $|k_z|$ replaced by $\kappa>0$, free energy injected at $\kappa$ carries the flux
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pmflux}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}=s\,c_m\,\kappa\,W(m)\simeq s\kappa\sqrt{2m}\,W(m)\qquad(m\gg1),
\end{equation}
so it moves up the Hermite ladder at the speed $\dd m/\dd t\simeq s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$, and none of it returns to lower $m$.

\emph{A uniform sink.} The turbulence carries the compressive distribution to small perpendicular scales, where the spatial dissipation, which acts alike on every Hermite moment, removes it. We call this removal the perpendicular sink. Every Hermite moment is carried by the same $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, and in the frame of the flow the moments differ only in how they stream along the field lines (section~\ref{fr:solution}). We assume that this streaming does not change the rate at which the flow refines a moment across the field, so that the turbulence cascades every moment as it would a passive scalar. This is the hypothesis of the model, and it is not a consequence of section~\ref{sec:frozen}. In a stochastic field, streaming along field lines that separate does refine structure across them \citep{RechesterRosenbluth1978}, and it does so most for the high moments, which are dominated by fast particles. A run with $s=0$ in the same flow would measure the sink of a passive scalar directly; we have not made it.

 If the perpendicular spectrum of $g_m$ has the same shape for every $m$, the dissipation removes free energy from each moment in proportion to its free energy,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniformsink}
  D_\perp(m)=\frac{W(m)}{T},
\end{equation}
with one time $T$ for all $m$, set by the turbulence and its dissipation. For single-point statistics such as $W(m)$, the nonlinearity of KRMHD then acts as a damping at a rate $1/T$ that does not depend on $m$. Section~\ref{tc:limits} replaces it by a rate that depends on the age of the free energy, which the simulations require (section~\ref{sim:beta}).

In the steady state of (\ref{eq:budget}), treated as continuous in $m$, at large $m$ and without collisions, the flux (\ref{eq:pmflux}) changes with $m$ only through the sink (\ref{eq:uniformsink}):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wbalance}
  \frac{\dd\Pi}{\dd m}=-\frac{W(m)}{T}=-\frac{\Pi(m)}{s\kappa T\sqrt{2m}} .
\end{equation}
Since $\dd\sqrt{2m}/\dd m=1/\sqrt{2m}$, this integrates to $\ln[\Pi(m)/\Pi(0)]=-\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa T)$. All the free energy enters the ladder through $g_0$, so $\Pi(0)=\eps$, the rate of injection into $g_0$, and $W=\Pi/(s\kappa\sqrt{2m})$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wmodel}
  W(m)=\frac{\eps}{s\kappa\sqrt{2m}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{s\kappa T}\Big).
\end{equation}
The prefactor is the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum of linear phase mixing, whose flux $\eps$ is the same at every $m$. The exponential can be read off by following the free energy up the ladder. It moves at $\dd m/\dd t=s\kappa\sqrt{2m}$, so $\dd\sqrt{2m}/\dd t=s\kappa$, and it reaches $m$ at the age
\begin{equation}\label{eq:age}
  \vartheta_m=\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{s\kappa} .
\end{equation}
The sink removes it at the rate $1/T$ on the way, so the fraction that reaches $m$ is $\exp(-\vartheta_m/T)$, which is the exponential in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}). The free energy is injected with a spread of field-aligned wavenumbers, because the forcing is a fixed pattern in the Eulerian frame and the field lines are tilted, so
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wspread}
  W(m)=\eps\int P(\kappa)\,\frac{1}{s\kappa\sqrt{2m}}\exp\!\Big(-\frac{\sqrt{2m}}{s\kappa T}\Big)\,\dd\kappa ,
\end{equation}
with each $\kappa$ breaking at its own $m$. For a single $\kappa$ the local slope is $\dd\ln W/\dd\ln m=-\tfrac12\big(1+\sqrt{m/m_*}\big)$, with $m_*$ defined in (\ref{eq:mstar}) below, which passes through $-1$ at $m=m_*$ and $-3/2$ at $m=4m_*$, so the spectrum is close to $m^{-1}$ around the break without being a power law. A spread of $\kappa$ makes the decline more gradual, because the free energy at large $\kappa$ phase mixes fastest and survives to the largest $m$. With the $P(\kappa)$ measured at $\beta_i=1$ and $M=64$ (section~\ref{sim:kpar}) and $m_*$ evaluated at its mean, the local slope of (\ref{eq:Wspread}) stays within $0.15$ of $-1$ over a factor of five in $m$ around $m_*$. A spectrum measured over a limited range near $m_*$ can therefore look like a power law somewhat steeper than $m^{-1}$. For the comparison with simulations we solve the forced linear hierarchy at each $\kappa$, with the damping rate $1/(2T)$ on every amplitude and the hypercollisions of the run, and sum over the measured $P(\kappa)$ (appendix~\ref{app:model}); this reproduces (\ref{eq:Wspread}) at large $m$.

The form (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) is that of \citet[eq.~112]{ZoccoSchekochihin2011} for the Hermite spectrum of growing modes, with their $2\gamma$ replaced by $1/T$, and \citet[eqs~2.58--2.59]{Schekochihin2016} solved the same continuity equation in $m$ with the collisional sink $2\nu m$ in place of $1/T$. What the model adds is the identification of the sink with the perpendicular cascade, and of the wavenumber at which the free energy phase mixes with the one at which it was injected, which section~\ref{sec:frozen} keeps fixed while the field lines are frozen.

\subsection{Scales}\label{tc:scales}

The exponential in (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) matters once the age (\ref{eq:age}) of the free energy reaches $T$. In $\sqrt{2m}$, which grows in proportion to the age, it falls by a factor $e$ over the decay length $\ell_*=s\kappa T$, the distance in $\sqrt{2m}$ that the free energy covers in the time $T$. The spectrum therefore follows $m^{-1/2}$ up to the break
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mstar}
  m_*=\frac{\ell_*^2}{2}=\frac{(s\kappa T)^2}{2},
\end{equation}
and beyond it decays exponentially in $\sqrt m$, not as a power law.

To see how $m_*$ depends on the plasma parameters, we characterise the outer-scale turbulence by its perpendicular wavenumber $k_{\perp,\rm out}$, its wavenumber along $z$, $k_{z,\rm out}$, and its $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity $u_{\rm out}$ (section~\ref{sim:setup} defines them as averages over the outer scale). They give the outer eddy-turnover time $\tau_{\rm nl}=1/(k_{\perp,\rm out}u_{\rm out})$ and the nonlinearity parameter $\chi=k_{\perp,\rm out}u_{\rm out}/(k_{z,\rm out}v_A)$, and we write $T=c_T\tau_{\rm nl}$. The field-aligned wavenumber $\kappa$ is S09's $k_{\parallel0}$ (\S6.3), which without a parallel cascade stays that of the large-scale stirring. It is therefore of the order of $k_{z,\rm out}$; in the run at $\beta_i=1$, $64^3$ and $M=32$ the injected pattern has $\kappa_1=1.14\,k_1$ and the outer scale has $k_{z,\rm out}=1.11\,k_1$. With $\kappa\approx k_{z,\rm out}$ we get $s\kappa T=c_T\,(\vth/v_A)\,k_{z,\rm out}v_A/(k_{\perp,\rm out}u_{\rm out})=c_T\beta_i^{1/2}/\chi$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mstar-scaling}
  m_*\approx\frac{c_T^2}{2}\,\frac{\beta_i}{\chi^2} .
\end{equation}
If $T$ were the outer eddy-turnover time, $c_T\sim1$, this would be the phase-mixing threshold $\kappa\vth\sim\sqrt m\,k_\perp u_\perp$ of \citet[eq.~4.5]{Schekochihin2016}, written in KRMHD variables, and for $\chi\sim1$ the crossover $m\sim\beta_i$ stated by \citet{Kawazura2019}. How large $c_T$ is depends on the unit of time. At $64^3$, $T$ is 0.7 to 1.1 times the energy-containing time $E_A/\eps_A$ of the Alfv\'enic turbulence, the ratio of its energy to its injection rate (tables~\ref{tab:runs} and~\ref{tab:mstar}), so in that unit the prefactor is of order one. The energy-containing time is about $15\,\tau_{\rm nl}$, so in units of $\tau_{\rm nl}$ the same $T$ gives $c_T=11$--16, and at $\chi\approx1$ the break lies at 60 to 130 times $\beta_i$. These are values for our $64^3$ runs, not properties of the plasma. $T$ grows with the resolution, to $c_T=25$ at $256^3$ and $\beta_i=0.3$, where it is 1.6 times $E_A/\eps_A$ (section~\ref{sim:resolution}).

\subsection{What the simulations test}\label{tc:predictions}

The model has two inputs, $T$ and $P(\kappa)$. We measure $P(\kappa)$ by tracing the injected pattern along field lines, and $T$ as the median over $m$ of $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$. The simulations test the model in four ways.
\begin{itemize}
\item[(i)] \emph{Uniform sink.} $D_\perp(m)/W(m)$ is independent of $m$. Because $T$ is the median of this ratio, (i) checks the assumption (\ref{eq:uniformsink}); it is not a prediction.
\item[(ii)] \emph{No return flux.} The field-aligned flux is the flux $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$ that $W(m)$ carries by phase mixing at its measured field-aligned wavenumber. A return flux would make the flux fall increasingly below it as $m$ grows. The perpendicular sink also lowers the ratio, but by a few per cent and without a trend in $m$ (section~\ref{sim:flux}). Since the simulations satisfy the budget (\ref{eq:budget}), (ii) and (i) together fix the shape of $W(m)$: with the measured $T$ and $P(\kappa)$, $W(m)$ and the flux follow the model.
\item[(iii)] \emph{Dependence on $\beta_i$.} At fixed $\chi$ the Alfv\'enic turbulence does not depend on $\beta_i$, so $P(\kappa)$ is unchanged and, if $T$ is too, $m_*\propto\beta_i$. With a sink that depends on the age of the free energy (section~\ref{tc:limits}), the effective $T$ falls somewhat as $\beta_i$ decreases, because the same moments then hold older free energy.
\item[(iv)] \emph{Frozen wavenumber.} The field-aligned spectrum of the free energy stays at the wavenumbers of the injected pattern and does not change as the inertial range lengthens with the resolution.
\end{itemize}
Of these, (ii) and (iv) test the model independently of the measured $T$. The echo picture \citep{Meyrand2019} predicts instead a return flux along the field, which reduces the net field-aligned flux below $s\,c_m\,\kappa_1(m)\,W(m)$ at large $m$, and a field-aligned spectrum of $G$ that extends to larger wavenumbers as the inertial range lengthens, as $G$ inherits the parallel cascade of the Alfv\'enic fields. The model also assumes that $T$ is set by the turbulence and not by the dissipation. Section~\ref{sim:resolution} tests this at four resolutions, and it remains open.

\subsection{Limits of the model}\label{tc:limits}

The model rests on two assumptions that the exact result does not guarantee. First, it assumes that the field lines stay frozen long enough for the injected $\kappa$ to be kept, although the hyperdissipation of any simulation, and resistivity in a plasma, reconnect them, which \citet{Meyrand2019} argued gives $G$ the parallel structure of the Alfv\'enic fields. Second, it assumes that the perpendicular cascade removes the free energy in a time set by the turbulence and not by the dissipation. For a passive scalar in turbulence the rate at which variance is dissipated stays finite as the diffusivity goes to zero \citep{ShraimanSiggia2000,FalkovichGawedzkiVergassola2001}. \citet{DrivasEyink2017} showed that this dissipative anomaly requires Lagrangian spontaneous stochasticity, the non-uniqueness of trajectories as the diffusivity goes to zero, and \citet[\S VIII.C]{Eyink2018} argued that the same stochasticity breaks flux freezing in a turbulent inertial range. If the two go together, the frozen $\kappa$ of the first assumption must fail at the scales where the anomaly of the second operates. The model can survive this only if the free energy that phase mixes sits at scales where the field lines are still effectively frozen, and the relabelling acts on free energy that the perpendicular sink is already removing.

If the sink depends on the time $\vartheta$ since injection rather than being uniform, as section~\ref{sim:beta} finds, free energy injected at $\kappa$ reaches $m$ at the age $\vartheta=\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa)$, and (\ref{eq:Wmodel}) becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Wage}
  W(m)=\frac{\eps}{s\kappa\sqrt{2m}}\exp\!\Big(-\int_0^{\sqrt{2m}/(s\kappa)}\gamma(\vartheta)\,\dd\vartheta\Big),
\end{equation}
with $\gamma(\vartheta)$ the rate at which the perpendicular sink removes free energy of age $\vartheta$. We expect $\gamma$ to be small at first, while the cascade carries the fresh pattern towards the dissipation scale, and to level off once the perpendicular spectrum of that free energy has filled in. A uniform $1/T$ then removes too much free energy at small $m$ and too little at large $m$. If the sink is set by the Alfv\'enic turbulence and its dissipation and not by the streaming, one $\gamma(\vartheta)$ describes every $\beta_i$ at fixed $\chi$ and resolution.
