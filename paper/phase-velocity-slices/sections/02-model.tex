\section{The compressive sector of KRMHD}\label{sec:model}

For a plasma with one ion species and isothermal electrons, KRMHD \citep{Schekochihin2009} consists of reduced magnetohydrodynamics (RMHD) for the Alfv\'enic fluctuations and a kinetic equation for the compressive ones. We use the form of CMM \citep{Chandran2026}, whose section~3.2 and appendix~B restate and extend S09; their equation numbers refer to arXiv:2607.27981v1. Appendix~\ref{app:notation} compares our notation with that of CMM and S09.

\subsection{Alfv\'enic fields}\label{mod:alfven}

We set the Alfv\'en speed $v_A=1$ unless stated otherwise and take the mean field $\boldsymbol B_0$ along $\zhat$. The stream function $\Phi$ and the flux function $\Psi$ give the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ velocity $\uE=\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Phi$ and the perpendicular magnetic fluctuation $\boldsymbol b_\perp\equiv\delta\boldsymbol B_\perp/(4\pi n_im_i)^{1/2}=\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$, and to first order the unit vector along the field is $\bhat=\zhat+\boldsymbol b_\perp$; both $\uE$ and $\bhat$ are divergence-free. With the bracket $\{f,h\}=\partial_xf\,\partial_yh-\partial_yf\,\partial_xh=\zhat\cdot(\nabla f\times\nabla h)$, advection by $\uE$ is $\{\Phi,\cdot\}$ and the derivative along the field is $\gradpar\equiv\bhat\cdot\nabla=\partial_z+\{\Psi,\cdot\}$. The RMHD equations are \citep[eqs~17--18]{Schekochihin2009}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rmhd}
  \partial_t\Psi+\{\Phi,\Psi\}=\partial_z\Phi,\qquad
  \partial_t\nabla_\perp^2\Phi+\{\Phi,\nabla_\perp^2\Phi\}=\gradpar\nabla_\perp^2\Psi .
\end{equation}
The first, the induction equation $\partial_t\Psi=\gradpar\Phi$, states that the electric field along the total magnetic field vanishes. Equations (\ref{eq:rmhd}) are CMM's eqs~(3.23)--(3.24) in their normalised potentials (3.2) and do not contain the compressive fluctuations. The Elsasser potentials $z^\pm=\Phi\pm\Psi$ obey $\partial_tz^\pm=\pm\partial_zz^\pm$ linearly, so $z^+$ propagates toward $-z$ and $z^-$ toward $+z$.

\subsection{Kinetic equation and the two branches}\label{mod:branches}

The ion thermal speed is $\vth=(2T_i/m_i)^{1/2}$, so $s\equiv\vth/v_A=\beta_i^{1/2}$; we write $\tau=T_i/T_e$ and $Z=q_i/e$. The compressive part of the ion distribution is the function $g$ of S09 (CMM's $g_{09}$, their eq.~3.26), which obeys CMM's eq.~(3.25) (S09 eq.~157 without collisions),
\begin{equation}\label{mod:kinetic}
  \frac{\dd g}{\dd t}+v_\parallel\gradpar\Big[g+\Big(\frac{Z}{\tau}\frac{\delta n}{n}+\frac{v_\perp^2}{\vth^2}\frac{\delta B}{B}\Big)F_0\Big]=0,
  \qquad \frac{\dd}{\dd t}=\partial_t+\{\Phi,\cdot\},
\end{equation}
where $F_0$ is the three-dimensional Maxwellian. Quasineutrality and perpendicular pressure balance give (CMM eqs~3.27--3.28; S09 eqs~158--159),
\begin{equation}\label{mod:fields}
  \overline{g}=\frac{\delta n}{n}-\frac{\delta B}{B},\qquad
  \overline{(v_\perp^2/\vth^2)\,g}=-\frac{Z}{\tau}\frac{\delta n}{n}-2\Big(1+\frac{1}{\beta_i}\Big)\frac{\delta B}{B},
  \qquad \overline{(\cdots)}\equiv\frac{1}{n_i}\int\dd^3v\,(\cdots).
\end{equation}
These relations are algebraic: $\delta n/n$ and $\delta B/B$ at a point are linear functionals of $g$ at that point, with coefficients that do not depend on the wavenumber. At finite $k_\perp\rho_i$ they contain gyroaveraging operators.

We expand $g$ in Laguerre polynomials of $v_\perp^2/\vth^2$ with coefficients $G_l(v_\parallel)$ (S09 eq.~205; CMM eq.~B8; appendix~\ref{app:branches}). The source term in (\ref{mod:kinetic}) is linear in $v_\perp^2$, so the coefficients with $l\ge2$ obey $\dd G_l/\dd t+v_\parallel\gradpar G_l=0$ (CMM eq.~B10; S09 eqs~210 and~212). The coefficients with $l=0,1$ are coupled through $\delta n/n$ and $\delta B/B$; the combinations $G^\pm$ of CMM's eqs~(B5)--(B6) (S09 eqs~183--184) obey CMM's eq.~(B4) (S09 eq.~181),
\begin{equation}\label{mod:Gpm}
  \frac{\dd G^\pm}{\dd t}+v_\parallel\gradpar G^\pm
  =\frac{v_\parallel F_M(v_\parallel)}{\Lambda^\pm}\,\gradpar\int G^\pm(v_\parallel')\,\dd v_\parallel',
\end{equation}
where $F_M(v_\parallel)=\pi^{-1/2}\vth^{-1}e^{-v_\parallel^2/\vth^2}$ and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Lambdapm}
  \Lambda^\pm=-\frac{\tau}{Z}+\frac{1}{\beta_i}\pm\Delta,\qquad
  \Delta=\Big[\Big(1+\frac{\tau}{Z}\Big)^2+\frac{1}{\beta_i^2}\Big]^{1/2}
\end{equation}
(CMM eq.~B7; S09 eqs~182 and~204). We call the right-hand side of (\ref{mod:Gpm}), of strength $1/\Lambda^\pm$, the Landau coupling; in linear theory it produces the collisionless damping of the compressive fluctuations (S09, \S6.2.2). The coefficients with $l\ge2$ obey (\ref{mod:Gpm}) with $1/\Lambda=0$, and we call them the sector $\Lambda=\infty$. The compressive sector therefore consists of independent kinetic equations for $G^+$, $G^-$ and each $G_l$ with $l\ge2$, all advected by the same Alfv\'enic fields. Since $\Delta>1+\tau/Z$ and $\Delta>1/\beta_i$, $\Lambda^+-1>\beta_i^{-1}$ and $\Lambda^-<-\tau/Z$, so $\Lambda^+>1$ and $\Lambda^-<0$ for all $\beta_i,\tau/Z>0$ (S09, below eq.~200); at $\beta_i=\tau=Z=1$, $\Lambda^\pm=\pm\sqrt5$.

\subsection{One branch in the variable \texorpdfstring{$u$}{u}}\label{mod:onebranch}

We now drop the branch label, set $u=v_\parallel/\vth$, and write $F_M(u)=\pi^{-1/2}e^{-u^2}$ and $M_k[G]=\int u^kG\,\dd u$. Since $v_\parallel=su$ when $v_A=1$, equation (\ref{mod:Gpm}) becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:branch}
  \partial_tG+\{\Phi,G\}+s\,\gradpar(LG)=0,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:L}
  LG=uG-\frac{uF_M}{\Lambda}\,M_0[G]
\end{equation}
combines the streaming of the particles with the Landau coupling. It acts on the velocity dependence of $G$ at each point, and for $\Lambda=\infty$ it is multiplication by $u$. It contains no wavenumber, because the field relations (\ref{mod:fields}) are algebraic.

For given $\Phi$ and $\Psi$, equation (\ref{eq:branch}) is linear in $G$. Its nonlinear terms are the advection $\{\Phi,G\}$ and the part $s\{\Psi,LG\}$ of the streaming, which follows the perturbed field lines. The Alfv\'enic fields obey (\ref{eq:rmhd}) whatever $G$ does, so the compressive sector is passive. At finite $k_\perp\rho_i$ this fails; the induction equation, for example, contains $\gradpar(\delta n/n)$ (CMM, \S3.1).

\subsection{Hermite moments}\label{mod:hermite}

Phase mixing is made explicit by expanding $G$ in Hermite polynomials \citep{Kanekar2015,Schekochihin2016}, as CMM do (their eqs~B27 and~B30):
\begin{equation}\label{mod:hermiteexp}
  G(u)=\sum_{m=0}^\infty\frac{H_m(u)F_M(u)}{\sqrt{2^mm!}}\,g_m,\qquad
  g_m=\int\frac{H_m(u)}{\sqrt{2^mm!}}\,G(u)\,\dd u .
\end{equation}
The $g_m$ are CMM's $\tilde G_m$. The first two are the density moment $g_0=M_0[G]$ and the flow moment $g_1=\sqrt2\,M_1[G]$. Since $uH_m=H_{m+1}/2+mH_{m-1}$, the streaming term couples each moment to its two neighbours, and (\ref{eq:branch}) becomes
\begin{equation}\label{mod:hierarchy}
\begin{aligned}
  &\frac{\dd g_0}{\dd t}+s\gradpar\frac{g_1}{\sqrt2}=0,\qquad
  \frac{\dd g_1}{\dd t}+s\gradpar\Big[g_2+\Big(1-\frac1\Lambda\Big)\frac{g_0}{\sqrt2}\Big]=0,\\
  &\frac{\dd g_m}{\dd t}+s\gradpar\Big(\sqrt{\frac{m+1}{2}}\,g_{m+1}+\sqrt{\frac m2}\,g_{m-1}\Big)=0,\qquad m\ge2,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with $\dd/\dd t=\partial_t+\{\Phi,\cdot\}$ as in (\ref{mod:kinetic}). The Landau coupling enters only the equation for the flow moment $g_1$, where the parallel electric field and the mirror force change the coefficient of $g_0$ from $1/\sqrt2$ to $(1-1/\Lambda)/\sqrt2$. These are the equations of \citet[eqs~4.3--4.5]{Kanekar2015} with their $\alpha=-1/\Lambda$ and without their source and collisions, except that the moments are advected by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and stream along the perturbed field. For the vector $\boldsymbol g=(g_0,g_1,\dots)$ of the moments we write them as $\dd\boldsymbol g/\dd t+s\gradpar(A\boldsymbol g)=0$, where the ladder matrix $A$, the Hermite form of $L$, holds the coefficients of the streaming terms: $A_{m,m+1}=A_{m+1,m}=\sqrt{(m+1)/2}$, except $A_{10}=(1-1/\Lambda)/\sqrt2$.

The free energy of the branch, in units of $n_iT_i/2$, is $\sum_mW(m)$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fe}
  W(m)=p_m\langle g_m^2\rangle,\qquad p_0=1-\frac1\Lambda,\quad p_m=1\ \ (m\ge1),
\end{equation}
is the Hermite spectrum and $\langle\cdot\rangle$ is the average over the periodic box. In terms of $G$, $\sum_mW(m)=\langle\int G^2/F_M\,\dd u-M_0[G]^2/\Lambda\rangle$, which is S09's invariant $W^\pm_{\rm compr}$ (their eq.~198; CMM eq.~B13) up to the factor $n_iT_i/2$. It is positive because $\Lambda^+>1$, $\Lambda^-<0$ and $\Lambda=\infty$ for the other branches (S09, \S6.2.3). Since $p_mA_{m,m+1}=p_{m+1}A_{m+1,m}$, what the streaming term removes from one moment it adds to its neighbour, so that without forcing and dissipation the free energy is conserved; the same holds for the free energy at each field-aligned wavenumber.

We truncate the hierarchy at $m=M$ with the closure $g_{M+1}=0$, which keeps the first $M+1$ rows and columns of $A$. GANDALF \citep{Kanekar2025} integrates (\ref{eq:rmhd}) in Elsasser form together with (\ref{mod:hierarchy}) for one branch, with dissipation, hypercollisions and forcing. We checked to round-off that its right-hand side without dissipation and hypercollisions is (\ref{mod:hierarchy}) for both signs of $\Lambda$.

The rest of the paper concerns one equation (\ref{eq:branch}) with $\Lambda>1$, $\Lambda<0$ or $\Lambda=\infty$, which we call a branch whether it describes $G^\pm$ or a Laguerre coefficient with $l\ge2$. Section~\ref{sec:frozen} treats a collisionless and unforced branch except where stated; sections~\ref{sec:twochannel} and~\ref{sec:simulations} add forcing, dissipation and hypercollisions.

\subsection{The Hermite flux}\label{mod:flux}

Free energy moves between Hermite moments only through the streaming term. We multiply the equation for $g_n$ in (\ref{mod:hierarchy}) by $2p_ng_n$ and average over the box. The advection drops out because the flow is incompressible, and the streaming couples $g_n$ to $g_{n-1}$ and $g_{n+1}$. When these equations are summed over $n\le m$, the couplings cancel in pairs, because $p_nA_{n,n+1}=p_{n+1}A_{n+1,n}$ and $\langle f\gradpar h\rangle=-\langle h\gradpar f\rangle$ for $\nabla\cdot\bhat=0$. Only the coupling between $m$ and $m+1$ survives, and the free energy $E_{\le m}=\sum_{n\le m}W(n)$ in the moments up to $m$ obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fluxCD}
  \frac{\dd E_{\le m}}{\dd t}=-\Pi_{m+1/2},\qquad
  \Pi_{m+1/2}=s\,b_m\big\langle g_m\gradpar g_{m+1}-g_{m+1}\gradpar g_m\big\rangle,
\end{equation}
with $b_0=(1-1/\Lambda)/\sqrt2$ and $b_m=\sqrt{(m+1)/2}$ for $m\ge1$. The flux $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ is the free energy per unit time and volume that passes from moment $m$ to $m+1$. The flux must be computed with the full $\gradpar$: the Hermite flux of \citet{Meyrand2019} uses it, whereas a flux computed with $\partial_z$ alone, which we call the $k_z$-only flux, omits the part of the streaming that follows the tilt of the field lines.

With forcing of $g_0$ at the rate $\eps$, dissipation that acts on every $g_m$ in the same way and hypercollisions, the free energy in each moment obeys
\begin{equation}\label{eq:budget}
  \frac{\dd W(m)}{\dd t}=\Pi_{m-1/2}-\Pi_{m+1/2}+\eps\,\delta_{m0}-D_\perp(m)-D_\nu(m),\qquad \Pi_{-1/2}=0,
\end{equation}
where $D_\perp(m)$ and $D_\nu(m)$ are the rates at which the spatial dissipation and the hypercollisions remove free energy from moment $m$. The brackets and the hyperdissipation of GANDALF act on all moments alike, so (\ref{eq:budget}) holds for its equations up to the error of the dealiased brackets, with the flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) evaluated with the same brackets. In the simulations of section~\ref{sec:simulations} each term of (\ref{eq:budget}) is measured separately (appendix~\ref{app:methods}). We say that the budget closes when the measured terms, averaged over time, satisfy (\ref{eq:budget}). With the flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) they do so to better than $10^{-3}\,\eps$ in every moment of every run. The $k_z$-only flux omits part of the streaming, so by construction it cannot close the budget. The residual it leaves, up to 8\% of $\eps$ in our runs, measures the part of the flux carried by the streaming along the tilt of the field lines.

For one Fourier mode whose density moment is driven by white noise that injects free energy at the rate $\eps$, the steady state of linear phase mixing is known exactly \citep[eq.~4.10]{Kanekar2015}. At $\Lambda=\infty$ it is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linforced}
  W(m)=\frac{\eps}{s|k_z|}\,\frac{\Gamma(m+\frac12)}{\sqrt2\,m!}\;\simeq\;\frac{\eps}{s|k_z|\sqrt{2m}}\quad(m\gg1),
\end{equation}
where $\Gamma(\cdot)$ is Euler's gamma function; at every $\Lambda$ the large-$m$ form is $\eps/(s|k_z|\sqrt{2m})$. This is the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum of linear phase mixing. The flux (\ref{eq:fluxCD}) is $\eps$ through every $m$, so the flux and the spectrum are related by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cm}
  \Pi_{m+1/2}=c_m\,s|k_z|\,W(m),
\end{equation}
with $c_m=\sqrt2\,m!/\Gamma(m+\frac12)\simeq\sqrt{2m+\frac12}$ at $\Lambda=\infty$. At finite $\Lambda$ we compute $c_m$ from the same forced linear problem. Its spectrum is $W(m)=p_m\mathsf C_{mm}$, with $\mathsf C$ the stationary covariance of the hierarchy at one wavenumber, which solves the Lyapunov equation of appendix~\ref{app:model} with $1/T=0$ and no hypercollisions on an untruncated ladder; its flux is $\eps$ through every $m$. The resulting $c_m$ is slightly larger at small $m$ (1.68 against 1.60 at $m=1$ for $\Lambda=\sqrt5$) and has the same large-$m$ limit. We compute it for the $\Lambda$ and the forced moment of each run. Free energy injected at $m=0$ thus moves up the ladder at the speed $\dd m/\dd t=\Pi/W=c_ms|k_z|\simeq s|k_z|\sqrt{2m}$ and reaches $m\simeq(sk_zt)^2/2$ after a time $t$.
