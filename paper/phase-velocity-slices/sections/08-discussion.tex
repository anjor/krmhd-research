\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

The exact result of this paper is the frozen-line solution of section~\ref{sec:frozen}. With an ideal induction equation, smooth fields and no spatial dissipation of the compressive distribution, every volume-integrated single-point statistic, including $W(m)$ and the field-aligned Hermite flux, evolves as in linear phase mixing along the initial field lines, so such dynamics produces no stochastic echoes in $W(m)$.

The two-channel model extends this result to turbulence, where these conditions fail, by treating the spatial dissipation as a sink that removes free energy from every Hermite moment alike, while phase mixing proceeds at the injected field-aligned wavenumber. Its independent content is the relation between the flux and the spectrum and the frozen field-aligned wavenumber, and both hold in our runs from $64^3$ to $256^3$ grid points. The field-aligned flux is the flux that $W(m)$ carries by phase mixing at its measured field-aligned wavenumber, with no deficit that grows with $m$ in any run, so there is no return flux. At $\beta_i=0.3$ the field-aligned spectrum of the free energy is the same from $64^3$ to $256^3$, apart from a tail holding less than 0.5\% of it, so in our runs the relabelling of field lines that \citet{Meyrand2019} invoke does not give $G$ the parallel structure of the Alfv\'enic fields.

Our spectrum at $\beta_i=1$ differs from that of \citet{Meyrand2019}, who found $W(m)$ somewhat steeper than $m^{-1}$ at the same $\beta_i$ and a net flux to high $m$ much smaller than the injection rate. Their set-up differs from ours in four ways that matter here. They forced the compressive fluctuations at $m=1$, the parallel flow, on modes with $|k_x|,|k_y|,|k_z|\le2$, where we force $g_0$ at $|k_z|=k_1$; they damped $G$ with $\mu k_\perp^8$ and $\nu m^6$, where we use a fourth power of $k_\perp$ normalised to the grid and $\nu(m/M)^6$; they ran at $256^3$ to $512^3$ with $M=32$ to 128, against our runs at $\beta_i=1$ on $64^3$ and $128^3$ with $M=32$ and 64; and their Hermite flux is resolved in $k_\perp$ shells, which only in its sum over shells is a pure flux in $m$. In our model, forcing up to $|k_z|=2k_1$ raises the injected $\kappa$ and with it the break $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$, which flattens the spectrum. We tested the forcing directly (section~\ref{sim:mf1}). With $g_1$ forced on $|k_z|=k_1$ and $2k_1$ at $64^3$, $W(m)$ steepens at small $m$, from $m^{-0.54}$ to $m^{-0.66}$ over $2\le m\le8$, keeps a slope of about $-0.7$ beyond, and is reproduced by the model with the injected $P(\kappa)$ to within 3.5\%, with no return flux; at $m=16$ the net flux still carries 75\% of the injection. The forcing at $m=1$ therefore does not produce their spectrum at our resolution. That leaves the different dissipation of $G$ and their higher resolution, which our runs do not test. Their evidence that $G$ inherits the parallel cascade of the Alfv\'enic fields comes from tracing the perturbed field lines (their figure~3). The parallel spectra of the density and field-strength fluctuations along the lines track that of the Alfv\'enic velocity, and the parallel coherence length of the compressive fluctuations grows with their perpendicular one as that of the Alfv\'enic fluctuations does. In our runs the first does not hold. Along the same lines the spectrum of the density moment stays at the injected wavenumbers, with 0.18\% of its variance beyond $4\,k_1$ at $128^3$ against 3.2\% of the energy of the velocity (section~\ref{sim:kpar}). The second compares the fields at a given perpendicular separation, a scale-by-scale measure like the band-resolved wavenumber of section~\ref{sim:kpar}, which gives a copy of $g_0$ frozen to the field lines by construction the same growth with $k_\perp$ as $g_0$ itself. Running our diagnostics, including the frozen copy, on their set-up would show whether the difference lies in the set-up or in the measurement.

The remaining results are measurements at $64^3$. There the model accounts for how the break moves with $\beta_i$. At fixed $\chi$, $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$ grows as $\beta_iT^2$, and with the same Alfv\'enic turbulence, lowering $\beta_i$ from 1 to 0.1 brings the break down to $m_*\approx7$ and steepens $W(m)$ from small $m$. The trend that \citet{Kawazura2019,Kawazura2020} found in gyrokinetic simulations, $m^{-1/2}$ at high $\beta_i$ and close to $m^{-1}$ at $\beta_i=0.1$, is the trend the model gives without any echo, because $m_*$ falls with $\beta_i$. With $M$ raised until the break is resolved, $m_*$ grows as $\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$ for $0.05\le\beta_i\le0.3$ at $\chi\approx0.95$, close to proportional. At $\beta_i=1$ the break of our $64^3$ runs lies beyond $m=M$, and extending the measurement to $\beta_i=1$ is ongoing work. The prefactor is large. $T$ is 11 to 16 outer eddy-turnover times, so $m_*\approx(c_T^2/2)\beta_i/\chi^2$ is 60 to 130 times $\beta_i$ at $\chi\approx1$, not of order $\beta_i$ as the estimate with $T\sim\tau_{\rm nl}$ gives. At $64^3$, $T$ is 0.7 to 1.1 times the energy-containing time $E_A/\eps_A$ of the Alfv\'enic turbulence, which may be why it spans many outer eddy-turnover times, but at $\beta_i=0.3$ it grows faster than $E_A/\eps_A$ with the resolution. The sink is not uniform in $m$ but grows with the age of the free energy, and with that refinement (\ref{eq:Wage}) the sinks of all three $\beta_i$ collapse onto one function of age. At $\beta_i=0.05$, $m_*$ grows faster than $\chi^{-2}$ as $\chi$ falls, because $c_T$ rises; these runs also differ in $\eta$, so they do not separate $\chi$ from the dissipation. With the sink averaged over the range where the break is fitted, the decay length is still $s\kappa_1T$ to within 9\%, so what departs from $\chi^{-2}$ is $T$, not the competition between phase mixing and the sink.

The main open question is the limit of high resolution. The model needs a perpendicular cascade whose rate does not depend on the dissipation coefficients and field lines that stay frozen for the free energy that phase mixes. \citet{DrivasEyink2017} and \citet{Eyink2018} give reasons to expect that the first requires the failure of the second at small enough scales (section~\ref{tc:limits}). Our runs are not converged in the perpendicular resolution. Up to $256^3$ grid points $T$ grows by about the same amount with each doubling of the resolution, from 13 to 25 outer eddy-turnover times at $\beta_i=0.3$ (section~\ref{sim:resolution}), while the field lines stay frozen for the free energy that phase mixes, whose field-aligned spectrum does not change (section~\ref{sim:kpar}). This is consistent with that argument, but because $T$ has not converged it does not test it. If $T$ levels off at higher resolution, the argument implies that the field lines stop being frozen at the scales where the sink acts, and the model then holds only if the free energy that phase mixes sits at larger scales (section~\ref{tc:limits}). Our runs do not determine $T$, and with it $m_*$, independently of the dissipation. Whether $T$ converges once the inertial range is long enough, or keeps growing so that the perpendicular cascade loses ground to phase mixing as the dissipation weakens, needs higher resolution than we reach.

The results hold for $k_\perp\rho_i\to0$. At finite $k_\perp\rho_i$ the Landau coupling depends on the wavenumber, the advection depends on $v_\perp$ and the compressive fluctuations act back on the Alfv\'enic ones \citep{Chandran2026}, so the distribution is no longer carried by a single flow, its streaming term no longer acts at each point separately, and the frozen-line solution does not hold in this form.
