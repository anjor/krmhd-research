\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

Two results of this paper are exact. The compressive sector of KRMHD is a continuum of passive slices labelled by phase velocity, for each branch and in truncated hierarchies, for any smooth Alfv\'enic fields and without collisions (theorems~\ref{thm:slice} and~\ref{thm:trunc}), so the nonlinearity moves no free energy between phase velocities. With an ideal induction equation, smooth fields and no spatial dissipation of the compressive distribution, every volume-integrated single-point statistic, including $W(m)$ and the field-aligned Hermite flux, evolves as in linear phase mixing along the initial field lines (theorem~\ref{thm:frozen}), so such dynamics produces no stochastic echoes in $W(m)$.

The two-channel model extends these results to turbulence, where they do not apply directly, by treating spatial dissipation as a sink that removes free energy from every Hermite moment at the passive-scalar cascade rate. In the pilot simulation at $\beta_i=1$ and $\chi\approx1$ the model holds to the accuracy of the measurement. The perpendicular dissipation is close to an $m$-independent sink, and $W(m)$ and the field-aligned flux are those of phase mixing at the injected field-aligned wavenumber with that sink, with no deficit at large $m$. At $64^3$ the density moment acquires substantial structure along the field only at the perpendicular scales where the hyper-resistivity acts. In this run the relabelling of field lines that \citet{Meyrand2019} invoke happens, but only to free energy that is being removed at the same scales, and the free energy that phase mixes keeps its injected wavenumber.

Our spectrum at $\beta_i=1$ differs from that of \citet{Meyrand2019}, who found $W(m)$ somewhat steeper than $m^{-1}$ at the same $\beta_i$ and a net flux to high $m$ much smaller than the injection rate. Their set-up differs from ours in four ways that matter here. They forced the compressive fluctuations at $m=1$, the parallel flow, on modes with $|k_x|,|k_y|,|k_z|\le2$, where we force $g_0$ at $|k_z|=k_1$; they damped $g$ with $\mu k_\perp^8$ and $\nu m^6$, where we use a fourth power of $k_\perp$ normalised to the grid and $\nu(m/M)^6$; they ran at $256^3$ to $512^3$ with $M=32$ to 128, against our $64^3$ to $256^3$ with $M=32$ and 64; and their Hermite flux is resolved in $k_\perp$ shells, which only in its sum over shells is a pure flux in $m$. In our model each of the first three differences raises the break rather than lowering it: forcing up to $|k_z|=2k_1$ raises the injected $\kappa$, and a longer inertial range with sharper dissipation lengthens $T$, as our runs at $96^3$ to $256^3$ show, and $m_*=(s\kappa T)^2/2$ grows with both. The model therefore does not account for their steeper spectrum by these differences alone. We tested the forcing directly (section~\ref{sim:mf1}). With $g_1$ forced on $|k_z|=k_1$ and $2k_1$ at $64^3$, $W(m)$ steepens at small $m$, from $m^{-0.54}$ to $m^{-0.66}$ over $2\le m\le8$, keeps a slope of about $-0.7$ beyond, and is reproduced by the model with the injected $P(\kappa)$ to within 4\%, with no return flux; at $m=16$ the net flux still carries 75\% of the injection. The forcing at $m=1$ therefore does not produce their spectrum at our resolution. That leaves the different dissipation of $g$ and effects that appear only at their higher resolution, such as echoes that our runs do not resolve; in our runs a higher resolution makes the spectrum shallower, not steeper. Running our field-aligned diagnostics on their set-up would decide it. A diagnostic built on the Eulerian $k_z$ reports a backward flux of about 12\% of the forward flux in our runs at $64^3$, about 20\% at $128^3$ and about 30\% at $256^3$, where the field-aligned measures show none, so splits and fluxes of the free energy should be computed along the perturbed field. And the field-aligned wavenumber of $g$ measured with $k_z$ grows with $k_\perp$ much as that of the Alfv\'enic fields does, whereas measured along the field lines it grows more slowly, at $256^3$ from $1.0\,k_1$ to $1.6\,k_1$ between $k_\perp=1.5\,k_1$ and $6\,k_1$, against $1.1\,k_1$ to $2.2\,k_1$ for $\Phi$.

At fixed $\chi$ the model predicts that $m_*$ grows with $\beta_i$, as $\beta_iT^2$. At $\beta_i=1$ the break lies beyond the highest Hermite moment we keep, $m=M$; at $\beta_i=0.1$ it lies well below it and $W(m)$ steepens from small $m$, as predicted, with the same Alfv\'enic turbulence in every run. The trend that \citet{Kawazura2019,Kawazura2020} found in gyrokinetic simulations, $m^{-1/2}$ at high $\beta_i$ and close to $m^{-1}$ at $\beta_i=0.1$, is the trend the model gives without any echo, because $m_*$ falls with $\beta_i$. Fitted to the measured spectra, the decay rate in $\sqrt{2m}$ is the predicted $1/(s\kappa_1T)$ to within 7\% at every $\beta_i$ and both $M$. With $M$ raised until the break is resolved, $m_*$ grows as $\beta_i^{1.09\pm0.04}$ at $\chi\approx0.95$ for $0.05\le\beta_i\le0.3$, close to the $m_*\propto\beta_i$ of (\ref{eq:mstar-scaling}). Extending this measurement to $\beta_i=1$, where resolving the break needs $M$ of several hundred, is ongoing work. The comparison also showed where the model is too simple: the perpendicular sink is not uniform in $m$ but grows with the age of the free energy, and with that refinement (\ref{eq:Wage}) the sinks of all runs collapse onto one function of age. Against $\chi$ the estimate $m_*\sim\beta_i/\chi^2$ holds only near $\chi\approx1$. At $\beta_i=0.05$ the local exponent of $m_*$ in $\chi$ is $-2.5$ and $-2.7$ down to $\chi\approx0.56$ and $-4.1$ between 0.56 and 0.40. In units of the outer eddy time the sink is close to the same function of age at $\chi\ge0.56$ and weaker at $\chi=0.40$, as expected when the outer-scale turbulence becomes weak. With the rate averaged over the range where the break is fitted, the decay length is still $s\kappa_1T$ to within 9\%, so what fails at small $\chi$ is the assumption $T\propto\tau_{\rm nl}$, not the competition between phase mixing and the sink. The model also predicts that the share of the free energy dissipated by collisions falls as the collisional cutoff $m_c$ rises, as $\exp(-\sqrt{2m_c}/(s\kappa T))$; the runs at $M=32$ and $M=64$ show this fall, and with the traced $P(\kappa)$ the model gives the shares to within 0.06. If this holds as $m_c\to\infty$, the free energy that phase mixes is in the end removed at small perpendicular scales, and the fraction dissipated by collisions in velocity space at fixed $\beta_i$ and $\chi$ is set by where the collisions act; in a real plasma the perpendicular cascade of the slices stops at the ion gyroscale, beyond the reach of KRMHD.

The main open question is the limit of high resolution. The model needs a perpendicular cascade whose rate does not depend on the dissipation coefficients and field lines that stay frozen for the free energy that phase mixes. \citet{DrivasEyink2017} and \citet{Eyink2018} give reasons to expect that the first requires the failure of the second at small enough scales (section~\ref{tc:limits}). Our runs do not reach a cascade rate independent of the dissipation. Up to $256^3$ grid points $T$ grows by about the same amount with each doubling of the resolution, from 13 to 25 outer eddy times at $\beta_i=0.3$, and the density moment gains field-aligned structure gradually through the longer inertial range, although the field-aligned wavenumber of the moments as a whole rises only from about $1.25\,k_1$ to $1.35\,k_1$ (section~\ref{sim:resolution}). With the $T$ and $\kappa$ measured at each resolution the model still describes $W(m)$ to within $-9\%$ and $+13\%$, and there is still no return flux. The model therefore describes the spectrum at each resolution with the $T$ measured there, but in our runs $T$, and with it $m_*$, has no value independent of the dissipation. Whether $T$ converges once the inertial range is long enough, or keeps growing so that the perpendicular cascade loses ground to phase mixing as the dissipation weakens, needs higher resolution than we reach.

The results hold for $k_\perp\rho_i\to0$. At finite $k_\perp\rho_i$ the Landau coupling depends on the wavenumber, the advection depends on $v_\perp$ and the compressive fluctuations act back on the Alfv\'enic ones \citep{Chandran2026}, so the slices no longer decouple. Velocity-dependent collisions are the only terms in KRMHD that exchange free energy between slices (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}).
