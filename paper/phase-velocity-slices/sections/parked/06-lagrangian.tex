\section{Frozen field lines}\label{sec:lagrangian}

When the field lines are frozen into the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow, which is common to all slices (\ref{eq:Uu}), each slice streams, in the frame of the flow, along field lines that do not move. Under the hypotheses (\ref{eq:hyp}) below, every volume-integrated single-point statistic, including the Hermite spectrum, then evolves as in linear phase mixing along the initial field lines (corollary~\ref{cor:singlepoint}), and stochastic echoes in $W(m)$ require terms that these hypotheses exclude. This Lagrangian linearity is due to S09 (\S6.3), who showed with the Cauchy solution for the perturbed field that in the Lagrangian frame of $\uE$ the derivative $\bhat\cdot\nabla$ is taken along the field lines at $t=0$ (their eqs~214--216), and concluded that the compressive fluctuations do not cascade to small scales along the moving field lines. \citet[\S\S2.6 and~4.1]{Kunz2015} restated the result for pressure-anisotropic plasmas as a one-dimensional linear equation, and \citet[\S2.3.1]{LithwickGoldreich2001} noted that a passive scalar in MHD turbulence would, without dissipation, be frozen to the field lines and could not cascade along them. We add the solution operator, the hypotheses with the terms that violate them, and the use of volume preservation to obtain exact statements about volume-integrated statistics. None of the works cited considers the Hermite spectrum or echoes.

\subsection{The frozen-line theorem}

We treat one branch at a time, with state $g(\boldsymbol r,t)$: the function $G(\boldsymbol r,\cdot,t)$ of $u$, or $(g_0,\dots,g_M)$ in a truncated hierarchy, where $A$ replaces $L$. With collisions $-\mathcal Cg$ as in (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}), (\ref{eq:branch}) reads
\begin{equation}\label{lag:kin}
  \partial_tg+\{\Phi,g\}+s\,\gradpar(Lg)=-\mathcal Cg .
\end{equation}
The Laguerre sectors obey it with $\Lambda=\infty$ and share the advecting fields (\ref{eq:sameflow}), so the results apply to the whole compressive distribution.

Let $D_t=\partial_t+\{\Phi,\cdot\}$ and $\gradpar(t)=\partial_z+\{\Psi(\cdot,t),\cdot\}$, let $\boldsymbol X_t$ be the flow map of $\uE$, with $\partial_t\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi)=\uE(\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi),t)$ and $\boldsymbol X_0(\boldsymbol\xi)=\boldsymbol\xi$, and let $\mathcal X_th=h\circ\boldsymbol X_t^{-1}$ turn a function of the labels $\boldsymbol\xi$ into a function of position. The first of equations (\ref{eq:rmhd}) states that the residual $R=\partial_t\Psi+\{\Phi,\Psi\}-\partial_z\Phi$ vanishes. The hypotheses are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hyp}
\begin{minipage}{0.86\linewidth}
\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\leftmargin}{2.9em}\setlength{\labelwidth}{2.5em}\setlength{\labelsep}{0.4em}\setlength{\itemsep}{0.6ex}\setlength{\parsep}{0pt}\setlength{\topsep}{0pt}}
\item[(H1)] \emph{Ideal induction.} On $[0,T]$ the residual $R$ depends on $z$ and $t$ only. Nothing is assumed about the vorticity equation: $\Phi$ may be forced, viscous or prescribed.
\item[(H2)] \emph{Kinetic equation.} The branch has $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, and $g$ obeys (\ref{lag:kin}), where $\mathcal C$ is a linear operator on the velocity variable that acts at each point with a kernel independent of $\boldsymbol r$ and of the fields (it may depend on $t$), and is bounded or dissipative, $\mathrm{Re}\int\fe{\bar f}{\mathcal Cf}\,\dd^3r\ge0$. There is no spatial dissipation of $g$ and no source.
\item[(H3)] \emph{Smoothness.} $\Phi$, $\Psi$ and all their spatial derivatives are continuous on $\mathbb T^3\times[0,T]$, except that $\Phi$ may jump at finitely many times; $[0,T]$ lies within the interval on which the Alfv\'enic solution exists and is smooth.
\end{list}
\end{minipage}
\end{equation}
Under (H3), $\boldsymbol X_t$ is a volume-preserving diffeomorphism of $\mathbb T^3$, continuously differentiable in $t$; only continuity of the fields in $t$ is needed, so white-noise forcing of $\Phi$ is allowed, and across kicks of $\Phi$ the relabellings compose. The frozen generator $L_0=s\,\gradpar(0)\,L+\mathcal C$ describes the linear problem with the field lines fixed in their initial shape and no flow; $e^{-tL_0}$ is its solution operator (a propagator if $\mathcal C$ depends on $t$).

\begin{theorem}[Frozen-line theorem]\label{thm:frozen}
Assume (\ref{eq:hyp}), and let $g$ be a classical solution of (\ref{lag:kin}) on $[0,T]$, with $g$ and $Lg$ continuously differentiable in $(\boldsymbol r,t)$ between the jump times of $\Phi$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozen}
  g(t)=\mathcal X_t\,e^{-tL_0}\,g(0),\qquad 0\le t\le T .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With $\bhat=\zhat+\zhat\times\nabla_\perp\Psi$ one has $\uE\times\bhat=\nabla_\perp\Phi+\{\Phi,\Psi\}\,\zhat$, so $\partial_t\bhat-\nabla\times(\uE\times\bhat)=\zhat\times\nabla R$, which vanishes if and only if $R$ depends on $z$ and $t$ only; (H1) is the ideal induction equation for $\bhat$. Since $\nabla\cdot\uE=\nabla\cdot\bhat=0$, it has Cauchy's solution \citep[example~3]{Webb2014} $\bhat(\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi),t)=(\partial\boldsymbol X_t/\partial\boldsymbol\xi)\,\bhat(\boldsymbol\xi,0)$, and the chain rule gives, for a differentiable function $h$ of the labels,
\begin{equation}\label{lag:intertwine}
  \gradpar(t)\,\mathcal X_th=\mathcal X_t\,\gradpar(0)h .
\end{equation}
Equivalently, the Jacobi identity gives
\begin{equation}\label{lag:commutator}
  [D_t,\gradpar]\,f=\{R,f\},
\end{equation}
which vanishes under (H1); since $D_t\mathcal X_th=0$, $\gradpar(t)\mathcal X_th$ is then carried by the flow from $\gradpar(0)h$. Now write $g(t)=\mathcal X_t\hat g(t)$. By (\ref{lag:kin}), $\partial_t\hat g=(D_tg)\circ\boldsymbol X_t=-[s\gradpar(t)(Lg)+\mathcal Cg]\circ\boldsymbol X_t$. The operators $L$ and $\mathcal C$ act at each point with kernels independent of $\boldsymbol r$ (for $L$ because the field relations of KRMHD are algebraic), so they commute with composition by $\boldsymbol X_t$, and (\ref{lag:intertwine}) gives $\partial_t\hat g=-L_0\hat g$ with $\hat g(0)=g(0)$, whose solution is unique (see below).
\end{proof}

Without collisions, (\ref{eq:frozen}) decouples into slices: the map $\mathcal A$ to the amplitudes (\ref{eq:aunit}) acts on $u$ alone, commutes with $\mathcal X_t$ and $\gradpar(0)$, and takes $L$ to multiplication by $u$ (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}), so that
\begin{equation}\label{lag:sliceform}
  a_u(t)=\mathcal X_t\,T_{sut}\,a_u(0),\qquad T_\ell=e^{-\ell\gradpar(0)} ,
\end{equation}
where the unitary operator $T_\ell$ moves a function a distance $\Delta z=\ell$ along the field lines of $\bhat(0)$. Each slice is carried rigidly along the initial field lines at the speed $su$ and then relabelled by the flow; this makes exact, for the slices, the remark of S09 (\S6.2.4) that the ballistic response is also the nonlinear solution in Lagrangian variables. The same holds at the nodes $u_j$ of a truncated hierarchy (theorem~\ref{thm:trunc}). With collisions the slices stay coupled through $\mathcal C$ (\ref{eq:sliceforcing}), but the flow is still removed.

For $\Lambda\notin[0,1]$, $\|f\|^2=\int\fe{\bar f}{f}\,\dd^3r$ is a norm (theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}) for which $s\gradpar(0)L$ is antisymmetric, so the collisionless $e^{-tL_0}$ preserves the free energy, a dissipative $\mathcal C$ decreases it, and the same estimate for the difference of two solutions (with Gronwall's inequality for a bounded $\mathcal C$) gives uniqueness. For $0<\Lambda<1$ the frozen problem is ill posed, because $L$ has the eigenvalues $\pm\ii\gamma$ (appendix~\ref{app:spectral}). Whether solutions of the ideal three-dimensional RMHD equations stay smooth is not known in general, so (H3) is an assumption on $[0,T]$.

\subsection{Single-point statistics}

Let $O$ be a function of the local state, all the velocity-space information at one point for every branch and Laguerre sector, and of finitely many of its derivatives along the field. We call
\begin{equation}\label{lag:Odef}
  \mathcal O_t[f]=\int O\big(f,\gradpar(t)f,\gradpar(t)^2f,\dots\big)\,\dd^3r
\end{equation}
a single-point statistic. Volume preservation and $\gradpar(t)^k\mathcal X_t=\mathcal X_t\gradpar(0)^k$ give $\mathcal O_t[\mathcal X_ty]=\mathcal O_0[y]$ whenever either side is defined.

\begin{corollary}[Single-point statistics]\label{cor:singlepoint}
Assume (\ref{eq:hyp}), and let $h(t)=e^{-tL_0}g(0)$. Then, for every single-point statistic (\ref{lag:Odef}) and $0\le t\le T$,
\begin{equation*}
  \mathcal O_t[g(t)]=\mathcal O_0[h(t)] .
\end{equation*}
Every volume-integrated single-point statistic of $g$ therefore takes the value it has in the linear problem along the field lines frozen at $t=0$, with no flow.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By theorem~\ref{thm:frozen}, $g(t)=\mathcal X_th(t)$; take $y=h(t)$ above.
\end{proof}

The corollary covers the Hermite spectrum $W(m,t)=p_m\langle g_m^2\rangle$, the free energy, the variances of $\delta n$ and $\delta B_\parallel$, the slice correlation matrix $C(u,u';t)=\langle a_ua_{u'}\rangle$, the collisional dissipation rate $\varepsilon_\nu$, the Hermite flux $\Pi_{m+1/2}$ with the full $\gradpar$ (\ref{eq:fluxCD}), and the one-point distributions of the $g_m$. It does not cover spectra in $k_\perp$ or $k_z$ or fluxes resolved in $k_\perp$ shells, which involve Fourier filters that do not commute with $\mathcal X_t$, the flux computed with $\partial_z$, or two-point statistics at a fixed Eulerian separation.

Without collisions, (\ref{lag:sliceform}), volume preservation and the unitarity of $T_\ell$ give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:frozencorr}
  C(u,u';t)=\big\langle a_u(0)\,T_{s(u'-u)t}\,a_{u'}(0)\big\rangle ,
\end{equation}
the correlation of the initial slices at the field-aligned separation $s(u'-u)t$. This integrates the two-slice law of proposition~\ref{prop:twoslice}: phase mixing is the decay of the initial correlations with separation along the frozen field lines, and (\ref{eq:frozencorr}) fixes $W(m,t)$ through (\ref{eq:dictionary}). Section~\ref{sec:numerics} and figure~\ref{fig:frozen} compare covered and uncovered statistics with integrations of GANDALF's right-hand side.

\subsection{Forcing}

With a source $F(\boldsymbol r,t)$ added to (\ref{lag:kin}), Duhamel's formula in the labels reads
\begin{equation}\label{lag:duhamel}
  g(t)=\mathcal X_t\Big[e^{-tL_0}g(0)+\int_0^te^{-(t-t')L_0}\,\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}F(t')\,\dd t'\Big] .
\end{equation}
By (\ref{lag:intertwine}), $\mathcal X_{t'}e^{-(t-t')L_0}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}=e^{-(t-t')L_{t'}}$ with $L_{t'}=s\,\gradpar(t')\,L+\mathcal C$: the source injected at $t'$ phase-mixes along the field lines frozen at $t'$ and is then relabelled by the flow. Let $(\mathfrak F_t)_{t\ge0}$ be a filtration to which $g(0)$, the Alfv\'enic fields and the forcing are adapted, and let $F\,\dd t=\dd Y$, where $Y$ is a Wiener process with spatially smooth realisations and covariance operator $\Sigma$, whose increment $Y(t'')-Y(t')$ is independent of $\mathfrak F_{t'}$ for $t''>t'$; we call such forcing non-anticipating. Alfv\'enic forcing correlated with $Y$ at the same instant is allowed, since the flow responds to a kick only after it.

\begin{corollary}[White forcing]\label{cor:forcing}
Let the forcing be non-anticipating, let (H1) and (H3) hold for every realisation, and let $g$ obey (\ref{lag:kin}) with the source $F$ and otherwise satisfy (H2). For every single-point statistic (\ref{lag:Odef}) whose integrand is a quadratic form,
\begin{equation}\label{lag:meanforced}
  \mathrm E\,\mathcal O_t[g(t)]=\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_0\big[e^{-tL_0}g(0)\big]+\int_0^t\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_{t'}\big[e^{-(t-t')L_{t'}}\chi\big]\,\dd t' ,
\end{equation}
where $\chi$ is a random field with zero mean and covariance $\Sigma$, independent of all other variables. In particular, $\mathrm E\,W(m,t)$ is the sum of the linear responses to the initial condition and to the individual injections, each phase-mixing along the field lines frozen at its injection time.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}[Sketch of proof]
In the labels, $\dd\hat g=-L_0\hat g\,\dd t+\mathcal X_t^{-1}\dd Y$, solved by the bracket in (\ref{lag:duhamel}) with an It\^o integral whose integrand is fixed by $\mathfrak F_{t'}$; the noise is additive and $\boldsymbol X_t$ is differentiable in $t$, so the It\^o and Stratonovich readings coincide. Relabelling gives $\mathcal O_t[g(t)]=\mathcal O_0[\hat g(t)]$. The cross term between $e^{-tL_0}g(0)$, fixed by $\mathfrak F_0$, and the stochastic integral has zero mean, the It\^o isometry turns the rest into $\int_0^t\mathrm E\,\mathcal O_0[e^{-(t-t')L_0}\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}\chi]\,\dd t'$, and $\mathcal O_0[\mathcal X_{t'}^{-1}y]=\mathcal O_{t'}[y]$ with $y=e^{-(t-t')L_{t'}}\chi$ gives (\ref{lag:meanforced}).
\end{proof}

The same holds, with sums in place of integrals, for independent zero-mean kicks $g\to g+\Delta Y_n$ added once per time step. The corollary concerns the mean: a single realisation contains cross terms between injections at different times, which involve the relabelling between them. For forcing correlated in time, the mean cross terms between injections closer together than the correlation time survive and carry that relabelling. A quartic statistic such as $\langle g_m^4\rangle$ contains products of two injections at one point and is not covered. Because (H1) excludes resistivity, the corollary concerns finite intervals of smooth evolution, not resistive steady states.

\subsection{Field-aligned wavenumbers}

Since $\bhat(t)$ is smooth and divergence-free, $-\gradpar(t)$ generates a strongly continuous unitary group of translations along its field lines on $L^2(\mathbb T^3)$, so $K_\parallel(t)=-\ii\,\gradpar(t)$ is self-adjoint by Stone's theorem \citep[e.g.][]{Kato1966}. Its spectral variable $k_\parallel$ is the field-aligned wavenumber; its spectral measure $E_t$ acts on position alike at every velocity and reduces in a uniform field to the Fourier filter in $k_z$. The field-aligned spectrum of the free energy is $\mu_t(I)=\|E_t(I)\,g(t)\|^2$, and that of a slice uses the norm of $L^2(\mathbb T^3)$.

\begin{corollary}[Field-aligned wavenumbers]\label{cor:kpar}
Assume (\ref{eq:hyp}). For each $t\in[0,T]$ the operator $K_\parallel(t)$ is self-adjoint, and for every Borel set $I\subset\mathbb R$
\begin{equation}\label{lag:kparsub}
  E_t(I)\,g(t)=\mathcal X_t\,e^{-tL_0}\,E_0(I)\,g(0) .
\end{equation}
The spectral subspaces of $K_\parallel(0)$ are therefore invariant under the dynamics in the labels; for real $g$, the complex-conjugate parts $E_t(0,\infty)g$ and $E_t(-\infty,0)g$ evolve separately, so no part of $g$ changes the sign of its field-aligned wavenumber. Without collisions, $\mu_t(I)=\mu_0(I)$ for every $I$, and the field-aligned spectrum of each slice is constant in time. If $\mathcal C$ is dissipative, $\mu_t(I)$ does not increase.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By (\ref{lag:intertwine}) and the unitarity of $\mathcal X_t$, $E_t(I)=\mathcal X_tE_0(I)\mathcal X_t^{-1}$, and $E_0(I)$ commutes with $\gradpar(0)$, hence with $e^{-tL_0}$, so (\ref{eq:frozen}) gives (\ref{lag:kparsub}) and $\mu_t(I)=\|E_0(I)\,e^{-tL_0}g(0)\|^2$. Without collisions, $e^{-tL_0}$ multiplies the component of the slice at $u$ at field-aligned wavenumber $k_\parallel$ by $e^{-\ii suk_\parallel t}$, which gives the statements for each slice and, by theorem~\ref{thm:spectrum}, $\mu_t(I)=\mu_0(I)$. With collisions, $y(t)=e^{-tL_0}E_0(I)g(0)$ obeys $\dd\|y\|^2/\dd t=-2\,\mathrm{Re}\int\fe{\bar y}{\mathcal Cy}\,\dd^3r\le0$.
\end{proof}

In a uniform field the two parts are those with $k_z>0$ and $k_z<0$, and the corollary makes precise the conclusion of S09 (\S6.3) that the compressive fluctuations do not cascade along the moving field lines. In the frozen-in shear $\Phi=-Szy$, $\Psi=-Sty$, an exact but not periodic solution of (\ref{eq:rmhd}) with $\gradpar(t)=\partial_z+St\,\partial_x$ and $\boldsymbol X_t(\boldsymbol\xi)=(\xi_x+S\xi_zt,\,\xi_y,\,\xi_z)$, a Fourier mode of the labels becomes a Fourier mode of position with
\begin{equation}\label{lag:shear}
  k_z(t)=k_z-Stk_x,\qquad k_\parallel(t)=k_z(t)+Stk_x=k_z ,
\end{equation}
where $k_\parallel(t)$ is its eigenvalue under $K_\parallel(t)$. Its Eulerian wavenumber changes sign at $t=k_z/(Sk_x)$ while its field-aligned wavenumber stays fixed, so a diagnostic that sorts the free energy by the sign of $k_z$ counts the mode as reversed, although nothing has changed along the field.

\subsection{What breaks the theorem}

A non-ideal term in the induction equation, such as resistivity or hyper-resistivity on $\Psi$, parallel dissipation of $z^\pm$, or forcing of $\Psi$ (for example independent forcing of $z^+$ and $z^-$), in general makes $R$ depend on $x$ and $y$, so that (\ref{lag:commutator}) does not vanish and the field lines are not carried by the flow. Dissipation of $g$ that depends on $k_\perp$ or $k_z$ does not in general commute with the relabelling and violates (H2). Fourier truncation does not commute with the relabelling either; its error vanishes as the resolution increases at fixed $t$, but not uniformly in $t$. In the equations that GANDALF integrates, the perpendicular and parallel damping act alike on $z^\pm$ and every $g_m$ and violate (H1) and (H2), the hypercollisions satisfy (H2), and forcing that adds the same increment to $z^+$ and $z^-$ keeps (H1). Section~\ref{sec:numerics} tests these statements by the dependence on resolution of the departure from the frozen-line prediction.

\subsection{Echoes}

Under (\ref{eq:hyp}), $W(m,t)$ and the field-aligned Hermite flux evolve as in the linear problem $\partial_th=-L_0h$, so a return of free energy to low $m$ must already be present there, as a linear echo of initial unmixing correlations, the recurrence of a truncated hierarchy, or slow phase mixing at small field-aligned wavenumbers; with white forcing the same holds for $\mathrm E\,W(m,t)$. In this sense ideal, smooth dynamics without spatial dissipation of $g$ cannot produce stochastic echoes in $W(m)$.

In drift kinetics with a straight guide field \citep{Schekochihin2016,Parker2016} and in a solvable Vlasov--Poisson model \citep{AdkinsSchekochihin2018} the particles stream along a fixed direction. In drift kinetics $[D_t,\partial_z]f=-\{\partial_z\Phi,f\}\neq0$: the $z$-dependent flow couples positive and negative $k_\parallel$, which is the mechanism of the stochastic echo, and a flow independent of $z$ gives no echo \citetext{\citealp[appendix~B]{Schekochihin2016}; \citealp[\S5.2]{KanekarThesis2015}}. In KRMHD the shear $\partial_z\uE$ that would change $k_\parallel$ also bends the field lines together with $g$, so under (H1) the commutator (\ref{lag:commutator}) vanishes and no part of $g$ moves between positive and negative field-aligned wavenumbers (corollary~\ref{cor:kpar}), although its Eulerian $k_z$ can change sign (\ref{lag:shear}).

\citet{Meyrand2019} found in simulations of KRMHD turbulence a Hermite spectrum a little steeper than $m^{-1}$, against $m^{-1/2}$ for linear phase mixing, and attributed it and the return flux to stochastic echoes, in which scattering by a counter-propagating Alfv\'en wave reverses the $k_\parallel$ of a compressive wave; \citet{Kawazura2020} suggested that an $m^{-1}$ spectrum at $\beta_i=0.1$ and $k_\perp\rho_i\ll1$ may indicate a suppression of phase mixing by stochastic echoes, in gyrokinetic simulations that also resolve $k_\perp\rho_i\gtrsim1$ and to which the theorem does not apply directly. The theorem does not contradict these measurements. \citet{Meyrand2019} used hyper-resistivity and a term $-\mu k_\perp^8g_m$, which violate (H1) and (H2), and argued, citing S09, that any resistivity destroys the identities of the field lines, and with them the Lagrangian frame, within about one eddy-turnover time. An echo of the kind they describe requires such terms, or a failure of deterministic flux freezing for rough fields as $\eta\to0$, which (H3) excludes. Such a breakdown was reported by \citet{Eyink2013} at high conductivity; \citet[\S VIII.C]{Eyink2018} argued that in a turbulent inertial range the ideal Ohm's law does not imply flux freezing, and on these grounds disputed the flux-freezing statements of S09 and \citet{Kunz2015}; \citet{Jafari2026} argued that magnetic flux is then conserved only statistically, along stochastic path lines. The Hermite flux of \citet{Meyrand2019}, resolved in $k_\perp$ shells, is constrained only in its sum over shells. \citet{Adkins2025}, in agreement with \citet{Zhou2023}, found that echoes play no significant role in the kinetic reduced electron heating model of \citet{ZoccoSchekochihin2011}, and suggested that the passivity of the KRMHD compressive sector may be why echoes appear there; the theorem shows that passivity together with ideal induction excludes stochastic echoes in $W(m)$ for smooth solutions without spatial dissipation of $g$. Which non-ideal mechanism operates in the simulations, and whether its effect survives $\eta\to0$, is left for future work.
