We show that in kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics (KRMHD) the compressive fluctuations are exactly a continuum of passive scalars, or slices, labelled by the parallel phase velocity $u$. The slices are the Case--Van Kampen components of the compressive distribution, which in KRMHD do not depend on the wavenumber. Without collisions the slices evolve independently in any smooth Alfv\'enic fields, each carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed field at its own speed. Every slice therefore conserves its energy, which gives an infinite family of quadratic invariants. The free energy and the invariants $\Gamma$ and $\Hph$ of Chandran, Mallet \& Meyrand (2026) weight the slice energies by $1$, $u$ and $1/u$, and $\Gamma$ is the net flux of free energy along the guide field. Phase mixing is the decorrelation of slices by their relative streaming, and fully decorrelated slices give the $m^{-1/2}$ spectrum of forced phase mixing. Linear phase mixing conserves all the invariants while it moves all of the free energy to arbitrarily large Hermite order $m$, so none of them limits the transfer to large $m$ in freely evolving solutions, unlike the enstrophy in the argument of Fj{\o}rtoft for two-dimensional flow. A Hermite hierarchy truncated at order $M$ is exactly $M+1$ passive slices for the closures $g_{M+1}=0$ and $g_{M+1}=g_{M-1}$. For the first they sit at the Gauss quadrature nodes of the Case--Van Kampen spectral density, and the phase-space helicity of the retained moments is conserved only at odd $M$. We check these results with GANDALF.
