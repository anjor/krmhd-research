We show that in kinetic reduced magnetohydrodynamics (KRMHD) the compressive fluctuations are exactly a continuum of passive scalars, or slices, labelled by the parallel phase velocity $u$. The slices are the projections of the compressive distribution on the Case--Van Kampen modes of each branch, beams of particles dressed by the Landau coupling, which do not depend on the wavenumber because the field relations of KRMHD are algebraic. Without collisions the slices evolve independently in any smooth three-dimensional Alfv\'enic fields, each carried by the $\boldsymbol E\times\boldsymbol B$ flow and streaming along the perturbed magnetic field at its own speed. The energy of every slice is therefore conserved, which gives an infinite family of quadratic invariants: the free energy and the invariants $\Gamma$ and $\Hph$ of Chandran, Mallet \& Meyrand (2026) weight the slice energies by $1$, $u$ and $1/u$, and $\Gamma$ is the net flux of free energy along the guide field. Phase mixing is the decorrelation of slices by their relative streaming along the field, and fully decorrelated slices give the $m^{-1/2}$ Hermite spectrum of forced linear phase mixing. The invariants fix the free energy at each phase velocity but not the correlations between slices, and linear phase mixing conserves all of them while it moves all of the free energy to arbitrarily large Hermite order $m$. None of them therefore constrains the transfer of free energy to large $m$ in the way that, by the argument of Fj{\o}rtoft, the enstrophy constrains the transfer of energy to small scales in two-dimensional flow; this concerns freely evolving solutions, not forced steady states. A Hermite hierarchy truncated at order $M$ is exactly $M+1$ passive slices for either closure, $g_{M+1}=0$ or $g_{M+1}=g_{M-1}$. For $g_{M+1}=0$ they sit at the nodes of the Gauss quadrature rule of the Case--Van Kampen spectral density, and the phase-space helicity of the retained moments is conserved at odd $M$ but not at even $M$. The fastest slice, whose phase velocity grows as $(2M)^{1/2}$, sets the time step of an explicit scheme. We check these results with the GANDALF code.
